# Web research 2026-09-02 — Wave 62
## RAS 022 series census: a near-uniform c.30 × 48.5 cm working format across marked and unmarked catalogue states

Date: 2026-09-02

Status: paper-history mainline following Waves 60–61. This wave upgrades the half-Royal/format-standardization hypothesis from selected examples to a near-series-level catalogue census. It remains a dimensional reconstruction pending direct edge, formation and transmitted-light inspection.

## Executive result

The Royal Asiatic Society describes the Hodgson architectural collection as mainly a series of fifty views of Nepalese architecture made by Raj Man Singh Chitrakar in Kathmandu in 1844.

Source:
https://royalasiaticcollections.org/collection/hodgson-drawings/

A systematic pass over the currently surfaced online item records recovered paper dimensions for **47 numbered Raj Man Singh drawings**. Three number positions (`33`, `34`, and `50`) have not yet been recovered as individual online pages and are therefore excluded rather than inferred.

The result is much tighter than the earlier diagnostic sample.

### Long-format family

`43 / 47` recovered numbered drawings fall in a single dimensional family with long side at least 47 cm.

Observed statistics:

- n = 43;
- mean = **30.10 × 48.54 cm**;
- sample standard deviation = **0.21 × 0.25 cm**;
- observed range = **29.6–30.6 × 47.5–49.0 cm**.

Against old English Royal drawing paper:

`Royal whole sheet = 19 × 24 in = 48.26 × 60.96 cm`

so a simple half-sheet is:

`30.48 × 48.26 cm`.

The 43-sheet mean differs from theoretical half Royal by only about:

- short dimension: `-1.25%`;
- long dimension: `+0.58%`.

Current state:

`RAS_NUMBERED_ARCHITECTURAL_LONG_FORMAT = HALF_ROYAL_DRAWING_SHEET_CANDIDATE_VERY_HIGH`.

This still requires direct physical confirmation through cut/deckle edges, watermark/countermark topology, laid/chain-line orientation and possible later trimming.

### Short-format exceptions

Only four recovered numbered drawings fall clearly outside the long-format cluster:

- `022.012` — 27.1 × 45.4 cm;
- `022.014` — 27 × 43.8 cm, watermark dated 1841;
- `022.018` — 27.8 × 44 cm;
- `022.026` — 31.6 × 43.8 cm, watermark dated 1821.

The first three form a plausible trimmed half-Medium subgroup; the 1821 sheet is dimensionally different and remains an irregular/older-stock case.

Core mechanisms:

- `WORKING_FORMAT_STANDARDIZATION_IS_SERIES_LEVEL`
- `COMMERCIAL_PARENT_SIZE_CAN_STRUCTURE_A_VISUAL_PROGRAM`
- `FORMAT_STANDARDIZATION_OVER_HETEROGENEOUS_STOCK`
- `CATALOGUE_WATERMARK_SILENCE_DOES_NOT_DEFINE_A_FORMAT_FAMILY`
- `WORKING_FORMAT_IS_MORE_STABLE_THAN_VISIBLE_WATERMARK_STATE`.

## 1. The long-format family is not an impressionistic cluster

The recovered 43 long-format drawings include both entries with explicit watermark dates and entries with no watermark statement in the online catalogue.

Representative catalogue records include:

- `022.001` — 30.2 × 48.4 cm;
- `022.002` — 30 × 48.4 cm;
- `022.003` — 30.4 × 48.4 cm, watermark 1841;
- `022.007` — 30.1 × 48.7 cm, watermark 1841;
- `022.019` — 30 × 48.7 cm;
- `022.023` — 30.6 × 48.8 cm, watermark 1837;
- `022.024` — 30.2 × 48.2 cm;
- `022.031` — 29.8 × 47.5 cm, watermark 1841;
- `022.041` — 30.1 × 49 cm, watermark 1841;
- `022.048` — 30.5 × 48.7 cm;
- `022.049` — 30 × 48.3 cm, watermark 1841.

Sources are the individual Royal Asiatic Society Online Collections item pages linked from:
https://royalasiaticcollections.org/collection/hodgson-drawings/

The variation is on the order of millimetres, not centimetres.

The resulting object is therefore best described as a **standardized working format**, with small deviations compatible with cutting, trimming, shrinkage, measurement convention and handmade/machine paper dimensional variation.

Mechanism:

`DIMENSIONAL_CLUSTERING_CAN_REVEAL_A_CUTTING_STANDARD`.

## 2. Marked and catalogue-unmarked long sheets are dimensionally indistinguishable

Within the 43-sheet long family:

### Explicit dated-watermark subset

n = 23.

- 22 are catalogued with watermark date `1841`;
- 1 (`022.023`) is catalogued with watermark date `1837`.

Mean:

`30.09 × 48.56 cm`.

Sample standard deviation:

`0.23 × 0.29 cm`.

### No dated-watermark statement in the online record

n = 20.

Mean:

`30.12 × 48.53 cm`.

Sample standard deviation:

`0.19 × 0.20 cm`.

The difference between group means is only about:

- `0.03 cm` on the short side;
- `0.03 cm` on the long side.

This is far below the scale required to identify a distinct paper-format regime.

Thus:

`CATALOGUE_WATERMARK_STATEMENT != FORMAT_CLASS`.

The absence of a watermark statement in the catalogue can mean several things:

1. no mark is visible on that cut portion;
2. only countermark/fragment is present and not recorded;
3. the paper was not examined in transmitted light;
4. catalogue description depth varied;
5. a watermark is absent although the format still derives from the same commercial size class.

The dimensional census cannot decide among these. What it does decide is that the catalogue-unmarked objects do **not** form a separate dimensional population.

Mechanisms:

- `CATALOGUE_SILENCE_IS_NOT_MATERIAL_ABSENCE`
- `WATERMARK_RECORDING_IS_A_CENSORED_OBSERVATION`
- `FORMAT_EVIDENCE_AND_MARK_EVIDENCE_MUST_BE_SEPARATE_FIELDS`.

## 3. Watermark-year heterogeneity survives inside the same long format

The long family contains at least:

- 1841-dated paper in twenty-two directly catalogued cases;
- 1837-dated paper at `022.023` — 30.6 × 48.8 cm.

The older 1837 paper is metrically indistinguishable from the 1841 long-format stock.

Therefore even if future watermark study proves different makers/batches, the operational format remained stable across stock-time layers.

Strong claim:

**format standardization was downstream of paper manufacture date.**

In other words, stock with different temporal histories could be admitted to the same task format.

Mechanisms:

- `STOCK_TIME_PRECEDES_WORKING_FORMAT`
- `WORKFLOW_CAN_STANDARDIZE_DIFFERENT_AGE_STOCKS`
- `FORMAT_IDENTITY_NOT_BATCH_IDENTITY`.

## 4. The dominant format is almost exactly half Royal

British Association of Paper Historians reference table:
https://baph.org.uk/resources/reference-material/old-english-paper-sizes/

Old English Book and Drawing `Royal`:

`19 × 24 in`.

Metric whole-sheet equivalent:

`48.26 × 60.96 cm`.

Half-sheet equivalent:

`30.48 × 48.26 cm`.

Observed 43-sheet RAS mean:

`30.10 × 48.54 cm`.

This correspondence is now based on almost the entire currently recovered numbered drawing programme rather than a selected group.

The hypothesis therefore strengthens from:

`HALF_ROYAL_CANDIDATE_HIGH`

to:

`HALF_ROYAL_CANDIDATE_VERY_HIGH_BY_DIMENSION`.

But source control remains essential. A dimension match cannot tell us whether:

- the paper was sold as Royal;
- the original parent sheet was exactly 19 × 24 in;
- the parent sheet was cut in Kathmandu or before arrival;
- the observed edges are original cut/deckle edges;
- later mounting/conservation trimmed the sheets.

Those questions require physical inspection.

## 5. The three medium-like exceptions suggest a second format operation

`022.012` — 27.1 × 45.4 cm

`022.014` — 27 × 43.8 cm

`022.018` — 27.8 × 44 cm

Mean:

`27.3 × 44.4 cm`.

Old English Medium:

`17.5 × 22.5 in = 44.45 × 57.15 cm`.

Theoretical half Medium:

`28.58 × 44.45 cm`.

The observed long dimension matches almost exactly; the short dimension is about 4.5% smaller on average.

This is compatible with:

`half Medium -> trimming`

but remains weaker than the half-Royal result.

State:

`SHORT_012_014_018 = TRIMMED_HALF_MEDIUM_CANDIDATE`.

Importantly, `022.014` carries an 1841 watermark. The same watermark year therefore appears both in the dominant half-Royal-like family and a short-format object.

Possible explanations remain:

- multiple commercial paper sizes in the same dated stock environment;
- one dated maker producing different sheet sizes;
- later trimming from a larger parent;
- catalogue watermark date shared across distinct brands/makers.

Only direct mark morphology and edge evidence can decide.

## 6. The 1821 sheet is a separate format problem

`RAS Hodgson 022.026`:

- c.1844 use;
- watermark date 1821;
- paper `31.6 × 43.8 cm`.

It is neither part of the half-Royal-like long population nor a clean fit to the three medium-like short sheets.

This strengthens its status as a materially distinct old-stock event.

Current code:

`022.026 = OLD_STOCK_IRREGULAR_FORMAT_DIRECT`.

Possible histories:

1. older blank sheet retained for more than two decades;
2. older paper recirculated into Hodgson/Raj Man Singh's working stock;
3. a differently sized parent stock selected opportunistically;
4. later trimming changed its dimensions;
5. catalogue watermark/date needs physical verification.

The format census therefore makes the 1821 object more exceptional, not less: it is unusual in both **stock time** and **working dimensions**.

## 7. The numbered programme differs sharply from the surviving preliminary sketches

Several unnumbered/extra RAS Hodgson sketches associated with the architectural programme occupy a distinctly shorter, more variable paper zone:

- `022.052` — 27.6 × 46 cm;
- `022.053` — 31 × 41.5 cm;
- `022.054` — 31 × 41.5 cm;
- `022.055` — 26.1 × 43.4 cm;
- `022.056` — c.30 × 43.4 cm;
- `022.057` — c.31.3 × 42.7 cm;
- `022.058` — 29.3 × 44.8 cm.

Some are explicitly described by RAS or BL as preliminary/original sketches for finished drawings.

This produces a clearer work-stage contrast:

`preliminary/reference sketches = heterogeneous shorter formats`

versus

`numbered Raj Man Singh programme = overwhelmingly standardized c.30 × 48.5 format`.

Do not convert this into an absolute rule: numbered items 012, 014, 018 and 026 remain short-format exceptions.

But at series level the paper allocation pattern is now strong enough to test physically.

Mechanisms:

- `FORMAL_SERIES_PRODUCTION_INCREASES_FORMAT_STANDARDIZATION`
- `PRELIMINARY_WORK_TOLERATES_FORMAT_HETEROGENEITY`
- `FINISHED_PROGRAMME_CAN_NORMALIZE_SUPPORT_DIMENSIONS`.

## 8. This changes the interpretation of the RAS architectural corpus

The 1844 collection should no longer be described simply as fifty drawings on various papers.

A materially sharper reconstruction is:

`heterogeneous available paper stock`
-> `dominant conversion/selection into c.30 × 48.5 working units`
-> `numbered architectural drawing programme`
-> `standard 55 × 66 mounts for most surviving objects`.

The support standardization sits between stock and image production.

This offers an operational explanation for why:

- dated papers from different years can look materially uniform at object scale;
- catalogue-marked and catalogue-unmarked objects share the same format;
- preliminary sketches can retain different formats;
- later mounts add another layer of dimensional standardization.

A paper-history formulation:

**The architectural series was standardized twice: first when heterogeneous paper stock was converted into a recurrent working sheet, and again when most drawings were mounted on a recurrent 55 × 66 cm support. Manufacture, work format, and archival/display format are therefore three different dimensional regimes.**

Mechanisms:

- `MULTI_STAGE_DIMENSIONAL_STANDARDIZATION`
- `WORKING_FORMAT_PRECEDES_MOUNT_FORMAT`
- `PAPER_OBJECT_ACQUIRES_SUCCESSIVE_DIMENSIONAL_REGIMES`.

## 9. Measurement discipline

Wave 61 showed that British Library item dimensions phrased `within black frame` describe an image field rather than secure full sheet dimensions.

RAS records, by contrast, explicitly distinguish `paper size` from `mount size` or `frame size` in most relevant entries.

Therefore the current series census uses only values explicitly described as `paper size`.

Required schema field:

`dimension_semantic_type = PAPER_SIZE_DIRECT`.

This distinction must remain mandatory in all future cross-institution comparisons.

## Source-control rules

1. The census covers 47 currently recovered numbered RAS online records; do not silently invent dimensions for 33, 34 or 50.
2. `No watermark statement` means `CATALOGUE_NOT_STATED`, not `NO_WATERMARK`.
3. The half-Royal interpretation is a dimensional hypothesis, not a direct historical trade label on the sheets.
4. Do not use dimensional identity to claim one maker or one paper batch.
5. Treat 1837 and 1841 as directly catalogued watermark-date states, not yet fully identified maker states.
6. Keep working paper size separate from mount/frame size.
7. Keep numbered programme and preliminary sketch populations analytically separate.
8. Direct transmitted-light and edge inspection outranks size reconstruction.

## Bottom line

The RAS architectural series now provides series-level evidence for paper formatting as a research operation. Forty-three of forty-seven currently recovered numbered drawings occupy an extraordinarily tight c.30 × 48.5 cm dimensional family, almost exactly half of standard Royal drawing paper. Explicitly dated-watermark sheets and catalogue-unmarked sheets are dimensionally indistinguishable, while at least 1837 and 1841 stocks enter the same format. Only four numbered objects break the pattern. The paper support was therefore standardized at a stage downstream of manufacture and stock age. The most economical material hypothesis is that available parent sheets were repeatedly cut or selected into a half-Royal-like working unit for the numbered 1844 architectural programme, with a small set of exceptions that now become especially valuable for reconstructing alternate stock and trimming histories.
