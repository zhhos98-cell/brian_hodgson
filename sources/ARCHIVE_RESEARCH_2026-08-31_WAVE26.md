# Archive research wave 26 — intermediate states, diagnostic abstraction, institutional relays, and self-evidence

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: deep archive/source pass following Wave 25. This wave combines newly uploaded archive scans with narrowly targeted external verification where source-state questions required it. Uploaded-source claims remain separated from external corroboration and inference.

## Executive result

Wave 26 materially sharpens the repo's core mechanism. Across zoology, ethnography, Buddhist studies, manuscript circulation, learned-society membership and retrospective reputation, Hodgson repeatedly works through **intermediate documentary surfaces** that are neither raw field material nor final knowledge products.

These surfaces do at least four different things:

1. **compress dispersed observations into a comparable totality**;
2. **preserve uncertainty and future correction inside the working object**;
3. **extract diagnostic relations from larger contexts so they can circulate independently**;
4. **make institutional relations operational across distance**.

A fifth result is crucial: an intermediate state can itself become public accidentally. Hodgson's later explanation of his 1857 ethnographic papers gives direct evidence that hints for correction/addition, intended to remain temporary working marks, entered print because he left India suddenly and could not correct the press.

This makes the repo's earlier `copy-state` and `reconciliation` framework substantially more general:

`working state -> transfer/publication/receipt -> state becomes fixed or actionable -> later correction/reconstruction`

The archive therefore records not only completed knowledge, but the material mechanisms by which provisional states were stabilized, propagated, mis-propagated, reopened and repurposed.

## 1. 1828: lists already function as operational metadata

### Source

Printed reprint in uploaded `书籍 2025年3月28日(OCR)`:

`Notices of the Languages, Literature, and Religion of Nepaul and of Tibet` (originally printed from the 16th volume of the Asiatic Transactions of Bengal, 1828).

### Direct statement

Hodgson opens by referring to the various contributions he had forwarded to the Library and Museum of the Asiatic Society **and the lists by which they had been accompanied**. He says these contributions and lists would have put the Society in possession of the information he had collected respecting the articles presented.

### Mechanism

Code:

`LIST_AS_OPERATIONAL_METADATA`

The list is not a later finding aid added after collection. It accompanies the transferred material and carries information required to make the articles intelligible after separation from Hodgson.

Operational sequence:

`article/object -> accompanying list -> distant institution -> information remains addressable`

This pushes the repo's documentary-infrastructure chronology back into the 1820s.

### Article value

Very high. This is an early explicit source-level statement for the central claim that circulation required a paired representational surface.

## 2. 1835 and 1838: Wallich as a cross-domain transmission relay

### 1835 manuscript transmission

In Hodgson's letter dated Nepal, 2 April 1835, later read before the Royal Asiatic Society, he says that **through Dr. Wallich** he had transmitted a copy of the `Sata Sahasrika Prajna Paramita / Raksha Bhagavati`. He then sets out a projected sequence of further copies: the nine Nava Dharma works, followed by other Pauranika and Tantrika books of the Saugatas.

He describes it as his hope and ambition to deposit in the Society's archives a complete series of these Sanskrit repositories.

### 1838 zoological transmission

The Linnean `SP 514` letter of 20 February 1838 likewise states that Hodgson submits **through his friend Dr. Wallich** a Classified Catalogue of the mammals known to him from Nepal.

### Mechanism

Code:

`CROSS_DOMAIN_TRANSMISSION_RELAY`

Wallich appears here not merely as a botanist or correspondent but as a relay in at least two differently constituted knowledge flows:

- Sanskrit Buddhist manuscript copy -> Royal Asiatic Society;
- classified mammal catalogue -> Linnean Society.

The common function is institutional transfer across distance.

### Stronger implication

The knowledge network should not be modelled only by discipline-to-discipline correspondence. One person/institutional position can operate as a **transport and trust interface** across domains.

This is consistent with, but stronger than, a generic `network` formulation because the sources specify the operation performed: transmission.

## 3. 1838 zoology: classification as provisional totalization

### Source

Linnean Society `DA-ENG-2-SP-514`, Hodgson to Secretary, 20 February 1838.

### Direct evidence

Hodgson calls the object a `Classified Catalogue of all the mammals known to me as inhabitants of Nepal` and says he has inserted characters of new forms.

He simultaneously marks the classificatory state as provisional. In the Cervidae discussion he is sceptical about the necessity and sufficiency of a proposed separation, while expecting further enquiry to provide fuller grounds of distinction.

The next page distinguishes two publication states:

- particular descriptions of species had already appeared `from time to time` in Bengal Asiatic Society publications;
- an `orderly and complete enumeration of the whole` could still have value.

### Mechanisms

Codes:

- `PROVISIONAL_CLASSIFICATION_SURFACE`
- `DISPERSED_TO_TOTALIZED_KNOWLEDGE`

The catalogue does not simply preserve already settled taxonomy. It performs a reformatting operation:

`piecemeal species descriptions -> ordered comparative whole`

while also recording that some relations inside that whole remain revisable.

### Cross-domain significance

This becomes the first half of a strong comparison with Hodgson's 1857 ethnography. In both cases the classification surface is useful before it is final.

## 4. 1857 ethnography: a classification can be deliberately usable while explicitly wrong

### Source

Uploaded `Adobe Scan 2025年3月28日 (6)` contains Hodgson's `Comparative Vocabulary of the Languages of the Broken Tribes of Nepal`, letter dated Darjiling, 4 October 1857, plus later reprint material.

External control: Hodgson, `Miscellaneous Essays Relating to Indian Subjects`, vol. I (1880), digitized text.

### Direct 1857 statement

Hodgson says the `arrangement and nomenclature` made some time earlier are `not quite correct`, but that they will `serve the present end` and can be corrected when particulars are reached.

He then says the value of the Kiranti subdivisional names is not always equal and that he has indicated this **on the right hand**. This is important materially: correction/provisional hierarchy is encoded in page layout as well as prose.

He further states that he was obliged to `begin in the dark` when deciding which varieties would best serve as dialect samples, and that the selections he hit upon were not always of equal value.

### Mechanism

Code:

`FIT_FOR_PRESENT_PURPOSE_PROVISIONALITY`

The classificatory surface is openly instrumental:

`imperfect grouping -> sufficient for present comparison -> later particulars -> correction`

This is not a hidden error discovered by later historians. Hodgson himself describes the working epistemic status.

### Comparison with 1838

Zoology 1838:

`new forms + uncertain grouping + further enquiry`

Ethnography 1857:

`imperfect nomenclature + unequal samples + present utility + future correction`

The shared mechanism is not merely taxonomic uncertainty. It is **managed provisionality on a documentary surface**.

## 5. 1857/1880: publication can freeze an intermediate working state

### External verification of the reprint note

The 1880 reprint supplies a direct authorial explanation of a publication-state failure.

Hodgson explains that the first two comparative-vocabulary papers had been completed but were held back for a fuller mode of investigation represented by the later grammatical analyses. He then found himself suddenly obliged to quit India. Judging it better not to delay the papers further, he sent all to press.

In the hurry, he says, he failed to erase from papers 1 and 2 certain **hints for correction or addition** generated by increasing knowledge but not yet worked out. Those temporary marks should have been removed before publication. His inability to correct the press compounded the problem.

### Source-state control

The 1857 letter and the later 1880 explanatory note should be distinguished. The note retrospectively explains how the earlier printed state was produced; it is not simply part of the 1857 letter event.

### Mechanism

Code:

`INTERMEDIATE_STATE_PUBLICATION`

or more specifically:

`WORKING_HINTS_FROZEN_IN_PRINT`

Operational sequence:

`completed comparison -> held back for deeper investigation -> working corrections/hints accumulate -> sudden departure -> own-use/working state sent to press -> no press correction -> provisional marks become public`

### HPS value

This is a direct material-production account of how epistemic provisionality enters print.

It pairs unusually well with Wave 24's `COPY_STATE_COUNTING_CONFLICT`:

- Wave 24: a retained copy preserves incompatible numerical states;
- Wave 26: a publication preserves temporary correction/addition hints that should have been removed.

In both cases the surviving documentary state is not a transparent representation of a single settled authorial state.

### Article consequence

Add a broader mechanism:

**intermediate-state fixation** — transfer, copying, publication or receipt can stabilize a state that was provisional, internally inconsistent, or meant to be temporary.

## 6. 1860 Buddhist symbols: diagnostic abstraction as a knowledge-making operation

### Source

Uploaded `Adobe Scan 2025年3月28日 (3) (1)`, `Notice on Buddhist Symbols`, corrected-autograph/printed witness dated The Rangers near Dursley, 26 September 1860.

### Earlier abandoned project

Hodgson says the first 33 symbols were gathered from published coins and the remainder from Saugata temples/images in Nepal. The corpus had been assembled years before with the intention of obtaining explanations from learned Saugatas. More pressing work intervened; the project dropped from view and, as he recalls, no explanations were obtained.

### Stronger operation visible later in the paper

Hodgson explains that Buddhist images had often been misidentified as Brahmanical gods because observers failed to attend to significant accessories inseparable from the images.

He therefore had symbols/emblems from architectural, sculptural and pictorial representations **brought together**, detaching each diagnostic mark from the temple, statue or picture in which it appeared.

### Mechanism

Code:

`DIAGNOSTIC_ABSTRACTION`

This is a highly specific conversion operation:

`complex image/monument -> selected diagnostic accessory -> detached comparative representation -> corpus -> identification across cases`

The corpus becomes useful precisely because contextual complexity is reduced to comparable marks.

### Cross-domain comparison

This is structurally comparable, though not identical, to:

- a zoological catalogue extracting characters of new forms;
- comparative vocabularies selecting lexical/dialect samples;
- manuscript catalogues extracting title/support/extent fields.

The common operation is **selective feature extraction for distant comparison**.

### Reactivation

The 1860 publication also exemplifies:

`ABANDONED_CORPUS_REACTIVATION`

A research object assembled for one unrealized explanatory operation survives and is later repurposed for publication under a weaker but still useful evidential claim.

## 7. Lepcha copies: future work is built into page architecture

### Source

SOAS MS380882 later `List of Lepcha Manuscripts` describing Hodgson Collection material in the India Office Library.

### Direct physical inference by the later describer

The Lepcha books are described, with possibly one exception, as copies prepared for Hodgson with future translation in view. The evidence cited is:

- empty space on the right side of the Lepcha text;
- Nepalese translation already inserted in places.

The describer also distinguishes at least two hands and notes that some texts written or dictated by `bong thing` or `mun` specialists show difficult handwriting and orthographic errors.

### Mechanism

Retain Wave 25 code:

`FUTURE_OPERATION_RESERVE`

with subtype:

`TRANSLATION_READY_COPY_LAYOUT`

### New comparative significance

The blank right-hand field and the 1857 ethnographic `right hand` annotations show two different uses of marginal/adjacent page space:

- reserved future translation;
- present correction/hierarchical qualification.

This suggests a broader paper-level test:

**adjacent blank or marginal zones can be operational space, not unused space.**

This should be tested on original manuscript images rather than generalized from the later catalogue alone.

## 8. Linnean membership: fellowship as continuing logistics and information entitlement

### Source

Uploaded `Adobe Scan 2025年3月25日 (3)` contains Linnean Society administrative correspondence concerning Hodgson. Handwriting OCR is poor for names/date but the core administrative content is stable enough to identify the operation.

### Direct administrative content

The writer reports receiving a letter from Hodgson, identified as a member of the Linnean Society, complaining that he had for some time not received Society books to which, as a member, he was entitled. The recipient is asked to determine whether further books had been published and, if so, have them transmitted to Hodgson.

### Mechanism

Code:

`MEMBERSHIP_INFORMATION_ENTITLEMENT`

or:

`LEARNED_SOCIETY_LOGISTICS_MAINTENANCE`

Membership produces continuing operational obligations:

`fellowship -> publication entitlement -> dispatch -> failed receipt -> complaint -> publication-state check -> renewed transmission`

### Comparison with 1834 and 1838

The Linnean Society sequence can now be treated as a lifecycle rather than three isolated facts:

`1834 credential assembly / election process -> 1838 knowledge submission -> later continuing information entitlement and delivery maintenance`

This makes institutional membership materially visible through paper traffic.

### Caution

Exact correspondent names, date and bibliographic volumes must not be reconstructed from current OCR.

## 9. Kew Inwards Book: receipt is one state in a richer condition register

### Source

Uploaded Kew `Inwards Book 1848-1858`, within `Adobe Scan 2025年3月26日 (1)`.

### Hodgson entry

OCR securely identifies an entry `Received ... from Mr. Hodgson`, with `Sikkim`, `Rhododendron`, and `Hill Bamboo` recoverable. Exact date, number of seeds and parenthetical altitude-like figure remain uncertain and should not be normalized from OCR.

### Register context

Neighbouring entries show that the Inwards Book can record more than arrival. One returned glazed-case entry explicitly notes plants as `unfortunately all dead` while naming other material associated with the returned case.

### Mechanism

Retain:

`INSTITUTIONAL_RECEIPT_REGISTER`

but sharpen to:

`RECEIPT_PLUS_CONDITION_STATE`

The register is capable of converting an arrival into an institutional record that includes sender/origin/content and, in some cases, condition/survival.

### Implication

`safe arrival` and `biological/scientific usability` are analytically distinct.

The Kew register offers a botanical test of the same broader principle already established for manuscripts and zoological specimens.

## 10. 1861 Linnean letter: replace `self-refusal` with a narrower source-controlled reading

### Source

Linnean `DA-ENG-9-LL-8`, The Rangers/Dursley, 15 May 1861.

### What can be said now

Repeated OCR passes support a narrow reconstruction: Hodgson writes because circumstances/remoteness from Dursley will prevent him from being present at a Linnean Society meeting on the 24th.

### What cannot yet be said

Current OCR does not support a strong interpretation of:

- voluntary withdrawal from science;
- ideological self-refusal;
- a general decline in institutional engagement.

### Code

Replace provisional interpretive label with:

`ATTENDANCE_PREVENTED / EXACT_CAUSE_NEEDS_TRANSCRIPTION`

The research value may later lie in the relation between residence/distance and institutional participation, but that requires a manual reading of the original page.

## 11. India Office 1864 gift: preserve the date conflict rather than repairing it

### Uploaded source

The retrospective printed file explicitly instructs the reader, for Hodgson's unpublished writings and MSS, to see his **letter of 2 August 1864** to the Secretary of State for India and the reply.

A later reproduced acceptance page is headed:

`Secretary of State for India's Acceptance of the Gift of Mr. Hodgson's Unpublished Writings and Oriental MSS.`

Its OCR reads the date as `India Office, 12th August. 1846.`

The letter body says it acknowledges Hodgson's letter of the `2nd inst.` presenting a collection of Oriental manuscripts made during his residence in Nepal.

### External control

Archives Hub independently catalogues a **draft letter from Brian Houghton Hodgson to Sir Charles Wood, Secretary of State for India, dated 2 August 1864**.

### Source-control rule

The external record confirms the outgoing 2 August 1864 gift letter. It does not, by itself, license silent replacement of the acceptance-page OCR date.

Code:

`DATE_TRANSCRIPTION_CONFLICT`

with:

- outgoing gift-letter date: `1864-08-02`, externally corroborated;
- acceptance-page OCR: `12 August 1846`, suspect/unresolved;
- likely relationship: response in same transaction, but exact acceptance date requires page-image verification or independent catalogue witness.

### Why this matters

This is a small but useful demonstration of the repo rule:

`contextual plausibility != permission to normalize a witness`

## 12. 1868 Testimonials: heterogeneous prestige is normalized into one evidence surface

### Source

Uploaded `Adobe Scan 2025年3月28日 (5)` plus printed retrospective testimonial material in `Adobe Scan 2025年3月28日`.

### Self-curation act

Hodgson labels a volume `Testimonials to the value of my labours in literature, science and education ...`, dated 12 March 1868.

### What the surrounding testimonial material contains

The retrospective source juxtaposes recognitions from very different institutional regimes, including:

- Asiatic Society praise of scholarship and the credit brought to the Society;
- government and senior official praise of his 1841–42 diplomatic negotiations;
- ethnological assessments by Latham, Briggs and others;
- Hooker's botanical dedication praising Hodgson's physical geography and ethnology;
- later scientific assessments of zoology/crania/ethnological work;
- Hunter's dedication praising Hodgson for recognizing the duty of studying the governed population.

### Mechanism

Retain:

`SELF_CURATED_REPUTATION_DOSSIER`

and add:

`CROSS_DOMAIN_TESTIMONIAL_NORMALIZATION`

The bound dossier performs a conversion:

`administrative commendation + learned-society praise + scientific citation + dedication + disciplinary endorsement -> adjacent documentary proofs of personal value`

These endorsements were originally non-equivalent acts in different institutional contexts. The self-archive makes them comparable as one category: `testimony to value`.

### Strong long chronology

1834 Linnean recommendation:

`others assemble credential for Hodgson`

1868 Testimonials:

`Hodgson assembles others' credentials about himself`

This is a particularly strong arc for the repo's `recursive archive and reputation` section.

## 14. Synthesis: from `reconstructibility after movement` to `intermediate-state fixation`

Waves 17–25 established that numbers, lists, catalogue marks, receipts, containers and copy states preserve enough relations for distributed objects to be audited or reconstructed.

Wave 26 adds a more temporal mechanism.

### New general model

A documentary surface can hold an intermediate knowledge state:

`observation / object / local statement`

-> `selection, list, catalogue, table, copied corpus, annotated working sheet`

-> `transfer / publication / receipt / institutional filing`

-> `intermediate state becomes fixed and actionable`

-> `later correction, comparison, reactivation, redistribution or self-curation`

### Positive and negative forms

**Deliberate provisionality**

- 1838 mammal catalogue: uncertain separation, further enquiry anticipated;
- 1857 ethnography: not-quite-correct arrangement sufficient for present purpose;
- Lepcha copies: blank translation field reserves future work.

**Accidental fixation**

- 1857/1880: correction/addition hints meant to remain temporary enter print;
- Wave 24: retained copy preserves incompatible counts.

**Productive abstraction**

- 1860 symbols: diagnostic marks detached from complex images for comparison;
- catalogues/vocabularies similarly select features into comparable fields.

**Institutional maintenance**

- 1828 lists travel with objects;
- Wallich relays objects/documents across institutions;
- Linnean membership generates continuing publication-delivery obligations;
- Kew register makes arrivals and condition locally operable.

**Retrospective reassembly**

- abandoned symbol corpus reactivated;
- 1868 testimonials convert distributed judgments into a bound self-evidence system.

### Strongest revised formulation

**Hodgson's infrastructure did not merely make knowledge portable. It made provisional relations materially persistent. Lists, catalogues, page layouts, extracted symbols, receipts, working annotations and testimonial compilations preserved particular states long enough to be transferred, acted on, corrected or retrospectively reassembled. The same persistence that enabled reconstruction could also freeze error or unfinished thought into institutional and printed records.**

## Bottom line

Wave 26 moves the project from a model of `objects made reconstructible after movement` toward a sharper history of **state persistence**. Hodgson's lists, catalogues, annotated classifications, translation-ready copies, extracted symbols, receipts and retrospective dossiers are all material surfaces on which an epistemic or institutional state is temporarily stabilized. That stabilization is what allows distance, delayed work and later correction; it is also what makes unfinished, inconsistent or context-stripped states capable of acquiring durable authority.
