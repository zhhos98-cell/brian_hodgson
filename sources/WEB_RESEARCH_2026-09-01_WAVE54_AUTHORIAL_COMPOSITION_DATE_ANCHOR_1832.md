# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 54
## Hodgson's own retrospective dating anchors composition to 1832

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: production-stemma refinement following Wave 53.

## Executive result

The new 1857 retrospective footnote does more than update recruitment policy. Its opening phrase dates the earlier paper by authorial retrospection:

`Since this paper was written (twenty-five years back)`.

Because the note is printed in 1857, this points to approximately **1832** as Hodgson's own remembered composition date.

That aligns unusually closely with Cambridge's surviving fair manuscript:

`MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1 — Origin and classification of the Military tribes of Nepal — 10-1832`.

The 1833 JASB state is therefore better treated as publication rather than composition origin.

Strongest current chronology:

`1831 rough classificatory working`
-> `October 1832 fair tabular manuscript/copy state`
<-> `parallel fair copy`
-> `1833 journal publication`
-> `1857 retrospective statement that paper was written twenty-five years earlier`.

Mechanisms:

- `AUTHORIAL_RETROSPECTIVE_DATE_ANCHORS_PREPUBLICATION_STATE`
- `COMPOSITION_DATE_DISTINCT_FROM_PUBLICATION_DATE`
- `LATER_PARATEXT_CAN_DATE_EARLIER_DOCUMENTARY_STATE`
- `FAIR_MANUSCRIPT_DATE_GAINS_INDEPENDENT_SUPPORT`.

## 2. Cambridge manuscript alignment

Cambridge inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html

`MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1, ff.1–12b`:

- title: `Origin and classification of the Military tribes of Nepal`;
- date: `10-1832`;
- support: machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- tabular view of family names of Brahman, Khasa, Magar and Gurung;
- cross-reference to another copy at vol.9/10;
- cross-reference to 1833 publication.

The volume-level catalogue also says all manuscripts in vol.6 are copies.

Thus the correct claim is **not** `vol.6/1 is Hodgson's original autograph composition manuscript`.

The stronger controlled claim is:

**vol.6/1 is a surviving fair copy dated to the same month/year-scale that Hodgson later retrospectively identifies as the paper's composition period.**

## 4. Why 1832 vs 1833 matters

A purely bibliographic narrative begins in 1833 because that is when the JASB article appears.

The paper-object history instead distinguishes:

`working/classificatory production in 1831–32`
from
`public journal fixation in 1833`.

This gives the publication transition a concrete prehistory on paper.

The 1857 footnote retrospectively confirms that Hodgson himself thought of the work as written before its journal publication.

Mechanism:

`PUBLICATION_DATE_DOES_NOT_DEFINE_DOCUMENT_ORIGIN`.

## 6. Connection to asynchronous revision

The same 1857 footnote does three different jobs simultaneously:

1. dates the prior paper to c.1832;
2. updates the recruitment-policy layer using later experience;
3. leaves older historical/physiognomic body readings in place.

A later paratext therefore both **dates** and **revises** an earlier paper object without replacing all of its textual states.

Mechanism:

`PARATEXT_AS_RETROSPECTIVE_DATE_AND_REVISION_SURFACE`.

## Source-control rules

1. `twenty-five years back` is approximate authorial dating, not a day-level composition date.
2. Do not force exact arithmetic beyond c.1832; the 10-1832 manuscript is an unusually close alignment, not proof that the footnote refers specifically to that surviving copy.
3. Cambridge says vol.6 manuscripts are copies; do not call vol.6/1 autograph.
4. Do not call vol.6/1 printer's copy without compositor/printer marks or direct textual descent evidence.
5. Keep composition, fair-copy production, society submission/reading and print publication as distinct operations.

## Bottom line

The military-tribes paper should no longer be narrated as a text that simply appeared in 1833. Hodgson's 1857 retrospective note dates its writing to roughly twenty-five years earlier, independently aligning the composition period with the surviving October 1832 fair manuscript state. This gives the article a materially recoverable pre-publication history and sharply increases the evidential value of MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1 without overclaiming that it was the printer's copy.
