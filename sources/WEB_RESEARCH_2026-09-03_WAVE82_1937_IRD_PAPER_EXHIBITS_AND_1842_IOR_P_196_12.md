# Web/archive research 2026-09-03 — Wave 82
## Abdul Ali's 1937 Imperial Record Department paper exhibits, direct pre-1944 provenance, and the 11 May 1842 `IOR/P/196/12` volume

Date: 2026-09-03

Status: active paper-history mainline. Follow-up to Waves 80–81.

This wave produces two materially different advances. First, it recovers a **1937 Imperial Record Department exhibition list signed by A. F. M. Abdul Ali** that physically/archivally attests the 1832 Nepal-paper consultation cluster and the exact 29 Nov 1841 O'Shaughnessy bleaching consultation seven years before Abdul Ali's 1944 article. Second, a later documentary edition ties the **11 May 1842 India Political consultation** to `IOR/P/196/12`, making that volume a high-confidence exact consultation-volume target for Abdul Ali's No.70, though the specific No.70 item has not yet been image-controlled.

## Executive result

### A. 1937: Abdul Ali directly signs the exhibition list containing the paper consultations

The *Indian Historical Records Commission Proceedings of Meetings*, vol. 14, December 1937, contains a `Descriptive List of Historical Manuscripts, Paintings, Seals and Coins, etc., exhibited at Lahore in connection with the fourteenth Annual Meeting`.

Immediately before the exhibit list, the publication states that the complete list follows and is signed:

`A. F. M. ABDUL ALI, Secretary, Indian Historical Records Commission`.

The list identifies `Imperial Record Department` as the first Government Archives exhibitor.

Under `From the Imperial Record Department`, two consecutive exhibits are directly relevant:

**Exhibit 9**

`Indigenous method of manufacturing paper in the Himalayan tracts (Nepal, etc.) in the early part of the 19th century. (Pol. Con. 13 Jan. 1832, Nos. 18–20.)`

**Exhibit 10**

`Report on the method of bleaching paper manufactured at Darjeeling by Dr. W. B. O'Shaughnessy, M.D., Chemical Examiner of Calcutta ... (Pol. Con. 29 Nov. 1841, No.147.)`

Internet Archive full text:

https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.96997/2015.96997.Indian-Historical-Records-Commission-Proceedings-Of-Meetingsvol14december-1937_djvu.txt

This is much stronger provenance than the 1944 article alone.

Encode:

`POL_CON_1832_01_13_NOS18_20 = PHYSICALLY/ARCHIVALLY_ATTESTED_AS_IRD_EXHIBIT_1937`;

`POL_CON_1841_11_29_NO147 = PHYSICALLY/ARCHIVALLY_ATTESTED_AS_IRD_EXHIBIT_1937`;

`ABDUL_ALI_SIGNED_1937_EXHIBITION_LIST = DIRECT`.

Do not encode that Abdul Ali personally selected every object unless a separate source proves selection authorship. What is direct is that he signed the complete exhibit list as Commission Secretary while serving as Keeper of the Records of the Government of India.

The exhibition wording is significant:

`Indigenous method of manufacturing paper in the Himalayan tracts (Nepal, etc.)`.

This shows that the Imperial Record Department in 1937 was itself reclassifying a Political Consultation cluster as an object of technological/material history.

This adds another documentary state to the project's derivative graph:

## Bottom line

Wave 82 gives the paper project a pre-1944 archival survival and curatorial history. In December 1937, while A. F. M. Abdul Ali was Keeper of the Records of the Government of India and ex-officio Secretary of the Indian Historical Records Commission, the Imperial Record Department exhibited two of the exact consultation families now central to the project: the 13 Jan 1832 Nos.18–20 Himalayan/Nepal paper-manufacture cluster and O'Shaughnessy's 29 Nov 1841 No.147 report on bleaching paper manufactured at Darjeeling. Abdul Ali signed the complete exhibition list. Seven years later he cited these consultation records in `The Daphne Paper of Nepal`. This does not prove the article was written from exhibition notes, but it establishes unusually tight archival and curatorial provenance. At the same time, the London route has sharpened: an independent documentary citation places 11 May 1842 India Political consultation material in `IOR/P/196/12`, so Campbell's No.70 should now be sought there rather than merely somewhere between `/10` and `/13`. The 1841 exact IOR/P volume remains deliberately unresolved. The next decisive step is item-level recovery, especially `IOR/P/196/12, 11 May 1842, No.70`, and a direct late-November 1841 volume identification for No.147.
