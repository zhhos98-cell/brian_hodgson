# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 34
## Branching derivative states: Jumla revenue and Kathmandu–Peking routes

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: paper-centred reconstruction of the two strongest Cambridge branching systems. The aim is to move beyond support labels and compare source extent, derivative extent, writing quality, corrections, format, support, likely next operation and later custody.

## Executive result

Two source families now function as controlled tests of documentary material versioning.

### A. Jumla revenue

`vol.59/38` — Nepali handmade, Nepali, 36 folios
→ `vol.8/1` — English, machine-made, 48.5 × 38 cm, chart, Campbell, good condition, 8 folios
AND
→ `vol.102/21` — Farsi, Nepali handmade, very neat hand, table, 2 folios.

### B. Kathmandu–Peking route of Dalbhanjan Pande

`vol.59/23` — Nepali handmade, Nepali, 8 folios
→ `vol.2/3` — English, imported thick paper, fair hand, ink with pencil corrections/addenda, 3 folios, held inside the `Route Book Private`
AND
→ `vol.102/19` — Urdu/Farsi, Nepali handmade, poor hand, incomplete, 2 folios.

The key result is not `translation causes paper change`. Both families branch into derivatives with different support states. Even format cannot explain the Jumla split because both downstream states are tabular/chart-like.

Mechanisms:

- `BRANCHED_DERIVATIVE_SUPPORT_STATES`
- `DERIVATIVE_COMPRESSION_AS_VERSIONING`
- `SUPPORT_AND_FORMAT_JOINTLY_RECODE_INFORMATION`
- `DERIVATIVE_STATE_NOT_LANGUAGE_DETERMINES_SUPPORT`
- `PROVENANCE_GRAMMAR_ON_DERIVATIVE_SURFACE`
- `CUSTODY_RECOMBINES_VERSION_STATES`

## 1. Jumla source state

Cambridge inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol059.html

`MSS EUR HODGSON/59, item 38, fols.205–240`

- title: Revenue and levies from the whole of Jumla;
- support: Nepali hand-made paper;
- language: Nepali;
- extent: approximately 36 folios;
- contents: daras, villages, households, village landholding, land revenue and multiple levies;
- catalogue explicitly points to the English summary in `vol.8/1` and Farsi summary in `vol.102/21`.

The source is therefore a relatively extended local-language revenue record rather than a small table copied from an English analytical state.

## 2. Jumla English derivative — large machine-paper chart

Cambridge inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol008.html

`MSS EUR HODGSON/8, item 1, fols.1–8b`

- date: 1840;
- support: machine-made paper;
- dimensions: 48.5 × 38 cm;
- medium: natural black ink;
- hand: written in chart form by Archibald Campbell;
- condition: good;
- language: English;
- extent: approximately 8 folios;
- content retains named daras and villages, households with caste breakdown, fields, taxes, duties and fines;
- catalogue explicitly identifies `vol.59/38` as the Nepali original and `vol.102/21` as Persian summary/translation.

Interpretation:

This derivative is not merely translated. It is materially reformatted onto large machine-made sheets and compressed from the source's c.36 folios into c.8 chart folios.

Mechanisms:

- `SOURCE_TO_LARGE_FORMAT_ANALYTICAL_CHART`
- `DERIVATIVE_COMPRESSION_AS_VERSIONING`
- `CAMPBELL_ANALYTICAL_CHART_STATE`.

## 3. Jumla Farsi derivative — compact country-paper chart

Cambridge inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol102.html

`MSS EUR HODGSON/102, item 21, fols.115–116`

- support: Nepali hand-made paper;
- language: Farsi;
- hand: very neat;
- format: table;
- extent: 2 folios;
- content: taxes and revenues from 17 Jumla villages;
- catalogue cross-references both `vol.8/1` and `vol.59/38`.

Interpretation:

The Farsi derivative is even more compressed than the English chart but remains on Nepali handmade paper. Because both derivative states are tabular, `chart/table format` alone cannot explain the support difference.

The cleanest working contrast is therefore:

`same source family + tabular derivative`
→ `English/Campbell/large machine-paper chart`
versus
→ `Farsi/very-neat/Nepali-paper compact chart`.

This is currently the strongest documentary quasi-experiment in the paper article.

## 4. Jumla custody recombination in the 1864 India Office gift

William Wilson Hunter, *Life of Brian Houghton Hodgson* (1896), Appendix B reproduces a `List of MSS. Presented by Mr. Hodgson to the India Office Library, August, 1864`.

Digital scan:
https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/24484/GIPE-000910-Contents.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2

The list states that the sixth large bundle contained, **in Khas and in English**, the statement of the Revenue of the Province of Jumla.

This is highly significant for the paper argument.

After documentary branching had produced materially different Khas/Nepali and English version states, later archival transfer **recombined at least two linguistic/material states into one custody unit**.

Sequence:

`source/derivative differentiation`
→ `parallel Khas + English version states`
→ `shared large bundle in 1864 transfer`
→ `later separate volume/item identities in the modern catalogue`.

Mechanism:

`CUSTODY_RECOMBINES_VERSION_STATES`.

Source-control:

The 1864 manifest does not by itself prove that every modern `vol.59/38` and `vol.8/1` sheet was physically in precisely the same lesser wrapper. It does establish a named large-bundle unit containing Jumla revenue in both Khas and English.

## 8. Publication pathway makes the English derivative more than a translation

Hodgson's published paper:

B. H. Hodgson, `Route of two Nepalese Embassies to Pekin with remarks on the water-shed and plateau of Tibet`, *Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal* 25 (1856), 473–497.

Digital scan:
https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Tibet%20and%20China/1857%20Route%20of%20two%20Nepalese%20Embassies%20to%20Pekin%20by%20Hodgson%20from%20JASBv25%20s.pdf

Hodgson states that the underlying papers were official summaries of Nepalese embassies, that the translations were literal, and that additional information he inserted was distinguished from the translated source by **brackets**, while further additions were placed in footnotes.

This creates a new mechanism:

`PROVENANCE_GRAMMAR_ON_DERIVATIVE_SURFACE`.

The derivative paper/document state does not merely contain translated information. It visibly differentiates:

- source-derived translation;
- Hodgson's inserted information;
- later explanatory notes.

Material/graphic formatting therefore becomes an apparatus for source control.

Source-control:

The published paper and Cambridge `vol.2/3` are strongly linked by catalogue citation and content, but object-level textual comparison is still required before claiming the surviving manuscript is the exact printer's copy or direct copy-text of the 1856 article.

## 9. Compression must become a paper variable

The current cases show major changes in documentary extent:

### Jumla

- source: c.36 folios;
- English chart: c.8 folios;
- Farsi chart: 2 folios.

### Peking route

- source: 8 folios;
- English derivative: 3 folios;
- Urdu/Farsi derivative: 2 folios and incomplete.

Do not interpret folio reduction as simple information loss. Compression may involve:

- tabulation;
- omission;
- aggregation;
- translation density;
- larger sheet dimensions;
- selection for downstream use;
- incomplete copying.

Required variable:

`documentary_compression_state`.

Mechanism:

`DERIVATIVE_COMPRESSION_AS_VERSIONING`.

## 10. Revised causal hypothesis

The evidence now falsifies several simple models:

`language -> support`

`actor ethnicity -> support`

`table format -> support`.

A better working hypothesis is:

`derivative material state = available stock × workplace access × writer/task assignment × target format × completeness × correction/revision requirement × intended next operation`.

This remains heuristic until physical inspection and a larger denominator are complete.

## Bottom line

The two best branching systems now show that Hodgson's paper archive is a versioning system, not simply a multilingual collection. The same source can generate derivative states that differ simultaneously in paper stock, writer, scale, extent, neatness, completeness, correction practice and downstream use. In the Peking route case, the English derivative eventually carries an explicit graphical provenance grammar separating literal translation from Hodgson's additions. In the Jumla case, the source and English derivative were later recombined in a single 1864 India Office custody bundle. Paper therefore operates at three linked levels: it differentiates documentary versions, provides surfaces for provenance control, and is later reorganised by archival custody.
