## Finding 42 — the printed 1832 article exposes an unnamed enquiry layer, but does not identify the technical informant

Evidence status: **SECURE for the printed wording; technical source identity remains OPEN.**

Hodgson's January 1832 article, `On the Native Method of making the Paper, denominated in Hindustan, Nipalese`, gives unusually fine-grained manufacturing detail: implements, preparation and strength of the ash liquor, boiling, pounding, churning, coarse screening, floating cloth-bottom frame, manual distribution of pulp, drying, polishing, sizing, sheet size, and dried pulp prepared for transport.

The article also describes paper-makers' practice at low level. It states that paper-makers commonly use inner-bark peelings fresh from the plant, while noting that freshness is not indispensable.

Yet when Hodgson reaches the history of the craft, his source position changes visibly. He writes, in part, `I cannot learn ... but the Nipalese say ...`. The article therefore directly proves that he was making enquiries of unnamed Nepalese informants at least about the history/tradition of papermaking. It does **not** name the person or persons from whom the technical procedure itself was obtained, and it does not state that Hodgson personally observed the full process at a manufacturing site.

Primary printed witness:
- B. H. Hodgson, `On the Native Method of making the Paper, denominated in Hindustan, Nipalese`, *Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal* 1 (January 1832), 8–11.
- searchable scan: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/38/Journal_of_the_Asiatic_Society_of_Bengal_%28IA_journalofasiatic01asia%29.pdf
- short scan: https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Nepal/1832%20Native%20Method%20of%20Making%20Paper%20in%20Hindustan%20Nipalese%20by%20Hodgson%20from%20JASBv1%20s.pdf

### Source rule

Do not write `Hodgson observed the process` unless a manuscript or letter states observation directly. The current secure formulation is narrower:

- Hodgson produced a highly operational technical description;
- he knew specific paper-maker practices;
- the printed article preserves at least one explicit layer of unnamed Nepalese testimony;
- the channel by which the detailed technical procedure reached him remains unidentified.

## Finding 43 — BL Mss Eur Hodgson/6, item 6, ff. 77–81 is a precise manuscript target, but it is copy-state material

Evidence status: **SECURE catalogue-level control.**

The Hodgson Papers inventory describes `Mss Eur Hodgson/6, item 6, ff. 77–81 (December 1831)` as a letter from Hodgson to H. T. Prinsep concerning a sample of Nepali paper and papermaking in Nepal, consisting of a **covering letter and attached note on paper making**.

The same volume-level description states that **all manuscripts in Volume 6 are copies** and that most were published. This matters for the source genealogy: ff. 77–81 are a high-value textual witness, but should not automatically be treated as the original outgoing letter or earliest physical state of the technical account.

Inventory witness:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html

### Bounded inspection questions

1. Separate the covering letter from the attached technical note.
2. Ask whether the covering letter says how Hodgson obtained, checked, witnessed or had reported to him the manufacturing particulars.
3. Distinguish Hodgson's hand from copyist hand if the object permits.
4. Compare the attached note with the January 1832 printed text for omissions, names, marginal additions or source-language not carried into print.

This is a bounded manuscript question, not a reason for generic Hodgson correspondence harvesting.

## Finding 44 — the inventory cross-references around the 1831 paper item are not safe duplicate witnesses

Evidence status: **SECURE catalogue discrepancy.**

The Volume 6 inventory attaches cross-references from the 1831 paper item to `vol 06/09 fol.181-184` and `vol 14/16 fol.172`.

Direct inspection of the inventory shows that:

- `vol.6/9, ff.181–184` is Campbell's **July 1841** Darjeeling papermaking letter;
- in Volume 14, **f.172 begins item 15**, Campbell's **4 July 1841** letter to J. R. Colvin about Nepali papermaking; the inventory calls it a duplicate of the Campbell letter in Volume 6;
- Volume 14 item 16 actually begins at **f.177a** and is Hodgson's 1831 report on the commerce of Nepal, not a paper-manufacture duplicate.

Inventory witnesses:
- https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html
- https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol014.html

### Source rule

Preserve this discrepancy. Do **not** cite the 1841 Campbell items as additional copies of Hodgson's 1831 covering letter/note merely because the online inventory cross-references them. The reference is thematic, malformed, or otherwise insufficient to establish textual identity.

## Finding 45 — question-led mediated enquiry is source-visible in Hodgson's 1831 working environment, but no named aide can yet be joined to the paper case

Evidence status: **SECURE contextual calibration; direct paper-source attribution UNCONTROLLED.**

The same archive preserves explicit examples of Hodgson's information-gathering method around the same period and subject-neighbourhood:

- Volume 14, ff. 69–75: Hodgson instructs his assistant **Ramtanu / Ram Tunnoo** to collect information on agricultural labourers, farmers, servants and village trades;
- Volume 14, ff. 125–132 (1831): village-system material appears as questionnaires with answers;
- Volume 6, ff. 81b–156 (September 1831): law and police information is frequently presented in question-and-answer form;
- later Volume 14 material explicitly identifies field information and letters supplied by Ramtanu.

Inventory witnesses:
- https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol014.html
- https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html

This makes question-led, mediated enquiry a source-visible part of Hodgson's working practice. It does **not** license an inference that Ramtanu, Jatanlal, Amritananda, or any other known aide supplied the papermaking account. No named paper-case join has been recovered.

### Article consequence

If the precise 1831 manuscript/consultation packet does not name a technical source, preserve the asymmetry rather than fill it:

- Hodgson's technical prose is highly explicit;
- the makers or informants behind its fine-grained particulars are not named in the currently controlled text;
- the archive shows that Hodgson worked through questionnaires, assistants and supplied information elsewhere;
- the exact mediation chain for this paper account remains unresolved.

That is historically usable without converting absence into a generic `local knowledge` claim.
