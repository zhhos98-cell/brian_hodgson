# Paper drawing copy-state bifurcation, 1844–1875 — original sheets, transmission copies, loose portfolios, and bound manuscript volumes

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred source-control note. The purpose is to distinguish paper states of Hodgson's zoological drawings without expanding into a general custody history.

## Core question

**How did copying, sheet format, loose storage, binding and later institutional handling change what Hodgson's zoological drawings were as working paper objects?**

The working sequence is:

`original drawing sheet -> copied transmission sheet -> loose portfolio corpus -> reordered working set -> bound manuscript volume / institutional object`.

The point is not simply provenance. It is that each paper state changes:

- what can be compared;
- what can be counted;
- what relations remain visible;
- what can be transported;
- what can be rebound or regrouped;
- what later users understand as the scientific object.

Primary mechanism:

`DRAWING_PAPER_COPY_STATE_BIFURCATION`.

---

## 1. 1844: Hodgson explicitly distinguishes original drawings from transmission copies

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/4/8`, Hodgson to J. E. Gray, 27 Dec. 1844.

Hodgson states that he checked drawings sent to the British Museum against:

`my own original drawings from which those ... were copied for transmission to England`.

This establishes at least two paper states:

1. retained original drawing sheets;
2. copied sheets made for transmission.

Mechanisms:

- `MASTER_DRAWING_TRANSMISSION_COPY`
- `PAPER_COPY_STATE_REDUNDANCY`.

The same zoological content therefore existed in materially distinct paper witnesses. Loss of one copy state did not automatically erase all evidence relations because another paper witness remained.

---

## 2. Transmission copies had a distinct paper format and ordering regime

The same 1844 correspondence describes the transmitted series as:

- sent in regular numerical order;
- made on a smaller scale, around 20 × 12 inches;
- distinct from an earlier, ruder, larger-scale series.

Paper properties here are not decorative details.

They define at least three operational variables:

- `copy state`;
- `sheet dimensions / scale`;
- `serial order`.

Mechanism:

`FORMAT_CHANGE_ACROSS_DRAWING_COPY_STATES`.

The transmission copy is therefore not simply a duplicate image. It is a reformatted paper object optimized for circulation and ordered inspection.

---

## 3. Original/copy distinction complicates later totals

A later count of `bird drawings` can refer to:

- retained originals;
- transmission copies;
- earlier large-scale sheets;
- later smaller-scale sheets;
- mixed surviving states.

Therefore:

`drawing count != unique represented subject count`

and also:

`drawing count != unique image-design count`.

The same represented bird may survive in more than one paper copy state.

Mechanism:

`COPY_STATE_CARDINALITY_INFLATION_RISK`.

This must be kept separate from the already established problem that one sheet can contain several subjects.

Two different divergences exist:

`one design -> multiple paper copies`

and

`one paper sheet -> multiple represented subjects`.

---

## 4. 1870: 1,104 sheets are a loose portfolio corpus

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/9`, Hodgson to G. F. L. Marshall, 11 Feb. 1870.

Hodgson describes four portfolios containing 1,104 drawing sheets and associated notes/manuscripts.

Earlier correspondence of 2 Feb. 1870 states that the material is scattered `pell mell` across the four portfolios and must be brought together by species/group for proper inspection.

Paper state:

`loose sheets distributed across multiple portfolios`.

Mechanisms:

- `LOOSE_PORTFOLIO_PAPER_CORPUS`
- `PORTFOLIO_SCATTER_TO_SPECIES_REASSEMBLY`.

The scientific relation is not physically coextensive with the container relation.

`portfolio adjacency != taxonomic adjacency`.

The user has to reconstruct the scientific object by moving paper sheets across container boundaries.

---

## 5. Loose-sheet format permits reordering, but also destabilizes relations

A loose portfolio corpus has strong affordances:

- sheets can be extracted;
- reordered by species or group;
- compared side by side;
- temporarily loaned;
- recombined with notes;
- separated for publication.

The same affordances create risks:

- original adjacency can disappear;
- copied/original states can be mixed;
- sheet order can change;
- a relation once carried by sequence must instead survive through numbers/labels.

Mechanism:

`LOOSE_SHEET_REORDERABILITY_AND_RELATIONAL_RISK`.

This is a paper-specific reason why Hodgson's external numbering system matters so much.

---

## 6. Numbering compensates for loose-paper mobility

When a corpus is stored as loose sheets, physical sequence is unstable.

A drawing number can preserve an address even after:

- sheets are moved between portfolios;
- taxonomic order changes;
- copies are separated from originals;
- notes are detached and later rejoined.

Mechanism:

`NUMBERING_AS_LOOSE_PAPER_RELATION_STABILIZER`.

This links directly to the HOD/1/3 manners notebooks, where descriptions are keyed to numbers attached to drawings.

The number is therefore especially valuable because the sheet is materially mobile.

---

## 7. Current ZSL description introduces a bound-manuscript state

The Zoological Society's present institutional description states that its Hodgson holdings include six manuscript volumes of original bird drawings and extensive notes, along with two mammal volumes.

This indicates a paper state different from the 1870 loose four-portfolio corpus:

`loose drawing sheets -> bound manuscript volume`.

The exact historical relation between these states remains unresolved.

Possible transformations include:

- original sheets later bound;
- selected loose sheets bound while others remained separate;
- already bound original series distinct from loose transmission copies;
- institutional rebinding after accession.

Mechanism:

`LOOSE_SHEET_TO_BOUND_VOLUME_TRANSFORMATION`.

Do not identify the six current volumes with the 1,104/1,115 loose sheets without direct binding/provenance evidence.

---

## 8. Binding changes the ontology of the drawing corpus

Binding converts a mobile sheet collection into a fixed-sequence paper object.

Potential gains:

- physical protection;
- stable internal sequence;
- easier shelf identity;
- reduced risk of individual sheet dispersal.

Potential losses:

- side-by-side comparison across distant leaves becomes harder;
- taxonomic reordering becomes constrained;
- original portfolio/group relations may disappear;
- sheet recto/verso, edge and watermark access may be reduced;
- later users may treat the bound volume as the primary object rather than the individual sheet series from which it was made.

Mechanism:

`BINDING_FIXES_ONE_RELATIONAL_STATE`.

Binding preserves paper while potentially freezing one historically contingent arrangement.

---

## 9. Original sheet, copy sheet, loose corpus and bound volume are distinct epistemic states

A minimum ontology:

### `ORIGINAL_DRAWING_SHEET`

Function:

- master visual witness;
- source for transmission copy;
- local working reference.

### `TRANSMISSION_COPY_SHEET`

Function:

- circulation;
- remote inspection;
- publication/institutional use.

### `LOOSE_PORTFOLIO_SHEET_STATE`

Function:

- flexible sorting and recombination;
- temporary research use;
- serial count as sheets.

### `BOUND_MANUSCRIPT_VOLUME_STATE`

Function:

- institutional preservation;
- stabilized sequence;
- shelf/codex identity.

Mechanism:

`PAPER_STATE_CHANGES_SCIENTIFIC_OBJECT_IDENTITY`.

The phrase `Hodgson's bird drawings` should therefore be avoided unless the paper state is specified.

---

## 10. 1874–75 is best treated as a paper-state problem, not a simple custody contradiction

Current ZSL institutional history broadly associates Hodgson's zoological manuscripts with the 1874 donation.

Yet a 30 Jan. 1875 Sclater letter indicates that bird drawings were still in A. Hume's hands and that Hume was being notified of their intended destination.

Instead of forcing a single chronology, preserve the possibility that different paper states were moving differently:

- bound/original manuscript volumes may already have entered ZSL;
- loose portfolio drawing sheets may still have remained with Hume;
- later institutional descriptions may aggregate these accessions retrospectively.

Mechanism:

`PARALLEL_PAPER_STATES_WITH_DIFFERENT_CUSTODY_TIMELINES`.

This interpretation is only a working hypothesis until object-level accession evidence is recovered.

---

## 11. Article-level use

This material belongs in the paper article only where it demonstrates a paper operation.

Best formulation:

**Hodgson's zoological drawings existed in several paper states rather than as one stable collection. Retained originals generated transmission copies on a different scale; loose sheets could be scattered across portfolios and reassembled by species; external numbers preserved relations while those sheets moved; later binding converted mobile sheets into manuscript volumes with a fixed sequence. Copying, format and binding therefore changed not just where drawings were kept, but what kind of scientific object they were and what comparisons they permitted.**

This keeps the analysis centred on paper rather than general provenance.

## 13. Direct paper checks required

1. Identify paper stock, watermark/countermark and dimensions for retained original drawings versus transmission copies.
2. Test whether the c.20 × 12 inch transmitted series uses a consistent paper stock distinct from the earlier larger series.
3. Determine whether drawing numbers were copied identically across original and transmission witnesses.
4. Inspect whether current ZSL bound bird volumes contain original sheets, copied sheets or mixed states.
5. Record binding date/state, sewing, guards, trimming and whether original sheet edges survive.
6. Test whether sheets now bound together were historically adjacent before institutional binding.
7. Compare the 1870 four-portfolio 1,104-sheet state with present bound-volume contents without assuming identity.
8. Keep `sheet`, `copy`, `subject`, `drawing identifier`, `portfolio` and `bound volume` as separate count units.

## Bottom line

The paper question is stronger than the custody question. Hodgson's drawings moved through materially different states: original sheet, transmission copy, loose portfolio unit and bound manuscript volume. Each state changes comparison, mobility, countability and relational stability. The scientific object is therefore partly produced by paper format and paper handling, not merely carried by them.
