# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 35
## The 1864 India Office transfer as version-family archival design

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: custody/packing follow-up to Wave 34. This wave tests whether the Jumla `Khas + English` co-bundling was exceptional or part of a systematic transfer logic.

## Executive result

The August 1864 India Office manifest shows repeated deliberate co-custody of source, translation, abstract and multilingual derivative states.

Jumla was not an isolated case.

Across chronicles, itineraries, Newar institutions, army materials and revenue records, Hodgson's transfer arrangement repeatedly keeps related documentary versions together at large-bundle or lesser-bundle level.

The strongest formulation is:

`research version family -> archival transfer family`.

This is different from arranging papers by language, paper type or modern discipline.

Mechanisms:

- `VERSION_FAMILY_CO_BUNDLING_AS_ARCHIVAL_DESIGN`
- `CUSTODY_RECOMBINES_VERSION_STATES`
- `TRANSFER_CONTAINER_PRESERVES_DERIVATIVE_RELATIONS`
- `LANGUAGE_DIVERSITY_WITHIN_CUSTODY_UNIT`
- `ARCHIVAL_PACKING_AS_RELATIONAL_METADATA`

Primary source basis:

William Wilson Hunter, *Life of Brian Houghton Hodgson* (1896), Appendix B, reproducing Hodgson's 2 August 1864 presentation letter and the `List of MSS. Presented by Mr. Hodgson to the India Office Library, August, 1864`.

Digital scan:

## 1. Hodgson explicitly described the collection as raw but usable relational material

In the presentation letter Hodgson says the manuscripts had been accumulated over many years to illustrate Nepal's natural and civil history, literature, languages, religion, institutions and resources. He notes that English and Hindi lists of the contents were already in the librarian's hands.

The transfer was therefore accompanied by descriptive/control surfaces rather than being a mass of unstructured paper.

The later trunk-and-bundle list shows that container structure itself also encoded relations.

Mechanism:

`ARCHIVAL_PACKING_AS_RELATIONAL_METADATA`.

---

## 2. Newari chronicles: source, Persian translation, abstract and English translations co-bundled

`Trunk No. I, first large bundle` contains the chronicles of Nepal in 25 lesser bundles, split into Newari and Gurkhali parcels.

Within the Newari chronicle section the manifest lists:

- multiple royal/dynastic histories;
- `Persian translation` of one chronicle state;
- Jit Mohan's abstract of the preceding materials;
- repeated/sundry papers;
- English translations of the Vasavalis in two volumes;
- Persian addenda;
- the English and Persian derivative work attributed to Hodgson's `office people`.

Thus one large archival unit preserves:

`chronicle source states`
+ `translation state`
+ `abstract state`
+ `repetition/copy states`
+ `English derivative volumes`
+ `Persian addenda`.

Mechanism:

`CHRONICLE_VERSION_FAMILY_CO_CUSTODY`.

This is close to explicit documentary version control at packing scale.

---

## 3. Gurkhali chronicles: twelve source/history states plus five English translation volumes

In the Gurkhali parcel, the manifest lists twelve historical/source items, including:

- a large roll given by the King of Nepal;
- conquest history supplied by Balbhanjan Pande;
- royal and Thapa Vasavalis;
- histories, genealogies and chronology states;
- a court-munshi Vasavali;
- an account of Regent Ran Bahadur.

Item 13 is:

`English translations of the above, by my office people, in five volumes.`

This relation is unusually explicit.

At custody level:

`source set 1–12 -> translation set 13`

remains inside the same large chronicle bundle.

Mechanisms:

- `SOURCE_SET_PLUS_TRANSLATION_SET_CO_BUNDLING`
- `OFFICE_TRANSLATION_LABOUR_PRESERVED_WITH_SOURCE_FAMILY`.

---

## 4. Itineraries: multilingual versions are themselves a single lesser-bundle class

`Trunk No. I, second large bundle`, item 2 is described as:

`Various Itineraries, in Nagri, in Persian, and in English`.

This matters directly for the Peking-route branching system.

The archive-transfer ontology does not separate itineraries into:

`Nagri collection`

`Persian collection`

`English collection`.

It groups multilingual itinerary states together as one functional documentary family.

Mechanism:

`ITINERARY_VERSION_FAMILY_AS_CUSTODY_UNIT`.

This strengthens the interpretation of `vol.59/23 -> vol.2/3 + vol.102/19`: multilingual route versions belonged to a broader historically meaningful packing class.

---

## 5. Newar institutions: original palm-leaf deeds plus English/Persian translations

The same second large bundle contains an account of Newar institutions and customs, including annual festivals, caste classification and agriculture. The manifest explicitly says it contains a large collection of **original Talpatras/palm-leaf title deeds**, and that some materials were translated into English or Persian.

Thus one custody family contains:

`original non-paper/palm-leaf legal evidence`
→ `paper copy/translation states in English or Persian`.

This is the packing-level analogue of the Cambridge multistage support conversion chain:

`palm leaf -> Nepal-paper copy -> machine-paper Urdu translation`.

Mechanism:

`CROSS_SUPPORT_VERSION_FAMILY_CO_BUNDLING`.

---

## 6. Army bundle: English volumes remain with Persian and Khas papers on the same subjects

`Trunk No. 2, second large bundle` contains army, classification, judicial and customs materials.

The manifest lists:

- English scraps on the army;
- an English volume on the army;
- three English volumes covering classification of the people, judicial system, customs and army;
- `some papers in Persian and in Khas on the same subject`.

This is direct evidence that language is not the top-level custody classifier.

The unit is organised around related subject/work families with multiple linguistic documentary states retained together.

Mechanism:

`MULTILINGUAL_SUBJECT_VERSION_CO_CUSTODY`.

---

## 7. Revenue bundle: source/derivative co-custody recurs

`Trunk No. 2, third large bundle` contains:

- revenue materials for Patan/Bhatgaon;
- questions and answers on the judicial system;
- the manifest notes that originals of that paper are included in another related bundle/item;
- five English volumes and scraps on Nepal land revenue;
- two English volumes plus Hindi scraps on Tarai revenue.

Even before the explicit Jumla entry, the transfer arrangement repeatedly places English analytical/derivative volumes alongside local-language source/scrap states in related revenue clusters.

Then the sixth large bundle states unambiguously:

`in Khas and in English, Statement of the Revenue of the Province of Jumla`.

Mechanism:

`REVENUE_VERSION_FAMILY_CO_CUSTODY`.

---

## 8. Archival packing preserves relations that modern volume order can obscure

The 1864 manifest demonstrates that historical container identity and modern shelfmark identity are not equivalent.

A version family may once have been co-custodied in one large bundle even though its components are now separated as:

- English analytical volumes;
- Nepali/Khas originals;
- Persian/Urdu translations;
- other modern subject volumes.

Therefore modern archival dispersion can conceal older relational architecture.

The correct reconstruction sequence is:

`historical version relation`
+ `historical container relation`
+ `modern shelfmark relation`.

Do not let the modern catalogue substitute for either of the first two.

Mechanism:

`MODERN_SHELFMARK_DISPERSION_OBSCURES_HISTORICAL_VERSION_FAMILY`.

---

## 9. Packing becomes an epistemic operation

This evidence sharpens the general container argument.

A bundle can do more than protect paper.

Here it can preserve:

- source–translation relation;
- source–abstract relation;
- multilingual equivalence or comparison;
- subject/version family membership;
- the products of office translation labour;
- the relation between original evidence and derivative analytical states.

Thus:

`container = relational storage device`.

For the paper article, the stronger formulation is:

`transfer bundle = version-family carrier`.

Mechanism:

`TRANSFER_CONTAINER_PRESERVES_DERIVATIVE_RELATIONS`.

---

## 10. Article consequence

The paper story now has a full cycle:

`source paper/object`
→ `derivative material versions`
→ `branching across languages/supports/formats`
→ `publication/provenance marking`
→ `archival recombination in version-family containers`
→ `later catalogue dispersion`.

This makes paper versioning historically visible before, during and after knowledge conversion.

The key claim is not that Hodgson had a modern archival theory. It is narrower:

**the 1864 transfer arrangement repeatedly preserved multilingual and derivative relations by co-bundling source and translated/abstracted states.**

## Source-control rules

1. Hunter 1896 reproduces the 1864 list; locate the original India Office transfer list before treating every transcription detail as diplomatically final.
2. `same large bundle` does not mean `same lesser wrapper` or immediate sheet adjacency.
3. Co-bundling demonstrates custody relation, not necessarily direct textual descent among every item in the unit.
4. Modern volume/item mapping must be reconstructed separately.
5. Do not infer paper support from language in the 1864 manifest; support must come from object/catalogue evidence.
6. `English translations of the above` is a strong declared relation; `papers on the same subject` is a weaker family relation and should remain separately coded.

## Bottom line

The 1864 India Office transfer preserves a systematic archival logic that matches the paper article's emerging versioning model. Chronicles, itineraries, legal/institutional documents, army papers and revenue materials were repeatedly transferred with source, translation, abstract and multilingual derivative states kept within the same larger custody families. The transfer container was therefore not merely packaging. It could preserve the relational architecture of documentary versions that paper, translation and analytical work had generated.
