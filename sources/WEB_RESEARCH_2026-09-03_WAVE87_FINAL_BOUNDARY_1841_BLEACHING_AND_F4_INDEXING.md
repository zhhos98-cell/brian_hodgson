## 1841 O'Shaughnessy bleaching report: bounded London search and the limits of public item-level indexing

Date: 2026-09-03

Status: final public-web/archive-catalogue pass for the current Hodgson paper-history run. This wave adds no speculative shelfmark. It records the strongest remaining boundary evidence for the 1841 bleaching report and closes the search before object-level archive consultation.

## Executive result

The British Library catalogue readily exposes several O'Shaughnessy Board's Collections from 1841–42 when their subjects were separately catalogued. Examples include:

- `IOR/F/4/1949/84712`, reports by J. McClelland and W. B. O'Shaughnessy on manufacturing medicines in India, Mar–Jun 1841;
- `IOR/F/4/2015/90071`, experimental manufacture of sulphate of magnesium from Madras magnesite, Jun 1841–Jul 1842.

The same catalogue directly exposes the later Darjeeling paper experiment in:

- `IOR/F/4/2006/89528`, Mar–Nov 1842, whose scope includes `report by Dr Campbell of the result of his experiment in making paper from the bark of the paper tree`.

By contrast, repeated public-catalogue searches did not expose a separately catalogued F/4 item for the exact 1841 title recorded by the Imperial Record Department exhibition:

`Report on the method of bleaching paper manufactured at Darjeeling`

with archival address:

`Political Consultation, 29 Nov 1841, No.147`.

This search asymmetry matters. O'Shaughnessy experimental-manufacture material from the same period is visible in F/4. Campbell's 1842 Darjeeling paper report is visible in F/4. The 1841 bleaching report is securely identified at India-side consultation level and was physically exhibit-ready in the Imperial Record Department in 1937. Its failure to surface as a public F/4 item is therefore best treated as an item-level indexing / later regrouping / finding-aid problem. It is weak evidence for absence from the London derivative system.

Mar–Jun 1841.

https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-000780231

`IOR/F/4/2015/90071`

Title:

`Results of the experimental manufacture of sulphate of magnesium from magnesite obtained from Madras`.

Date range:

Jun 1841–Jul 1842.

These records show that O'Shaughnessy-related experimental manufacture business from the immediate period entered Board's Collections and is searchable at item level when separately described.

This does not identify the paper bleaching file. It narrows the explanation for its current invisibility.

## 3. No.147 remains secure as an India-side consultation object

1937 Indian Historical Records Commission exhibition evidence identifies:

`Pol. Con. 29 Nov. 1841, No.147`

as O'Shaughnessy's report on bleaching paper manufactured at Darjeeling.

Abdul Ali's 1944 article uses the same archival address when describing O'Shaughnessy's bleaching method.

Thus title, consultation date, consultation number, author/office and paper subject are already controlled without a London shelfmark.

The unresolved question is repository crosswalk, not document identity.

## 4. Final interpretation of the London gap

British Library's F/4 series description says Political Board's Collections may be treated as a virtually complete duplicate of Indian Political Proceedings. Board's Collections were formed from papers extracted from Indian Proceedings for use in reviewing London draft despatches.

Given that architecture, plus the visible O'Shaughnessy experimental-manufacture files and the visible 1842 Campbell paper file, the strongest cautious interpretation is:

`NO147_LONDON_DERIVATIVE = EXPECTED_BY_SERIES_ARCHITECTURE`

while

`NO147_EXACT_LONDON_ITEM = REQUIRES_REGISTER_OR_VOLUME_INSPECTION`.

Public catalogue searching has reached diminishing returns.

## Bottom line

The remaining 1841 problem has changed form. The O'Shaughnessy paper is no longer an unidentified archival rumour. Its consultation date, number, title and subject were explicit enough for the Imperial Record Department to exhibit it in 1937. The London Board's Collections preserve other O'Shaughnessy experimental-manufacture files from 1841–42, and the 1842 Campbell paper report is separately catalogued in F/4. The missing piece is therefore an item-level crosswalk. That is a bounded archival task. The public record is already sufficient for a paper-history argument about how Nepal paper moved through specimen circulation, chemical experiment, administrative consultation and London documentary reassembly.
