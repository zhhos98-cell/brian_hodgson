# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 47
## 1833 -> annotated prior print -> 1874 revised reprint: the military-tribe paper remains a versioned object after publication

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: paper-centred publication-state follow-up to Waves 45–46. This wave asks whether the 1833 `Origin and Classification of the Military Tribes of Nepal` remained textually stable when incorporated into Hodgson's 1874 collected `Essays`.

## Executive result

The 1874 collection demonstrates that print was not an endpoint in Hodgson's paper workflow.

The editor's `NOTICE` states that Hodgson had made numerous manuscript additions in his own copies of earlier collective publications, including the `Selections`, and that corrections/additions representing nearly thirty years of changing views were introduced into the 1874 reprint.

For the military-tribe article itself, the explicit Part II `List of Additions and Corrections` records only two corrections within pp.37–44:

1. p.39, line 17: `caste` -> `cast`;
2. p.40, line 4 from top: `some` -> `about 100`.

The correction list records no alteration to the classificatory table on pp.42–44.

Thus the 1874 state appears to preserve the basic 1833 classificatory architecture while locally sharpening:

- a physiognomic/type vocabulary (`cast`, not social `caste`);
- the chronology of Khas/Gorkhali expansion (`some years ago` -> `about 100 years ago`).

Strong mechanism:

`PRINT -> MANUSCRIPT_ANNOTATION_SURFACE -> REVISED_PRINT`.

Additional mechanisms:

- `PRINT_STATE_REMAINS_REVISABLE`
- `AUTHOR_COPY_AS_VERSION_CONTROL_SURFACE`
- `CLASSIFICATORY_ARCHITECTURE_PERSISTS_ACROSS_REPRINT`
- `LOCAL_SEMANTIC_AND_CHRONOLOGICAL_RECALIBRATION`

## 1. The 1874 book explicitly derives from annotated earlier printed copies

The 1874 `NOTICE` explains the reprint history.

Professor J. Summers obtained Hodgson's permission to reprint earlier journal papers in the `Phoenix`. The editor states that Hodgson's later or `improved and extended views` were embodied in manuscript additions made in his own copies of earlier printed collections. The notice says these corrections and additions were introduced into the new reprint and represented phases of Hodgson's thinking spread across nearly thirty years.

Text source:
https://www.scribd.com/document/674819438/Essays-on-Language-00-Ho-Dg

This supplies a material chain:

`earlier print`
-> `Hodgson manuscript marginal/additional layer`
-> `Phoenix/reprint preparation`
-> `1874 collected print`.

Mechanism:

`ANNOTATED_PRINT_AS_INTERMEDIATE_MANUSCRIPT_STATE`.

This is directly parallel to the manuscript versioning elsewhere in the archive: a materially stable printed object can be reopened as a writable surface and become the carrier of a later version.

## 2. The military-tribe paper occupies Part II pp.37–44 in 1874

The 1874 contents identify:

`III. Origin and Classification of the Military Tribes of Nepal`

- originally JASB vol.II (1833), p.217;
- reprinted in the earlier `Selections`;
- reprinted in 1874 at Part II p.37 onward.

The 1874 text records the paper as read before the Bengal Asiatic Society on 9 January 1833.

The text still combines:

- historical/ethnological narrative;
- recruitment evaluation;
- the tabular classification of Brahmanical and military tribes.

The 1874 table retains the Khas, Ekthariya, Thakuri, Magar and Gurung classificatory architecture.

## 3. Correction 1: `caste` -> `cast` on p.39

The Part II corrections list states:

`Page 39, line 17, for "caste" read "cast".`

In context, p.39 is discussing Magars and Gurungs. The 1874 body text as set reads that their physiognomies have peculiarities proper to each, but with the general `caste and character` fully developed in both.

The correction changes this to:

`general cast and character`.

This matters semantically.

`caste` would invoke social-caste classification;

`cast` means general form, type, look or character and fits the immediately preceding word `physiognomies`.

Thus the correction removes an ambiguity between:

`social rank/category`

and

`physical/type appearance`.

Mechanism:

`PHYSIOGNOMIC_SEMANTIC_DISAMBIGUATION_IN_REPRINT`.

Source control:

It may have originated as correction of a printing mistake rather than a newly formed ethnological opinion. The historical significance is therefore the corrected semantic distinction, not proof of a new 1874 theory.

---

## 4. Correction 2: `some` -> `about 100` on p.40

The correction list states:

`Page 40, line 4 from top, for "some" read "about 100".`

The relevant body-text sentence says that the Khas issued from Gorkha under Prithvi Narayan `some years ago` to acquire the dominion associated with the Gorkhali dynasty.

The correction converts this to:

`about 100 years ago`.

This changes a vague temporal expression into a retrospective chronological estimate.

Mechanism:

`RETROSPECTIVE_CHRONOLOGICAL_PRECISION`.

It does not alter the classificatory taxonomy; it recalibrates the historical narrative that surrounds it.

---

## 5. No listed corrections to the classificatory table on pp.42–44

The Part II corrections list proceeds from p.40 directly to later pages (p.46 and beyond) without listing changes to pp.41–44.

The military-tribe table occupies pp.42–44.

Therefore the explicit 1874 correction apparatus contains no listed change to:

- the twelve named Khas subdivisions;
- Ekthariya;
- Thakuri;
- Magar subdivisions;
- Gurung classification;
- the detailed family-name lists visible in the table.

Source-control formulation:

**No correction to the table is listed in the 1874 correction apparatus.**

Do not overstate this as proof that no silent typographical or editorial change occurred; a diplomatic 1833-versus-1874 comparison is still required.

Mechanism:

`CLASSIFICATORY_ARCHITECTURE_PERSISTS_IN_EXPLICIT_REVISION_APPARATUS`.

## 6. The 1874 correction apparatus is itself a provenance surface

The correction list keeps a record of the difference between:

`set/reprinted text`

and

`intended corrected reading`.

It therefore functions like the brackets and footnotes in Hodgson's Peking-route publication:

- it does not erase the earlier state;
- it declares a relation between two states;
- it allows a reader to reconstruct what is being changed.

Mechanism:

`CORRECTION_LIST_AS_VERSION_DIFFERENCE_SURFACE`.

This is a printed analogue of manuscript correction layers.

## 7. Revised production/version chain

The military-tribe paper can now be represented at multiple stages:

`1825 administrative military problem family`
-> `1831 rough classificatory source pool`
-> `October 1832 fair tabular manuscript`
-> `parallel fair copy`
-> `9 January 1833 society-reading state`
-> `May 1833 print`
-> `later printed-copy / manuscript annotation environment`
-> `1874 revised collected reprint + correction apparatus`.

Only some arrows are textual-copy arrows.

The 1825->1831 relation is currently `problem recoding`, not direct textual descent.

The 1832->1833 relation is a high-confidence publication-family relation, but printer-copy identity remains unproven.

The pre-1874 author-copy annotation layer is established at collection level by the 1874 `NOTICE`; the exact physical annotated copy used for this individual paper still requires recovery.

## 8. Paper-centred significance

This sequence adds another category to material versioning.

Earlier types:

1. support switch;
2. support continuity with state change;
3. branching support states;
4. manuscript -> print medium change.

Wave 47 adds:

5. `PRINT_REOPENED_AS_WRITABLE_VERSION_SURFACE`.

A printed paper can be:

- read;
- annotated by hand;
- corrected over decades;
- used to prepare another reprint;
- accompanied by a printed correction apparatus.

Thus print does not close the paper object's operational history.

---

## 9. Historiographical consequence

The 1833 paper should not be cited as a single timeless text if the argument depends on precise wording.

At minimum distinguish:

- 1833 journal state;
- 1874 collected/reprint state;
- explicit 1874 correction apparatus.

For the current paper article, the strongest result is not that Hodgson radically changed the classification by 1874. The opposite is more interesting:

**the classificatory architecture appears strikingly persistent, while specific language around physiognomy and chronology remains revisable.**

That asymmetry can be tested against the manuscript states.

## Source-control rules

1. Do not assume every entry in the 1874 correction list is necessarily a late intellectual change; some may correct typographical errors.
2. `caste -> cast` is semantically consequential but may be restoration of intended wording.
3. `some -> about 100` is a substantive increase in chronological precision.
4. Absence of listed table corrections is not proof of diplomatic identity between 1833 and 1874.
5. Recover and compare the 1833 and 1874 tables before making exact textual-stability claims.
6. The 1874 `NOTICE` establishes an annotated-author-copy workflow at collection level; identify the specific annotated precursor of the military-tribe paper before claiming its exact manuscript annotation history.

## Bottom line

The military-tribe paper remained versioned after its first publication. Hodgson's broader reprint workflow transformed annotated earlier printed copies into a new collected edition. The explicit 1874 correction apparatus leaves the article's classificatory table untouched while correcting `caste` to physiognomic `cast` and sharpening `some years ago` to `about 100 years ago`. Print therefore becomes another writable and revisable paper state rather than the terminal form of the knowledge object.
