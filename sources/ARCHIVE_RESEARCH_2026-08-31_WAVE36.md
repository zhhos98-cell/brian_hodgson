# Archive research wave 36 — from literary paper to scientific paper technologies: authorship, composition, form, reuse, and the sheet as operational unit

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: historiographical and archive-facing bridge. This wave does not import Georgina Wilson's literary claims wholesale into the history of science. It converts selected paper questions into falsifiable tests against the Hodgson archive and positions the project relative to Boris Jardine, Volker Hess & J. Andrew Mendelsohn, and Simon Werrett.

## Executive result

The paper-centred Hodgson project can now be located in a specific history-of-science conversation without reducing it to the generic language of `materiality`.

Three historiographical mechanisms are especially useful:

1. **paper can flicker between visible material and apparently transparent support** (Jardine);
2. **paper technologies generate knowledge through ordering, seriality, bookkeeping, administration and labour organization, not only through specialized scientific instruments** (Hess & Mendelsohn);
3. **reuse, repurposing and material shifts are productive scientific practices rather than merely signs of scarcity** (Werrett).

Georgina Wilson's `Authorship / Composition / Form / Reuse` architecture adds a neighboring book-history/literary-history vocabulary that can be converted into four archive questions:

- who materially makes the scientific document?
- how does paper permit heterogeneous elements to be composed into one working object?
- how do sheet/leaf/page/folio and physical dimensions create or delimit scientific objects?
- what happens when a paper surface is reused, corrected, recopied, rebound, dispersed or digitized?

Hodgson's archive adds a distinctive nineteenth-century imperial-science case in which **paper itself is simultaneously experimental material, local commodity, imported reporting medium, copy technology, visual field, accounting unit, package component, translation space and later archival classification criterion**.

## 1. Wilson as a question-generator, not a theory to be applied

Georgina Wilson's `Paper and the Making of Early Modern Literature` is structured around:

- Chapter 1: Authorship
- Chapter 2: Composition
- Chapter 3: Form
- Chapter 4: Reuse
- Coda: paper's later disciplinary/archival future.

Her introductory claim is that paper helps make categories such as authorship, composition, form and reuse rather than merely carrying already constituted texts.

### Hodgson translation

Do not write:

`Wilson shows paper is agentic, therefore Hodgson's paper is agentic.`

Instead ask:

`Which identifiable operation becomes possible, impossible, more expensive, more stable or differently classifiable because of the paper surface in question?`

This preserves an HPS standard of mechanism-level explanation.

## 2. Authorship -> documentary labour below the scientific inscription

Wilson's authorship chapter uses watermark/paper depth to shift attention away from the canonical writer and toward papermaker and ragpicker labour embedded in the physical sheet.

### Hodgson analogue

A Hodgson scientific/documentary object may involve:

- paper maker;
- paper trader/procurer;
- Hodgson as commissioner/editor;
- pandit or local specialist;
- copyist;
- writer/translator;
- artist;
- binder;
- carpenter;
- porter;
- institutional clerk/cataloguer;
- later conservator/binder.

### Mechanism

Code:

`PAPER_MEDIATED_DISTRIBUTED_AUTHORSHIP`

### Important limit

These actors should not all be called `authors` in a strong semantic sense.

The paper-history claim is narrower:

**the final scientific/documentary witness is materially co-produced by labour that can be recovered through support, hand, copy-state, binding, packaging and custody traces.**

### High-value Hodgson cases

- five Nepali paper makers transferred to Darjeeling in 1841;
- manuscript-copying bills including paper, binding, carpentry, labour and porters;
- artist/writer/copyist hands on zoological and linguistic paper surfaces;
- 1921 India Office binders physically redefining archival adjacency.

## 3. Jardine's `flickering materiality` fits Hodgson unusually well

Boris Jardine's `State of the field: Paper tools` argues that paper can alternate between:

- apparently transparent bearer of marks;
- conspicuous material to be weighed, traded, manipulated, examined or discarded.

### Hodgson sequence

#### Material comes into view

- Nepali paper is described and sampled in 1831;
- bark/fibre/pulp/paper-making labour becomes an experimental object in 1841–42;
- filtration, bleaching, waterproofing and machine-web behaviour are tested;
- large purchases of Nepali paper are counted and priced in 1843.

#### Material recedes behind inscription

- the same archive uses paper as route account, vocabulary, catalogue, drawing, legal questionnaire, receipt or translation;
- support can become almost invisible until copy-state, brittleness, format or rebinding creates a problem.

### Mechanism

Code:

`PAPER_MATERIALITY_VISIBILITY_SHIFT`

### Strong formulation

Hodgson's archive allows the historian to follow **the same class of material moving in and out of epistemic visibility** across manufacture, inscription, circulation and archival afterlife.

## 4. Hess & Mendelsohn: paper technology is organization, not only instrument

Hess and Mendelsohn's `Case and Series: Medical Knowledge and Paper Technology, 1600–1900` treats medical knowledge as shaped by histories of:

- printing and learned reading;
- bookkeeping;
- state administration;
- management and organization of labour;
- serial accumulation and comparison.

They explicitly distinguish this from a narrower history of specialized scientific `paper tools`.

### Hodgson fit

This is highly relevant because Hodgson's scientific paper infrastructure grows directly through:

- Residency administrative formats;
- private accounts;
- standardized questionnaires;
- lists of copies/leaves/verses;
- bundle/box/receipt counts;
- catalogues and distribution ledgers;
- multilingual translation/copying offices;
- institutional processing in London and Calcutta.

### Mechanism

Code:

`ADMINISTRATIVE_PAPER_TECHNOLOGY_AS_SCIENTIFIC_INFRASTRUCTURE`

### Distinctive Hodgson contribution

In Hodgson, the paper technology of knowledge production **crosses formal boundaries between Residency administration, private patronage, natural history, manuscript scholarship, route/geographical intelligence and learned societies**.

The same paper practices can migrate across these domains.

## 5. Werrett: reuse is an operation, not just a second life

Simon Werrett's work on `thrifty science` treats reuse, repurposing, improvisation, substitution and alteration as productive practices of experiment and material culture.

His 2023 paper specifically uses paper to challenge linear raw-material/supply-chain narratives and stresses used goods as epistemic resources.

### Hodgson cases

- verso of 1843 paper bill reused for army battalion notes;
- copied palm-leaf documents converted into paper surrogates;
- printed papers receiving Hodgson corrections;
- existing drawings and notes recomposed into later research packages;
- blank zones reserved for translation;
- older documentary supports copied into new paper forms;
- later archive binding reuses older paper objects within a new institutional arrangement.

### Mechanism

Code:

`PAPER_REUSE_AS_EPISTEMIC_REALLOCATION`

### Distinction

Not every second inscription proves economic thrift.

The safe claim is operational:

`existing paper surface/object -> new allocation -> new informational or evidential function`.

Economic motivation must be shown separately.

## 6. Composition -> paper as an assembly surface

Wilson's composition chapter argues that paper permits material/semiotic elements to be brought together into partially coherent wholes; the page itself can compose image and text.

### Hodgson cases

#### Buddhist symbol sheets

Two sheets collectively contain 143 Buddhist symbols assembled from:

- printed coin sources;
- temple imagery;
- statues;
- pictures/drawings.

The paper surface converts dispersed visual objects into a comparable diagnostic array.

#### Zoological drawing sheets

A sheet may combine:

- image;
- number;
- scientific/local names;
- anatomical detail;
- measurement;
- locality;
- notes.

#### Mixed-paper dossiers

Different hands/languages/copy states are bound or grouped into one working documentary object.

#### Translation-ready Lepcha pages

Current text and anticipated translation are spatially composed on one page.

### Mechanism

Code:

`PAPER_AS_HETEROGENEOUS_COMPOSITION_SURFACE`

### Scientific importance

Composition is not merely rhetorical arrangement.

A surface can make **comparison** possible by bringing otherwise separated signs into one inspectable field.

## 7. Form -> sheet, leaf, page and folio are epistemic boundaries

- sheet;
- folio;
- loose leaf;
- pothi book;
- pre-bound notebook;
- broad-sheet;
- bundle;
- box;
- portfolio;

`PAPER_FORMAT_AS_EPISTEMIC_BOUNDARY`

## 8. The sheet is a historical operational unit, not a neutral page count

This is a major cross-source finding.

### Wilson / Stationers

Wilson notes that the sheet was an economic and logistical unit in Stationers' Company practice, including taxation/licensing and partnership accounting.

### Hodgson / Nepali paper procurement

The 1843 Wallich bill counts:

`1,012 bundles x 10 sheets per bundle`.

Here the sheet is a purchased/material quantity nested inside the bundle.

### Hodgson / zoological drawings

A later table records:

- 1,241 bird drawing sheets;
- 557 mammal drawing sheets;
- 55 reptile etc. sheets;
- total 1,863 sheets.

The source explicitly warns:

`many sheets contain several subjects`.

### Mechanism

Code:

`SHEET_AS_OPERATIONAL_COUNT_UNIT`

### Crucial ontology

`sheet count != represented subject count`

`sheet count != species count`

`sheet count != drawing-event count`

`bundle count != sheet count`

The sheet is a material unit whose relation to semantic objects depends on the operation being performed.

This should become one of the article's strongest paper-specific quantitative arguments.

---

## 9. Sheet-count and object-count divergence is an epistemic problem

The Hodgson drawing table's footnote is unusually explicit: multiple represented subjects can occupy one folio sheet.

### Mechanism

Code:

`MATERIAL_SEMANTIC_CARDINALITY_DIVERGENCE`

### Cross-domain parallels

- one manuscript physical volume may contain several textual works;
- one bundle contains multiple manuscripts;
- one sheet can contain multiple visual subjects;
- one box can contain multiple packages/leaves/copies.

### Paper-centred formulation

The historical problem is not simply `counting error`.

It is that **paper/container units and intellectual/object units belong to different ontologies**.

The archive becomes unstable when institutions silently substitute one for another.

## 10. Form and composition meet in the Buddhist symbol sheets

In 1860 Hodgson transmitted **two sheets collectively containing 143 symbols**.

The first 33 came from coin images already published in JRAS; the remainder came from Saugata temples/images/drawings in Nepal.

### Mechanism

Codes:

- `VISUAL_EXTRACTION_TO_SHEET_ARRAY`
- `COMPARISON_BY_PLANAR_AGGREGATION`

### Scientific operation

`distributed material/visual sources -> select diagnostic feature -> redraw/extract -> place on finite sheet -> compare as series`.

The sheet is therefore both:

- a physical boundary;
- a device for creating a new scientific visual object.

This is exactly the kind of case where `paper tool` should be used literally, not metaphorically.

## 11. Blankness is not one category

The BL inventory records many blank folios/pages.

These need at least three distinct states:

### A. designed blank reserve

Example: Lepcha copies with right-side blank space prepared for future translation.

Code:

`INTENTIONAL_FUTURE_OPERATION_SPACE`.

### B. terminal/interstitial blank leaf

Examples: blank final folios in copied/translated manuscript units.

Code:

`STRUCTURAL_BLANK_LEAF` unless evidence of intended later use exists.

### C. extended blank run inside a working dossier

Example: vol.14 net-revenue item includes ff.115–120 blank amid a larger manuscript range.

Code:

`UNINTERPRETED_INTERNAL_BLANK_RUN` pending image/codicological inspection.

### Rule

Do not romanticize every blank as `potentiality`.

Blank paper becomes historically significant when layout, subsequent inscription, ruling, foliation, binding position or explicit description shows what operation the space was expected to support.

## 12. Correction/addenda create stratified paper time

BL inventory descriptions repeatedly record:

- rough and fair hands;
- ink and pencil;
- corrections and addenda;
- additional pages;
- printed copies with handwritten additions.

Examples:

- 1834 Governor-General letter on thick/thin imported paper;
- 1828 Buddhist essay on two types of machine-made paper, later republished repeatedly;
- commerce report drafts on multiple machine paper types;
- printed Ho vocabulary with Hodgson correction/addenda.

### Mechanism

Code:

`STRATIFIED_INSCRIPTION_TIME_ON_PAPER`

A paper object can preserve multiple temporal states simultaneously.

The later mark does not erase the earlier state; it changes the witness into a layered object.

### Link to `INTERMEDIATE_STATE_FIXATION`

Paper's ability to retain abandoned hints, provisional corrections and later additions is one reason working states survive into archives or print.

## 13. Reuse -> archive is an active paper technology

Wilson's coda emphasizes that keeping, curating, dispersing and digitizing paper shape its later intellectual and physical afterlives.

### Hodgson 1921 case

India Office Library binding partly by document dimensions can separate:

`original` from `translation/copy`

because their paper sizes differ.

### Mechanisms

- `ARCHIVE_AS_PAPER_TECHNOLOGY`
- `SIZE_BASED_RELATIONAL_BREAK`
- `ARCHIVAL_RECOMPOSITION`

### Strong distinction

The archive does not merely preserve a previous paper order.

It performs a new composition:

`historical loose/mixed relations -> dimension-based binding -> new adjacency -> new researcher-visible object`.

This is directly comparable to scientific paper practices earlier in the chain, though the institutional goal is preservation/management rather than primary research.

## 14. Digitization adds another reuse state

The present project often encounters Hodgson paper through:

- catalogue description;
- digital page image;
- OCR/VLM transcription;
- cropped image blocks;
- flattened PDF scan;
- repository extraction.

### Mechanism

Code:

`DIGITAL_REUSE_AS_EPISTEMIC_FILTER`

### Examples of what digitization can obscure

- watermark visibility;
- chain lines;
- paper thickness;
- recto/verso relation;
- fold sequence;
- sheet conjugacy;
- scale;
- binding structure.

### Examples of what it can amplify

- searchable repeated words;
- cross-file comparison;
- count-state detection;
- handwriting/layout triage;
- dispersed provenance joins.

The digital surrogate is therefore not simply less material. It redistributes observability.

## 15. Whatman watermark work belongs under authorship/provenance, but requires sheet reconstruction

Balston's Whatman studies give an important methodological warning:

- letters are often on **half-sheets**, leaving only watermark or countermark visible;
- whole sheets permit watermark/countermark relation to be reconstructed;
- `J WHATMAN` marks were imitated/forged;
- watermark text alone is insufficient for maker identification.

### Mechanism

Code:

`WATERMARK_REQUIRES_SHEET_LEVEL_RECONSTRUCTION`

### Hodgson protocol

For each suspected Whatman sheet record:

- watermark;
- countermark;
- which half survives;
- chain/wire lines;
- full or half sheet;
- folds/cuts;
- document orientation;
- dimensions;
- document date;
- candidate mill/maker;
- confidence.

This is not decorative bibliographical detail. It can recover the material production history lying beneath scientific inscription.

## 16. Hodgson extends the paper-tools literature in four directions

### A. Cross-paper-regime science

The same knowledge system uses:

- Nepali hand-made paper;
- imported/machine-made paper;
- treated paper;
- palm leaf originals;
- printed paper;
- mixed-paper dossiers.

This permits direct comparison of **support conversion**, rather than analysis of one paper tradition alone.

### B. Paper as experimental object and paper tool in the same archive

Nepali paper is itself tested for manufacture, bleaching, filtration and machine compatibility, while paper simultaneously serves as the tool for reporting those tests.

### C. Administrative/scientific paper overlap

Residency accounts, bills, routes, questionnaires, catalogues and scientific drawings share paper practices and personnel.

### D. Colonial/archive afterlife

A nineteenth-century knowledge system is later rebound by the India Office according partly to sheet dimensions, creating a second institutional paper technology that changes object relations.

## 17. Revised paper-centred article thesis

A stronger provisional claim is now possible:

**Hodgson's Himalayan knowledge was made through changing paper operations rather than on a neutral paper substrate. Nepali paper could become an experimental material and quantified commodity while machine-made sheets reported and priced it; old palm-leaf records became portable paper surrogates; blank zones reserved translation; broad sheets enabled visual comparison; one folio sheet could carry multiple zoological subjects; corrections accumulated as stratified working time; and later archivists used sheet dimensions to rebind and separate documents that had once belonged together. Paper therefore made scientific objects by setting units, surfaces, relations and limits, while each change of paper regime or archival interface created new possibilities for comparison, reuse and error.**

This formulation keeps natural history, Buddhism, geography and administration as cases of paper operation rather than separate disciplinary narratives.

## 19. Immediate next tests

1. Build a **sheet ontology table** across procurement, manuscripts, drawings and archive binding: sheet / leaf / page / folio / subject / work / bundle / box.
2. Transcribe/inspect the two 1860 Buddhist symbol sheets and test whether arrangement on the finite sheet encodes taxonomic or source grouping beyond the textual description.
3. Identify examples where blank paper was later filled and distinguish them from terminal/structural blanks.
4. Compare ink vs pencil additions on the same paper surface to reconstruct inscription chronology.
5. Reconstruct full-sheet geometry for Linnean `SP 514` and `CR 48`, including folds and watermark/countermark placement.
6. Pair every Whatman identification with sheet-geometry evidence rather than watermark text alone.
7. Use duplicate route witnesses to test whether copy-state change coincides with support change.
8. Treat 1921 binding as a measurable reordering event: identify original/translation pairs now separated solely by dimensions.
9. Quantify paper-support categories across the BL inventory where catalogue descriptions permit it, but report catalogue visibility bias.
10. Compare Hodgson with Hess/Mendelsohn only at the level of paper operation: serialization, forms, copying, administrative crossover and institutional management.

## Bottom line

The project now has a clear paper-history-of-science spine. Wilson helps formulate how paper can constitute authorship, composition, form and reuse; Jardine supplies a vocabulary for paper moving in and out of material visibility; Hess and Mendelsohn show how administrative and organizational paper technologies can make scientific knowledge; Werrett makes reuse and repurposing historically productive. Hodgson gives a corpus in which all four problems can be observed across multiple paper regimes, languages, sciences and institutional afterlives. The distinctive contribution will come from reconstructing the operations, not from repeating the general proposition that paper matters.
