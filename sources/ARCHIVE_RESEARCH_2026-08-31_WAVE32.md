# Archive research wave 32 — Hodgson's local research office: task standardization, field delegation, actor-role permeability, and archive circulation control

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: reconstruction primarily from the Cambridge/SOAS project inventory of Brian Houghton Hodgson's British Library private papers. The inventory is a modern archival description of primary manuscripts, not a substitute for page-level transcription. Actor and operation claims are therefore coded by confidence level and should be verified against the BL folios before quotation-level use.

## Executive result

The archive no longer supports a useful model of `Hodgson + informants`.

A better working model is a **local research office** composed of differentiated but permeable roles:

- principal research assistant;
- regional field assistant;
- trader-research assistant;
- painter / Chitrakar;
- writer / translator;
- shikaree / field observer;
- named local source or specialist;
- household/logistical worker;
- Hodgson as commissioner, question-setter, classifier, editor and archive manager.

The evidence further suggests organization-level operations:

`standardized questionnaire -> delegated regional collection -> multilingual transcription/translation -> title/colophon production -> comparison/checking -> archive registration -> later publication/use`.

This does not mean Hodgson ran a modern laboratory or bureaucratic institute. `Research office` is an analytic shorthand for a recurrent, paid, task-differentiated documentary organization whose operations can be observed in the archive.

## 1. The archival project introduction explicitly describes a paid local research staff

The Cambridge Hodgson Papers project introduction states that Hodgson retained **at his own expense a group of local research assistants** and trained himself and some of his staff as naturalists, especially in ornithology.

### Source-control status

This is a modern biographical synthesis by the archival project, not itself a Hodgson payroll document.

### Mechanism

Code:

`PAID_LOCAL_RESEARCH_STAFF`

### Falsification target

The strong form should be grounded in surviving accounts, salary/payment sheets and named worker lists wherever possible.

## 2. Standardized questionnaire demonstrates task formalization

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/58, ff.28–32`

Description:

A **standard questionnaire and list of key points for investigation**, prepared by Hodgson and his research assistants, covering district-level inquiry across:

- geographical introduction;
- settlements;
- population;
- castes and communities;
- property / land / houses;
- history and historical sites;
- arts and artisans;
- routes, roads and trails;
- trade and commerce;
- markets;
- hunting and equipment;
- land and agriculture;
- weaving;
- justice/courts;
- land revenue;
- flora and fauna.

The surviving manuscript is in a mixture of local dialects similar to Hindi-Urdu and Maithili, written in Kaithi script rather than Nagari.

### Mechanisms

- `STANDARDIZED_FIELD_QUESTIONNAIRE`
- `TASK_FORMALIZATION`
- `MULTILINGUAL_RESEARCH_INTERFACE`

### High-value implication

The archive contains an explicit conversion device between Hodgson's research programme and delegated field inquiry.

`research question universe -> standardized prompts -> assistant/local response -> documentary return`.

This is stronger than reconstructing repeated topics after the fact.

## 3. Jatanlal is a regional field research assistant, not a generic correspondent

### BL inventory witnesses

`MSS EUR HODGSON/3, f.134`:

- Jatanlal's March 1842 report on Morang District;
- people, religion/culture, territory, products, revenue and tax;
- inventory identifies Jatanlal as Hodgson's **Research Assistant working and reporting from the Morang and Saptari-Mahottari areas of Nepal's Tarai**.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/104, ff.25–26`:

- another very neat Urdu/Persian-state report of Morang by Jatanlal;
- describes peoples including Rajbamsi and Koch, religious belief, customs, settlement, food/drink, dress, territory and topography.

Other Tarai revenue records are likewise attributed to Jatanlal.

### Mechanisms

- `REGIONAL_FIELD_DELEGATION`
- `ASSISTANT_REPORTING_NODE`
- `FIELD_TO_RESIDENCY_DOCUMENT_FLOW`

### Operational relation

`research centre / Residency -> Jatanlal in Tarai -> regional observation/data gathering -> report/revenue record -> Hodgson archive/publication`.

The geographic distance is part of the research design.

## 4. Ashraf Ali converts mercantile mobility into research infrastructure

### BL inventory witnesses

`MSS EUR HODGSON/9, ff.65–71`:

- questions and answers about Bhote/Tibetans;
- Ashraf Ali identified as a **Kashmiri trader and one of Hodgson's research assistants**;
- topics include language, geography, governance, culture, society and economy.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/94`:

- popular account of Tibet collected/written by Ashraf Ali;
- route Kathmandu–Lhasa by Ashraf Ali;
- related route/topography work also uses other Kashmiri traders.

### Mechanisms

- `TRADER_AS_RESEARCH_ASSISTANT`
- `MOBILITY_TO_INFORMATION_CONVERSION`
- `QUESTION_ANSWER_RESEARCH_PROTOCOL`

### Important point

Commercial identity and scholarly field role are not mutually exclusive.

The same mobility/access that makes a trader useful economically can make him a high-value geographical/linguistic research node.

## 5. Khardar Jitmohan is a principal assistant with cross-domain production authority

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/79` describes **Khardar Jitmohan** as a learned official of the British Residency and Hodgson's **principal research assistant**.

He jointly compiles a Lepcha `La-zong` / language-practice work in Darjeeling with:

- Minister/Karthak Samprit of Takbar;
- Lepcha Bonthing Cho-sam Lapcha of Temarbau.

The work contains:

- Lepcha alphabet and writing practice;
- language textbook material;
- Nepali translation;
- traditional letter-writing forms;
- ritual/auspicious-time text;
- title descriptions and colophon-like records of writers, place, date and Hodgson's sponsorship.

### Other Jitmohan operations

The inventory also attributes to Jitmohan:

- a Sanskrit-Newari vocabulary of c.2,800 words (`MSS EUR HODGSON/33`);
- Gorkha genealogical history in a very neat hand (`vol.51`);
- sketch maps (`vol.7`);
- Limbu religious/cultural text organization/title (`vol.85`);
- Lepcha Munthum compilation with Hamsabaj Subba-Limbu (`vol.60`);
- multiple ethnographic/topographic collections (`vol.56`).

### Mechanisms

- `PRINCIPAL_RESEARCH_ASSISTANT`
- `CROSS_DOMAIN_DOCUMENT_PRODUCTION`
- `COMPILATION_TRANSLATION_TITLE_AUTHORITY`
- `LOCAL_EDITORIAL_LABOUR`

### High-value implication

Jitmohan cannot be modeled as a passive source.

His operations include designing/compiling linguistic objects, producing maps, organizing texts, translating, titling and documenting provenance.

This is close to editorial/research authorship at the documentary level.

## 6. Rajman Chitrakar crosses the boundary between representation and specimen routing

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/58, f.59b`:

A Nepali copy of Hodgson's letter concerning bird collecting records:

- shipment of a new species of bird to Hodgson in Darjeeling;
- **Rajman Chitrakar / Rajmansing's role in selecting and recommending birds for shipment and drawing them**;
- Hodgson's suggestion that all living and dead birds coming from Tibet be sent to Darjeeling.

### Mechanisms

- `ARTIST_AS_SPECIMEN_SELECTION_GATEKEEPER`
- `ARTIST_AS_LOGISTICS_MEDIATOR`
- `BIRD_SELECTION_DRAWING_COUPLING`
- `ROLE_PERMEABILITY`

### Major correction to a thin artist model

Rajman's role is not confined to making representations of specimens already selected by Hodgson.

At least in this catalogue-described case, he helps decide **which birds should move** into the Darjeeling research system and then draws them.

`selection -> recommendation -> shipment -> drawing` can be one actor-linked chain.

### Caution

This still does not prove that Rajman is the unnamed `native painter` responsible for the 1870 Hindi list. That join remains open.

## 7. Chitrakar also appears as an ethnographic mediation category

### BL inventory witnesses

`MSS EUR HODGSON/4`:

The Newar customs manuscript begins with `mārphat Citrakāra`, literally `through a Chitrakar`. The inventory notes Hodgson's official painter Rajman Singh as a plausible contextual identity but does not prove the specific intermediary is Rajman.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/60, ff.197–217`:

Newar marriage/death material is described as information collected by `chitrakār (Rajman Sing?)`.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/59, ff.73–78`:

Newar land-tenure/inheritance information is described as collected and written by a `citrakār`, presumed by the inventory to be Rajman Singh.

### Mechanism

Code:

`CHITRAKAR_AS_CROSS_DOMAIN_MEDIATION_ROLE`

### Source-control rule

Use two levels:

- secure: `Chitrakar` operates as knowledge mediator/collector beyond visual drawing;
- provisional: specific Chitrakar = Rajman Singh where the manuscript itself does not name him.

## 8. Shankarlal is a named collecting actor in the early ethnographic archive

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/59, f.1`:

A list of major Newar festivals is described as **information collected by Shankarlal in 1830**, with English title description by Hodgson.

### Mechanism

Code:

`NAMED_LOCAL_COLLECTOR`

The document separates:

`information collection -> English documentary framing`.

This is the same general division seen elsewhere between local data production and Hodgson's title/editorial layer.

## 9. Research assistants can themselves coordinate other local experts

Jitmohan's Lepcha work is jointly produced with a minister and a Bonthing; his Limbu work involves named Limbu collaborators; other documents preserve named ritual specialists and narrators.

### Mechanism

Code:

`ASSISTANT_AS_SUBNETWORK_COORDINATOR`

The research structure need not be radial:

`Hodgson -> every local source`.

It can be nested:

`Hodgson -> principal assistant -> specialist / ritual expert / regional source -> compiled/translated document`.

This is an important correction to ego-network historiography.

---

## 10. Hodgson's staff perform verification, not only collection

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/59, ff.9–10`:

Population/household figures for Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur are described as **checked and compared by Hodgson's staff** against population sizes around the period before/after the Gorkha conquest.

### Mechanism

Code:

`ASSISTANT_VERIFICATION_LABOUR`

The staff role therefore includes:

- collecting;
- checking;
- comparing historical states;
- producing corrected/validated figures.

This parallels the later museum reconciliation work in London.

## 11. The archive contains a private circulation-control register

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/59, f.54b`, dated 1855:

A rough note records:

- issuance of manuscripts from Hodgson's own Darjeeling archive;
- contents of documents borrowed by Hodgson and his wife on different dates;
- the inventory characterizes this as evidence of a **strict record-keeping system** for his collection.

### Mechanisms

- `PRIVATE_ARCHIVE_ISSUANCE_LEDGER`
- `INTERNAL_CUSTODY_CONTROL`
- `HOUSEHOLD_ARCHIVE_CIRCULATION`

### High-value implication

Custody checksum is not only an inter-institutional phenomenon.

Hodgson's own working archive appears to include internal issue/borrowing control.

`archive item -> borrower -> date -> return/circulation state`.

This is a private analogue of institutional circulation registers.

## 12. Household management and research labour overlap materially

### BL inventory witness

`MSS EUR HODGSON/58, ff.19–22`, 1857:

Account of Hodgson's kitchen and other expenses incurred by **Lachuman / Laxman**, including names of Hodgson's regular paid workers (`kullis`).

### Mechanism

Code:

`HOUSEHOLD_RESEARCH_LABOUR_ACCOUNT_SURFACE`

### Caution

The inventory does not establish that every named paid worker was a research assistant.

The significance is narrower:

- recurring paid labour existed;
- household and working-expense documentation can preserve otherwise invisible staff names;
- the original folios are a priority source for reconstructing labour infrastructure and cost.

## 13. Research organization produces multilingual documentary layers

Across the named assistant corpus, one operation may move through:

- oral statement;
- local script/language;
- Nagari transliteration;
- Nepali/Hindi/Persian translation;
- English title description or summary;
- later print.

Examples include:

- Ashraf Ali's Tibet materials;
- Jitmohan's Lepcha/Limbu productions;
- Tarai bird local names with English notes;
- Persian translations of Nepali legal/topographic material;
- multilingual animal-name lists.

### Mechanism

Code:

`MULTI_LAYER_TRANSLATION_PIPELINE`

Translation is distributed across actors and stages, not a final operation performed once by Hodgson.

## 14. The assistant network is cross-domain rather than discipline-specific

The same office/staff structure produces material now classified as:

- zoology;
- ethnology;
- linguistics;
- geography/topography;
- revenue/economy;
- law;
- religion/Buddhism;
- route knowledge;
- history/genealogy.

### Mechanism

Code:

`CROSS_DOMAIN_RESEARCH_OFFICE`

### Historiographical implication

Modern disciplinary boundaries cut across a historical organization that reused personnel, questionnaires, translation practices, lists and archive controls across subjects.

This gives an empirical reason not to begin the article from modern disciplinary compartments.

## 15. A provisional organizational model

### Commission / question-setting layer

Brian Houghton Hodgson:

- chooses problems;
- drafts/adapts questionnaires;
- commissions work;
- supervises selected processes;
- writes titles/English framing;
- compares/corrects/classifies;
- manages archive and circulation;
- routes results to institutions/print.

### Principal assistant/editorial layer

Khardar Jitmohan:

- compilation;
- translation;
- maps;
- dictionary;
- textual organization;
- provenance/title/colophon work;
- coordination with specialists.

### Regional/mobile assistant layer

- Jatanlal: Tarai regional reporting;
- Ashraf Ali: Tibet via trader mobility;
- other Kashmiri traders / route informants.

### Skilled visual/selection layer

- Rajman Singh / Chitrakar;
- Tursmoney Chittakar;
- other unnamed Nepali artists;
- artist may also select/recommend specimens or mediate ethnographic data.

### Field-observation layer

- shikarees;
- named narrators;
- ritual specialists;
- local political/administrative actors.

### Translation/scribal layer

- Hodgson's writer;
- Munshi and translators;
- multilingual copyists.

### Material/logistical layer

- kullis;
- carriers;
- preparers/stuffers;
- packers;
- household/collection managers.

This model remains provisional and should be reconstructed from payments and individual documents rather than imposed as a fixed hierarchy.

## 16. `Research office` is testable through management traces

Evidence expected if the model is correct:

1. repeated salaries/payments to named staff;
2. task-specific letters/instructions;
3. standardized questionnaires;
4. regional reporting patterns;
5. multiple hands/translation stages;
6. borrowing/issuance records;
7. archival grouping by task or assistant;
8. cross-domain recurrence of same staff;
9. error/correction layers attributable to different actors;
10. evidence of supervision or assistant-to-assistant coordination.

The current archive already satisfies several items.

## 17. Relation to Wave 29–31 natural-history argument

Wave 29:

`shikaree -> writer -> numbered behavioural memorandum -> drawing/specimen graph`.

Wave 30:

`artist + Hodgson + museum staff -> parallel production and independent error channels`.

Wave 31:

`museum sorting/naming/distribution -> asynchronous institutional processing`.

Wave 32 adds the upstream organizational layer:

`research office -> differentiated task assignment -> local/regional evidence production -> relational documents -> later museum/institutional processing`.

The British Museum did not receive the work of one isolated naturalist. It received objects already shaped by a multi-actor research organization.

## 18. Cross-domain comparison with later institutional processing

There is a striking structural symmetry worth testing carefully.

### Kathmandu/Darjeeling research office

- questionnaire;
- assistants;
- collecting;
- translation;
- checking;
- list-making;
- internal archive circulation.

### British Museum processing office

- unpacking;
- sorting;
- naming;
- copying;
- set construction;
- list updating;
- catalogue production;
- distribution.

### General mechanism

`SEQUENTIAL_ORGANIZATIONAL_TRANSLATION`

Knowledge passes from one organized labour regime into another.

Each regime can add new fields, remove relations, correct earlier states or introduce new errors.

This offers a stronger institutional history than a simple `local knowledge -> metropolitan science` transfer model.

## 20. Immediate falsification targets

1. Inspect `MSS EUR HODGSON/58 f.59b` directly and transcribe the Rajman bird-selection/shipment letter.
2. Inspect `vol.58 ff.28–32` questionnaire and reconstruct exact question fields, languages, handwriting layers and evidence of co-production with assistants.
3. Inspect `vol.59 f.54b` issuance ledger and extract document IDs, borrowers, dates and return states.
4. Transcribe `vol.58 ff.19–22` expenses to recover names/payments of regular workers and distinguish household labour from research labour.
5. Build a prosopography of named assistants: Jitmohan, Jatanlal, Ashraf Ali, Shankarlal and others.
6. Track each assistant across all BL volumes and publications rather than treating each surviving manuscript as an isolated contribution.
7. Determine whether Jitmohan's role includes explicit payment/salary records and whether `Khardar` is an office held within Residency administration, research employment, or both in each context.
8. Test whether Rajman's handwriting can be linked to the 1870 native painter Hindi bird list.
9. Distinguish manuscripts written by assistants from manuscripts merely titled/described by Hodgson.
10. Compare assistant-generated local/Nepali/Persian states with surviving English summaries/publications to measure transformation depth.
11. Identify named shikarees or writer in HOD/1/3 covers/marginalia or BL parallel documents.
12. Reconstruct local research-office -> British Museum processing-office handoff as two sequential organizational regimes.

## Bottom line

The Hodgson archive is increasingly legible as the residue of an organized local research operation rather than the notebook-world of a solitary polymath. Standardized questionnaires formalized tasks; regional assistants returned reports; trader-assistants converted mobility into Tibetan information; Jitmohan compiled, translated, mapped, titled and coordinated specialists; Rajman selected and recommended bird specimens as well as drawing them; staff checked demographic figures; and Hodgson's Darjeeling archive maintained internal issuance records. These practices generated versioned documentary objects before those objects entered British museums and learned societies. The crucial historical movement is therefore not simply from `local informants` to `European science`, but from one differentiated organizational system of knowledge production into another.
