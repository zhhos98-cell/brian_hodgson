# Web research 2026-08-31 — Wave 31
## Conversion branching, support continuity and catalogue-relation control

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred expansion of Wave 30. This wave widens the explicit source→derivative corpus and, crucially, adds cases where conversion does **not** change support or where one source branches into derivatives on different supports.

## Executive result

Wave 30 established that some explicit source→translation/summary/abstract chains coincide with a change of support. The expanded corpus now shows three distinct relation types:

1. **support switch** — source and derivative occupy different support classes;
2. **support continuity** — source and derivative remain on the same support class despite linguistic/documentary conversion;
3. **support branching** — one source is linked to multiple derivative states using different supports.

The third type is especially important. The Kathmandu→Beijing route survives as a Nepali hand-made-paper source, an English summary on imported thick paper, and an incomplete Urdu/Farsi translation on Nepali hand-made paper. Translation therefore does not mechanically produce a support switch. Support choice belongs to a larger conversion package involving target document state, intended use, binding/format, actor and available stock.

Mechanisms:

- `SUPPORT_SWITCH_AT_KNOWLEDGE_CONVERSION`
- `SUPPORT_CONTINUITY_THROUGH_KNOWLEDGE_CONVERSION`
- `BRANCHED_DERIVATIVE_SUPPORT_STATES`
- `CONVERSION_PATHWAY_NOT_LANGUAGE_DETERMINES_SUPPORT`
- `CATALOGUE_RELATION_REQUIRES_FOLIO_CONTROL`

## 12. Revised article claim

Wave 30 formulation:

`epistemic transformation can include support transformation`.

Wave 31 makes it more exact:

**knowledge conversion generates a new documentary state whose support may switch, remain continuous, or branch across multiple derivative states.**

Therefore the variable to explain is not `translation -> machine paper`.

It is:

`target support = source support × available stock × actor/workplace × derivative format × intended operation × custody/publication pathway`.

The paper article should treat support choice as part of the architecture of **document versioning**.

## 13. Why the route branching case matters conceptually

The Kathmandu→Beijing chain gives a quasi-controlled comparison because the source relation is shared.

One source leads to:

- a fair English state on imported thick paper;
- an incomplete Urdu/Farsi state on Nepali handmade paper.

This means support conversion cannot be explained solely through:

- source subject;
- source language;
- translation as such;
- or a simple local/European opposition.

The next variables to test are:

- translator/writer identity;
- destination archive or recipient;
- whether the derivative was intended as working translation, fair copy or publication-ready summary;
- binding/notebook incorporation;
- paper availability at the moment of copying;
- whether imported paper was reserved for particular fair/reporting states.

Mechanism:

`DERIVATIVE_STATE_SELECTS_SUPPORT_WITHIN_SHARED_INFORMATION_LINEAGE`.

## 14. Catalogue source-control problem

The Cambridge online inventory is extremely valuable but its cross-reference syntax must not be treated as perfectly stable object identity.

At least one shop-list relation gives a vol.14 item number/folio combination that does not align with the current vol.14 item boundaries, while the folio range points into the commerce report material.

Rule for the conversion dataset:

`folio range + title/content relation > item number alone` when the current inventory is internally inconsistent.

Store separately:

- source catalogue ref as printed;
- current resolved item;
- folio range;
- resolution status.

Mechanism:

`CATALOGUE_RELATION_REQUIRES_FOLIO_CONTROL`.

## Bottom line

The new evidence changes the question. Hodgson's archive does not show a one-way movement from local handmade paper into colonial machine paper. It shows **versioned material branching**. Some source records are remade as machine-paper English notebooks, tables or reports; some English and Persian/Urdu derivatives remain on Nepali handmade paper; one route lineage produces both outcomes. Paper therefore belongs to the version history of knowledge objects. The historically productive question is not whether translation changed the paper, but **which derivative state was being made, for whom, with what material stock, and for what next operation**.
