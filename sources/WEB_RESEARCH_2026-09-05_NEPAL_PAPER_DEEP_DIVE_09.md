# Web research — 2026-09-05 — Nepal paper deep dive, workforce geography

This file continues the September 5 deep-dive logs. It records a source-control correction to the geography and composition of Campbell's five-person papermaking party. The point is actor reconstruction, not ethnic typology.

## Finding 35 — Campbell's `Ham` can be historically localized to the Ilam administrative region

Evidence status: **STRONG historical-geographical cross-control; Campbell's 1842 printed wording remains the direct source for the workforce description.**

Campbell's published 1841 report distinguishes the five papermakers as:

- a head maker and mate/first assistant, both Newars of the Nepal Valley;
- three ordinary workers described in the period's language as `low caste Purbutteahs` from the neighbourhood of `Ham`.

Historical administrative geography identifies **Tehsil Ham** as an eastern-Nepal tehsil whose chief officer had headquarters at **Ilam**. This allows `Ham` in Campbell's report to be localized to the Ilam region rather than left as an unidentified or generic hill locality.

Public historical-geography witness:
- historical administrative description: `Tehsil Ham ... head-quarters ... at Ilam`.

Source-control rule:

Use `Ham/Ilam region` as geographic normalization only. Preserve Campbell's own social vocabulary as source language when historically necessary, and do not map nineteenth-century categories mechanically onto modern caste/ethnic identities.

## Finding 36 — the 1841 maker party was internally differentiated by rank, pay and geography

The party should no longer be narrated simply as `five Nepali papermakers`.

Current secure structure:

- **head maker** — Newar, Nepal/Kathmandu Valley; Rs 10/month;
- **mate / first assistant** — Newar, Nepal/Kathmandu Valley; Rs 8/month;
- **three ordinary workers** — from the Ham/Ilam region; Rs 8/month each.

This does not by itself establish task division beyond the hierarchy named by Campbell, nor does it explain why this particular combination of men was selected by the Nepal Durbar.

But it does establish that the transferred technical group was not socially or geographically homogeneous.

### Consequence for Case 2

The article should ask at a low historical level:

- what did `head maker` and `mate` actually do that distinguished them from the other three?
- did Valley Newar leadership reflect workshop mastery, administrative convenience, market mediation, or something else?
- why were three additional workers drawn from the Ham/Ilam region?
- did the Durbar assemble a pre-existing team or compose one for the Darjeeling request?

All of these remain OPEN until selection/despatch/pay records are recovered.

## Finding 37 — do not collapse Hodgson's 1831 manufacturing geography into the 1841 recruited workforce

Hodgson's 1831/32 article says the paper called `Nipalese` was principally manufactured not in Nepal Proper but among Cis-Himalayan Bhote communities east of the Kali, with Nepal/Kathmandu functioning as a major market.

Campbell's 1841 transferred workforce, by contrast, is described as a combination of Valley Newars and workers from Ham/Ilam.

These two descriptions do **not** map neatly onto one another.

This mismatch is historically productive but currently explanatory-neutral.

Do not infer without further evidence that:

- papermaking skill had simply `diffused` from Bhote communities to Newars/Ilam workers;
- the five men came from the same workshops Hodgson had described;
- the Valley Newars were merchants/intermediaries rather than makers;
- the Ilam workers were recruited from a known paper village;
- caste/ethnic identity explains technical rank.

### Revised archive question

The pre-dispatch 1841 selection record matters not merely because it might recover names. It could reveal **how the Nepal Durbar defined a competent papermaking team** and from which existing labour/workshop relations it assembled that team.

That is a more historically precise question than generic `local agency` or `embodied knowledge transfer`.
