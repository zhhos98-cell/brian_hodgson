# Web research 2026-08-31 — Wave 22
## Hodgson mandala drawings as explicit cross-media copy chains

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred source-control note. This wave extends the copy-chain analysis from manuscript repair and manuscript-to-manuscript copying to visual surrogation. The central evidence consists of Hodgson-associated Nepalese mandala drawings whose inscriptions explicitly name source objects such as a temple structure, a monastery original, a Nepalese picture, and a picture on cloth.

## Executive result

The Hodgson material now supplies a second directly documented form of paper conversion, analytically distinct from the palm-leaf repair in Cambridge Add. 1644.

In the mandala drawings, the source object remains elsewhere while its visual/spatial information is extracted onto paper:

`temple / monastery mandala / cloth picture / Nepalese picture -> drawn paper surrogate -> index numbers and inscriptions -> Hodgson archival title/metadata -> institutional circulation`.

This is not support replacement in the narrow conservation sense. It is **cross-media surrogation**. Paper converts objects embedded in architectural, ritual, and pictorial settings into portable planar records capable of being numbered, compared, titled, bundled and transmitted.

Mechanisms:

- `ARCHITECTURAL_RITUAL_OBJECT_TO_PAPER_SURROGATE`
- `CLOTH_IMAGE_TO_PAPER_DIAGRAM`
- `NEPALESE_PICTURE_TO_PAPER_COPY`
- `VISUAL_COPY_CHAIN_WITH_EXPLICIT_SOURCE`
- `PAPER_AS_CROSS_MEDIA_COMPARISON_SURFACE`
- `SOURCE_CONTEXT_TO_INDEXED_PLANAR_SURROGATE`

The evidential advantage is unusually strong: the copy relation is written on the drawings themselves rather than inferred from visual similarity.

## 1. Modern British Library identification: Add Or 5332–5334

The British Library now catalogues three surviving sheets as `Add Or 5332-5334`, parts of larger sets of Nepalese caitya and mandala drawings associated with Hodgson.

The collection-level record states:

- three sheets;
- ink and pencil on **Nepalese country paper**;
- 44 × 34 cm;
- date range 1820–1843;
- inscriptions suggest that they originally belonged to collections formed by Brian Houghton Hodgson;
- the sheets were once part of `IO San 3976f`;
- they were apparently once in the collection of the Institut Impérial de France, Académie des Inscriptions et Belles Lettres.

The modern shelfmarks therefore preserve a fragment of a larger nineteenth-century paper-image programme whose institutional history includes at least India Office and French-academy archival layers.

Source: British Library Archives and Manuscripts Catalogue, `Add Or 5332-5334`, current catalogue record.

Mechanisms:

- `LARGER_VISUAL_SET_SURVIVES_AS_FRAGMENTARY_SHEETS`
- `INSTITUTIONAL_REHOUSING_OF_PAPER_SURROGATES`

Do not infer a full transfer chronology from the French institutional association alone; the secure claim is that the present BL record identifies that provenance layer.

## 2. Add Or 5334: paper compresses heterogeneous source media into one visual set

The British Library describes Add Or 5334 as a sheet drawn on both sides with different Buddhist mandalas. The principal mandala, no. 6, occupies the whole sheet and the sheet bears the signature:

`Rajbeer Chittrakar Nepal.`

The BL identifies the artist as Rajbir Citrakar, active 1820–1844.

One surviving Hodgson-associated inscription reads:

`Dharmachakra mandal copied from a picture on cloth`.

The same sheet also carries numbered references to Yogambara, Vasundhara, Jñāneśvarī and other mandalas, with several smaller mandalas spatially assigned to the corners of a larger one.

This gives an explicit conversion:

`cloth picture -> paper drawing`.

The source was not simply another paper manuscript. Its material and viewing context changed.

Mechanisms:

- `CLOTH_IMAGE_TO_PAPER_DIAGRAM`
- `CROSS_MEDIA_COPY_WITH_SOURCE_INSCRIPTION`
- `NUMBERED_VISUAL_AGGREGATION`

Paper performs two operations at once:

1. it makes the image portable outside the cloth object;
2. it makes the copied image available for serial numbering and aggregation with other mandalas.

## 3. The older India Office catalogue preserves a larger source taxonomy

The earlier India Office catalogue description of the Hodgson mandala set, recoverable through its catalogue/index relations, preserves a larger numbered sequence than the three sheets now separately catalogued by the BL.

The local OCR of the older entry drops or corrupts parts of the shelf/entry numbering. Catalogue no. `7750` is nevertheless strongly recoverable through the printed subject-title index, which repeatedly assigns the numbered mandalas `(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)` etc. to 7750. Do not silently normalize the historical General Collection shelf number until the printed page is checked.

The older description characterizes the surviving unit as a small paper set with drawings on recto and verso, index numbers and corresponding Devanagari particulars. It also records titles/notes in Hodgson’s hand.

Most importantly, the individual inscriptions specify heterogeneous sources.

### No. 1 — Dharmadhātu

Hodgson’s description identifies it as derived `from the original structure` opposite the east face of the temple of Svayambhunath near Kathmandu.

Relation:

`architectural / ritual structure -> paper mandala representation`.

### No. 2 — Durgatipariśodhana

Described as `copied from a picture`.

Relation:

`picture, substrate unspecified -> paper copy`.

### No. 3 — Vajradhātu

Described as from a `Nepalese picture`.

Relation:

`Nepalese picture -> paper copy`.

### No. 4 — Vajradhātu

Described as `copied from original in Maha Vihar of Patan`.

Relation:

`monastery original -> paper copy`.

### No. 5 — Dharmadhātu

Described as copied from Rudra Vihar / Maha Vihar of Patan, with the source located in the upper storey of a temple structure.

Relation:

`enshrined monastery/temple mandala -> paper copy`.

### No. 6 — Dharmacakra

Described as `copied from a picture on cloth`.

Relation:

`cloth picture -> paper copy`.

### No. 7 — Yogāmbara

Also described as copied from a picture on cloth, with 57 deities indexed.

Relation:

`cloth picture -> numbered paper diagram`.

### Nos. 8–9 — Vasundharā and Jñāneśvarī

Described as copied from Nepalese pictures.

Relation:

`Nepalese picture -> paper copy`.

The source taxonomy is therefore not retrospective analytical invention. Hodgson’s own descriptive layer differentiates architectural originals, monastery originals, cloth pictures and Nepalese pictures.

Mechanism:

`SOURCE_MEDIUM_PRESERVED_IN_HODGSON_METADATA`.

## 4. Rajbir Citrakar makes the labour chain visible

The surviving Add Or 5334 is signed `Rajbeer Chittrakar Nepal`, and the modern BL catalogue identifies Rajbir Citrakar as artist.

This matters because the paper surrogate is not adequately described as `Hodgson copied a mandala`.

At minimum, the recoverable production chain contains distinct roles:

`local visual source -> Nepalese painter/draughtsman -> paper drawing -> Hodgson English title/source note -> collection/archival handling`.

The drawing and the English metadata therefore expose a distributed documentary labour structure.

Mechanisms:

- `LOCAL_VISUAL_LABOUR_VISIBLE_ON_PAPER`
- `DRAWING_AND_METADATA_DIVIDED_LABOUR`
- `ATTRIBUTED_INTERMEDIARY_IN_COPY_CHAIN`

Do not collapse `Citrakar` into a generic occupational label without checking the specific signed form and catalogue identification. The modern BL record supplies the personal attribution for Add Or 5334.

## 5. Paper changes the epistemic object

Calling these drawings `copies` should not imply exact facsimile equivalence.

The secure operation is source relation plus transformation:

- three-dimensional or architecturally situated structures become planar;
- cloth images become detachable paper images;
- source location is converted into written metadata;
- multiple source objects are renumbered into one serial field;
- Devanagari particulars and Hodgson’s English descriptions become additional inscription layers;
- scale, surface, ritual emplacement and potentially colour/material properties can be reduced or lost.

Thus the paper surrogate is simultaneously preservative and reductive.

Mechanisms:

- `PORTABILITY_BY_CONTEXT_REDUCTION`
- `RITUAL_LOCATION_TO_METADATA`
- `MATERIAL_SEMANTIC_REDUCTION_IN_VISUAL_SURROGATION`
- `PLANARIZATION_AS_KNOWLEDGE_OPERATION`

The article should avoid the phrase `faithful copy` unless visual comparison with the surviving source object establishes fidelity.

## 6. Relation to the 1860 Buddhist-symbol sheets

Hodgson’s later `Notice on Buddhist Symbols` describes two sheets carrying 143 symbols gathered from printed coins and from temples, images and the larger Nepalese drawing corpus.

The mandala sheets supply a materially explicit upstream analogue for that operation:

`situated object/image -> paper surrogate -> numbered visual corpus -> possible later extraction and recombination`.

At present this is a **structural connection**, not a demonstrated item-by-item genealogy between Add Or 5334 / catalogue 7750 and the 143-symbol sheets.

Mechanism:

`VISUAL_SURROGATE_CORPUS_AS_PRECONDITION_FOR_RECOMBINATION`.

Publication wording should preserve the limit: the same paper operations are visible, but direct derivation of particular symbols has not been shown.

## 7. Comparison with Add. 1644

The two strongest direct support/copy cases now form a useful pair.

### Add. 1644

`damaged palm leaf -> paper supply leaf`

Function:

- repair;
- preservation;
- transfer of ending, colophon and old date;
- source and surrogate remain physically joined.

### Hodgson mandala set

`architectural / ritual / cloth / pictorial source -> Nepalese paper drawing`

Function:

- extraction;
- portability;
- indexing;
- comparison;
- source and surrogate are spatially separated.

The shared operation is not simply `copying`. It is:

**paper creates a second material state in which information from another object can survive and circulate under altered spatial relations.**

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_SURROGATION_WITH_SOURCE_RETAINED_ELSEWHERE`
- `PAPER_SURROGATION_WITH_SOURCE_DECAYING`

This distinction may organize a major section of the article.

## 8. Article-level formulation

**Hodgson’s paper archive did more than receive textual copies. It converted objects embedded in other material settings into portable visual surrogates. A surviving mandala sheet now catalogued as British Library Add Or 5334 is signed by the Nepalese artist Rajbir Citrakar and labels one composition as a Dharmacakra mandala copied from a picture on cloth. The larger Hodgson sequence distinguished further sources: an original structure near Svayambhunath, monastery originals at Patan, cloth pictures and Nepalese pictures. These distinctions remained legible because they were written onto the paper copies. Paper therefore did not simply reproduce visual content. It planarized and serialised heterogeneous ritual objects, attached source metadata to them, and made geographically separated forms available inside one numbered documentary field.**

Follow with the limit:

**The word `copied` establishes genealogy, not fidelity. The paper image changes scale, material, emplacement and potentially colour even as it preserves a claim about where its represented form came from.**

## 9. Source-control notes

- Modern BL shelfmark: `Add Or 5334` for the surviving signed mandala sheet; parent group `Add Or 5332-5334`.
- Modern BL parent record: Nepalese country paper, 44 × 34 cm, dates 1820–1843, formerly part of `IO San 3976f`.
- Older India Office catalogue: use catalogue no. 7750 provisionally/securely at catalogue-number level via repeated title-index cross-references; the old General Collection shelf number in OCR requires printed-page verification before publication.
- Do not infer a full institutional transfer sequence from the `Institut Impérial de France` stamp/association alone.
- `copied from` identifies source relation; it does not prove facsimile accuracy.
- Artist attribution to Rajbir Citrakar is secure for Add Or 5334 via signature plus modern BL cataloguing; do not automatically propagate the same artist to every drawing in the larger numbered series without object-level evidence.

## Bottom line

The copy-chain argument has moved beyond manuscripts. Hodgson-associated paper drawings explicitly convert temple structures, monastery originals and cloth pictures into numbered paper surrogates. Because the source medium and location are written into the documentary layer, the archive exposes the transformation rather than hiding it. Paper’s historical operation here is not merely to preserve an image but to detach visual relations from their original material setting and make them transportable, serial and comparable.
