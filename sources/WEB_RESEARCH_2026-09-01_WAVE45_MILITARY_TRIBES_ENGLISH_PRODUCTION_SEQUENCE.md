# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 45
## `Origin and classification of the military tribes of Nepal`: English production sequence without forced copy-direction

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: paper-centred follow-up to Wave 44. This wave reconstructs the surviving English production states around Hodgson's 1833 article while separating secure chronology, strong source-family relation, and still-unresolved textual descent.

## Executive result

The surviving states support a strong production chronology:

`1831 rough classificatory working`
→ `October 1832 fair tabular copy state`
→ `9 January 1833 learned-society reading`
→ `May 1833 printed article`.

A second undated fair copy survives in vol.9/10 and is explicitly linked by the Cambridge catalogue to the October 1832 copy and the 1833 publication, but its exact place in the copy stemma remains unresolved.

The strongest current formulation is therefore:

`rough source pool -> fair prepublication state(s) -> printed table/narrative`

rather than a forced sequence of manuscript A -> manuscript B -> print.

Mechanisms:

- `ROUGH_SOURCE_POOL_TO_FAIR_PUBLICATION_STATE`
- `PREPUBLICATION_FAIR_COPY_ON_MACHINE_PAPER`
- `TABULAR_CLASSIFICATION_AS_DOCUMENTARY_REFORMATTING`
- `CONTENT_SURVIVAL_ACROSS_VERSION_STATES`
- `COPY_DIRECTION_REQUIRES_SHARED_ERROR_CONTROL`

## 1. 1831 rough source pool — MSS EUR HODGSON/7 item 20

Cambridge describes `vol.7/20`, ff.123–137, as:

- dated 1831;
- machine-made paper;
- Hodgson's own rough writing in pen and pencil;
- English;
- title: `Castes of Parbattiah army tribes`.

Its contents include:

- family names of Brahmans of the eastern and western hills;
- Khasa thars;
- castes of high Gorkha officials;
- some Newar castes;
- Bara-Magarant as the original home of Magars and some Magar family names;
- Gurung clans;
- Pande groups;
- Shah genealogy.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol007.html

Interpretation:

This is not yet safely identifiable as a direct draft of the 1833 article. It contains material broader than the printed article and includes genealogical/court material not necessary to the final paper.

It is, however, securely part of the source pool from which the military-tribe classification was assembled.

Mechanism:

`ROUGH_CLASSIFICATORY_SOURCE_POOL`.

---

## 2. Distinctive content survives from the 1831 rough state into print

The 1831 rough description specifically records a note on `Bara-Magarant` as the original home of the Magars.

The 1833 printed article likewise states that the original seat of the Magars was the Bara Mangrath and then gives the associated central-western Nepal place sequence.

The printed article also proceeds into a large classificatory table of Brahmans, Khasas, Magars and Gurungs.

This does not prove direct copying from vol.7/20. It does demonstrate survival of a distinctive classificatory/geographical component from the 1831 working pool into the 1833 publication state.

Mechanism:

`DISTINCTIVE_CONTENT_SURVIVAL_ACROSS_VERSION_STATES`.

## 3. October 1832 fair tabular state — MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1

Cambridge describes `vol.6/1`, ff.1–12b, as:

- `Origin and classification of the Military tribes of Nepal`;
- dated `10-1832`;
- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- tabular view of family names including Brahman, Khasa, Magar and Gurung;
- another copy survives as vol.9/10;
- explicitly linked to Hodgson's 1833 and 1874 publication states.

The surrounding vol.6 description is important:

- the volume retains historical `Box 2 / Item 12` metadata;
- the manuscripts in the volume are described as copies;
- most were published.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html

This is the strongest currently surviving prepublication candidate.

Mechanism:

`PREPUBLICATION_FAIR_TABULAR_STATE`.

Source control:

Do not call it the printer's copy until page-level comparison demonstrates direct descent from this exact manuscript state to the printed text.

## 4. Independent dating triangulation — Oldfield's October 1832 paper

Henry Ambrose Oldfield later reproduced a condensed tabular view of the military tribes and explicitly stated that it was condensed from a paper by Brian Hodgson dated October 1832.

His condensed structure retains:

- Brahmans;
- Khasas with twelve subdivisions;
- Ekthariya;
- Thakuri;
- Magars;
- Gurungs.

This independently matches the month-year date of Cambridge vol.6/1.

The triangulation is therefore unusually strong:

`Cambridge vol.6/1 = October 1832 fair tabular manuscript`
+
`Oldfield = Hodgson paper dated October 1832`
+
`1833 publication = same titled classificatory paper and expanded table`.

This raises vol.6/1 to `PREPUBLICATION_CANDIDATE_HIGH`.

It still does not by itself establish printer-copy identity.

## 5. Second fair manuscript state — MSS EUR HODGSON/9 item 10

Cambridge describes `vol.9/10`, ff.59–62, as:

- `Origin and classification of the military tribes of Nepal`;
- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- English;

- linked directly to the 1833/1874 printed paper and vol.6/1.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol009.html

The catalogue does not provide a secure date for vol.9/10.

Therefore the present relation is:

`vol.6/1 <-> vol.9/10`

as a declared copy-family relation, with direction unresolved.

Mechanism:

`PARALLEL_FAIR_COPY_STATES_WITH_UNRESOLVED_DIRECTION`.

## 6. Society-reading and print state

The Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal article is `Origin and Classification of the Military Tribes of Nepal`, vol.2 (1833), pp.217–224.

The journal header records the paper as read at the meeting of 9 January 1833; it appeared in the May 1833 number.

The printed article combines two documentary operations:

1. narrative ethnological/historical argument;
2. a detailed classificatory table.

The article explicitly introduces the table as a classified view of the military tribes with their subdivisions.

This matters materially because the fair manuscript state and print are not only related by content; both adopt tabulation as the final classificatory surface.

Mechanism:

`TABULAR_CLASSIFICATION_SURVIVES_PUBLICATION_TRANSITION`.

### Strong source-family inference

### High-confidence prepublication candidate

`vol.6/1 -> 1833 print`

is highly plausible from exact title, date, fair/tabular state, catalogue publication link and Oldfield's independent October 1832 attribution.

But this arrow remains `HIGH_CANDIDATE`, not `PROVEN_DIRECT_COPY`.

### Unresolved copy relation

`vol.6/1 <-> vol.9/10`.

Direction requires shared-error/order comparison.

## 8. Why this matters for the paper article

This sequence gives a compact example of **documentary versioning inside one paper support class**.

All three surviving English manuscript states are on machine-made paper, yet their documentary states differ sharply:

- rough source pool;
- fair tabular state;
- parallel fair copy.

Thus paper type alone does not define version state.

The material transition is subtler:

`same broad support class + different writing/correction/layout state -> different epistemic function`.

The sequence therefore complements the support-switch cases.

Earlier mechanism:

`support may change when knowledge changes operational state`.

New mechanism:

`knowledge can also change operational state while support class remains constant, through handwriting, tabulation, correction, selection and publication formatting`.

Mechanism:

`VERSIONING_WITHIN_CONSTANT_SUPPORT_CLASS`.

## 10. Immediate falsification targets

1. Inspect vol.7/20, vol.6/1 and vol.9/10 page by page.
2. Transcribe the family-name order in each state.
3. Record shared misspellings, omissions, insertions and corrections.
4. Compare section breaks and heading sequence against the 1833 print.
5. Determine whether vol.6/1 or vol.9/10 preserves compositor-facing marks, pagination cues or printer annotations.
6. Test whether the 1831 Bara-Magarant place sequence matches print order exactly.
7. Compare the 1874 reprint and its `List of Additions and Corrections` before treating the 1874 state as textually identical to 1833.

## Bottom line

The military-tribe paper now has a defensible English production sequence without overclaiming textual descent. A broad 1831 rough source pool was reformatted into at least one fair tabular machine-paper state by October 1832; the paper was read in January 1833 and printed in May. Vol.6/1 is the strongest surviving prepublication candidate, independently reinforced by Oldfield's identification of an October 1832 Hodgson paper. Vol.9/10 is a second fair copy whose direction remains unresolved. The next decisive step is shared-error and list-order comparison, not further thematic matching.
