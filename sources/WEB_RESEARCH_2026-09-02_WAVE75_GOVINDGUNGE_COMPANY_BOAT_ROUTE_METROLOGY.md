# Web/archive research 2026-09-02 — Wave 75
## Govindgunge before Hodgson: Company boat-hire metrology and the pre-existing river network behind the 1842 pulp route

Date: 2026-09-02

Status: active paper-history mainline. Follow-up to Waves 73–74. This wave moves from local road/river conditions to the Company's prior administrative calibration of river transport. It does not determine the actual route taken by Hodgson's twenty-four pulp packages.

## Executive result

The 1842 Hodgson letter proposes that, if land carriage from Motihari toward Patna is expensive relative to water carriage, the twenty-four packages of Nepalese paper pulp should be sent from `Govind Gang` directly to Calcutta by a small safe boat under a Burkundaz.

A directly contemporary-administrative predecessor now shows that this water option rested on an already metrized Company transport network.

*The New Annual Bengal Directory and General Register* for **1825** prints a section headed:

`RATES OF BOAT HIRE`

and immediately defines:

`Time allowed for Boats to proceed to any of the undermentioned Places, as per Company's Allowance.`

Under `UP THE RIVER`, it lists a large network of destinations from Calcutta/Presidency and assigns standard travel periods. One line groups:

`Mahabad, Guazypore, Benares, Chunar, Daory-Ghaut, Assam Goalparrah, Govindgunge, Mullye, and Jegyepore`

with an allowed period of:

`2 months 0 days`.

The same schedule lists:

- Patna and associated destinations at `1 month 28 days`;
- Goruckpore at `2 months 8 days`;
- Bettiah at `2 months 15 days`;
- many nearer and farther destinations in a graduated travel-time table.

The adjacent `BAGGAGE BOATS, &c.` section then prices boats by carrying capacity, from **200 maunds** through **1000 maunds**, at specified monthly rupee rates.

This gives the route an administrative metrology:

`destination`
× `standard allowed travel time`
× `boat carrying capacity`
× `monthly hire rate`.

Govindgunge was therefore already legible to the Company as a named river-transport destination at least seventeen years before Hodgson's March 1842 pulp shipment.

Combined sequence:

`1825 Company boat-hire schedule: Govindgunge = standardized river destination`
-> `1838 Rankine: Govind Gunge roads difficult; river routes available but materially constrained`
-> `1842 Hodgson: compare land cost with water cost; if favorable, Govind Gang -> Calcutta by boat`.

Core mechanisms:

- `ROUTE_NODE_PREEXISTS_EXPERIMENT`
- `TRANSPORT_ROUTE_IS_ADMINISTRATIVELY_METRIZED`
- `TIME_ALLOWANCE_IS_LOGISTICS_STANDARDIZATION`
- `BOAT_CAPACITY_AND_MONTHLY_RATE_MAKE_ROUTE_COST_CALCULABLE`
- `COMPANY_TRANSPORT_INFRASTRUCTURE_PRESTRUCTURES_MATERIAL_CIRCULATION`
- `PAPER_METROLOGY_AND_ROUTE_METROLOGY_ARE_PARALLEL_ACCOUNTING_SYSTEMS`
- `EXPERIMENTAL_MATERIAL_ENTERS_PREEXISTING_LOGISTICS_REGIME`.

## 3. The time allowance is a standard, not an observed journey duration

The 1825 table states the `Time allowed` under Company allowance rules.

Therefore:

`Govindgunge = 2 months`

means:

`ADMINISTRATIVE_ALLOWED_TRIP_PERIOD = 2 MONTHS`.

It does **not** mean:

- every voyage took exactly two months;
- Hodgson's 1842 packages took two months;
- the route remained unchanged between 1825 and 1842;
- seasonal conditions were identical;
- the voyage was necessarily continuous without transshipment.

The distinction is historically useful. The table shows how the Company converted route distance/difficulty into an administratively payable time allowance.

Mechanisms:

- `STANDARD_TIME_NOT_OBSERVED_DURATION`
- `ADMINISTRATIVE_METROLOGY_CONVERTS_ROUTE_INTO_PAYABLE_UNIT`.

## 7. Parallel with paper count metrology

Waves 68–72 reconstructed local paper-count systems:

`tau / dhep / kori`

and Company stationery packages:

`sheet / quire / ream / chest`.

Wave 75 adds transport counting/calibration:

`destination / allowed time / boat capacity / monthly rate`.

These are not analogous because they use the same units. They are analogous because each system converts heterogeneous material processes into administratively actionable quantities.

### Paper metrology

turns sheets into accountable stock/package quantities.

### Route metrology

turns space, travel and boat capacity into accountable freight time/cost.

The experimental paper pulp enters both:

- physical package count: 24 packages;
- transport regime: route/time/capacity/cost calculation.

Working proposition:

**colonial material circulation depended on multiple metrologies layered around the object: the material had to be counted into packages, placed on a route whose travel time was administratively standardized, and assigned to carrying capacity priced by the month.**

Mechanisms:

- `MATERIAL_CIRCULATION_IS_MULTI_METROLOGICAL`

## Bottom line

Govindgunge was already part of a Company-calibrated river network before Hodgson's paper-pulp shipment. The 1825 Bengal Directory places it in the official `Rates of Boat Hire` table with a two-month allowed trip period and follows that destination schedule with a graduated monthly-price table for baggage boats from 200 to 1000 maunds carrying capacity. Rankine's 1838 description then supplies the physical counterpart: difficult roads, bridge deficiencies, seasonal passability and constrained but usable river navigation in the same corridor. Hodgson's March 1842 instruction to compare land carriage with direct water carriage from `Govind Gang` to Calcutta therefore inserted twenty-four pulp packages into a pre-existing material and administrative transport regime. The route was already measured before the experiment moved through it.
