# Web/archive research 2026-09-03 — Wave 77
## Campbell's 1837 Nepal-paper procurement scheme, learned-society experimentation, Govindgunge's triple transport role, and Political O.C. anchors around 1842

Date: 2026-09-03

Status: active paper-history mainline. Follow-up to Waves 75–76. This wave adds a pre-1842 government procurement and sample-circulation layer. It does **not** prove that the March 1842 twenty-four-package pulp consignment was the same batch used at Serampore, nor that all 1831–42 paper initiatives formed one continuously authorized programme. It reconstructs a recurring administrative-material problem space in which Nepal paper was evaluated for durability, government substitution, price, markets, carriage, experimental sampling and commercial circulation.

## Executive result

Two source families can now be separated cleanly.

### A. Direct published primary witness — 1838

*Transactions of the Agricultural and Horticultural Society of India*, vol. V, Serampore Press, 1838, directly records:

- article LII: `On Nepalese Paper, by Dr. Campbell`, p. 220;
- a Society request dated **20 November 1837** for `a small supply of each size and quality of paper manufactured at Nepaul for experimental purposes`;
- Campbell's reply of **28 November 1837**, promising to transmit a quantity;
- Campbell's suggestion that samples of each variety be forwarded to the **Chamber of Commerce**;
- Campbell's enclosure of copies of the recent correspondence between **T. C. Scott and himself** on the paper for the Society's interest.

This gives a directly evidenced transformation chain:

`government correspondence on Nepal paper`
-> `copies transmitted to Agricultural & Horticultural Society`
-> `paper samples requested by size and quality for experiment`
-> `Campbell promises material shipment`
-> `commercial evaluation via Chamber of Commerce proposed`
-> `correspondence published as learned-society paper`.

The material and its documentary description therefore circulated together but through different institutional channels.

### B. Abdul Ali 1945 — secondary archival extraction

A. F. M. Abdul Ali, `The Daphne Paper of Nepal`, *B. C. Law Volume*, 1945, pp. 377–391, explicitly states that his nineteenth-century reconstruction draws on contemporary Foreign and Political Department records and gives a series of `Political O.C.` date/number citations.

His article preserves or paraphrases Campbell's **15 November 1837** letter to T. C. Scott, including:

- comparison of old Nepal-paper office records with English foolscap;
- advocacy of Nepal paper for Government records and stationery;
- market geography including Patna, Sarun/Tirhut nodes and `Govindgunge`;
- reported Nepal-paper price ranges;
- an estimated Nepal-to-Patna transport charge of about **Rs 1-12 per maund**;
- a proposed seasonal procurement regime: annual purchasing during the rains and carriage toward the Ganges during the four cold months.

It also supplies archival anchors for the later Darjeeling paper project, including `Political O.C., 29 Nov 1841, No.147` and `Political O.C., 11 May 1842, No.70`, the latter associated with Campbell's April 1842 correspondence to G. A. Bushby and the closure/economics of the Darjeeling paper operation.

These references are valuable retrieval addresses but the underlying Political O.C. files have **not** been directly inspected in this wave. The exact modern shelfmark and the expansion of Abdul Ali's abbreviation `Political O.C.` remain unresolved.

Core mechanisms:

- `ARCHIVE_AS_LONGITUDINAL_MATERIAL_TEST`
- `GOVERNMENT_PAPER_SUBSTITUTION_IS_A_LOGISTICS_PROBLEM`
- `PAPER_MARKET_GEOGRAPHY_PRECEDES_1842_PULP_ROUTE`
- `SEASONAL_PROCUREMENT_CALENDAR_STRUCTURES_MATERIAL_FLOW`
- `MATERIAL_SPECIFIC_FREIGHT_ESTIMATE_PRECEDES_PULP_SHIPMENT`
- `GOVERNMENT_CORRESPONDENCE_BECOMES_LEARNED_SOCIETY_PAPER`
- `SAMPLES_AND_DOCUMENTS_CIRCULATE_IN_PARALLEL`
- `LEARNED_SOCIETY_EXPERIMENT_OPENS_COMMERCIAL_EVALUATION`
- `GOVINDGUNGE_HAS_LAYERED_MARKET_AND_TRANSPORT_FUNCTIONS`
- `POLITICAL_OC_DATE_NUMBER_IS_A_RETRIEVAL_ADDRESS_NOT_YET_A_DIRECT_WITNESS`

## 2. Society request: paper samples were explicitly wanted for experiment

The Society proceedings record Campbell's letter of 28 November 1837 as a reply to the Secretary's letter of 20 November.

The Secretary had asked for:

`a small supply of each size and quality of paper manufactured at Nepaul for experimental purposes`.

The important features are operational:

- `small supply` — a bounded specimen/material shipment;
- `each size and quality` — deliberate variation across material classes;
- `for experimental purposes` — the material is requested as a comparative experimental set, not merely as a curiosity or library sample.

This creates a pre-1842 evidence state in which Nepal paper is already moving as a deliberately differentiated experimental material.

Mechanisms:

- `SAMPLE_SET_PRESERVES_MATERIAL_VARIATION`
- `EXPERIMENTAL_TOKEN_IS_DEFINED_BY_SIZE_AND_QUALITY`
- `MATERIAL_CLASSIFICATION_PRECEDES_LABORATORY_OR_MARKET_TEST`.

## 3. Campbell proposes a second destination: Chamber of Commerce

The same proceedings state that Campbell intended to transmit a quantity and suggested that:

`samples of each variety be forwarded to the Chamber of Commerce`.

This is a major institutional edge.

The proposed paper movement is no longer only:

`Nepal -> learned society`.

It becomes:

`Nepal paper varieties`
-> `Agricultural & Horticultural Society experimental sample set`
-> `Chamber of Commerce sample evaluation proposed`.

The paper object can therefore change institutional function while remaining materially comparable:

- experimental specimen;
- commercial sample;
- possible government-stationery candidate.

Mechanisms:

- `EXPERIMENTAL_SAMPLE_CAN_BECOME_COMMERCIAL_SAMPLE`
- `MATERIAL_EVALUATION_CROSSES_LEARNED_AND_COMMERCIAL_INSTITUTIONS`.

Guardrail:

The proceedings prove Campbell **suggested** forwarding samples to the Chamber of Commerce. They do not yet prove the Chamber received, tested or endorsed the samples.

Encode:

`CHAMBER_OF_COMMERCE_SAMPLE_EDGE = PROPOSED_DIRECT`

not

`CHAMBER_OF_COMMERCE_RECEIPT = PROVEN`.

## 6. Campbell's office records become a durability experiment

Abdul Ali identifies Campbell's 15 November 1837 letter to T. C. Scott, Deputy Secretary to Government, as a central witness.

Campbell compares old Government-office records written on Nepal paper with English foolscap under long-term archival use. He points to office records dating from **1817** on Nepal paper as remaining fresh/usable and uses this observation to argue for the remarkable durability of the material.

The methodological significance is substantial.

The archive is functioning as a longitudinal material test:

`same institutional storage/use environment`
+ `different paper supports`
+ `elapsed administrative time`
-> `comparative durability evidence`.

Mechanisms:

- `ARCHIVE_AS_LONGITUDINAL_MATERIAL_TEST`
- `BUREAUCRATIC_USE_GENERATES_MATERIAL_EVIDENCE`
- `OLD_RECORDS_BECOME_PROOF_OBJECTS_FOR_NEW_PROCUREMENT_POLICY`.

This is stronger than a generic claim that Nepal paper had a reputation for toughness. Campbell operationalizes archival survival as evidence for prospective stationery substitution.

## 7. Nepal paper is proposed as a Government-office substitution material

Abdul Ali's extraction shows Campbell recommending substitution of Nepal paper for plains paper in Government offices.

The proposed substitution is not argued from strength alone. It combines:

- durability;
- purchase price;
- market geography;
- government monopoly effects in Kathmandu;
- carriage cost;
- purchasing season;
- route toward the Ganges;
- availability from hill manufactories outside the Kathmandu monopoly channel.

Thus:

`MATERIAL_SUBSTITUTION = PERFORMANCE × PRICE × MARKET_ACCESS × SEASON × TRANSPORT`.

Mechanisms:

- `GOVERNMENT_PAPER_SUBSTITUTION_IS_A_LOGISTICS_PROBLEM`
- `PROCUREMENT_POLICY_IS_MATERIAL_HISTORY`.

This is a prehistory to later Darjeeling manufacture and 1842 pulp movement, but current evidence does not justify writing the whole 1831–42 sequence as one uninterrupted programme of authorization.

Safer relation:

`RECURRING_GOVERNMENT_PAPER_PROBLEM_SPACE_WITH_DOCUMENTED_CONTINUITIES_AND_GAPS`.

## 8. Campbell maps a distributed paper market

Abdul Ali's account of the 15 November 1837 correspondence lists a broad market geography for Nepal paper in the plains/hill interface.

Names in the 1945 print/OCR include variants corresponding to:

- Patna;
- places in Sarun/Saran;
- Janakpur;
- Darbhanga/Tirhut;
- Purnea;
- `Govindgunge`;
- other Sarun and Gorakhpur-area markets;
- Oudh-area markets.

Exact historical spellings should be controlled against the 1838 article image before normalization in publication.

The substantive result is secure at the level of Abdul Ali's extraction:

Nepal paper was represented in 1837 not as a commodity available only at Kathmandu but as circulating through multiple plains-facing markets.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_SUPPLY_IS_A_DISTRIBUTED_MARKET_NETWORK`
- `KATHMANDU_MONOPOLY_DOES_NOT_EXHAUST_PLAINS_SUPPLY_PATHS`.

## 9. Govindgunge now has three distinct paper/transport functions across 1825–1842

This is the strongest new cross-wave connection.

### 1825 — transport-metrology node

Company `Rates of Boat Hire` lists `Govindgunge` as an up-river destination with a standard allowed travel period.

Function:

`ADMINISTRATIVELY_METRIZED_RIVER_DESTINATION`.

### 1837 — Nepal-paper market/procurement node

Campbell's paper-supply geography, as preserved by Abdul Ali from the T. C. Scott correspondence, includes `Govindgunge` among markets relevant to paper procurement/distribution.

Function:

`PAPER_MARKET_OR_SUPPLY_NODE`.

### 1842 — pulp-forwarding node

Hodgson's 25 March instruction offers `Govind Gang` as the point from which the twenty-four pulp packages could be sent directly to Calcutta by small safe boat if water carriage were cheaper.

Function:

`SPECIFIC_EXPERIMENTAL_MATERIAL_FORWARDING_NODE`.

Combined:

`same historical node`
× `generic Company route metrology`
× `paper-market geography`
× `specific pulp forwarding decision`.

Do not flatten these functions into one claim.

The correct proposition is:

**Govindgunge was repeatedly legible within paper circulation because it already existed as both a transport-calculation node and a market-facing node.**

Mechanisms:

- `TRANSPORT_NODE_CAN_ACQUIRE_COMMODITY_SPECIFIC_FUNCTION`
- `MARKET_AND_ROUTE_INFRASTRUCTURE_INTERLOCK_WITHOUT_BEING_IDENTICAL`.

## 10. A material-specific freight estimate appears before the 1842 pulp shipment

Abdul Ali reports Campbell's 1837 estimate for transporting Nepal paper to Patna at about:

`Rs 1-12 per maund`.

He also reports Nepal-paper price ranges in terms of sheets per Nepalese rupee and Company rupees per maund.

This is important because it is material-specific and route-specific in a way the 1825 general boat-hire table is not.

But the object is different:

- 1837 evidence concerns Nepal paper as a traded finished-sheet commodity;
- 1842 event concerns `twenty-four packages of Nepalese paper pulp`.

Therefore:

`1837_NEPAL_PAPER_FREIGHT_CONTEXT != ACTUAL_1842_PULP_FREIGHT_COST`.

Use the figure to demonstrate that Nepal-paper procurement was already costed in route terms, not to calculate Woodcock's unrecovered bill.

Mechanisms:

- `MATERIAL_SPECIFIC_FREIGHT_ESTIMATE_PRECEDES_EXPERIMENTAL_PULP_ROUTE`
- `FINISHED_PAPER_COST_CANNOT_BE_TRANSFERRED_TO_PULP_PACKAGE_COST`.

## 11. Procurement is seasonal

Abdul Ali reports Campbell proposing annual purchasing during the rains and forwarding toward the Ganges during the four cold months.

This makes procurement temporally structured:

`purchase season`
-> `holding/aggregation interval`
-> `cold-season carriage window`
-> `river-facing distribution`.

This adds a time layer to the paper-market model.

Mechanisms:

- `SEASONAL_PROCUREMENT_CALENDAR_STRUCTURES_MATERIAL_FLOW`
- `MARKET_AVAILABILITY_AND_TRANSPORT_WINDOW_ARE_COUPLED`.

Do not apply this 1837 proposed calendar mechanically to the March 1842 pulp consignment. The latter is a different material state and a separately documented movement.

## 12. Political O.C. anchors around the Darjeeling paper project

Abdul Ali supplies several archival date/number pointers around the later government paper experiment.

High-value anchors include:

- `Political O.C., 29 Nov 1841, No.147`;
- `Political O.C., 11 May 1842, No.70`.

The latter is associated in his notes with Campbell's April 1842 correspondence to G. A. Bushby and the economics/closure of the Darjeeling paper operation.

His account states that the Darjeeling manufactory was worked until **16 April 1842** and then closed as uneconomical under the existing arrangement unless machinery/process conditions changed.

A note identifies a Campbell letter to Bushby dated **21 April 1842** under the 11 May 1842, No.70 Political O.C. reference.

These exact underlying records remain uninspected here.

## 13. March–May 1842 is a dense paper-event window, not yet a causal chain

`TEMPORAL_PROXIMITY != CONSIGNMENT_IDENTITY`

7. Keep proposed Chamber of Commerce forwarding separate from proven receipt/testing.

12. Do not describe 1831–42 as one uninterrupted government programme unless initiating and continuation orders are recovered. Use `recurring government paper problem-space` or `programme-level continuity candidate`.

## Bottom line

Wave 77 moves the paper history backward from the 1842 pulp shipment into a directly evidenced 1837 circulation system. The Agricultural & Horticultural Society asked Campbell for differentiated Nepal-paper samples by size and quality explicitly for experiment; Campbell promised a quantity, proposed sending varieties to the Chamber of Commerce, and transferred copies of his recent official correspondence with T. C. Scott into the Society, where the material became `On Nepalese Paper` in the 1838 *Transactions*. Abdul Ali's later archival extraction adds the government-procurement content of that correspondence: durability demonstrated through old office records, substitution for Government stationery, market geography, seasonality, prices and a Nepal-to-Patna freight estimate. Govindgunge is consequently no longer only an 1825 boat-hire destination or an 1842 pulp forwarding option. It also appears inside the 1837 Nepal-paper supply geography. The emerging object is therefore not simply `Nepal paper` but a material repeatedly translated among archive proof-object, procurement commodity, experimental sample, commercial sample, learned-society text, transport package and later industrial feedstock. Those translations share infrastructure and actors, but they remain separate evidence states until direct documents join them.
