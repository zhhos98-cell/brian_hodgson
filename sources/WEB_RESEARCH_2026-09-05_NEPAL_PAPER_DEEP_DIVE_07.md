## Finding 31 — Political O.C. 13 Jan 1832 no.19 is likely to contain more than a sample-covering letter

Evidence status: **STRONG SECONDARY ARCHIVAL EXTRACTION + direct published-text cross-control; underlying O.C. not yet inspected.**

Abdul Ali cites `Pol. O.C., no.19, Jany. 13, 1832` not only for Hodgson's 8 Dec 1831 transmission of specimens, but also after a long quotation from Hodgson concerning:

- where `Nepalese` paper was actually manufactured;
- Cis-Himalayan Bhote / Rangbo producers;
- forest-shed manufacturing geography;
- access to firewood, ash and clear water;
- dried pulp bricks/tiles for transport to England;
- north/south circulation of the paper.

This suggests that the Original Consultation may preserve Hodgson's attached manufacturing account and/or associated enclosures rather than merely a short cover note.

Consequence:

`Political Original Consultation, 13 Jan 1832, no.19` is now an **article-level Case 1 retrieval target**, especially because Original Consultations may preserve enclosures not copied into proceedings.

## Finding 32 — Campbell's 1837 evidence shows an already dense use ecology before the Darjeeling experiment

Evidence status: **SECONDARY ARCHIVAL/PUBLISHED EXTRACTION via Abdul Ali, with parts traceable to Campbell's 1838 Agri-Horticultural Society publication.**

Abdul Ali's reconstruction of Campbell's 1837 material records concrete uses of Nepal paper beyond office records:

- medicine packing at the Calcutta General Dispensary comparison level;
- cold-drawn castor-oil manufacture, where fibre tenacity prevented paper shreds contaminating the oil;
- lining the Kathmandu post-office roof;
- waterproof/waxed paper as substitute for wax cloth for letter mails and bhangy parcels in the rains;
- rice-water coating, polishing and arsenical colouring/preservation using harital/munsila.

Campbell also identifies Kimchat and Dholoka as two paper-manufacturing villages producing the finest varieties; both were described as entirely inhabited by papermakers at the time of his Nepal residence. The common sheet sizes and ability to order very large sheets show a differentiated production system rather than a single generic `Nepal paper`.

Consequence:

The 1841 Darjeeling experiment should not begin from a material awaiting a use. It relocated and modified a paper technology already embedded in multiple regional operations and grades.

Article rule:

Use these examples selectively as the **pre-existing practical ecology** against which Campbell's later attempts at whitening, filtration, government stationery and industrial manufacture can be understood. Do not turn them into a separate fourth case.
