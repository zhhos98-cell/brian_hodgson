## Executive result

Wave 20 produces three additional exact-work cross-support comparisons, two of them explicitly cross-referenced by Bendall to the RAS Hodgson catalogue:

1. `Gaṇḍavyūha`: RAS MS 2 palm leaf ↔ Cambridge Add. 917 paper;
2. `Ekallavīra/Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa-tantra`: RAS MS 46 palm leaf ↔ Cambridge Add. 1319 paper;
3. `Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā`: RAS MS 1 palm leaf ↔ Cambridge Add. 1544 paper and Add. 1625 paper.

The strongest new result is not simply more evidence for width expansion. It is **geometrical divergence under the same support**.

The same named Aṣṭasāhasrikā work exists in:

- RAS palm leaf at 22½ × 2½ in.;
- twelfth-century Cambridge paper at 20 × 2 in.;
- 1828 Cambridge paper at 14 × 4½ in.

Thus paper can preserve and even intensify a narrow pothi-like geometry, or it can occupy a much broader page regime. Support enlarges the possible design space but does not specify one geometry.

Mechanisms:

- `TEXT_CONTROLLED_SUPPORT_COMPARISON`
- `PAPER_GEOMETRY_BIFURCATION_WITHIN_SAME_WORK`
- `SUPPORT_AFFORDANCE_NOT_FORMAT_DETERMINISM`
- `CHRONOLOGY_AND_PRODUCTION_REGIME_MEDIATE_SUPPORT_FORM`
- `SAME_SUPPORT_CAN_PRESERVE_OR_DEPART_FROM_POTHI_GEOMETRY`

# 1. Gaṇḍavyūha: extreme cross-support widening

## RAS Hodgson MS 2

Cowell and Eggeling:

- `Gaṇḍa-vyūha`;
- 289 palm leaves;
- 22½ × 2 in.;
- six lines per page.

This is one of the longest and narrowest objects in the Hodgson catalogue.

## Cambridge Add. 917

Bendall:

- paper;
- 309 leaves;
- 11 lines per page;
- 13½ × 6½ in.;
- modern;
- `Gaṇḍa-vyūha`;
- explicitly directs the reader to `R. A. S. Cat. No. 2`.

The digitised text renders the fractions imperfectly in some OCR surrogates, but the printed catalogue gives 13½ × 6½ in. This dimension should still be checked against the page image before final publication quotation.

## Geometry

RAS palm leaf:

- aspect ratio: 11.25;
- area proxy: 45 sq. in.

Cambridge paper:

- aspect ratio: c.2.08;
- area proxy: 87.75 sq. in.

Relative to the RAS witness:

- long axis falls to 60%;
- short axis expands 3.25×;
- area proxy expands 1.95×;
- line count rises from 6 to 11.

This is an extreme example of `SHORT_AXIS_EXPANSION_WITH_LONG_AXIS_REDUCTION`.

It is still not direct copy-lineage evidence. Bendall’s cross-reference establishes work-level identity/control, not descent from the RAS object.

Mechanisms:

- `EXACT_WORK_CROSS_SUPPORT_PAIR_WITHOUT_COPY_LINEAGE`
- `SHORT_AXIS_EXPANSION_WITH_LONG_AXIS_REDUCTION`
- `PAPER_PAGE_AREA_EXPANSION`
- `EXTREME_OBLONG_TO_BROAD_PAGE_SHIFT`

# 2. Ekallavīra / Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa: paper changes length, not width

## RAS Hodgson MS 46

Cowell and Eggeling:

- `Ekallavīratantram [Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantram]`;
- twenty-five paṭalas;
- 50 palm leaves;
- 8½ × 3 in.;
- seven or eight lines per page;
- old.

This palm-leaf witness is already unusually broad for the RAS palm-leaf corpus, with aspect ratio c.2.83.

## Cambridge Add. 1319

Bendall:

- paper;
- 70 leaves;
- 6 lines per page;
- 10½ × 3 in.;
- dated N.S. 944 / A.D. 1824;
- `Ekaravīra-tantra (Caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa-tantra)`;
- explicitly `See R. A. S. Cat. No. 46`;
- also consists of twenty-five paṭalas.

This is a high-quality exact-work control because title equivalence, the RAS cross-reference, and the twenty-five-section structure all align.

## Geometry

RAS palm leaf:

- 8.5 × 3 in.;
- aspect ratio c.2.83.

Cambridge paper:

- 10.5 × 3 in.;
- aspect ratio 3.5.

Relative change:

- short axis: unchanged;
- long axis: +23.5%;
- area proxy: +23.5%;
- aspect ratio becomes more elongated, not less.

This pair directly blocks a deterministic `paper -> wider short axis` model.

The paper witness expands primarily along the long axis while preserving the same short axis.

Mechanisms:

- `LONG_AXIS_EXPANSION_WITH_SHORT_AXIS_STABILITY`
- `PAPER_CAN_EXTEND_POTHI_GEOMETRY`
- `SUPPORT_AFFORDANCE_IS_DIRECTIONALLY_NONDETERMINISTIC`

# 3. Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā: one work, three geometry regimes

## RAS Hodgson MS 1 — palm leaf

Cowell and Eggeling:

- complete in thirty-two chapters;
- 204 palm leaves;
- 22½ × 2½ in.;
- six lines per page;
- old.

Aspect ratio: 9.0.

## Cambridge Add. 1544 — early paper

Bendall:

- paper;
- 172 leaves;
- seven lines per page;
- 20 × 2 in.;
- twelfth century;
- `Aṣṭasāhasrikā-Prajñāpāramitā`.

This paper witness is *narrower* than the Hodgson palm-leaf witness in the short axis and has a more extreme aspect ratio:

- aspect ratio: 10.0;
- long axis: 88.9% of RAS;
- short axis: 80% of RAS;
- area proxy: 71.1% of RAS.

This is evidence for persistence of an extreme long-narrow manuscript geometry on paper.

Mechanisms:

- `EARLY_PAPER_PRESERVES_EXTREME_POTHI_GEOMETRY`
- `PAPER_SUPPORT_WITHOUT_WIDTH_EXPANSION`

## Cambridge Add. 1625 — late paper

Bendall:

- paper;
- 406 leaves;
- seven lines per page;
- 14 × 4½ in.;
- dated N.S. 948 / A.D. 1828;
- `Aṣṭasāhasrikā-Prajñāpāramitā`;
- bound in coloured boards, with painted design and page-border decoration.

Relative to RAS MS 1:

- long axis falls to 62.2%;
- short axis expands 1.8×;
- area proxy is c.1.12×;
- aspect ratio falls from 9.0 to c.3.11.

This is a materially different paper regime from Add. 1544.

## Strong result

The same named work on paper can occupy two sharply different geometries:

`twelfth-century paper: 20 × 2 in.`

versus

`1828 paper: 14 × 4½ in.`

Therefore the argument should move from:

`palm leaf vs paper`

onto a more exact historical model:

`support × date × hand × copying regime × binding/container × visual programme -> realised manuscript geometry`.

Paper changes the feasible space, but manuscript format remains historically selected within that space.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_GEOMETRY_BIFURCATION_WITHIN_SAME_WORK`
- `SUPPORT_AFFORDANCE_REQUIRES_HISTORICAL_SELECTION`
- `CHRONOLOGY_MEDIATES_SUPPORT_FORM`

# 4. The Cambridge collection itself is a paper-history event

Bendall’s preface supplies a major institutional context.

The Cambridge Buddhist Sanskrit collection was chiefly assembled from manuscripts collected by Dr Daniel Wright while he was attached to the British Residency at Kathmandu and received at Cambridge between February 1873 and May 1876.

Bendall states that, on Professor Cowell’s suggestion, Wright was initially asked to procure **copies made to order** from works still extant in Nepal. Specimen copies were sent and became Add. 1042. Wright subsequently found that originals could also be procured.

This means the Cambridge corpus does not represent a passive survival sample.

Its paper objects emerge partly from a late nineteenth-century institutional acquisition and recopying programme consciously conducted in the wake of Hodgson’s earlier manuscript discovery and distribution.

Mechanisms:

- `INSTITUTIONAL_RECOPYING_AS_MANUSCRIPT_PRODUCTION`
- `CATALOGUE_COMPARISON_CORPUS_CREATED_BY_ACQUISITION_PROGRAMME`
- `HODGSON_TO_WRIGHT_COLLECTION_SUCCESSION`
- `PAPER_COPYING_AS_COLLECTION_BUILDING`

Critical limit:

Do not assume every modern Cambridge paper manuscript was commissioned by Wright. Bendall only explicitly identifies Add. 1042 as the initial specimen-transcription set and says originals later became procurable. Each witness requires separate acquisition/copy-state control.

# 5. Revised cross-support argument

Wave 19 alone could still be read as suggesting a simple page-broadening tendency because Bodhicaryāvatāra changed from 12½ × 1¾ palm leaf to 10¼ × 4½ paper.

Wave 20 breaks that simple trajectory:

- `Gaṇḍavyūha`: very strong widening on paper;
- `Bodhicaryāvatāra`: very strong widening on paper;
- `Ekallavīra`: same short axis, longer paper witness;
- `Aṣṭasāhasrikā`, twelfth-century paper: paper is narrower and proportionally *more* elongated than the Hodgson palm-leaf witness;
- `Aṣṭasāhasrikā`, 1828 paper: paper is much broader.

The better formulation is:

**Paper does not encode a single replacement geometry. It enlarges and reconfigures the range of manuscript formats available to scribes and patrons. Exact-work comparisons show that this affordance can be realised as short-axis expansion, long-axis expansion, or the persistence of an extreme pothi-like form. Chronology and production regime are therefore constitutive variables rather than residual noise.**

This is stronger than the original aggregate-only claim because it survives controlled counterexamples.

# 6. Article-level paragraph

**Cross-collection comparison makes the support argument more complicated, but also more historical. Hodgson’s palm-leaf Gaṇḍavyūha measures roughly 22½ by 2 inches, whereas Bendall’s Cambridge paper witness, explicitly cross-referenced to the RAS copy, is about 13½ by 6½ inches. Yet the Ekallavīra/Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa comparison behaves differently: the palm-leaf copy is 8½ by 3 inches and the 1824 paper copy 10½ by 3, extending the long axis while leaving the short axis unchanged. Most revealing is the Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā. Hodgson’s palm-leaf witness is 22½ by 2½ inches; a twelfth-century Cambridge paper copy remains even more narrowly elongated at 20 by 2, while an 1828 paper copy contracts to 14 by 4½. Paper therefore did not replace palm leaf with a single new page form. It created a different field of formal possibilities, selected differently across periods and production contexts.**

Immediately follow with:

**These are work-level comparisons, not demonstrated lines of descent between individual witnesses.**

## Bottom line

The cross-support matrix has now moved beyond one suggestive pair. Three additional exact-work comparisons show that paper’s historical effect is not reducible to widening. The strongest paper-history claim is a change in **format possibility space**, with realised geometry selected by chronology, scribal density, textual programme, binding/container and institutional production regime.
