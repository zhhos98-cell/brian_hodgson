# Web research wave 17 — catalogue labour economy in the Asiatic Society, 1869–1878

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: nineteenth-century institutional-record reconstruction. Core evidence comes from published Proceedings / Annual Reports of the Asiatic Society of Bengal and the 1877 descriptive catalogue project. This wave tests and strengthens the `catalogue labour economy` mechanism introduced in Wave 16.

## 1. 1869 accounts make manuscript legibility an explicit expenditure category

The Society's published accounts for 1869 contain a distinct heading:

**`Conservation of Sanscrit MSS.` — total Rs 458-10-6.**

The disbursement table breaks this into operational costs:

- salary of the Travelling Pandita — Rs 106;
- salary for compiling Catalogue of Sanscrit MSS. — Rs 87;
- travelling allowance — Rs 61-8-6;
- transcribing the Ekamvara Purana from Oriya to Nagari — Rs 32;
- stationery — Rs 31-15;
- printing **2,000 copies of a Sanskrit tabular form** — Rs 70;
- copying MSS. — Rs 40;
- binding Sanskrit MSS. — Rs 11-10-9;
- a **japanned paper box** — Rs 11-3-9;
- postage for letters to the Travelling Pandita — Rs 4-4-6;
- **banghy expenses for sending MS.** — Rs 3.

The same accounts separately record Rs 30 salary to Munshi Abdul Hakim for cataloguing Persian and Arabic MSS.

Public searchable witness:
https://www.scribd.com/document/420567799/Proceeding-of-the-Asiatic-Society-of-Bengal-1870

### Analytical importance

This table makes `cataloguing` materially decomposable. Institutional legibility required:

**expert salary + travel + manuscript movement + writing/transcription + paper/form printing + copying + binding + container + correspondence + transport.**

A catalogue is therefore not an immaterial description added after preservation. The budget groups description, conservation, copying, rebinding and movement in one production system.

The `japanned paper box` is especially useful for the wider article because it puts a named protective container inside the same budget line as catalogue labour. Object preservation and metadata production are literally costed together.

## 2. The travelling Pandit makes the library network mobile

The travelling Pandit was not simply a reader stationed inside the Society. Government/Society manuscript work required an expert to move between repositories, inspect external collections, prepare returns and sometimes arrange copying.

The 1869 expenditure therefore includes both:

- salary;
- travel allowance;
- postage to the Pandit;
- transport of manuscripts to him.

### Mechanism

The catalogue network is distributed in the same strong sense as Hodgson's earlier manuscript-production network:

**repository → travelling expert → list/form → copied/borrowed manuscript → Society/Government → printed notice/catalogue.**

The object can move to the expert, the expert can move to the object, or information can be converted into a standardized form and sent back.

---

## 3. Catalogue compilation and conservation overlap institutionally

The account heading `Conservation of Sanscrit MSS.` contains activities that a modern schema might divide among:

- conservation;
- cataloguing;
- acquisition survey;
- copying;
- binding;
- metadata form production;
- transport;
- storage.

The historical accounting category therefore does not map neatly onto modern professional boundaries.

### Rule for the repo

Do not retroactively split these expenditures into modern departments as though the historical actors had done so. Preserve the account heading and code the individual operations beneath it.

This matters because the Society's practical concept of `conservation` included making manuscripts findable, describable, reproducible and physically manageable.

## 4. 1870: catalogue revision is explicitly justified as accessibility infrastructure

The 1870 Annual Report states that a five-member subcommittee had been appointed to revise the Library catalogue. It further reports the appointment of:

- a Maulavi to check the catalogue of Persian MSS.;
- a Pandit to check the Sanskrit MSS.

The Council says the completed arrangements would **markedly increase the accessibility and consequent usefulness of the library**.

Public text witness:
https://www.scribd.com/document/420567799/Proceeding-of-the-Asiatic-Society-of-Bengal-1870

### Mechanism

This is unusually explicit nineteenth-century language for the project's `institutional legibility` mechanism:

**possession + shelves ≠ usefulness.**

Usefulness depends on a checked descriptive interface created by language-specific expert labour.

The report also connects classification of publications with improved accessibility to members and other `men of science`, strengthening the point that arrangement, indexing and cataloguing were treated as operational access technologies.

---

## 5. Government manuscript survey becomes a funded recurrent programme

A July 1870 Government letter acknowledges the Society's work in discovering/preserving Sanskrit literature and sanctions payment of **Rs 1,079-2-9** for expenses incurred during the second half of 1869–70. It also agrees to future advances, subject to **half-yearly adjustment**.

The Society/Government system therefore has:

- project expenditure;
- reimbursement;
- recurring advance;
- periodic reconciliation.

Public text witness:
https://www.scribd.com/document/84831377/Asiatic-Society-of-Bengal-Proceedingsofasi1871asia

### Relation to Hodgson project

This is a later institutional analogue to Hodgson's 1827 manuscript bills. Both convert manuscript knowledge work into auditable expenditure streams, but at different scales:

**Hodgson 1827:** paper + copying + binding + carpentry + porterage + shipment;

**Society/Government 1869–70:** travelling Pandit + catalogue compiler + forms + copying + binding + box + postage + manuscript transport + reimbursement cycle.

This upstream/downstream symmetry is becoming one of the strongest long-duration structures in the repo.

## 6. 1877: descriptive catalogue work has measurable throughput

The 1877 Annual Report lists the Sanskrit manuscript corpus by subject and gives a total of **1,110 MSS.** already noticed within the descriptive-catalogue programme. It reports that during the preceding year the Pandit prepared notices of **173 MSS.**

The same year Rajendralal Mitra submitted the first part of the *Descriptive Catalogue of Sanskrit MSS. in the Society's Library*, devoted to grammar.

Public searchable witness:
https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/58/Proceedings_of_the_Asiatic_Society_of_Bengal_%28IA_proceedingsofasi1877asia%29.pdf

Bibliographic control:
- *A Descriptive Catalogue of Sanskrit MSS. in the Library of the Asiatic Society of Bengal. Part First: Grammar*, Calcutta: C. B. Lewis at the Baptist Mission Press, 1877.
- Wellcome record: https://preview.wellcomecollection.org/works/xu9p6zf8

### Analytical importance

`catalogue production` now has an observable throughput:

- manuscripts noticed;
- notices per year;
- subject partition;
- printed part completed/submitted.

This allows a more precise distinction between:

- collection size;
- work queue;
- yearly analytical throughput;
- publication output.

Do not treat catalogue date as the date on which every included manuscript was first identified.

## 7. Hodgson's Buddhist collection becomes a separately funded analytical project

The 1877 report says Mitra had undertaken a **catalogue raisonné** of the Buddhist manuscripts from Nepal presented by Hodgson. The task was described as difficult because it required reading a large body of manuscripts, including Gatha/corrupt Sanskrit and Newari script.

It reports that **two Pandits were then working under Mitra**, with the expectation that they would help expose the contents of the collection.

This independently corroborates Mitra's 1882 retrospective account of divided labour.

### Mechanism

The Hodgson collection is not simply absorbed into a general catalogue queue. It becomes a special analytical subproject whose difficulty justifies dedicated expertise.

The institutional transformation is:

**donated physical collection → recognized difficult corpus → dedicated readers → analytical descriptions → print project.**

## 8. 1878: printing progress and physical arrangement are adjacent administrative achievements

The 1878 Annual Report states that considerable progress had been made in printing Mitra's analysis of Hodgson's Sanskrit Buddhist manuscripts.

Immediately nearby, it reports:

**the Books and Book-cases in the Library have been properly arranged and numbered.**

Public searchable witness:
https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/88/Proceedings_of_the_Asiatic_Society_of_Bengal_%28IA_proceedingsofasi1878asia%29.pdf

### Why this adjacency matters

Do not claim the report explicitly theorizes these as one system. But administratively they appear as parallel library achievements:

1. textual contents made printable and intelligible;
2. physical holdings and containers made locatable by arrangement and numbering.

The broader mechanism can therefore distinguish two axes of institutional legibility:

- **semantic addressability** — what is this manuscript / what does it contain?
- **physical addressability** — where is the book/container and by what number can it be found?

A functioning library needs both.

## 9. The catalogue project itself is versioned and unfinished

The 1877 grammar volume is bibliographically recorded as `Part First`, and modern records note that no more of that specific Mitra-edited series was published in that form.

This is useful because a catalogue project can fail or redirect without the underlying cataloguing work ceasing. The Society later continued descriptive cataloguing through other series, compilers and subject divisions.

### Rule

Separate:

- `catalogue_project`;
- `catalogue_volume`;
- `underlying_notices/descriptions`;
- `publication_series`;
- `later reuse`.

This is analogous to the repo's treatment of *Zoology of Nipal*: failure to stabilize as one publication can reveal the infrastructure more clearly than a finished series.

## 10. New mechanism: semantic vs physical addressability

Wave 17 suggests a useful refinement of `institutional legibility`.

### Semantic addressability

Produced by:

- reading;
- title identification;
- subject classification;
- description;
- internal segmentation;
- catalogue entry;
- index;
- translation/abstract.

### Physical addressability

Produced by:

- arrangement;
- shelf/case placement;
- numbering;
- labels;
- binding/container state;
- catalogue-to-location relation.

### Failure modes

An object may be:

- physically locatable but semantically misidentified;
- semantically identified but physically misplaced/unavailable;
- present in a numbered box but internally unreconciled;
- well described in print but no longer linked to its old shelfmark;
- preserved physically while catalogue relations drift.

This distinction should be tested against Hodgson zoological specimens and drawing portfolios.

## 11. Catalogue labour economy v0.2

The mechanism can now be stated more empirically:

**Catalogue labour economy** = the budgeted and administratively supervised allocation of expert reading, travel, transcription, form production, copying, binding, storage, transport, checking, indexing and printing required to turn possessed manuscripts into physically and semantically addressable institutional objects.

Evidence now exists at three levels:

1. **budget** — 1869 disbursement table;
2. **administration** — appointments, subcommittee, government reimbursement and recurrent advances;
3. **production throughput** — 1,110 MSS noticed, 173 notices in one year, subject catalogue printing and dedicated Hodgson analysis.

This is substantially stronger than an inference from Mitra's preface alone.

5. Do proofs sent to Hodgson contain corrections that reveal the distance between catalogue reader, Mitra and donor?
6. Are box/case numbers or wrapper inscriptions preserved independently of printed catalogue identities?

These are now sharply defined object-level tests.

## Bottom line

The Society's own records confirm that institutional legibility was a funded material production process. In 1869 `Conservation of Sanscrit MSS.` paid for experts, travel, catalogue compilation, forms, copying, binding, a japanned paper box, postage and manuscript transport. In 1870 catalogue checking was explicitly justified as increasing accessibility and usefulness; Government reimbursed more than Rs 1,000 and established recurring advances. By 1877 descriptive work had measurable throughput, while Hodgson's difficult Buddhist corpus had dedicated Pandits under Mitra. By 1878 the analysis was in print while books and book-cases were being physically arranged and numbered. The manuscript had to become both semantically and physically addressable before institutional possession became institutional use.
