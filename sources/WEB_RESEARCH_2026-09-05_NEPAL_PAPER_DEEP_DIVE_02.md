## Finding 8 — 1851 Great Exhibition preserves Darjeeling paper as an imperial exhibition category

Evidence status: **SECURE CATALOGUE-LEVEL WITNESS; continuity inference NOT ALLOWED.**

The official Great Exhibition catalogue for India, Class 9, `Papers, &c.`, lists:

- paper from young bamboo in Assam;
- paper made with the bark of *Daphne cannabina*;
- **paper made in Nepal and at Darjeeling**;
- paper from Kashmir;
- several other experimental fibre papers.

Public catalogue witness:
https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/22079393.pdf

Consequence:

By 1851 `paper made ... at Darjeeling` remained legible enough within the imperial material-exhibition system to be catalogued as a distinct product/material category. This is a real afterlife for the 1841–42 experiment.

However, this source does **not** prove continuous manufacture from 1841 to 1851. The displayed material could have been older experimental sheets. Therefore the later Darjeeling Gazetteer claim that Campbell supplied writing and packing paper `for some years` remains unconfirmed at contemporaneous production level.

Article use:

- useful as an epilogue or afterlife marker;
- do not use it to infer factory continuity, output, or commercial viability.

## Finding 9 — contemporaneous continuation search has not corroborated the later `for some years` factory claim

Evidence status: **NEGATIVE SEARCH RESULT / controlled non-finding.**

Targeted searches across 1842–1850 for `Darjeeling paper`, `Nipal/Nepaul paper`, `paper manufactory`, `manufacture of paper`, Campbell + paper, and *Daphne cannabina* have not produced a contemporaneous source stating that Campbell's Darjeeling manufactory continuously supplied writing/packing paper for several years.

Modern scholarship on early Darjeeling development likewise cites the 1842 Agricultural and Horticultural Society correspondence for the paper experiment, rather than a later run of production records.

Consequence:

The later district-gazetteer statement remains **P1 retrospective**. Stop treating it as an active P0 unless a highly specific contemporaneous citation surfaces incidentally.

Current working formulation:

- 1841–42 experimental production = secure;
- 1851 exhibition recognition of Darjeeling-made paper = secure;
- continuous commercial/factory production for `some years` = unresolved;
- 1849 continued procurement of Nepal-made paper for Hooker's fieldwork = independently secure and should not be conflated with Darjeeling manufacture.
