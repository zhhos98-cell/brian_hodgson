# Public-web research wave 9 — waterproof shipment, mixed-media cases, load units, and archival packets

Date: 2026-08-31

Scope: public web only. No new private/archive scans added.

## 1. The 1858 Institut de France gift is a near-complete packaging and routing case

David William Mitchell of ZSL wrote to Hodgson on 29 June 1858 that Hodgson's boxes were in a **Clearing Agent's hands** and that, with Hodgson's authority, Mitchell would remove them. This establishes an intermediate commercial/custodial node before institutional receipt.

Source:
https://zsl-archive.maxarchiveservices.co.uk/index.php/informationobject/browse?names=3307&places=53692&sf_culture=pt&sort=startDate&sortDir=asc&topLod=0

On 19 July 1858 Mitchell reported a new routing plan after consulting Jules Mohl in Paris:

- the Academy accepted Hodgson's gift;
- a **roll** was to be put into a **waterproof box**;
- that box and another existing box were to be entrusted to Mr Molini, bookseller and agent of the Institut;
- Molini would forward them `in the regular way`;
- the British Embassy route was deliberately rejected because it caused delay;
- Mitchell added that a paper Hodgson had previously sent by the Embassy route **never reached the Institut at all**;
- the Institut would pay for construction of the waterproof box.

Source:
https://zsl-archive.maxarchiveservices.co.uk/index.php/informationobject/browse?names=3307&places=53595&sf_culture=pt&sort=startDate&sortDir=asc&topLod=0

This is direct evidence that **previous transmission failure generated a new preservation-and-routing design**.

## 2. Receipt closes the transport loop

The Royal Asiatic Society catalogue preserves letters from the Institut de France:

- 13 August 1858: thanks Hodgson for his gifts;
- 26 October 1858: thanks Hodgson for **the two parcels which had arrived** at the Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres and discusses a catalogue.

Source:
https://royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/informationobject/browse?levels=241&places=618&sort=referenceCode&sortDir=asc&topLod=0

The chain is therefore unusually complete:

**Hodgson gift → clearing agent → Mitchell/Mohl route redesign → waterproof container → bookseller/institutional agent → forwarding → two-parcel receipt → catalogue**.

## 3. The two cases were Buddhist mixed-media knowledge objects

The Académie's 1859 report states that **two cases** sent by Hodgson contained a very considerable number of paintings and drawings concerning Buddhism in Nepal and Tibet. These paintings/drawings were accompanied by explanatory texts, mostly Sanskrit extracts from Buddhist sacred books prepared by the pandit Amritananda.

Source:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1859_num_3_1_66153

Later collection history describes the Institut holdings as:

- **27 scrolls of paintings**;
- **6 bundles of drawings**;
- some Nepalese and Tibetan manuscripts explaining them;
- Hodgson's own notes.

Source:
https://d1i1jdw69xsqx0.cloudfront.net/digitalhimalaya/collections/journals/ebhr/pdf/EBHR_03_03.pdf

Thus this is not merely transport of `art`. The cases contained a composite research apparatus linking **image ↔ explanatory manuscript/text ↔ collector's notes**.

## 4. Hodgson's own project description joins discovery, procurement, testing, and image accumulation

The RAS record for Hodgson's 1857 `Architectural illustrations of Buddhism` preserves his opening formulation: while engaged in **discovering, procuring and testing** the general character of the literary monuments of Buddhism in Nepal, he was simultaneously accumulating architectural and sculptural illustrations of the creed. The paper then lists drawings to be presented to the Institut de France.

The fair-copy annotation says the paper was delivered to the Institut on 16 January 1858 **through the British Embassy in Paris**.

Source:
https://royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/informationobject/browse?levels=241&names=2296&sort=alphabetic&sortDir=asc&subjects=643&topLod=0

This gives the July 1858 route redesign additional significance: Hodgson had already used the Embassy route for related material, while Mitchell's July letter reports that a paper sent by that channel never reached the Institut and recommends replacing that route with an agent-and-waterproof-box chain.

Do not assume without further matching that every January paper/drawing and every July box was exactly the same shipment stage; the chronology and collection context are strongly connected but should be reconstructed item by item.

## 5. Later catalogues themselves introduced shipment-date errors

Alfred Foucher's 1897 catalogue of the Institut Hodgson paintings apparently inferred from a note found in a case that the collection had been sent in **1866**. A contemporary JRAS note objected that Barthélemy Saint-Hilaire had already discussed the whole collection in 1863, so the paintings/drawings could not have arrived in 1866.

Source:
https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/DE9E75AAC85D8EA69B2A44A7E84A71AB/S0035869X00146805a.pdf/iii-notes-and-news.pdf

This is structurally comparable to the Hodgson Kanjur/Tanjur 1835/1838 chronology problem: **a later note/catalogue field can overwrite or flatten the actual acquisition/transport chronology**.

## 6. The packaging question also appears explicitly in Hodgson's Nepal commerce work

W. W. Hunter's 1896 biography quotes Hodgson's discussion of Himalayan load engineering. Hodgson argued that goods should be made up in parcels of **two Calcutta bazaar maunds each**. A single mountaineer could carry such a load, whereas packages above that threshold had to be broken apart on the road because two men could not effectively combine their strength, causing `great hazard and inconvenience`. He further recommended carrying apparatus for fixing two such parcels across a bullock's saddle.

Source:
https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Nepal/1896%20Life%20of%20Brian%20Houghton%20Hodgson%20by%20WW%20Hunter%20s.pdf

BL's inventory confirms that Hodgson's December 1831 commerce report to George Swinton survives in `Mss Eur Hodgson/6`, and that the material was later published in the 1857 Government of Bengal Selections and in Hodgson's 1874 essays.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html

The quoted packing passage has not yet been directly located in Hodgson's own digitised 1874 text in this wave. Until then, cite it explicitly as **Hunter quoting Hodgson**, not as a directly inspected Hodgson passage.

## 7. Transport units routinely crossed modern disciplinary boundaries

RAS records already show that Hodgson packed and repacked heterogeneous materials together:

- `List of Buddha Books & drawings, & of Statistics of Nepaul`: five boxes plus a sixth package, later repacked in 1844;
- `Details of Contents of Bastas or Bundles in Trunk No. 1`: Buddhism, chronicles/Vaṃśāvalīs, army details, catalogues, śāstras and other materials, repacked in 1844 and placed aboard the *Hardwicke*;
- a manuscript list updated in 1844 records which items had translations and notes that **the whole, original and translation, were with Hodgson on board the Hardwicke**.

Analytic implication: **transport unit ≠ disciplinary unit**. Boxes, trunks and bastas preserved assemblages that later libraries and scholarly disciplines separated.

## 8. Packaging surfaces could themselves function as catalogue surfaces

RAS `Photos of Himal-races` (1854–1879) consists of a list enclosed inside a **large sheet of paper folded as a packet**, while a similar list is written directly on the packet itself. A newspaper header was also reused as an inscribed series marker.

Source:
https://royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/informationobject/browse?creators=2578&levels=241&sort=identifier&sortDir=asc&topLod=0

The container and metadata are therefore literally the same paper surface.

A related RAS list of manuscripts began as a Hodgson **store list in 1858** and was reused as the **donation list to the India Office in 1864**, accompanied by a donation letter.

Source:
https://www.royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/informationobject/browse?collection=673&names=2296&showAdvanced=1&sort=identifier&sortDir=asc&topLod=0

This supports treating lists as mutable custody instruments rather than static bibliographic descriptions.

## 9. A 1870 zoological box deliberately reassembled a distributed research apparatus

On 11 February 1870 Hodgson told G. F. L. Marshall that he would send a **big deal box** containing the material to be used for Hume's proposed ornithology of India. The contents included:

- four portfolios of drawings;
- Hodgson's manuscript list of Nepal birds;
- his native painter's Hindi list including Sikkim;
- a red-bound volume of bird manners prepared by his writer from viva voce statements of Hodgson's shikaris;
- eight unbound Darjeeling volumes of similar material;
- Hodgson's Tarai manuscript memoranda from 1846;
- printed catalogues and Calcutta reprints;
- sundry printed papers.

Hodgson explicitly asked to hear of the **safe arrival of the box**.

Source:
https://zsl-archive.maxarchiveservices.co.uk/index.php/informationobject/browse?collection=12410&media=print&places=53595&sf_culture=en&sort=identifier&sortDir=desc&subjects=56987&topLod=0&view=table

Marshall's related correspondence says the four portfolios were so large and internally scattered that proper inspection required substantial room to lay them out and reassemble by species/group the materials dispersed among the four receptacles.

Source:
https://zsl-archive.maxarchiveservices.co.uk/index.php/informationobject/browse?levels=237&names=4543&onlyMedia=1&showAdvanced=1&sort=identifier&sortDir=asc&topLod=0&view=table

The box was therefore not a container for a single genre. It reassembled a **cross-media evidence system**:

**field testimony → writer/translation → manuscript behavioural notes → drawings → manuscript lists → printed catalogues/reprints**.

## 10. Boxes could preserve relations or prevent use

Natural-history evidence shows opposite effects of institutional handling.

- The British Museum actively processed Hodgson material but later removed/replaced many original labels, damaging provenance links.
- East India Company Museum curator Frederic Moore preserved original Hodgson labels, improving later auditability.
- Hodgson bird skins in the India Museum were reportedly still unpacked in 1874, preventing scholarly inspection for decades.

Packing therefore has no single epistemic effect. A closed box may preserve original associations while blocking comparison; active unpacking/cataloguing may enable knowledge production while destroying relational traces.

## 11. Direct padding/filling evidence remains unresolved

Targeted searches in this wave did **not** locate a Hodgson-specific use of cotton, tow, straw, shavings or similar void-filling material for zoological boxes.

Confirmed Hodgson-network preservation/packing materials now include:

- Nepali/brown paper as wrapping/interleaving and plant-drying paper;
- wax cloth;
- thread and twine;
- boxes/crates/trunks/bastas/packages;
- a purpose-built **waterproof box**;
- carpenters, porters, clearing agents and forwarding agents;
- route choice based on prior loss/delay.

Swainson 1822 remains a useful contemporary comparator for cotton/tow filling, but it should not be converted into Hodgson evidence.

## 12. Revised mechanism: container as relation-preserving device

A useful proposition emerging from this wave is:

> A nineteenth-century knowledge container did not merely protect its contents. It selected which relations would travel together. A trunk could bind Buddhism, statistics and military data into one custody unit; a waterproof case could keep images and explanatory texts together; a zoological box could reassemble drawings, Hindi lists, shikari testimony and printed catalogues for renewed scholarly work. Conversely, wrong routing, non-arrival, prolonged non-opening, relabelling and later cataloguing could dissolve those relations.

This suggests separating at least three functions:

1. **physical preservation** — keeping an object dry/intact;
2. **relational preservation** — keeping labels, lists, explanatory texts, drawings, translations or paired witnesses associated;
3. **institutional availability** — making the contents accessible for sorting, comparison and cataloguing.

These functions can conflict.
