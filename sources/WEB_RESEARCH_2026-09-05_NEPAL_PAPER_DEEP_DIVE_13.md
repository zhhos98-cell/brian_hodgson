## Finding 50 — `IOR/R/5` is the main pre-1872 local Residency correspondence corpus

Evidence status: **SECURE catalogue-level control.**

The British Library Archives & Manuscripts catalogue identifies:

`IOR/R/5 — Nepal Residency Records, 1792–1872`

as **135 files**, in English, divided into four correspondence series:

1. `IOR/R/5/1–33` — Letters received from the Government of India;
2. `IOR/R/5/34–63` — Letters sent to the Government of India;
3. `IOR/R/5/64–111` — Miscellaneous letters received;
4. `IOR/R/5/112–135` — Miscellaneous letters sent.

Catalogue root:
https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/033-000225724

This materially changes the retrieval model. The earlier handoff concentrated on:

- Hodgson's private-paper copy at `Mss Eur Hodgson/6, item 6, ff.77–81`;
- the central Foreign Political consultation of 13 January 1832;
- the Stiller/TUCL transcription route.

Those remain valuable, but `IOR/R/5` supplies the **Residency-side documentary architecture itself**. It is not a thematic secondary collection and it is not equivalent to the central consultation proceedings.

Correct model:

`Residency outgoing/incoming correspondence`
↔ `Government/Foreign Political consultation or proceeding`
↔ `private/copy state where preserved`.

These states can preserve different forwarding language, enclosures, endorsements, names, and copy relations.

## Finding 51 — `IOR/R/5/43` is a new highest-priority target for the 1831 paper account

Evidence status: **SECURE date-range locator; CONTENT NOT YET INSPECTED.**

British Library catalogue:

`IOR/R/5/43 — Letters sent to the Government of India, 1 November 1831–2 December 1832`

- two volumes;
- arrangement: Part 1, ff.1–87; Part 2, ff.88–179.

Direct catalogue record:
https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-000225769

A. F. M. Abdul Ali's later archival history, as summarized by P. K. Gode, states that Hodgson sent specimens of Nepal paper to H. T. Prinsep on **8 December 1831**, for communication to Lord William Bentinck, and recommended the paper for official-record use.

The date `8 December 1831` falls squarely inside `IOR/R/5/43`.

This produces a new retrieval priority:

`8 Dec 1831 Hodgson -> H. T. Prinsep`
-> search `IOR/R/5/43`
-> compare with `Mss Eur Hodgson/6 ff.77–81`
-> compare with `Political Consultation, 13 Jan 1832, Nos.18–20`
-> compare with printed JASB 1832 article.

Why this matters:

The remaining P0 is not merely the wording of Hodgson's technical description. It is whether any pre-publication documentary state preserves:

- a maker/informant/intermediary;
- personal observation language;
- an attached note or specimen provenance;
- an enclosure omitted or flattened in print;
- a distinction between Hodgson's own report and reported maker practice.

Do **not** claim that the paper letter itself has been located inside `/43` until the volume is inspected. The secure claim is that the dated outgoing correspondence target falls within the catalogue coverage of `/43`.

## Finding 54 — NAI remains the central-proceedings route, but the current public interface and holdings structure must be distinguished from Residency records

Evidence status: **SECURE institution/portal-level control; item-level paper records not yet retrieved in the current interface.**

The National Archives of India currently exposes Foreign Department records through Abhilekh Patal and reports a major recent digitization expansion.

Official NAI information pages:

- Information Technology / digitization:
  https://www.nationalarchives.nic.in/en/Information-Technology
- Abhilekh Patal:
  https://nationalarchives.nic.in/en/online-portal-abhilekh-patal
- Reference tools:
  https://www.nationalarchives.nic.in/en/research-and-references/reference-tools
- Public-record holdings:
  https://nationalarchives.nic.in/en/public-records-holdings/public-records

The IT page reports **20,41,63,325 digitized pages uploaded and online as of 27 April 2026**. The Abhilekh overview page contains older/staler aggregate figures. Preserve that version difference rather than silently harmonizing it.

The NAI public-record holdings page lists:

- Foreign Department: 1630–1913;
- Nepal Residency as a resident series only from 1872–1922.

This is compatible with older archival guidance that the pre-1872 Nepal Residency correspondence was preserved at the India Office while central Government copies/proceedings entered Foreign/Political public records.

Therefore for 1831–42 use a two-sided model:

`BL IOR/R/5 = local Residency correspondence state`

versus

`NAI Foreign/Political = central Government consultation/proceeding state`.

Neither substitutes for the other.

### Current public-search limit

Abhilekh Patal's current interface is JavaScript/captcha-heavy in this research environment. Search-engine indexing demonstrates that 1830s Foreign consultation blocks can surface as item-level digitized records, but exact public-index hits for:

- `Political Consultation, 13 Jan 1832, Nos.18–20`;
- `29 Nov 1841, No.147`;
- `11 May 1842, No.70`

have not yet been recovered in this pass.

This is an **interface/index non-finding**, not evidence of archival absence.

The NAI reference-tool page also exposes the `Guide to the Records in the National Archives of India, Part III: Foreign Department, 1756–1954`, which should be used to control series/proceeding structure rather than relying on free-text keyword search alone.

The remaining archive work is larger than the previous handoff suggested, but it is now bounded by record architecture rather than by subject keywords.
