# Web research wave 18 — Government manuscript metadata forms and remote usability, 1868–1871

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: institutional-document reconstruction from published Asiatic Society Proceedings reproducing Government correspondence. This wave isolates the metadata form itself as a historical knowledge technology.

## 1. Government did not simply request 'a catalogue': it specified a data structure

Published correspondence in the Asiatic Society Proceedings reproduces the Government of India's manuscript-survey form associated with the 1868 instructions.

The blank form had **nine columns**:

1. number;
2. name in Devanagari;
3. name in Roman character;
4. subject matter and name of author;
5. number of pages;
6. number of lines on each page;
7. substance on which written and character/script;
8. name of place where found and person with whom found;
9. remarks regarding accuracy and peculiarities.

Public text witness:
https://www.scribd.com/document/84831377/Asiatic-Society-of-Bengal-Proceedingsofasi1871asia

### Analytical importance

This is a nineteenth-century state-designed metadata schema. It combines several distinct kinds of information in one tabular object:

- identifier/order;
- title in original script;
- transliterated title;
- intellectual classification;
- physical/codicological extent;
- material support;
- script;
- provenance/location/custodian;
- qualitative assessment.

The form therefore attempts to make manuscripts comparable and actionable at a distance without requiring every recipient to see the original.

---

## 2. The schema explicitly couples textual identity to material and custody data

Modern catalogue systems often separate bibliographic, codicological and provenance fields. The Government form requires all of them together.

A usable manuscript record must answer simultaneously:

- what is it called?
- how can the name be read by someone using Roman script?
- what is it about / who wrote it?
- how large is it?
- how densely is it written?
- what is it physically written on?
- in what script?
- where is it located?
- who possesses it?
- how accurate or peculiar is it?

### Mechanism

The object is converted into a **portable descriptive surrogate** whose usefulness depends on retaining relations among intellectual, material and custodial properties.

This is directly compatible with the repo's central concern with auditability after separation.

---

## 3. Roman transliteration is an interoperability field

The separate requirement for:

- title in Devanagari;
- title in Roman character

shows that transcription was not only about preserving the local written form. The form creates an interface across script competence.

### Caution

Do not read this only as linguistic domination or standardization. At mechanism level it is an interoperability device whose consequences can then be studied historically:

- who can identify the record;
- how names change in transliteration;
- whether aliases collide;
- whether transliterated title becomes the dominant catalogue identity;
- what errors are introduced when script-to-script conversion is separated from physical manuscript inspection.

This can later be compared with Hodgson's Sanskrit names + European-form identifiers on drawings and with vernacular/Latin taxonomic naming in zoology.

---

## 4. 'Substance on which written' makes support a required information field

The Government form explicitly asks for the **substance** on which the manuscript is written, alongside script.

This is important for the paper project. Material support is not merely something modern conservators later noticed; it was already included in a nineteenth-century institutional survey protocol.

### Research question

How consistently did actual notices fill this field?

Possible outputs include:

- paper;
- palm leaf;
- other support categories;
- support omitted/unknown.

The field allows the repo to compare:

1. prescribed metadata;
2. actual catalogue completion;
3. surviving object-level material evidence.

Disagreement among these levels may itself reveal information loss.

---

## 5. 'Accuracy and peculiarities' turns expert judgement into a metadata column

The ninth column asks for remarks concerning **accuracy and peculiarities**.

This means the record is not intended to be purely descriptive. It also contains a preliminary quality/evidentiary assessment.

Possible dimensions historically folded into such a field may include:

- correctness of text;
- incompleteness;
- unusual readings;
- damage;
- rarity;
- scribal features;
- anomalies.

Do not infer which was intended in an individual entry without inspecting it.

### Mechanism

A catalogue form can embed expert judgement at the moment of data capture, converting evaluation into a portable attribute of the manuscript.

This creates a potential long afterlife: later scholars may inherit the judgement without re-inspecting the object.

---

## 6. Location and owner were mandatory because discovery without recoverability was insufficient

The Government correspondence criticising manuscript notices makes the problem explicit. A catalogue of external manuscripts was judged less useful when it failed to state where the manuscripts were and to whom they belonged.

The criticism argues that a practically useful catalogue should report:

- places searched;
- place where each manuscript is found;
- owner/custodian;
- enough information to consider whether Government should seek access/purchase/copying.

### Institutional consequence

Metadata is being designed around future operations:

**find → contact → inspect → borrow/copy/purchase → compare/use.**

A title without a recoverable custody address is epistemically informative but operationally weak.

This parallels specimen catalogues where a name/number becomes useless if the physical specimen cannot be located for comparison.

---

## 7. Remote European usefulness became an explicit evaluation standard

Government criticism says incomplete information materially reduced the notices' **value and usefulness, especially to European scholars**.

This should be handled carefully. It documents a specific user model built into the metadata system: a reader physically distant from the manuscript and potentially unable to inspect the repository directly.

### Mechanism

The form is designed to support **remote scholarly action**.

Required fields therefore reduce dependence on tacit local knowledge:

- Romanized title;
- location;
- owner;
- material/script description;
- extent;
- accuracy remarks.

The form is a technology for making local manuscript holdings queryable from elsewhere.

---

## 8. Metadata completeness becomes contestable and governable

The correspondence shows that the first submitted notices did not simply pass into publication. Government evaluated whether the information structure met its intended use and demanded supplementation.

The sequence is:

**blank form specification → fieldwork/inspection → notices → Government review → insufficiency diagnosis → supplementary data request.**

This is a data-quality control loop.

### Relation to repo mechanisms

It resembles several other Hodgson cases:

- shipment receipt followed by inventory reconciliation;
- specimen list followed by name/number correction;
- paper experiment followed by performance testing;
- catalogue entry followed by Mitra re-segmentation.

In each case, circulation produces a first portable representation that is later checked against an operational requirement.

---

## 9. Government funding turns metadata collection into an accountable programme

The 1870 correspondence also records Government sanction of **Rs 1,079-2-9** for Society expenses in manuscript discovery/preservation during the preceding half-year and provision for future advances subject to half-yearly adjustment.

Thus the metadata schema is linked to:

- formal instructions;
- paid field activity;
- standardized forms;
- returns;
- Government review;
- reimbursement;
- recurring audit.

This is a genuine information infrastructure rather than an isolated bibliographical convention.

## 10. New mechanism: operational metadata

### Definition

**Operational metadata** = descriptive fields selected not only to represent an object intellectually, but to enable specific future actions on it across distance and institutional boundaries.

For the Government Sanskrit manuscript survey, the actions include:

- identify;
- transliterate;
- classify;
- estimate extent;
- distinguish support/script;
- locate;
- contact custodian;
- assess accuracy/peculiarity;
- select for copying/acquisition/comparison.

### Why this term helps

It avoids treating catalogue fields as neutral mirrors of objects. A field exists because someone anticipates an operation.

This gives the article a way to compare apparently different metadata systems:

- Sanskrit manuscript survey form;
- Hodgson manuscript lists;
- zoological specimen tickets;
- drawing numbers;
- box/bundle lists;
- route tables;
- receipts.

The comparison should be field-by-field and operation-by-operation rather than metaphorical.

---

## 11. New mechanism: metadata checksum / completeness review

The Government critique also suggests a second concept:

**metadata completeness is relative to the operation the record is expected to support.**

A notice may accurately name a manuscript yet fail as a discovery tool if location/owner are missing.

Therefore code separately:

- `identity_complete`;
- `material_description_complete`;
- `custody_address_complete`;
- `evaluation_complete`;
- `actionability_for_stated_use`.

This avoids binary `complete/incomplete` judgments.

For any historical catalogue/list, distinguish:

1. fields the form asked for;
2. fields the compiler actually filled;
3. fields retained in publication;
4. fields preserved in later catalogues.

This allows direct study of metadata loss through publication and re-cataloguing.

## Bottom line

By 1868–71 the Sanskrit manuscript survey had become a governed data-production system. Government specified a nine-field form joining original-script and Roman titles, intellectual classification, physical extent, material support/script, location/owner and accuracy assessment. It then judged returned notices by whether those fields made manuscripts useful to distant scholars and demanded supplementation when provenance/location information was missing. Metadata here is operational: the record is built to support future finding, contacting, copying, purchasing and comparison. This gives the repo a historically explicit model of how an object becomes actionable after separation from the person who inspected it.
