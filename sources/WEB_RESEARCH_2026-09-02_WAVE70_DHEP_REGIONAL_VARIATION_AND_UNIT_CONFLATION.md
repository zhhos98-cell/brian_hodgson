# Web/archive research 2026-09-02 — Wave 70
## `Dhep` is a real Nepali paper unit, but its numerical depth is not a universal lexical constant

Date: 2026-09-02

Status: active paper-history mainline. This wave **supersedes the universal numerical reading of `dhep` in Wave 69 without deleting Wave 69 as research history**.

Wave 69 correctly recovered a real Nepali paper-count vocabulary and strongly linked Cambridge's 1843 catalogue transcription `ḍhyāp` to Nepali `ढेप / dhep`. Its error was narrower: it treated the late-twentieth-century `10 tau = 1 dhep` relation as if the numerical depth of the package could be generalized across Nepali paper practice.

New direct evidence requires a stricter model.

## Executive result

Three distinct evidential states now exist for the word `dhep` in Nepalese handmade-paper contexts.

### A. Ten-sheet convention — directly documented and lexically stabilized

Bhupahari Paudel, *Ancient Nepal* no.103 (Dec. 1987–Jan. 1988), printed p.33:

`10 tau = 1 dhep`

`20 dhep = 1 kori`

`1 kori = 200 tau`.

Modern Nepali lexicography independently defines:

`ढेप = दस ताउ नेपाली कागतको ठेली वा खात`

and `ताउ` as the sheet/folio-level paper unit.

Thus a **ten-sheet dhep convention is strongly documented**.

### B. Twenty-sheet convention — directly observed in western Nepal fieldwork

Griha Man Singh, `Archival Documents And Its Conservation & Restoration (Civil Servant Records 2001 B.S. Darsan Bhet)`, *Ancient Nepal* no.144 (March 2000), pp.13–27, includes an author's field study conducted in 2054–2055 B.S. / 1998–99 on handmade papermaking in **Myagdi, Parbat and Baglung**.

On printed p.16, under `Drying and folding`, the article directly states that:

- dried sheets are removed from the mould;
- **20 sheets** are tied together with thin bast strips;
- this single bundle is called **one Dhep**.

The same page specifies that raw material was collected from Ruma and Barangja in Myagdi and walked for about six hours from Myagdi district bazar.

This is not an OCR artifact. The printed page image has been directly inspected.

Therefore:

`WEST_NEPAL_FIELD_DHEP = 20 SHEETS DIRECT`.

### C. Two-hundred-sheet `dhep` — directly printed in a later NAST synthesis, but evidentially weaker as a production observation

A Nepal Academy of Science and Technology final report on indigenous technology (Jestha 2070 B.S., c.2013), in its Nepalese handmade-paper section, states:

- dried sheets are folded and made into bundles called `dhep`;
- `one dhep usually contains 200 pieces of paper sheets`.

The page image has been directly inspected.

However, the report's foreword explicitly says it relied **more on secondary sources**, with field visits mainly around Kathmandu Valley, and its paper section itself is a broad national synthesis rather than a locality-specific production observation.

Because `200 sheets` is exactly the quantity independently assigned to **one kori** in Paudel's hierarchy, the 2013 statement must currently be coded as:

`DHEP_200 = DIRECT_PRINTED_SECONDARY_SYNTHESIS / POSSIBLE_UNIT_LEVEL_CONFLATION`.

Do not silently correct it; do not promote it to a demonstrated workshop convention either.

## Revised conclusion

The strongest current model is:

**`dhep` is securely a Nepali handmade-paper bundle/count term, but its numerical contents must be treated as historically and locally contextual rather than supplied by the word alone.**

At minimum:

- `10 sheets = one dhep` is a strongly documented traditional/lexical convention;
- `20 sheets = one dhep` is a directly observed western-Nepal production convention in 1998–99;
- `200 sheets = one dhep` is a direct later printed statement whose evidential context makes unit conflation with `kori` a live possibility.

Mechanisms:

- `LOCAL_UNIT_NAME_DOES_NOT_FIX_PACKAGE_DEPTH`
- `PAPER_METROLOGY_CAN_VARY_BY_LOCALITY_WORKSHOP_OR_REPORTING_TRADITION`
- `LEXICAL_STANDARDIZATION_CAN_HIDE_PRACTICE_VARIATION`
- `SECONDARY_TECHNICAL_SYNTHESIS_CAN_COLLAPSE_ADJACENT_UNIT_LEVELS`
- `QUANTITY_PROVENANCE_REQUIRES_LOCALITY_DATE_AND_OBSERVATION_CONTEXT`.

## 1. Ten-sheet evidence remains strong, but must be bounded

Wave 69's sources remain valid evidence for a ten-sheet convention.

### Paudel 1987–88

Article:

Bhupahari Paudel, `लोप्रा (नोप्रा?) का कागते`, *Ancient Nepal* no.103, Dec. 1987–Jan. 1988, pp.23–40.

PDF:
https://d1i1jdw69xsqx0.cloudfront.net/digitalhimalaya/collections/journals/ancientnepal/pdf/ancient_nepal_103_06.pdf

Printed p.33 gives the hierarchy:

`10 tau -> 1 dhep -> 20 dhep -> 1 kori -> 200 tau`.

The same section uses `kori` for paper pricing and worker remuneration and describes paper sent to presses, stationery shops and offices in Kathmandu.

This is best coded as:

`TEN_SHEET_DHEP = DIRECT_DESCRIPTION_OF_TRADITIONAL_FACTORY/MARKET_PRACTICE`.

It is not automatically:

`ALL_NEPAL_ALL_PERIODS_DHEP = 10 SHEETS`.

### Lexicography

Modern Nepali dictionary entry:

`ढेप — दस ताउ नेपाली कागतको ठेली वा खात`.

Searchable lexical page:
https://ne.wiktionary.org/wiki/नेपाली_शब्दसमूह_२७_ढ

A Nepali-English dictionary tradition likewise glosses `dhep/ḍhepa` as ten sheets of Nepalese paper.

These sources show lexical stabilization of the ten-sheet value.

They do not override direct production evidence from another locality.

Mechanism:

`DICTIONARY_VALUE_IS_STANDARDIZED_LEXICAL_EVIDENCE_NOT_A_UNIVERSAL_WORKSHOP_RULE`.

## 2. Griha Man Singh 2000 supplies a direct locality-specific counterexample

Article identification:

Griha Man Singh, `Archival Documents And Its Conservation & Restoration (Civil Servant Records 2001 B.S. Darsan Bhet)`, *Ancient Nepal* no.144, March 2000, pp.13–27.

Digital Himalaya article PDF:
https://d1i1jdw69xsqx0.cloudfront.net/digitalhimalaya/collections/journals/ancientnepal/pdf/ancient_nepal_144_02.pdf

Digital Himalaya issue/index identifies the author and article; the *Ancient Nepal* cumulative index dates no.144 to March 2000.

Printed p.16 explicitly frames the technical account as:

`Author's field study (2054–2055 B.S.) (1998–1999) on paper making in hilly region of west Nepal (Myagdi, Parbat and Baglung)`.

It then states under `Drying and folding` that twenty dried sheets are tied with thin bast strips and the bundle is called one Dhep.

This is unusually strong evidence because it joins:

- named locality;
- named study period;
- observed production procedure;
- package-making operation;
- sheet count;
- local bundle name.

Material sequence:

`single dried sheet`
-> `twenty-sheet stack`
-> `thin bast strip fastening`
-> `one dhep`.

The counting unit is therefore not merely abstract arithmetic. It is a **material bundling operation**.

Mechanisms:

- `COUNT_UNIT_IS_PHYSICALLY_MADE_BY_BUNDLING`
- `BUNDLE_DEPTH_IS_A_WORKSHOP_OPERATION`
- `METROLOGY_IS_EMBODIED_IN_PACKAGING_PRACTICE`.

This matters for the article: a paper unit can be produced after manufacture by physically grouping sheets.

## 3. The NAST 200-sheet statement is a different evidence class

Source:

Nepal Academy of Science and Technology, final report submitted to the Ministry of Science, Technology & Environment, Jestha 2070 B.S., authors Vijay Singh, Hari Bhandari, Binod Prasad Shah and Subash Duwadi.

PDF:
https://nastlibrary.nast.org.np/elibrary/filestore/4/8_60773a99d769a3b/48_f84162f4583c2e3.pdf

Paper section, printed p.32 / PDF page 39, directly states:

- dried pulp sheets are folded into bundles called `dhep`;
- one `dhep` usually contains 200 paper sheets.

Direct visual inspection confirms that the page itself says `200`.

But source control matters.

The report states in its front matter that:

- it relied more on secondary sources;
- field visits for current status were confined mainly to Kathmandu Valley;
- it synthesizes indigenous technologies across Nepal.

It is therefore not equivalent to Griha Man Singh's locality-specific western field observation.

Additionally:

`200 sheets = 1 kori`

is independently established in the Paudel/lexical hierarchy.

Therefore the 2013 statement creates at least two live interpretations:

1. some production/marketing contexts really used `dhep` for a 200-sheet bundle;
2. the report collapsed or mislabeled the `kori` level as `dhep` while synthesizing secondary material.

Current code:

`NAST_DHEP_200 = DIRECT_TEXT / PRACTICE_STATUS_UNRESOLVED / UNIT_CONFLATION_CANDIDATE_HIGH`.

Do not choose between the two without its underlying source or a matching workshop observation.

Mechanisms:

- `DIRECT_PRINTED_STATEMENT_CAN_STILL_BE_SECONDARY_FOR_PRACTICE`
- `SOURCE_TYPE_AND_TEXTUAL_DIRECTNESS_ARE_SEPARATE`.

## 4. Revised interpretation of Hodgson's 1843 f.117

`MSS EUR HODGSON/12 f.117` remains the central nineteenth-century object.

Cambridge catalogue:

- bill made out to Nathaniel Wallich for Nepali paper and binding material;
- signed by Hodgson at Nepal Residency;
- bill itself on machine-made paper;
- catalogue romanizes the local paper unit as `ḍhyāp`;
- catalogue gloss says `10 sheets in one bundle`.

The lexical identity remains strong:

`Cambridge ḍhyāp <-> Nepali ढेप / dhep = VERY_HIGH_CANDIDATE`.

But Wave 70 changes the meaning of the ten-sheet gloss.

Correct state:

`F117_CATALOGUE_DHEP_DEPTH = 10 SHEETS AS CATALOGUED`

not:

`NEPALI_DHEP_UNIVERSALLY = 10 SHEETS`.

The 1843 catalogue/object pairing is potentially valuable precisely because it may document a **Kathmandu/Residency-context ten-sheet convention** earlier than the modern sources.

That historical localization cannot be completed until the original bill is seen.

Questions for the original image:

- Does the bill itself contain a local-script term or only numerals?
- Is `ḍhyāp` the catalogue editor's expansion/romanization?
- Is `10 sheets` written on the bill, inferred by the cataloguer, or supplied from external knowledge?
- Are multiple paper grades counted in different units?
- Does `kori` also occur?
- Do Rs 10/- and 14/- apply per dhep, per kori, or per another aggregate?

Mechanism:

`CATALOGUE_GLOSS_HAS_PROVENANCE_TOO`.

## 5. This strengthens the paper-history argument rather than weakening it

The conflict is analytically productive.

An abstract history of paper might normalize every bundle into a sheet count and discard the local term.

A material paper history has to ask how the bundle was physically made and what social/production system made its number meaningful.

Current comparison:

### England / Company

`sheet -> quire -> ream -> chest`

with quire depth and ream structure tied to wrapping, outside quires, quality sampling and stationer accountability.

### Nepal

`sheet/tau -> dhep -> kori` is a documented lexical/accounting hierarchy in one tradition, while observed western practice can make a twenty-sheet dhep.

### Kashmir / North India

`dasta` and `takhta` likewise show regional package-depth variation.

Therefore:

**paper quantity is not a universal number waiting to be converted. It is produced by local bundling, packaging, pricing and accounting operations.**

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_METROLOGY_IS_MATERIAL_PRACTICE`
- `PACKAGE_UNIT_IS_NOT_ONLY_A_NUMBER`
- `NORMALIZATION_CAN_ERASE_REGIONAL_PRODUCTION_HISTORY`.

## Source-control rules

1. Wave 69's identification of `dhep` as a real Nepali paper unit remains valid.
2. Supersede only its universal numerical claim.
3. Treat `10 sheets` as a strong documented convention, not a pan-Nepal invariant.
4. Treat Griha Man Singh's `20 sheets = one Dhep` as direct locality-specific field evidence.
5. Treat the NAST `200 sheets = one dhep` statement as direct text but secondary/synthetic evidence for practice; keep unit-conflation with `kori` live.
6. Never silently correct a source's numerical unit because another source disagrees.
7. Preserve `catalogue transcription`, `normalized lexeme`, `reported sheet depth`, and `historian normalization` as separate fields.
8. Do not normalize f.117 to a total sheet count until its original account layout is recovered.
9. English `quire`, Nepali `dhep`, `kori`, Kashmir/North Indian `dasta`, and `takhta` remain non-equivalent local systems.

## Immediate next actions

2. Search pre-1900 Nepali accounts, legal records and dictionaries for `ढेप`, `ताउ`, `कोरी` in paper contexts.

7. Revisit f.117 arithmetic only after unit and column structure are controlled.

## Bottom line

`Dhep` has become a better historical object by ceasing to be a fixed conversion factor. The word securely belongs to Nepalese handmade-paper practice, but the material bundle associated with it is documented at different numerical depths: ten sheets in a traditional/lexically stabilized hierarchy, twenty sheets in direct 1998–99 western-Nepal field observation, and two hundred sheets in a later national technical synthesis whose secondary character makes conflation with the adjacent `kori` level plausible. For Hodgson's 1843 bill, the Cambridge ten-sheet gloss remains valuable, but it should now be read as evidence for a particular accounting convention whose locality, historical continuity and original graphic form still need to be recovered. Paper metrology is therefore part of paper history: quantities were made by bundling practices before historians converted them into numbers.
