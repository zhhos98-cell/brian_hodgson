# Matched-work support comparison — Hodgson's Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa / Ekallavīra witnesses

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred matched-work note. This is a comparison of independent witnesses of the same work acquired by Hodgson, not yet a demonstrated direct exemplar-copy pair.

## Executive result

The Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra gives the paper project its first strong `same work / different support` comparison inside Hodgson's own collecting programme.

Modern textual scholarship states that Hodgson procured **at least three copies** of the Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra and sent two to England, where they survive respectively in the Royal Asiatic Society and the Bodleian Library.

The two English witnesses are materially different:

### Royal Asiatic Society, Hodgson MS 46

Cowell & Eggeling 1875:

- `Ekallaviratantram [Chandamaharoshanatantram]`;
- 25 paṭalas;
- **50 palm leaves**;
- **8 1/2 × 3 in.**;
- 7 or 8 lines per page;
- described as `Old`.

Current RAS online description:

- palm leaf;
- 7 or 8 lines on each folio;
- 25 paṭalas;
- acquired by Hodgson in Nepal;
- gifted to RAS 1835–36;
- modern page assigns `C19th?` while explicitly noting that Cowell & Eggeling called it `old`.

Recent specialist scholarship (Grimes & Szántó):

- Royal Asiatic Society London, Hodgson no. 46;
- **46 folios**;
- palm leaf;
- incomplete;
- undated;
- tentatively ca. 14th century.

### Bodleian Library, Hodgson witness 2

Hunter's 1881 consolidated Hodgson catalogue:

- `Kallavīratantra, or Khaṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra`;
- **61 folios**;
- Nepal letters;
- **paper**;
- explicitly identified as the same work called `Ekallasīratantra` in Cowell & Eggeling's RAS list.

Modern specialist scholarship refers to this as the Bodleian manuscript, `Hodgson 2`.

Its physical dimensions remain unresolved in the current pass.

## 1. The comparison is stronger than title coincidence

Hunter's consolidated catalogue directly links the Bodleian paper witness to Cowell & Eggeling's RAS title variant.

Modern scholarship independently says Hodgson procured at least three copies of the Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra and sent two to England, to the RAS and Bodleian respectively.

Therefore the secure relation is:

`same textual work + same Hodgson acquisition programme + different surviving support states`.

Mechanism:

`MATCHED_WORK_DIFFERENT_SUPPORT_WITNESSES`.

## 2. Do not call this a direct copy pair

The current evidence does **not** show:

- RAS palm-leaf manuscript -> Bodleian paper manuscript;
- Bodleian paper manuscript -> RAS palm-leaf manuscript;
- both copied directly from one identified exemplar.

Modern scholarship says Hodgson procured multiple copies, which makes independent acquisition/copying entirely possible.

The correct status is:

`MATCHED_WORK_SUPPORT_COMPARISON`

rather than:

`DIRECT_SUPPORT_CONVERSION_PAIR`.

This distinction is essential for the paper article.

## 3. The RAS witness may preserve a leaf-count survival discrepancy

Cowell & Eggeling in 1875 describe the RAS manuscript as **50 palm leaves**.

Recent textual scholarship describes the same `Royal Asiatic Society London, Hodgson no. 46` as **46 folios, incomplete**.

If both counts are accurate and refer to the same physical witness in comparable count units, at least four leaves recorded in 1875 are no longer present in the modern scholarly state.

Possible explanations:

1. `POST_1875_LEAF_LOSS`;
2. different counting conventions;
3. cataloguer/transcription error in 1875;
4. modern bibliographical shorthand/error;
5. detached leaves surviving outside the currently consulted sequence.

Do not yet state historical loss as fact.

Mechanism:

`HISTORICAL_LEAF_COUNT_DISCREPANCY`.

This is high priority for direct RAS object inspection because it could document material attrition within institutional custody.

## 4. The support difference is already secure even without Bodleian dimensions

RAS:

`palm leaf`.

Bodleian:

`paper`.

This matters because the same text circulated in materially distinct witness forms inside Hodgson's collection.

A textual title therefore does not imply one stable material format.

Mechanism:

`TEXTUAL_IDENTITY_SUPPORT_PLURALITY`.

The paper article can now make a stronger claim than the aggregate format census:

**at least one specific work acquired by Hodgson survives in both palm-leaf and paper witnesses.**

## 5. Leaf count differs substantially across support states

RAS 1875 state:

- 50 palm leaves.

Bodleian:

- 61 paper folios.

The same work therefore occupies different leaf cardinalities in the two witnesses.

This difference could arise from:

- different dimensions;
- line density;
- scribal abbreviation/expansion;
- textual completeness;
- textual variants;
- foliation conventions;
- missing leaves in one witness.

Do not attribute the 50 -> 61 difference to support until dimensions and textual completeness are compared.

Mechanism:

`SAME_WORK_DIFFERENT_MATERIAL_CARDINALITY`.

## 6. The RAS date metadata are internally stratified

Three temporal assessments now coexist:

- Cowell & Eggeling 1875: `Old`;
- current RAS web metadata: `C19th?` while citing Cowell & Eggeling's `old`;
- recent specialist textual scholarship: `ca. 14th c.`.

This is not merely a dating problem. It shows that catalogue metadata itself has successive interpretive states.

Mechanism:

`CATALOGUE_DATE_STATE_STRATIGRAPHY`.

For the paper project, support identification is stable (`palm leaf`) while age attribution is unstable.

## 7. High-value next test: recover Bodleian dimensions

The object-level support conversion question now depends disproportionately on one missing tuple:

`Bodleian Hodgson 2 -> long dimension + short dimension + line count + page architecture`.

If Bodleian paper is appreciably wider than 8.5 × 3 in., it would support the corpus-level `SHORT_AXIS_EXPANSION` result at matched-work level.

If it is approximately 8.5 × 3 in., that would instead show strong geometry persistence across support change.

If it is narrower/longer, that would falsify any simple directional model.

This is therefore a genuinely discriminating measurement, not a decorative detail.

## 8. Search path for the Bodleian object

Historical references identify the work in the Bodleian and modern scholarship calls it `Hodgson 2`.

Next sources to inspect:

## 9. Comparative witness context

The text survives in many manuscripts. Modern scholarship notes that George had access to sixteen witnesses and still did not exhaust the corpus; it also identifies other palm-leaf witnesses in Kathmandu and Göttingen.

This is useful control evidence:

`paper vs palm leaf` should not be treated as a simple old/new binary for the work as a whole.

The Hodgson pair matters specifically because both witnesses belong to the same early nineteenth-century collecting programme and were dispatched into British institutions.

## 10. Article-level formulation

**The support difference can be observed at the level of a single textual work. Hodgson acquired at least three copies of the Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra and sent two to England. The Royal Asiatic Society witness is palm leaf, recorded in 1875 as fifty leaves measuring 8 1/2 by 3 inches, while the Bodleian witness is a sixty-one-folio paper manuscript explicitly identified as the same work. These are not yet demonstrably exemplar and copy, but they provide a controlled same-work comparison in which textual identity remains stable while support and leaf cardinality differ. The missing Bodleian dimensions now become a decisive test of whether paper changed the manuscript's proportions or merely its material substrate.**

A secondary material-survival sentence, only after verification:

**The RAS witness also raises a possible custody-history problem: Cowell and Eggeling counted fifty palm leaves in 1875, whereas recent specialists describe forty-six folios and call the manuscript incomplete. Direct object inspection can determine whether this records post-catalogue leaf loss or merely incompatible counting descriptions.**

## Bottom line

The Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa witnesses move the paper project beyond aggregate support statistics. We now have a named work inside Hodgson's own collecting programme that survives once on palm leaf and once on paper. The comparison is not yet a direct conversion pair, but it is controlled enough to make Bodleian dimensions and page architecture a high-value falsification test. At the same time, the RAS witness may preserve a separate history of material loss between the 1875 catalogue state and modern scholarly use.
