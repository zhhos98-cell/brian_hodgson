## Finding 18 — the Darjeeling experimental manufactory appears to have closed on 16 April 1842

Evidence status: **STRONG SECONDARY ARCHIVAL EXTRACTION / primary Political O.C. still to be inspected directly.**

A. F. M. Abdul Ali, `The Daphne Paper of Nepal` (published in *Bengal Past & Present* in 1944 and again in the *B. C. Law Volume*, 1945), reconstructed the nineteenth-century paper history from contemporary Foreign and Political Department records. Existing repo Wave 77 records his statement that the Darjeeling manufactory was worked until **16 April 1842** and then closed because the arrangement was uneconomical unless machinery/process conditions changed.

Repo control:
- `sources/WEB_RESEARCH_2026-09-03_WAVE77_1837_NEPAL_PAPER_PROCUREMENT_NETWORK_AND_POLITICAL_OC_ANCHORS.md`

Associated dated archival sequence in Abdul Ali's reconstruction:
- 25 March 1842 — Hodgson sends twenty-four packages of Nepalese paper pulp toward Calcutta;
- **16 April 1842 — Darjeeling manufactory closes under the existing economic arrangement**;
- 21 April 1842 — Campbell writes to G. A. Bushby on the paper business;
- 11 May 1842 — `Political O.C. No. 70` archival pointer associated with the April correspondence / closure-economics phase.

Another controlled archival address remains:
- 29 November 1841 — `Political O.C. No. 147`, O'Shaughnessy's bleaching report.

### Evidence hierarchy

1. underlying Foreign/Political `Political O.C.` record — **PRIMARY, NOT YET DIRECTLY INSPECTED IN THIS WAVE**;
2. Abdul Ali 1944/45 — **secondary archival extraction with precise date/number retrieval pointers**;
3. P. K. Gode's later summary of Ali — **secondary-of-secondary corroboration**;
4. later Darjeeling Gazetteer claim that the factory supplied writing and packing paper `for some years` — **retrospective and now in direct tension with the archival-extraction chronology**.

### Consequence

Do not write the Darjeeling factory as continuing for several years unless direct contemporaneous evidence overrides Abdul Ali's archival extraction.

The current best working chronology is:

`1841 makers moved to Darjeeling -> experimental manufacture -> continuation/chemical and industrial tests -> Darjeeling arrangement judged uneconomical -> closure 16 Apr 1842`

while, separately:

`Nepal paper remains useful and continues to circulate -> Serampore experiments -> 1849 Hooker procurement/field use -> 1851 Great Exhibition category`.

This separation is article-level important. **The closure of one relocated manufacturing arrangement did not equal the obsolescence of Nepal paper or the collapse of Himalayan manufacture.**

It also weakens any machine-centred teleology: a failed/closed Darjeeling production scheme and an unattractive Serampore machine route coexisted with continued regional manufacture and scientific demand for Nepal-made paper.

## Finding 19 — `Political O.C. 11 May 1842, No.70` is now a high-value precise retrieval target

Evidence status: **ARCHIVAL ADDRESS via Abdul Ali; underlying record not yet directly inspected.**

Because the exact closure date, Campbell's 21 April letter to G. A. Bushby, and the later 11 May Political O.C. pointer cluster tightly, `11 May 1842, No.70` is now more valuable than broad searches for `Darjeeling paper` in the National Archives of India.

Questions to test against the underlying consultation:

- Does it explicitly state that the Darjeeling manufactory had been closed on 16 April?
- What exactly made the arrangement uneconomical: wages, carriage, fuel/ash, raw material, labour retention, product price, bleaching, machinery, or another factor?
- Does it name any of the five papermakers or specify which were retained/dismissed?
- Does it mention the two Lepcha trainees or any plan for local continuation after the Nepalese makers left?
- Does it distinguish closure of the experimental establishment from continued making by trained workers?

This is now a narrow archival target capable of changing both actor reconstruction and the economic ending of Case 2/3.

## Finding 20 — retrospective `for some years` continuity claim is now downgraded further

Evidence status: **CONFLICTING RETROSPECTIVE CLAIM.**

The later Darjeeling Gazetteer says Campbell's factory supplied strong writing and packing paper for some years before cheap plains paper displaced it. That statement is now not merely uncorroborated; it conflicts with Abdul Ali's record-based closure date of 16 April 1842.

Article rule:

- do not use the Gazetteer claim as narrative fact;
- mention the conflict only if useful for an afterlife/remembering point;
- prefer the Political O.C. chronology once the underlying file is inspected.
