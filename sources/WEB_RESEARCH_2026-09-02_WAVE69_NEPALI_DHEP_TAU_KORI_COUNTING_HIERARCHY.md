# Web/archive research 2026-09-02 — Wave 69
## The Nepali paper-count hierarchy recovered: `tau -> dhep -> kori` and the narrowing of the 1843 Hodgson bill problem

Date: 2026-09-02

Status: active paper-history mainline; **supersedes one evidential state in Wave 68 without deleting Wave 68 as research history**.

Wave 68 correctly treated the Cambridge catalogue's `ḍhyāp` and `10 sheets` gloss cautiously because no independent lexical confirmation had yet been found. That negative state is now superseded.

## Executive result

Independent Nepali lexical and paper-industry evidence strongly validates a traditional counting hierarchy:

`1 ताउ (tāu) = 1 sheet/folio of paper`

`10 ताउ = 1 ढेप (ḍhep / ḍhepa)`

`20 ढेप = 1 कोरी (korī)`

therefore

`1 कोरी = 200 ताउ = 200 sheets`.

The strongest source is not merely a modern dictionary. Bhupahari Paudel's article `लोप्रा (नोप्रा?) का कागते`, published in *Ancient Nepal* no.103 (December 1987–January 1988), directly states on printed p.33, in a section on a Nepali paper factory:

`10 tau = 1 dhep; 20 dhep = 1 kori; 1 kori = 200 tau`.

The same page treats `kori` as the factory's pricing and labour-remuneration unit.

Modern Nepali lexicography independently converges:

- `ढेप` — a stack/bundle of ten `ताउ` of Nepali paper;
- `ताउ` — a sheet/folio of paper;
- `कोरी` — a group of twenty `ढेप` of paper.

A Nepali-English dictionary source also glosses `ḍhepa` as a bundle/quire of Nepalese paper consisting of ten sheets.

This materially changes the interpretation of `MSS EUR HODGSON/12 f.117`.

Cambridge transcribes the 1843 bill as:

`1,012 bundles (ḍhyāp) of Nepali paper (10 sheets in one bundle ...)`.

The ten-sheet gloss is no longer an isolated catalogue assertion. It closely matches the independently documented Nepali unit **ढेप / ḍhep(a)**.

Current identification state:

`CAMBRIDGE ḍhyāp <-> NEPALI ढेप / ḍhep(a) = STRONG LEXICAL-STRUCTURAL MATCH`

but

`EXACT 1843 ORIGINAL ORTHOGRAPHY/ROMANIZATION = UNRESOLVED`.

The unresolved problem in f.117 has therefore shifted.

It is no longer primarily:

`does a ten-sheet Nepali paper unit exist?`

It is now:

`what exactly does the original account write, how are quantity/grade/rate columns structured, and why does the catalogue's normalized arithmetic fail?`

Core mechanisms:

- `LOCAL_PAPER_COUNTING_HIERARCHY_INDEPENDENTLY_VALIDATED`
- `CATALOGUE_ROMANIZATION_CAN_OBSCURE_RECOGNIZABLE_LOCAL_UNIT`
- `LEXICAL_TRIANGULATION_CAN_RECOVER_ACCOUNTING_STRUCTURE`
- `TRANSLATION_GLOSS_NOT_UNIT_EQUIVALENCE`
- `QUANTITY_PROVENANCE_REQUIRES_LAYOUT_AND_UNIT_HIERARCHY`
- `ARITHMETIC_CONFLICT_MOVES_FROM_UNIT_SIZE_TO_ACCOUNT_STRUCTURE`.

## 2. Nepali dictionary: `ढेप` is specifically a ten-sheet Nepali-paper bundle

A searchable Nepali lexical corpus gives:

`ढेप — दस ताउ नेपाली कागतको ठेली वा खात`.

In controlled paraphrase:

`ढेप = a stack/bundle of ten tau of Nepali paper`.

Source:
https://ne.wiktionary.org/wiki/नेपाली_शब्दसमूह_२७_ढ

This is important because the definition is paper-specific rather than a generic classifier meaning later applied to paper.

The English-language Nepali dictionary tradition independently gives:

`Ḍhepa (ढेप): a quire of Nepalese paper consisting of 10 sheets`.

Search-accessible source:
https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/dhepa

The latter derives from a modern Nepali-English dictionary and should not be treated as nineteenth-century lexical evidence. It nevertheless independently confirms the same unit value.

Evidence class:

`MODERN_LEXICOGRAPHIC_CONFIRMATION_OF_TRADITIONAL_PAPER_UNIT`.

## 3. `ताउ` is the sheet-level unit

Multiple Nepali-English lexical sources define:

`ताउ (tāu) = sheet of paper / paper folio`.

Examples:

- Nepali-English dictionary search result: `tau — n. sheet of paper`;
- Nepali-Esperanto dictionary: `ताउ — folio (de papero)`.

Sources:
https://paichl.cz/cestopisy/Nepal_2008/nep_eng.pdf
https://javabog.dk/filer/nepala-vortaro/versio118-finita-porpresi.pdf

A Hindi-English-Nepali dictionary likewise gives `ताव` with the Nepali `ताउ` among senses including `a sheet of paper`.

Thus the lower-level relation is independently controlled:

`TAU = SHEET/FOLIO UNIT`.

This makes Paudel's equation structurally transparent:

`10 sheets -> dhep`.

## 4. `कोरी` supplies the next paper-count level

Nepali lexical evidence defines `कोरी` in its counting sense as one score / group of twenty and specifically:

`बीस ढेप कागतको ठेली वा समूह`

that is:

`a bundle/group of twenty dhep of paper`.

Source:
https://ne.wiktionary.org/wiki/नेपाली_शब्दसमूह_१३_केकैकोकौ

Combined with the directly controlled `dhep = 10 tau`, the hierarchy gives:

`1 kori = 20 dhep = 200 tau/sheets`.

Paudel's paper-factory description independently prints exactly the same 200-tau total.

Modern Nepali handmade-paper industry literature continues to use:

`1 kori = 200 sheets/tau`.

For example, contemporary production-cost studies describe paper being sorted and packed in `1 kori (200 sheets)` lots and calculate labour/cost per `tau` and per `kori`.

This later continuity is useful but secondary to the direct Paudel + lexical convergence.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_COUNTING_HIERARCHY_HAS_NESTED_DECIMAL_AND_SCORE_LEVELS`
- `KORI_FUNCTIONS_AS_PRODUCTION_MARKET_AND_WAGE_UNIT`.

## 5. Why Cambridge's `ḍhyāp` now looks like a romanization problem rather than a different unit

Cambridge's f.117 catalogue text:

`1,012 bundles (ḍhyāp) of Nepali paper (10 sheets in one bundle ...)`.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol012.html

Independent Nepali evidence:

`ढेप / ḍhep(a) = ten sheets of Nepali paper`.

The semantic structure is almost exact:

- both are paper-specific;
- both are local Nepali units;
- both denote a ten-sheet group;
- both occur inside paper procurement/accounting contexts.

The spelling/transliteration does not match exactly:

`Cambridge: ḍhyāp`

versus

`lexical sources: ढेप / ḍhep(a)`.

Until the original f.117 is visible, several explanations remain possible:

1. Cambridge romanized a handwritten local term unusually;
2. a vowel/consonant was misread in the catalogue transcription;
3. the bill used a historical/local pronunciation or spelling variant;
4. the Cambridge gloss derives from cataloguer knowledge of the `dhep` unit even if the romanization is imperfect;
5. less likely, `ḍhyāp` is a distinct term with the same ten-sheet value.

Current controlled state:

`UNIT_IDENTITY = VERY_HIGH_CANDIDATE / ORIGINAL_GRAPHIC_FORM_UNREAD`.

Do **not** silently replace `ḍhyāp` with `ढेप` when transcribing Cambridge. Preserve both layers:

`catalogue_transcription = ḍhyāp`

`normalized_unit_candidate = ढेप / ḍhep(a)`.

Mechanism:

`CATALOGUE_TRANSCRIPTION_AND_NORMALIZED_LOCAL_TERM_ARE_SEPARATE_FIELDS`.

## 6. Important translation guardrail: English `quire` does not make dhep equivalent to an English quire

The Nepali-English dictionary glosses `dhep` as a `quire` of ten sheets.

But Wave 68 directly controlled the English/European technical paper unit:

- Moxon: a ream has twenty quires;
- a quire can have twenty-four or twenty-five sheets depending on the stock;
- two outside quires have a special cording/cassie function.

Therefore the English word `quire` in a dictionary definition of `dhep` is a **functional translation meaning a small paper bundle**, not a quantitative equivalence with the English paper-trade quire.

Never write:

`dhep = quire = 24/25 sheets`.

Correct representation:

`Nepali dhep = 10 tau/sheets`

while

`English trade quire = historically different unit structure`.

Mechanisms:

- `TRANSLATION_GLOSS_CAN_CREATE_FALSE_METROLOGICAL_EQUIVALENCE`
- `LOCAL_UNIT_VALUE_OVERRIDES_ENGLISH_GLOSS_FOR_QUANTITATIVE_HISTORY`.

## 8. The arithmetic problem is now more sharply localized

The ten-sheet gloss is no longer the main suspect.

Under the recovered hierarchy, f.117 potentially belongs to a local accounting system with at least three levels:

`tau = sheet`

`dhep = 10 tau`

`kori = 20 dhep = 200 tau`.

The catalogue prose may have flattened one or more of these levels.

Possible sources of the arithmetic conflict remain:

- `1,012` may flatten two columns, a compound quantity, or fractional notation;
- two paper grades may have different quantities/rates;
- Rs 10/- and Rs 14/- may apply to a higher batch unit rather than each dhep;
- ditto marks or unit headings may have been lost;
- line-item totals may have been merged into prose.

The newly recovered `kori` level creates a specific inspection question:

**does the original f.117 use both dhep and kori, or price one unit while counting another?**

This is a retrieval target, not a reconstruction claim.

Do not use modern or 1987 `kori` prices to back-calculate the 1843 bill.

Mechanisms:

- `UNIT_HIERARCHY_CAN_BE_LOST_WHEN_ACCOUNT_TABLE_IS_PROSIFIED`
- `PRICE_UNIT_MAY_DIFFER_FROM_COUNT_UNIT`.

## 9. Comparison with Kashmir and North India becomes cleaner

Wave 68 compared regional paper-count systems:

### Nepal

`tau -> dhep -> kori`

with:

`10 tau = 1 dhep`

`20 dhep = 1 kori`.

### Kashmir 1831

Jacquemont's observed paper manufacture: twenty-four finished sheets rolled together as one `dastā`.

### North Indian glossary, 1853/1877

Carnegy: a paper `dasta` contains twenty-four `takhtas`, with a takhta itself consisting of two, four or more sheets.

The conclusion is stronger now:

**different South Asian paper economies organized finished sheets into different nested counting units.**

No common English gloss (`bundle`, `quire`, `ream`) should flatten those systems.

## 11. Paper-history significance

The recovered unit hierarchy makes the 1843 procurement bill more than a source for `how much paper`.

It places Hodgson's manuscript-production economy inside a specifically local material metrology.

Paper reached the Residency not only as a substance or a flat sheet but as counted objects:

`tau`
-> bundled into `dhep`
-> potentially aggregated into `kori`
-> priced, purchased, copied upon, bound and shipped.

On the Company/imported side, paper was simultaneously organized through:

`sheet -> quire -> ream -> chest`.

These are not translations of each other. They are different material infrastructures governing how paper could be priced, handled, sampled and audited.

Article-level formulation:

**Hodgson's paper world sat between counting regimes. Company paper arrived through reams, quires and marked chests whose internal structure mattered to quality control; Nepali handmade paper was locally reckoned by tau, dhep and kori. The meeting of imported and local paper in the Residency was therefore also a meeting of material metrologies.**

## Bottom line

The local counting unit behind Hodgson's 1843 Nepali-paper bill is no longer lexically isolated. A directly inspected 1987 account of traditional Nepali papermaking states that ten `tau` make one `dhep`, twenty `dhep` make one `kori`, and one `kori` contains two hundred `tau`; Nepali dictionaries independently define `tau` as a paper sheet and `dhep` as a ten-sheet bundle. The Cambridge catalogue's `ḍhyāp (10 sheets)` is therefore a very strong structural match to Nepali `ढेप / ḍhep(a)`, although the original 1843 spelling remains unread. The research problem has moved from **unit existence** to **account structure**: quantity columns, grade distinctions, price units and catalogue romanization now have to be recovered from the physical bill.
