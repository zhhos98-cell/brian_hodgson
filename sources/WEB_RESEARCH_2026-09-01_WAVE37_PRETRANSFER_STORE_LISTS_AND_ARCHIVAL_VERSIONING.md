# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 37
## Pre-transfer store lists and the version history of Hodgson's archive itself

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: paper-centred archival-source-control follow-up to Waves 35–36. This wave moves behind Hunter's 1896 reproduction of the 1864 India Office presentation list and reconstructs the earlier inventory/list states preserved in the Royal Asiatic Society archive.

## Executive result

The 1864 India Office manifest was not an isolated catalogue created ex nihilo at the moment of donation.

The Royal Asiatic Society preserves a sequence of Hodgson inventory objects extending from at least 1837 to 1864:

`1837 Residency five-box list`
-> `1842 Kathmandu trunk/basta list`
-> `1844 repacking annotations`
-> `1845 box/drawing inventories`
-> `1852 Darjeeling trunk/store states`
-> `1854–58 List of Lists`
-> `1855 Trunk No.2 paper/drawing list`
-> `1858 private store list`
-> `1864 handwritten India Office donation list + donation letter`
-> `1864 annotated printed donation list`.

Most importantly, the RAS catalogue states explicitly that `BHH/11/14` **appears initially to have been a store list compiled for Hodgson in 1858 and then became the list of manuscripts given to the India Office in 1864**.

Therefore the 1864 transfer ontology has a demonstrable pre-institutional history inside Hodgson's own storage/listing practices.

This does **not yet prove** that every version-family relation visible in the 1864 printed manifest was already structured identically in 1858. Physical comparison of the list states is required.

Core mechanisms:

- `PRIVATE_STORE_LIST_TO_PUBLIC_TRANSFER_LIST`
- `ARCHIVE_ITSELF_HAS_VERSION_HISTORY`
- `CONTAINER_INVENTORY_PRECEDES_INSTITUTIONAL_DONATION`
- `HODGSON_ARCHIVAL_LISTS_ARE_VERSIONED_OBJECTS`
- `MANIFEST_HAS_HANDWRITTEN_AND_PRINTED_STATES`
- `ANNOTATED_PRINTED_MANIFEST_AS_WORKING_STATE`
- `STORE_ONTOLOGY_TO_TRANSFER_ONTOLOGY` — candidate mechanism pending image comparison

## 1. RAS describes listing as an operational practice of storage and transportation

The RAS `BHH/11` series description says Hodgson was a keen compiler of lists, often of specimens, drawings and donations, and notes that such lists were sometimes needed for **transportation or storing** the objects itemised.

RAS archive browse:
https://www.royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/informationobject/browse?collection=673&names=2296&showAdvanced=1&sort=identifier&sortDir=asc&topLod=0

This matters for the paper article because the list is not merely retrospective description.

It is an operational paper object inserted into custody transitions.

A list can:

- define a box/trunk/bundle as a countable unit;
- preserve internal membership;
- record repacking;
- support later recovery;
- carry instructions about future disposal;
- transform private store order into institutional transfer order.

Mechanism:

`LIST_AS_CUSTODY_CONTROL_SURFACE`.

## 2. 1837–1844: five boxes at the Residency and later repacking

RAS `BHH/11/1`:

`List of Buddha Books & drawings, & of Statistics of Nepaul`

Reference:
`GB 891 BHH-BHH/11-BHH/11/1`

Date:
1837–1844.

RAS catalogue:
https://www.royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/list-of-buddha-books-amp-amp-drawings-amp-amp-of-statistics-of-nepaul

The title itself records:

`left in Residency in five Boxes, by B.H.Hodgson, Dec 1st 1837`.

The catalogue states:

- each box is individually listed;
- a sixth package is also listed;
- further notes were later added;
- an additional heading records that the boxes were **repackaged in 1844 to take home**;
- the same list includes an instruction that Hodgson's literary and scientific stores should pass to his father in the event of his death, for disposal to the advantage of Hodgson's reputation.

Material state:

- handwritten;
- one piece;
- four sides.

This is already a **versioned inventory surface**: one list created for a 1837 storage state is later reused to record a 1844 custody transformation.

- `INVENTORY_SURFACE_ACCUMULATES_CUSTODY_STATES`
- `REPACKING_EVENT_WRITTEN_ON_PRIOR_LIST`

## 4. 1844–1852: lists remain attached to repeated geographic/custody moves

The RAS `BHH/11` series contains several inventory states connecting Kathmandu, Canterbury, maritime transport and Darjeeling.

### BHH/11/4 — Canterbury original drawings

`Total of Drawings at Canterbury - my original series, Decr 26, 1844.`

The catalogue describes zoological, ethnographical and architectural drawings at Hodgson's father's home in Canterbury, with a later note saying they were to be shipped with Hodgson in July 1845.

### BHH/11/5 — Buddhist drawings

`List of Buddhist Drawings, February 1845`.

Detailed series inventory with later note:

`Left in my house at Darjeeling Decr 1852`.

### BHH/11/7 — bird and mammal drawings

`Bird Drawings Jan 31, 1845`.

List of species drawings and drawing counts; later note records storage at Darjeeling in December 1852 in a chest of drawers.

### BHH/11/8 — literary boxes

`Memorandum of Boxes Literary 1845`.

The RAS catalogue describes trunks aboard the *Alfred* in 1845 and a later note recording that they were left at Darjeeling in 1852; a scrap separately lists contents of trunks and chests of drawers.

### BHH/11/9 — rough lists to guide another lister

Rough lists of ethnographical and architectural drawings and literary matters, including a `Summary of heads of matters contained in drawings and MSS to guide Khardan in listing them`.

This is especially important because listing itself becomes distributed labour.

The inventory is not only Hodgson describing possessions; it can function as an instruction surface for another person producing a further list.

Mechanism:

`LIST_GENERATES_FURTHER_LISTING_LABOUR`.

Reference:
`GB 891 BHH-BHH/11-BHH/11/11`.

This is directly relevant to the repo's `relational redundancy` theme, but in the paper article it should remain tied to custody/list operations rather than generalized into personality or biography.

## 8. 1858–1864: the private store list becomes the India Office donation list

RAS `BHH/11/14` is now the highest-priority source-control object for the 1864 transfer.

Reference:
`GB 891 BHH-BHH/11-BHH/11/14`.

Direct catalogue page:
https://www.royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/list-of-mss-presented-to-the-india-office

The RAS description states:

> the list seems to have initially been a store list for Hodgson compiled in 1858 but became the list of MSS given to the India Office in 1864.

The file also contains a copy of Hodgson's letter to Sir Charles Wood concerning acceptance of the donation and listing the articles to be given.

Material states:

- manuscript list: handwritten, 2 pieces, 4 sides;
- letter: handwritten, 4 pieces, 13 sides;
- date range: 1858–1864.

This is decisive for the history of the archival ontology.

The transfer list has a **private-store predecessor state**.

Secure claim:

`private store list -> donation list`.

Still unproven:

`1858 store structure == 1864 final printed version-family bundle structure in every detail`.

Physical comparison is required before making that stronger claim.

Mechanisms:

- `PRIVATE_STORE_LIST_TO_PUBLIC_TRANSFER_LIST`
- `STORE_ONTOLOGY_TO_TRANSFER_ONTOLOGY` — candidate, pending comparison.

## 11. Hunter 1896 must be downgraded in source hierarchy

Waves 34–35 used William Wilson Hunter's 1896 *Life of Brian Houghton Hodgson*, Appendix B, because it reproduces the 1864 presentation list in accessible print.

That remains useful for searchable transcription and article navigation.

But source hierarchy should now be:

### Tier 1 — surviving event-near objects

- `BHH/11/14` handwritten 1858–64 store/donation list;
- `BHH/5/18` 2 August 1864 draft donation letter;
- `BHH/19/5` August 1864 printed annotated donation list.

### Tier 2 — India Office originals/concordances, if located

- accession record;
- librarian Hall inspection/receipt records;
- English/Hindi content lists Hodgson says were already with the librarian;
- original institutional copy of the printed transfer list.

### Tier 3 — Hunter 1896 reproduction

Useful but downstream.

Mechanism:

`SOURCE_HIERARCHY_REBUILT_AROUND_SURVIVING_MANIFEST_STATES`.

## 12. The archive itself has a documentary version history

The larger paper-centred consequence is now clear.

`object collection`

It is produced by it.

## 13. New historical question: when did version-family co-bundling emerge?

Wave 35 established that the 1864 transfer co-bundled source, translation, abstract and multilingual derivative states.

Wave 37 changes the next question.

Because `BHH/11/14` began as a 1858 store list, ask:

**Was the 1864 version-family archival design already present in Hodgson's private store order before institutional donation?**

This is now directly testable by comparing:

- `BHH/11/14` handwritten store/donation list;
- `BHH/19/5` printed annotated 1864 list;
- Hunter 1896 reproduction;
- surviving 1837/1842/1855 trunk lists.

Possible outcomes:

### Outcome A

1858 list already preserves the same source/translation/abstract co-bundling.

Then:

`VERSION_FAMILY_CO_BUNDLING = private archival practice before donation`.

### Outcome B

1858 store list is materially or structurally different, with version families created/reordered during 1864 institutional preparation.

Then:

`VERSION_FAMILY_CO_BUNDLING = transfer-stage reorganisation`.

### Outcome C

Mixed case: some relations are old, others are created or sharpened for donation.

This is presently the most methodologically neutral expectation.

Do not choose among these until the list states are inspected.

## Source-control rules

1. Do not continue to cite Hunter 1896 as though it were the only surviving witness to the 1864 list.
2. Do not infer the content/structure of `BHH/11/14` beyond the RAS catalogue description without page inspection.
3. Do not assume annotations on `BHH/19/5` are Hodgson's until handwriting/provenance is checked.
4. `repacked in 1844` is a custody event recorded on earlier list surfaces; it does not mean the 1837/1842 container structure remained unchanged.
5. A sequence of lists does not by itself prove direct textual copying from one list to the next; distinguish chronological continuity from textual descent.
6. BHH/11/1 and BHH/11/3 are separate inventory objects describing overlapping but not automatically identical stores.
7. The term `archive` is used operationally for Hodgson's stored/listed working and research papers; avoid implying a modern institutional archival doctrine.

## Immediate next actions

1. Physically/image-transcribe `BHH/11/14` and compare its 1858 store-list layer with its 1864 donation-list layer.
2. Inspect annotations on `BHH/19/5` and compare printed wording/order against BHH/11/14 and Hunter 1896.
3. Inspect `BHH/11/1` and `BHH/11/3` for box/basta numbering and determine whether later 1844 notes alter membership or only transport state.

8. Build a container/list chronology from 1837 through 1921 without silently collapsing successive custody regimes.

## Bottom line

The archival history now moves substantially earlier than the 1864 India Office donation. Hodgson repeatedly made and revised paper inventories for boxes, trunks, bastas, drawings and literary stores across Kathmandu, Canterbury and Darjeeling. The crucial RAS source `BHH/11/14` was apparently a private store list in 1858 before becoming the 1864 India Office donation list, while a separate August 1864 copy survives as an annotated printed pamphlet. The 1864 manifest should therefore be treated as one state in a longer history of inventory versioning. The next falsifiable question is whether the version-family co-bundling visible at transfer was inherited from Hodgson's private store order or constructed during preparation for institutional donation.
