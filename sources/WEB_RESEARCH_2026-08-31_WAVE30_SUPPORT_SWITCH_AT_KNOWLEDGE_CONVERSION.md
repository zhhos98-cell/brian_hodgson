# Web research 2026-08-31 — Wave 30
## Support switch at knowledge conversion: paired source → abstract/translation cases

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred paired-case follow-up to Wave 28. Rather than compare unrelated documents, this wave traces explicit cross-references between local source manuscripts and later English/Urdu analytical, translated or tabulated states.

## Executive result

Several content-linked pairs show a material switch at documentary conversion.

The strongest pattern is:

`local source/copy on Nepali hand-made paper`
→ `English/Urdu abstract, translation, table or report on machine-made paper`.

This is not universal: some English translations remain on Nepali hand-made paper and some local-language texts use machine-made paper. But explicit pairs demonstrate that support change could coincide with transformation of the knowledge object.

Mechanisms:

- `SUPPORT_SWITCH_AT_KNOWLEDGE_CONVERSION`
- `SOURCE_TO_ABSTRACT_MATERIAL_RECODING`
- `TRANSLATION_AS_SUPPORT_CONVERSION`
- `CONTENT_CONTINUITY_WITH_MATERIAL_STATE_CHANGE`
- `PAPER_ALLOCATION_AS_RESEARCH_INFRASTRUCTURE`

## Counterexamples and limits

The conversion pattern is strong but not deterministic.

- vol.7/27 preserves English translations of routes on Nepali hand-made paper;
- vol.21/6 contains an English Buddhist account written by a native writer on Nepali hand-made paper;
- vol.60/6 is a Nepali Buddhist text on machine-made paper;
- vol.53/12 is a Nepali copy of a royal order on machine-made paper.

These are analytically useful. They show that language alone does not select support and that machine-made paper was available to local-language documentary work.

The stronger claim is therefore:

**material recoding is associated with operation, actor, copy-state, inventory availability and intended use; it is not a fixed language-to-paper rule.**

---

## Article consequence

The paper argument can now move beyond static allocation.

Earlier formulation:

`paper use = availability × procurement × stock duration × operation-specific selection`.

Paired evidence adds transformation:

`knowledge conversion = content selection + linguistic recoding + documentary reformatting + possible support conversion`.

A source may remain informationally related to its abstract while becoming a different material epistemic object.

The sheet change matters because translation/abstraction often also changes:

- writing space;
- table/list format;
- correction affordance;
- binding/notebook incorporation;
- portability;
- filing and publication readiness;
- actor access.

Thus support switch is not merely a consequence of translation. It can be part of the infrastructure by which translation becomes a new operative state.

## Immediate falsification targets

1. Inspect each paired source/abstract physically and verify that catalogue cross-references describe genuine content descent rather than loose thematic similarity.
2. Compare dimensions, laid/wove/machine structure, watermark/countermark and binding state across each pair.
3. Check handwriting to separate local writer, translator, assistant and Hodgson layers.
4. Test whether English machine-paper versions consistently contain more tabulation, corrections, headings and publication-oriented formatting than their source counterparts.
5. Search the full inventory for phrases `For English translation`, `For Nepali original`, `translated copy`, `summary`, `abstract`, `copy of` and construct a denominator-complete transformation-pair dataset.

## Bottom line

The strongest new paper evidence is relational rather than categorical. Hodgson's archive preserves explicit source-to-translation and source-to-abstract pairs in which informational continuity crosses a change of material support. Population data, revenue accounts, salaries, festival calendars, Tarai contracts and older land documents move from palm leaf or Nepali hand-made paper into machine-made English or Urdu working states. The switch is not universal, but where it occurs it makes knowledge conversion materially visible. Paper therefore participates not only in preserving knowledge but in **re-formatting its operational state**.
