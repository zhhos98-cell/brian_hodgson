# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 39
## Surviving pre-1921 container metadata inside the modern bound volumes

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: paper-centred archival-reconstruction follow-up to Waves 36–38. This wave asks whether the 1921 dimension-based rebinding destroyed all evidence of earlier shipment/bundle relations or whether historical container metadata survives physically inside the modern `MSS EUR HODGSON` volumes.

## Executive result

The older custody ontology survives in multiple forms inside the modern bound collection.

The Cambridge inventory records:

- pre-bound notebooks whose covers still bear **original shipment numbers** in Nepali script;
- notebook titles whose **original sack numbers** remain legible;
- cover pages from **different original bundles** now bound together in later volumes;
- a `content page of box No. 3 of original shipment`;
- `content of manuscripts from sack No. 8 of the original shipment`;
- separated leaves listing the content of manuscripts shipped from Kathmandu, including **bundle numbers and categories**;
- a broader list of papers sent to India from Kathmandu since 1821;
- original bundle-description leaves surviving as material fragments beside items whose modern volume adjacency is unrelated.

The key consequence is:

`1921 rebinding != total erasure of earlier container metadata`.

The old archive remains partially embedded as paper evidence within the new archive.

This supplies an independent route for reconstructing historical shipment/bundle relations without relying solely on Hunter's 1896 transcription or later catalogue prose.

Core mechanisms:

- `OLD_CONTAINER_METADATA_SURVIVES_REBINDING`
- `SHIPMENT_NUMBER_AS_RELATIONAL_IDENTIFIER`
- `ORIGINAL_BUNDLE_COVER_AS_CUSTODY_EVIDENCE`
- `BOX_CONTENT_PAGE_AS_PREVIOUS_ARCHIVAL_STATE`
- `SACK_CONTENT_LIST_AS_CONTAINER_INDEX`
- `MODERN_VOLUME_CONTAINS_FRAGMENTS_OF_PRIOR_ARCHIVE`
- `MATERIAL_REMAINDER_ENABLES_RELATION_RECONSTRUCTION`

## 1. Volumes 4 and 5 preserve original shipment/sack numbers on pre-bound notebooks

### MSS EUR HODGSON/4

Cambridge inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol004.html

The catalogue states that all items are in four pre-bound notebooks and that in most cases the cover pages bear:

- a Nepali title;
- the **original shipment number** in Nepali language/script.

The modern volume was labelled `ethnography and topography` during 1920s binding, but the catalogue notes that its actual contents do not fully match that title. The surviving pre-bound notebooks therefore preserve an older identity layer inside a later imposed volume category.

### MSS EUR HODGSON/5

Cambridge inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol005.html

Again, six pre-bound notebooks preserve Nepali titles and original shipment numbers. For the Newar Jātamālā notebooks, the catalogue specifically says titles and **original sack numbers** are written in Nepali.

Mechanisms:

- `SHIPMENT_NUMBER_AS_RELATIONAL_IDENTIFIER`
- `PREBOUND_NOTEBOOK_PRESERVES_PRE1921_IDENTITY`.

These numbers should become a new dataset field, not remain buried in descriptive prose.

This is exceptionally high-value evidence.

The present bound volume contains paper surfaces whose original function was to describe or identify historical containers rather than the substantive manuscript content now adjacent to them.

Mechanisms:

- `BOX_CONTENT_PAGE_AS_PREVIOUS_ARCHIVAL_STATE`.

## 4. Vol.94 preserves separated title/content leaves with bundle numbers and categories

Cambridge `MSS EUR HODGSON/94`, item 7:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol094.html

The catalogue describes:

`Separated leaves containing the content of the manuscripts shipped from Kathmandu`.

Material/support:

- Nepali hand-made paper;
- rough Farsi hand.

The leaves include:

- title folio for manuscripts concerning Newar castes and other hill peoples;
- a list of contents of the original shipment of Hodgson's Nepali manuscripts;
- details of **bundle number and categories**;
- Nepali title description.

This is perhaps the strongest currently catalogued surviving bridge between:

`historical shipment structure`

and

`modern dispersed volume structure`.

Mechanisms:

- `BUNDLE_NUMBER_SURVIVES_AS_EMBEDDED_METADATA`
- `SEPARATED_CONTROL_LEAF_PRESERVES_LOST_CONTAINER_RELATION`.

The phrase `separated leaves` is methodologically important: the control surface has lost its original container adjacency, but its textual identifier can still reconstruct that relation.

The modern volume therefore physically juxtaposes cover surfaces from distinct older groups.

Mechanism:

`MODERN_VOLUME_CONTAINS_FRAGMENTS_OF_PRIOR_ARCHIVE`.

This offers a direct test of Wave 36's claim that modern bound-volume proximity is not equivalent to historical documentary proximity.

## 6. Original shipment metadata survives on both machine-made and Nepali-paper documentary states

The historical identifier is not restricted to one paper regime.

Examples:

- volumes 4/5: English pre-bound notebooks on machine-made paper retain Nepali shipment/sack numbers;
- volumes 55/58/94: Nepali hand-made control leaves preserve box/sack/bundle membership.

Thus original shipment identity could be carried on:

- a notebook cover;
- a loose cover sheet;
- a box-content page;
- a sack-content list;
- a detached bundle-index leaf.

This supports a general rule:

`container relation can be externalised onto paper metadata surfaces`.

Mechanism:

`CONTAINER_RELATION_EXTERNALISED_TO_PAPER_CONTROL_SURFACE`.

## 7. The paper article now has a direct method for reversing archival dispersion

Wave 36 established:

`1921 size-based rebinding -> modern dispersion`.

Wave 39 adds the inverse procedure:

`modern volume`
-> `find surviving cover/list/shipment number`
-> `recover historical container identifier`
-> `match identifier across dispersed items`
-> `reconstruct earlier version/shipment family`.

This can be called:

`RELATION_RECONSTRUCTION_FROM_MATERIAL_REMAINDERS`.

The modern digital catalogue's cross-references repair relations semantically; the surviving old covers and content lists can repair them materially.

## 9. This may help distinguish successive shipment regimes

The corpus now contains several terms:

- box;
- trunk;
- basta/bundle;
- sack;
- shipment number;
- bundle number.

They should not be normalized into synonyms.

A `sack No.8` may belong to a different packing episode from `box No.3` or `Trunk No.1`.

The next task is to match identifier vocabularies and numbers against dated RAS inventory states:

- 1837 five boxes;
- 1842 Trunk No.1 / bastas;
- 1844 repacking;
- 1845 maritime/literary boxes;
- 1852 Darjeeling trunks;
- 1855 Trunk No.2;
- 1858 store list;
- 1864 India Office trunks/large bundles/lesser bundles.

Mechanism:

`PACKING_TERMINOLOGY_IS_EVENT_SPECIFIC`.

## 10. Article consequence

The archival-afterlife argument can now be made without treating the 1921 operation as a one-way loss.

**The India Office's twentieth-century rebinding partly reorganised Hodgson's papers according to physical dimensions and thereby separated related originals and translations. Yet the older archive was not completely erased. Original shipment numbers remained on pre-bound notebook covers; box, sack and bundle content pages survived as loose leaves; modern volumes still contain covers from earlier manuscript groups. Historical custody relations therefore survive as material residues inside the later classification system. The same paper collection records both the archival operation that broke relations and the paper metadata needed to reconstruct them.**

Full sequence:

`historical container relation`
-> `identifier/control surface`
-> `1921 rebinding and dispersion`
-> `control surface survives inside modern volume`
-> `digital/material relation reconstruction`.

## Source-control rules

1. `original shipment` is not assumed to mean the 1864 India Office donation unless independently dated.
2. Box, trunk, basta, sack and bundle are preserved as distinct historical container terms.
3. A surviving box/sack/bundle number proves a historical identifier, not its date or exact container hierarchy until cross-matched.
4. Modern adjacency of old cover leaves does not reconstruct their original adjacency.
5. Catalogue descriptions of original shipment numbers should be physically verified where article claims depend on exact numerals/scripts.
6. Shipment-number language/script must be recorded exactly; Nepali/Nagri/Farsi control surfaces may belong to different descriptive layers.

## Bottom line

The older Hodgson archive survives materially inside the modern bound collection. Notebook covers still carry original shipment and sack numbers; modern volumes contain old bundle covers, box-content pages, sack-content lists and detached shipment-index leaves. These residues make archival dispersion reversible in principle. Paper therefore does double historical work: its dimensions helped generate twentieth-century rebinding and separation, while other paper surfaces preserved the identifiers needed to reconstruct the relations that rebinding obscured.
