## Finding 15 — Campbell's core Darjeeling papermaking report is securely dated 4 July 1841

Evidence status: **SECURE catalogue-level chronology.**

The Cambridge/Digital Himalaya inventory of `MSS EUR HODGSON/14` catalogues the duplicate Campbell letter at `ff.172–177` as:

`Letter from A. Campbell dispatched from Darjeeling to J. R. Colvin 07-04-1841`.

The same inventory uses unambiguous month-day-year formatting elsewhere in the volume (for example `08-23-1841` and `10-25-1831`). The date is therefore **4 July 1841**, not 7 April 1841.

Catalogue:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol014.html

The parallel `MSS EUR HODGSON/6, ff.181–184` entry gives the date only as `07-1841` and describes the same report on the five-person papermaker party, salaries, enquiries and experiments.

Consequence:

The central 1841 chronology is now cleaner:

`mid-April arrival of five makers -> workshop/site/process experiments -> 4 July Campbell report to J. R. Colvin`.

Do not cite the letter as April 7.

## Finding 16 — the surviving Hodgson-paper copies of Campbell's report are low-probability sources for recovering the five papermakers' names

Evidence status: **SECURE catalogue relation; negative inference bounded.**

`MSS EUR HODGSON/14, ff.172–177` is explicitly catalogued as a duplicate of `MSS EUR HODGSON/6, ff.181–184`. The volume 6 introduction states that all manuscripts in that volume are copies and most were published.

The published 1842 correspondence preserves the five-person hierarchy and wages but not personal names:

- head maker;
- mate / first assistant;
- three other workers.

Consequence:

The Hodgson duplicate copies remain worth consulting if already available, but **do not treat them as the highest-probability route to names**. The higher-value actor-level target is the administrative chain before Campbell's July report:

`Auckland request -> Hodgson / Nepal Residency -> Nepal Durbar -> selection/despatch of five makers -> pay/allowance arrangements -> arrival at Darjeeling`.

Records generated at selection, dispatch, payment or retention are more likely than the retrospective Campbell report to preserve individual identities.

## Finding 17 — EAP880 Sikkim Palace digitisation is real but should not be manually trawled without a precise consultation target

Evidence status: **SECURE collection-level assessment.**

British Library EAP880 includes `EAP880/1/2/55/55`, **Political consultations regarding negotiations for Darjeeling and the Sikkim Terai region, 17 Oct 1833–24 May 1843**, 141 images. The material is English and the originals are in the Sikkim Palace Archives.

Catalogue:
https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-003650839

However, the parent file is explicitly a Sikkim Palace dossier on the **cession of Darjeeling**, assembled around territorial/diplomatic negotiations. It is not a complete run of Darjeeling general administrative Political Consultations. Searchable metadata does not identify papermaking or the 1841 maker experiment.

Consequence:

Do **not** scan 141 TIFFs simply because the date range overlaps. This would recreate archive over-density. Re-open EAP880/1/2/55/55 only if a specific Political Consultation date/number relevant to papermaker selection, dispatch, pay or retention can be shown to belong to the cession dossier.

Archive priority therefore remains:

1. exact Foreign/Political consultation or Residency correspondence generated by the 1841 maker transfer;
2. pay/allowance/retention records;
3. only then broader overlapping Darjeeling/Sikkim compilations.
