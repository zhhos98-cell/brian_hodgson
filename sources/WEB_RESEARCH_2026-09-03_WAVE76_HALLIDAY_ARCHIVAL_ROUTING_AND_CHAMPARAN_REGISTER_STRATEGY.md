# Web/archive research 2026-09-03 — Wave 76
## Halliday's dual secretariat roles, the 1842 proceedings problem, and a document-route strategy for the missing pulp freight bill

Date: 2026-09-03

Status: active paper-history mainline. Follow-up to Waves 73–75. This wave does **not** recover Woodcock's missing `bill of charges` or Halliday's initiating instruction. It advances the search by reconstructing the administrative and archival routes through which those documents were likely generated and preserved, while keeping each candidate record series explicitly unproven until a consignment-level match is found.

## Executive result

Wave 73 established the directly evidenced material event:

`25 March 1842`

`Nepal Residency`
-> `24 packages of Nepalese paper pulp`
-> `Woodcock / Champaran forwarding node`
-> `Patna land option OR Govind Gang water option`
-> `Calcutta`

with an unrecovered upstream instruction from `Mr. Secretary Halliday` and an unrecovered downstream `bill of charges` requested from Woodcock.

Wave 76 adds a **documentary-routing layer**.

The 1842 *East-India Register and Directory*, compiled from official returns, gives Frederick James Halliday two simultaneous secretariat locations:

- `secretary to the government of Bengal, judicial and revenue department`;
- `junior secretary to government of India, revenue, judicial, and legislative departments`.

Thus the phrase `by direction of Mr. Secretary Halliday` does not by itself identify a single departmental archive. Halliday was institutionally positioned across both Bengal and Government-of-India secretariat structures in 1842.

At the same time, surviving archival references show that contemporary Bengal proceedings were organized as dated, numbered proceedings and that Champaran incoming-letter registers could retain enclosures. A 2024 archival study cites a **1839** `Register of Letters Received from February 1839 to December 1839`, `Vol. 6`, Champaran District Records, Bihar State Archives, including an enclosed intelligence paper. Regmi's source for Hodgson's 25 March 1842 letter is another Champaran/Bihar State Archives incoming register, `Register of Letters Received from January 1842 to June 1842`, pp.344–45.

The recovery problem can therefore be represented as two linked but non-identical routes:

### material route

`Nepal pulp packages`
-> `Champaran handoff`
-> `Patna or Govind Gang route decision`
-> `Calcutta`

### documentary route

`Halliday directive`
-> `Hodgson action / outgoing instruction`
-> `Champaran incoming register`
-> `Woodcock route decision + charges`
-> `Woodcock outgoing reply/bill`
-> `[Residency / higher-government / Calcutta receipt copy still unknown]`.

Core mechanisms:

- `OFFICIAL_ROLE_MULTIPLICITY_SPLITS_ARCHIVAL_SEARCH`
- `DOCUMENTARY_ROUTE_PARALLELS_BUT_DOES_NOT_EQUAL_MATERIAL_ROUTE`
- `INCOMING_REGISTER_ANCHORS_ONE_SIDE_OF_HANDOFF`
- `MISSING_BILL_MUST_BE_SEARCHED_AT_BOTH_SENDER_AND_RECIPIENT_SIDES`
- `PROCEEDING_DATE_AND_NUMBER_FORM_A_RETRIEVAL_ADDRESS`
- `ARCHIVE_FORM_RECONSTRUCTION_CAN_NARROW_A_MATERIAL_HISTORY_SEARCH`.

## 1. Halliday's 1842 office is more complex than a single Bengal secretaryship

Direct contemporary source:

*The East-India Register and Directory for 1842*, compiled from official returns received at the East India House.

Public searchable witness:

https://archive.org/stream/eastindiaregister1842/EastIndiaRegister1842_djvu.txt

The Bengal civil-servant list identifies:

`Frederick James Halliday`

as both:

`secretary to the government of Bengal, judicial and revenue department`

and

`junior secretary to government of India, revenue, judicial, and legislative departments`.

This sharpens Wave 73's identification of `Mr. Secretary Halliday` while changing the retrieval problem.

Correct evidence state:

`MR_SECRETARY_HALLIDAY = FREDERICK_JAMES_HALLIDAY VERY_HIGH/SECURE_INSTITUTIONAL_MATCH`.

But:

`HALLIDAY_OFFICE_IN_1842 = MULTIPLE_SIMULTANEOUS_SECRETARIAT_ROLES`.

Therefore:

`HALLIDAY_IDENTITY_SECURE`

is not equivalent to:

`INITIATING_DEPARTMENT_SECURE`.

Mechanisms:

- `PERSON_IDENTITY_DOES_NOT_FIX_DEPARTMENTAL_PROVENANCE`
- `ONE_OFFICIAL_CAN_GENERATE_MULTIPLE_ARCHIVAL_BRANCHES`.

## 2. Why the upstream search must branch

The missing directive must predate or coincide with Hodgson's action on 25 March 1842, but the direct letter does not give:

- directive date;
- directive number;
- department;
- whether it came from Government of Bengal or Government of India;
- whether Halliday signed personally or transmitted an order taken elsewhere;
- whether the instruction named the later Woodcock/Champaran forwarding route.

A practical search window is therefore:

`1842-01-01 -> 1842-03-25`

but this is a **retrieval window**, not a claim that the order was issued after 1 January.

Candidate branches now include:

1. Government of Bengal Judicial/Revenue correspondence or proceedings involving Halliday;
2. Government of Bengal General Department (General) proceedings if the paper experiment was handled as a general administrative/scientific/material matter;
3. Government of India Revenue/Judicial/Legislative correspondence or proceedings if Halliday acted in his junior Government-of-India capacity;
4. surviving Nepal Residency correspondence copies/registers that may preserve the incoming instruction independently of central proceedings.

No branch is currently proven as the initiating one.

Rule:

`OFFICE_PROFILE -> SEARCH_BRANCH`

not

`OFFICE_PROFILE -> DEPARTMENTAL_IDENTIFICATION`.

`IOR/P/13/39 = CANDIDATE_CONTAINER`

Examples include 1842 references cited as:

## 5. The Champaran incoming register is a real archival node, not merely a later citation label

Wave 73 already fixed the direct source of Hodgson's 25 March 1842 letter as:

`Register of Letters Received from January 1842 to June 1842`, pp.344–45, Bihar State Archives, Patna.

A recent independent study of the India–Nepal borderland cites a closely related Champaran record:

`Register of Letters Received from February 1839 to December 1839`

and identifies it as:

`Vol. 6, Champaran District Records, 1839, Bihar State Archives, Patna`.

It also cites an **enclosure** retained within that register context.

Reference:

Catherine Warner, `The Making of the India-Nepal Borderland`, *Studies in Nepali History and Society* 29.2 (2024), archival notes to Champaran District Records, BSA.

Public PDF:

https://www.martinchautari.org.np/storage/files/sinhas-29-2-article-catherine-warner.pdf

This strengthens the archive-form model in three ways:

1. the incoming-letter sequence was organized into identifiable volumes;
2. the series could preserve more than bare register metadata;
3. enclosures can survive within the same documentary ecology.

Do **not** infer the 1842 volume number from the 1839 `Vol. 6` citation.

Current state:

`1842_REGISTER_TITLE_AND_PAGES = SECURE`

`1842_VOLUME_NUMBER = UNRESOLVED`

`ADJACENT_FOLIOS_AND_ENCLOSURES = HIGH_PRIORITY_IMAGE_TARGET`.

## 6. A later Champaran outgoing-register type provides a search form, not continuity proof

Mahesh C. Regmi later cited a report from H. A. Robinson, officiating Magistrate of Champaran, to the Commissioner of Circuit at Patna, 21 May 1860, from:

`Register of Letters Issued (General Department) from April 1859 to January 1862`, Bihar State Archives, Patna.

Public searchable witness:

https://www.pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Nepal/1988%20An%20Economic%20History%20of%20Nepal%201846-1901%20by%20Regmi%20s.pdf

This proves that a Champaran/BSA **outgoing General Department register form** is attested for the later 1859–62 period.

It does not prove that an identically titled register survives for March–April 1842.

Its value is retrieval-oriented: Woodcock's response and bill would be an outgoing act from the Champaran office, so an 1842 `letters issued` / `General Department` / accounts register is a more logically appropriate target than repeatedly searching the already known incoming register alone.

Mechanisms:

- `HANDOFF_GENERATES_OPPOSITE_DIRECTION_RECORDS`
- `INCOMING_SOURCE_DOES_NOT_LOCATE_OUTGOING_REPLY`
- `LATER_REGISTER_FORM_CAN_GUIDE_SEARCH_WITHOUT_PROVING_EARLIER_CONTINUITY`.

## 7. The missing bill should be searched from both ends

The physical event creates at least two possible archival homes for the missing `bill of charges`.

### Champaran / Woodcock side

Possible record forms:

- outgoing letter register;
- General Department correspondence;
- local account/bill register;
- correspondence to Patna Commissioner/Magistrate;
- forwarding memorandum or route instruction.

### Nepal Residency / Hodgson side

Possible record forms:

- incoming correspondence register;
- accounts or contingent-charge records;
- copied bills/vouchers;
- paper-experiment correspondence;
- Government instruction / reply chain.

A third possible trace may exist at the next transport node:

### Patna / Calcutta side

- receipt of packages;
- onward forwarding order;
- freight payment;
- receipt or acknowledgement;
- warehouse / institutional receipt if the final consignee can be identified.

The retrieval model is therefore:

`one missing document != one archive search`.

Instead:

`transaction edge -> sender copy + recipient copy + accounting copy + higher-level proceeding + later receipt possibilities`.

Mechanism:

`TRANSACTION_REDUNDANCY_CREATES_MULTIPLE_RECOVERY_PATHS`.

## 10. Paper-history significance

Wave 75 showed that the physical shipment entered multiple metrologies: package count, standard route time, carrying capacity and freight rate.

Wave 76 adds that the same event also entered multiple **document systems**.

The experimental material was therefore simultaneously:

- a pulp material;
- twenty-four transport packages;
- an object of route selection;
- a cost-generating movement;
- an incoming district-office letter;
- a likely outgoing forwarding/accounting action;
- a possible proceeding item at higher governmental level;
- a possible receipt at the next node.

The paper-history object can now be extended from:

`material state × route state × custody state × cost state`

to:

`material state × route state × custody state × cost state × documentary state`.

Working proposition:

**the logistics of experimental material produced a parallel documentary logistics. Reconstructing the paper-pulp route therefore requires following not only packages across space but instructions, registers, bills, proceedings and receipts across administrative jurisdictions.**

Mechanisms:

- `MATERIAL_MOVEMENT_GENERATES_DOCUMENT_MOVEMENT`
- `DOCUMENTARY_STATE_IS_PART_OF_MATERIAL_HISTORY`
- `ARCHIVAL_DISTRIBUTION_FOLLOWS_ADMINISTRATIVE_HANDOFFS`.

## Source-control rules

1. Keep Halliday's identity secure but the initiating department unresolved.
2. Do not collapse Government of Bengal and Government of India roles into one archive branch.
3. Treat `IOR/P/13/39` as an early-1842 Bengal Proceedings container, not as a proven pulp file.
4. Treat `General Department (General) Proceedings` as a candidate series until a paper-pulp entry is found.
5. Preserve Regmi's exact BSA title `Register of Letters Received from January 1842 to June 1842`, pp.344–45.
6. Do not infer the 1842 Champaran volume number from the 1839 `Vol. 6` reference.
7. Use the 1859–62 `Register of Letters Issued (General Department)` only as evidence that this outgoing-record form existed later, not that the same series survives for 1842.
8. Keep public-web non-recovery separate from archival non-survival.
9. Search the missing bill from both Champaran and Residency/receiver sides.
10. Treat adjacent folios, endorsements and enclosures as evidence, not merely the transcribed letter body.

## Bottom line

Wave 76 does not solve the 1842 route, but it converts two vague missing-document problems into a structured archival graph. Halliday's 1842 office profile shows that his directive can sit in more than one governmental branch; contemporary Bengal proceedings demonstrate date-and-number retrieval architecture; Champaran incoming registers demonstrably preserve volume-level correspondence and enclosures; and a later Champaran `Register of Letters Issued (General Department)` demonstrates the outgoing record form that would be logically suited to Woodcock's response. The twenty-four pulp packages thus generated a second circulation beside their physical journey: a documentary route of instruction, receipt, forwarding, accounting and acknowledgement. Recovering the material route now means reconstructing that documentary route node by node.
