# Archive research wave 39 — learned-society forms and the conversion of manuscript materiality into scholarly metadata

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred synthesis from uploaded Linnean/RAS scans, Linnean Society archive guidance, comparative Fellowship certificates, Cowell & Eggeling's 1875 Hodgson manuscript catalogue, and current RAS object descriptions.

## Executive result

Two institutional paper technologies now become visible on opposite sides of Hodgson's career:

1. **learned-society form** — the Linnean certificate turns a scientist's biography and personal endorsements into a standardized paper object that can enter an election procedure;
2. **material catalogue** — the Royal Asiatic Society later turns unique manuscript paper objects into portable descriptive tuples by recording support, leaf count, dimensions, line count, handwriting, wrappers, pasted tickets, folding and other physical features.

The shared mechanism is **normalization through paper**.

`singular career -> standardized credential surface`

and

`singular manuscript -> standardized material-description surface`.

But both preserve exceptions: autograph signatures individualize the certificate, while unusual supports/wrappers/folds individualize catalogue entries.

The stronger HPS formulation is:

**nineteenth-century learned institutions made heterogeneous persons and objects comparable by converting selected material/social features into repeatable paper fields.**

## 1. Hodgson's 1834 recommendation belongs to a durable Linnean paper genre

The Linnean Society describes Certificates of Recommendation as records of Fellowship dating back to the Society's beginnings and signed by several Fellows.

The uploaded `DA-EN-G-1-CR-48` page 1 shows:

- recommendation/qualification prose in the upper region;
- proposal date below;
- autograph signatories accumulated in the lower region.

Mechanisms:

- `LEARNED_SOCIETY_CREDENTIAL_FORM`
- `PRECOMPOSED_CREDENTIAL_PLUS_ACCRETIVE_SIGNATURES`
- `ENDORSEMENT_SURFACE`.

The sheet is institutionally actionable because it combines a shared proposition with multiple individual endorsements.

## 2. Richard Owen's 1835 certificate confirms a stable textual-spatial grammar

A published Linnean image/transcription of Richard Owen's 1835 recommendation shows the same broad operation:

- candidate/status/attainments summarized above;
- formula that he desires to become a Fellow;
- recommendation by Fellows from personal knowledge;
- many signatures below.

Source: `The Linnean`, vol. 7 no. 2 (1991).

Mechanism:

`REPUTATION_TO_STANDARDIZED_PAPER_SYNTAX`.

Hodgson's location at the Nepal court and heterogeneous scientific/literary activities become commensurable with another candidate's career because both are rendered into the same institutional documentary grammar.

## 4. Institutional form combines repeatability with autograph singularity

A certificate works through two inscription regimes:

- standardized/collective proposition;
- singular autograph signatures.

Mechanism:

`STANDARDIZED_PROPOSITION_PLUS_AUTOGRAPH_AUTHORITY`.

The paper surface therefore generates institutional authority through controlled accretion:

`same proposition + added qualifying signature -> changed institutional state`.

This is directly comparable at operation level to lists that accumulate corrections or distribution marks.

# Cowell & Eggeling 1875 — paper itself becomes catalogue evidence

## 5. The Hodgson manuscript catalogue explicitly establishes a material baseline

Cowell and Eggeling's `Catalogue of Buddhist Sanskrit Manuscripts in the possession of the Royal Asiatic Society (Hodgson Collection)` appeared in JRAS in 1875.

At the outset they state that manuscript material consists of **Indian paper unless otherwise stated**.

They also explain that their descriptions are meant to enable identification where full analytical comparison is impractical.

Source:

https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/abs/art-icatalogue-of-buddhist-sanskrit-manuscripts-in-the-possession-of-the-royal-asiatic-society-hodgson-collection/BCDBFD2BBAC2E056B6E489258AE74E2C

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_MATERIAL_AS_CATALOGUE_METADATA`
- `MATERIAL_BASELINE_EXCEPTION_CATALOGUING`.

Paper material is one of the properties that makes a manuscript remotely identifiable.

---

## 6. Catalogue identity is a material tuple, not a title alone

Entries repeatedly combine:

- support/material;
- leaf count;
- dimensions;
- lines per page;
- date/relative age;
- handwriting character;
- textual incipit/explicit;
- sometimes wrapper/ticket/folding notes.

Mechanism:

`MATERIAL_TUPLE_AS_REMOTE_IDENTIFIER`.

The catalogue converts physical handling into portable metadata:

`inspect object -> abstract selected physical fields -> print description -> enable distant comparison`.

---

## 7. Current RAS metadata retains parts of the 1875 material schema

Examples from current online Hodgson manuscript descriptions include:

- RAS Hodgson MS 50: `Paper; 5 lines on each page`;
- MS 65: `Paper; 6 lines on each page`;
- MS 66: `Paper; 6 or 7 lines on each page` plus note that the title occurs on the wrapper only;
- MS 13 and MS 70: palm-leaf support explicitly distinguished from paper.

Sources include:

- https://royalasiaticcollections.org/ras-hodgson-ms-65-adhivasanavidhi/
- https://royalasiaticcollections.org/ras-hodgson-ms-66-prayogamukham/
- https://royalasiaticcollections.org/ras-hodgson-ms-13-bodhicaryavatara/

Mechanism:

`MATERIAL_METADATA_PERSISTENCE_ACROSS_CATALOGUE_GENERATIONS`.

The paper-support observation made in nineteenth-century cataloguing survives into twenty-first-century digital object description.

---

## 8. Support deviation is made diagnostically visible by the catalogue baseline

The 1875 catalogue's default `Indian paper unless otherwise stated` means exceptional material states become high-information fields.

Uploaded examples include:

- palm leaf;
- blackened paper;
- alternating yellow/white writing on a dark support;
- later leaves supplied by a more modern hand.

Mechanism:

`DEFAULT_SUPPORT_EXCEPTION_SIGNAL`.

The catalogue's information economy depends partly on what it chooses **not** to repeat.

Material normality is compressed; material difference is expanded.

---

## 9. Dimensions and line count convert form into comparable data

The uploaded catalogue repeatedly records dimensions and writing density, e.g. combinations such as:

- 152 leaves, 12 × 2¾ inches, five lines per page;
- 69 palm leaves, 12 × 2¼ inches, eight or nine lines per page;
- 40 leaves, 7½ × 2½ inches, five lines per page.

Mechanism:

`FORMAT_METRICS_AS_IDENTIFICATION_FIELDS`.

This is a paper history of quantification:

`manuscript form -> measured dimensions / counted lines -> catalogue comparability`.

The scholarly object is partially remade as a numerical/material profile.

---

## 10. Folding can be part of manuscript identity rather than later damage

Cowell & Eggeling describe one manuscript as `40 leaves folded into one continuous roll`.

Mechanism:

`FOLDING_STATE_AS_BIBLIOGRAPHICAL_IDENTITY`.

This supplies a crucial distinction for the broader project:

- production/form fold;
- postal/transport fold;
- storage crease;
- later archival fold.

The presence of a fold is not self-interpreting. Its operation must be identified historically.

---

## 11. Wrapper may carry the only recoverable title

For RAS Hodgson MS 66, Cowell & Eggeling note that the text is incomplete and the **title is given on the wrapper only**. The modern RAS object record preserves that observation.

Mechanism:

`WRAPPER_AS_SEMANTIC_IDENTITY_SURFACE`.

Strong implication:

`text leaves survive + wrapper lost` can still produce an epistemic loss.

The wrapper is both protection and metadata-bearing paper.

This strengthens the repo's `RELATIONAL_PRESERVATION` argument in a specifically paper-centred way.

---

## 12. Pasted ticket creates a physically secondary but epistemically powerful layer

The 1875 catalogue records a manuscript where a **ticket pasted on the back of the last leaf** carries a descriptive/donor note.

Mechanism:

`PASTED_TICKET_AS_METADATA_LAYER`.

Sequence:

`historical manuscript leaf -> later paper ticket attached -> institutional/provenance reading becomes physically coupled to object`.

A secondary paper layer can become central to later identification.

---

## 13. Blank stripes and cord holes are structural evidence

In explaining photographic plates, Cowell & Eggeling describe manuscript pages divided into three text columns separated by blank stripes through which cord holes pass.

Mechanisms:

- `BLANK_SPACE_AS_BINDING_INFRASTRUCTURE`
- `CORD_HOLE_LAYOUT_AS_CODICOLOGICAL_EVIDENCE`.

This is an important control against treating all blank space as available writing reserve.

Here blankness performs a mechanical binding function.

Compare with the Lepcha case, where right-side blank space is described as a future translation reserve.

Same visible property (`blank paper`) can encode different operations.

---

## 14. The catalogue itself adds marks to reproduced blank space

Cowell & Eggeling explain that facsimiles of date numerals were added onto a blank stripe in a photographic specimen to clarify the colophon dating system.

Mechanism:

`REPRODUCTION_SURFACE_SCHOLARLY_INSERTION`.

The reproduced manuscript is not a transparent copy. The scholarly plate is an edited comparison surface.

This anticipates the project's digital-surrogate argument: reproduction redistributes and sometimes adds visibility.

---

## 15. Blackened paper and coloured writing are treated as observable identity features

The uploaded catalogue describes a manuscript on blackened paper with alternating yellow and white lines/paint.

Mechanism:

`SURFACE_TREATMENT_AS_IDENTIFICATION_FEATURE`.

This is a strong material claim because the colour contrast is historically recorded by cataloguers as an observable feature rather than symbolically inferred by the historian.

---

## 16. Photography creates an intermediate paper-to-image scientific object

Cowell & Eggeling use photographic specimens of old dated manuscripts to permit comparison of script/date forms.

Mechanism:

`MANUSCRIPT_PAPER_TO_PHOTOGRAPHIC_COMPARISON_SURFACE`.

Sequence:

`unique manuscript -> photograph -> printed plate -> distant scholarly inspection`.

Photography amplifies:

- letter shape;
- numeral form;
- column/layout comparison.

It reduces or loses:

- thickness;
- tactile fibre structure;
- full sheet relations;
- some colour/surface properties.

This gives a nineteenth-century analogue to Wave 37's digital-capture state problem.

# A new paper-history tension: normalization and re-singularization

## 17. Institutional form normalizes a person

`career / reputation -> certificate formula -> signature fields -> ballot object`.

Mechanism:

`PERSON_TO_STANDARDIZED_PAPER_OBJECT`.

## 18. Hodgson's author copies re-singularize print

Wave 38 establishes a recurrent series in which standardized printed impressions become copy-specific through annotation, loose inserts, cuttings and cover notes.

Mechanism:

`PRINTED_STANDARD_TO_SINGULAR_WORKING_COPY`.

## 19. Manuscript catalogue normalizes unique paper objects again

`unique manuscript -> support/dimensions/lines/wrapper fields -> printed catalogue tuple`.

Mechanism:

`UNIQUE_OBJECT_TO_STANDARDIZED_MATERIAL_METADATA`.

## 20. Combined mechanism

`NORMALIZATION_RE_SINGULARIZATION_CYCLE`.

Scientific paper culture repeatedly oscillates:

`singular -> standardized -> singular -> standardized`.

This is more precise than a linear manuscript-to-print model.

---

## 21. Article-level claim

A strong paper/HPS formulation is now possible:

**Learned institutions made heterogeneous people and objects comparable by turning selected features into repeatable paper fields. Linnean recommendation certificates rendered distinct careers into a common endorsement syntax. Hodgson then repeatedly re-singularized standardized print through handwritten work on particular copies. Later Cowell and Eggeling standardized unique manuscripts again by abstracting paper support, dimensions, line counts, wrappers, tickets and binding structures into catalogue metadata. Nineteenth-century scientific paper culture therefore alternated between making objects commensurable and making individual copies materially specific.**

## Bottom line

The paper-centred project now reaches beyond paper manufacture and support choice into the paperwork of learned institutions and the material science of cataloguing. Paper forms normalize careers; authorial use makes particular printed copies diverge; catalogues convert unique manuscript materiality back into standard fields. Hodgson's archive is unusually strong because all three movements survive in adjacent institutional collections.
