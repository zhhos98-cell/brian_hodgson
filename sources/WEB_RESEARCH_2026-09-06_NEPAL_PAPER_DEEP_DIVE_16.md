## Finding 74 — the 1850 Board of Customs, Salt and Opium treated packing as a manufacturing-quality variable

Evidence status: **SECURE direct contemporary Government index.**

The West Bengal State Archives has placed online the contemporary:

`Alphabetical Index to the Proceedings of the Board of Customs, Salt, and Opium, July to December 1850`.

Official PDF:
https://sadte.wb.gov.in/uploads/pdf/D12/D12944.pdf

The index records under `PATNA OR OPIUM` that on **28 November 1850** the decline in price of Behar opium was ascribed in part to **decrease of weight and defective packing of a large portion of the provision of 1847–48**. The same index contains nearby proceedings on risks from packing opium in chests made of unseasoned planks.

This does not prove that Nepal paper caused or solved the defective-packing problem. Its importance is narrower: within the same administrative-manufacturing system that was about to publish W. C. B. Eatwell's account, **packing was already treated as a variable capable of altering the marketable quality and value of opium**.

Correct use:

`opium manufacture -> packing condition -> product damage / value judgment`

not

`Nepal paper -> solved Patna packing problem`.

## Finding 75 — Eatwell's Nepal-paper wrapping was part of an official Benares Agency manufacturing procedure, not an incidental later anecdote

Evidence status: **SECURE direct official publication + secure Board index for publication route.**

Primary source:

W. C. B. Eatwell, *On the Poppy Cultivation, and the Benares Opium Agency* (Calcutta: Military Orphan Press, 1851), no. 1 of *Selections from the Records of the Bengal Government*.

British Library locator already controlled in `_15`:

`IOR/V/23/91, No. 1`.

Searchable full-text witness:
https://www.scribd.com/document/1069953932/Selections-From-the-Records-of-the-Gover

The printed paper is signed:

`W. C. B. Eatwell, M.D., First Asst. and Opium Examiner`

and dated:

`Board of Customs, Salt and Opium, 1st November 1850`.

For **Abkaree / internal-consumption opium**, Eatwell gives a discrete production chain:

- bring the drug to 90 per cent consistency by direct exposure to sun;
- mould it into square bricks of one seer each;
- wrap the bricks in **oiled Nepaul paper**;
- pack them in compartmented boxes.

Eatwell's own first-order verdict is that this form puts a large amount of drug into a limited space and makes it manageable for packing. The Nepal paper therefore appears inside a specified Government production/packing sequence, not merely as a descriptive material curiosity.

The West Bengal State Archives 1850 Board index independently records, under `PREPARATION OF OPIUM`, that on **28 November 1850** Eatwell's paper on preparation in the Benares Agency was recommended for inclusion in the printed `Selections from public records`.

Thus the documentary sequence is:

`Benares Agency production practice`
-> `Eatwell technical report, dated at Board, 1 Nov 1850`
-> `Board proceeding, 28 Nov 1850, recommends publication`
-> `Selections from the Records of the Bengal Government, 1851`.

### Correction / precision

Do **not** describe Eatwell's 1851 case as `Patna opium packing`.

His paper is explicitly about the **Benares Opium Agency** (central factory at Ghazeepore/Ghazipur). The 1850 Board index separately records defective packing of Behar/Patna opium. These belong to the same Board-controlled opium system but are not the same factory event.

Article consequence:

By 1850, Nepal paper had entered a large Government manufacturing regime as an operative wrapping layer for a particular product state: solid internal-consumption opium bricks. This is a strong controlled afterlife for `putting paper to work`, but it should remain a coda/afterlife rather than enlarge the 1831–49 central chronology unless the article needs the institutional consequence.

- later extension of the Nepal expedition;

### `IOR/F/4/712/19459`

Date range 1822–23, 16 folios, report on Wallich's scientific excursion to Nepal.

Consequence:

These are valid Nepal/Wallich archive containers but are **not currently evidenced as the specific home of the c.1820 finished-paper + material-for-making-it correspondence** already controlled through printed correspondence.

Do not reopen the Raffles/Wallich -> Hodgson causal bridge merely because these files exist. The earlier event remains a problem genealogy; inspect these Board's Collection files for paper only if physical/digital access is cheap or a specific cross-reference points into them.

## Finding 80 — a November 1855 prepared-pulp export is a high-value but currently uncontrolled lead

Evidence status: **QUARANTINE / secondary quotation whose underlying nineteenth-century source remains unidentified.**

Hayden Bellenoit, *The Formation of the Colonial State in India: Scribes, Paper and Taxes, 1760–1860* (2017), quotes a first-person nineteenth-century account describing Nepal paper and stating that the speaker sent:

- **two maunds of prepared pulp**;
- to **Messrs. Mackey & Co. of Calcutta**;
- in **November 1855**;
- cost `22 Rs 2a per maund` delivered at **Dinapore**;
- intended for `some fibre company in England`;
- no subsequent reply was received, leading the speaker to presume that it had not answered.

Searchable secondary witness:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317778857_The_Formation_of_the_Colonial_State_in_India_Scribes_Paper_and_Taxes_1760-1860

The surrounding first-person passage describes local observation near the Nepal Residency and has characteristics compatible with a Residency officer such as H. A. Oldfield, but **no source-level identification has yet been recovered and no attribution should be made**.

Attempts to match exact phrases against readily searchable Oldfield material have not closed the attribution.

Potential significance *if controlled*:

`1831 dried pulp bricks -> Britain / Cowan`

`1842 twenty-four packages -> Calcutta`

`1855 two maunds prepared pulp -> Mackey & Co. / intended English fibre company`

would constitute three distinct long-distance raw-material export episodes.

Until the underlying citation is recovered, keep the 1855 episode out of article prose and data joins.

## Revised afterlife map after this pass

The paper's controlled careers now include:

- durable office-record support / proposed Government substitution;
- graded stationery, packing and box-lining papers;
- medicine packing;
- reported castor-oil manufacture at Patna, where fibre shedding was a failure criterion;
- waxed postal wrapping and Post Office trial, with split general/local verdict;
- remanufacture / bleaching / filtration / waterproofing / machine trials;
- botanical field consumable by load in Hooker's 1849 work;
- **oiled wrapping in official Benares Agency Abkari-opium manufacture by 1850**;
- comparative/exhibition object by 1851.

The first-order historical point is becoming stronger without requiring a second-order opening claim:

**tests of Nepal paper did not culminate in one verdict. They placed different grades and preparations of the material into different operations, and those operations retained, rejected or re-specified the paper according to different failure thresholds.**

This is a conclusion to earn through source scenes, not a replacement for them.
