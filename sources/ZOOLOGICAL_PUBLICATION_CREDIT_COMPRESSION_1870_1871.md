# Zoological publication credit compression, 1870–1871 — from shikaree/writer paper chain to `Hodgson's manuscript observations`

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: source-controlled hypothesis note. The 1870 custody/reorganization chain is explicit. The final step linking particular published Capitonidae passages to particular surviving shikaree/writer notebook entries remains to be demonstrated by text-level collation.

## Executive result

The ZSL correspondence and the Marshalls' 1870–71 *Monograph of the Capitonidae* reveal a potentially important authorship/provenance transformation.

Inside Hodgson's archive, bird-behaviour knowledge is described with differentiated labour provenance:

`Shikaris -> viva voce statements -> Hodgson's writer -> manners volumes / memoranda`.

In the Marshalls' published monograph, at least one substantial behavioural passage is introduced as coming from:

`Mr. B. H. Hodgson's manuscript observations on the ornithology of Nepal`.

Between those two states the correspondence documents a series of paper operations:

`scattered portfolios -> lay out by species/group -> abstract relevant memoranda -> arrange/systematize notes -> publish selected observations`.

The article-level hypothesis is therefore:

**distributed documentary provenance may have been compressed during metropolitan sorting and publication into the bibliographically efficient category `Hodgson's manuscript observations`.**

Mechanism:

`PUBLICATION_CREDIT_COMPRESSION_OF_DISTRIBUTED_PROVENANCE`.

This is not yet a claim that every published Hodgson note originated with shikarees. The surviving archive contains multiple manuscript layers, including Hodgson's own field memoranda. The decisive next operation is passage-level collation.

---

## 1. 2 February 1870: the collection is physically `pell mell` and requires species-level reassembly

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/6`, Hodgson to G. F. L. Marshall, 2 Feb. 1870.

Hodgson describes the contents of four portfolios as scattered `pell mell` and says that proper inspection requires bringing together from the four receptacles everything relating to each species and group.

Paper operation:

`container-based scattering -> planar/relation-based regrouping by taxon`.

Mechanisms:

- `PORTFOLIO_SCATTER_TO_SPECIES_REASSEMBLY`
- `REORDERING_AS_PRECONDITION_OF_INTERPRETATION`.

This is a strong paper-history point: species coherence is not physically given by the archive container. It must be reconstructed by laying out and joining separated surfaces.

---

## 2. 7 February: Marshall has already abstracted Capitonidae memoranda

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/7`, A. Grote to Hodgson, 7 Feb. 1870.

Grote reports that Marshall appears already to have abstracted memoranda relevant to the Capitonidae, the group then under study, while intending to catalogue the rest of Hodgson's collection.

Paper operation:

`large heterogeneous corpus -> taxon-selective extraction -> working subset`.

Mechanism:

`TAXON_SELECTIVE_MEMORANDUM_EXTRACTION`.

This creates a direct chronological bridge between Hodgson's archive and the Marshalls' barbet publication project.

---

## 3. 8 February: proposed destination is Hume, but sorting/translation/arrangement is itself the problem

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/8`, G. F. L. Marshall to Hodgson, 8 Feb. 1870.

Marshall proposes sending Hodgson's materials to Hume so that they can be sorted, translated, arranged and incorporated into Hume's projected general ornithology of India. Grote prefers temporary London retention and sequential cataloguing.

Two competing paper regimes are visible:

`direct wholesale transfer to India -> sorting there -> incorporation into Hume`

versus

`temporary London custody -> one-by-one sorting/cataloguing -> later selective use`.

Mechanism:

`DESTINATION_DEPENDS_ON_PROCESSING_STRATEGY`.

The debate is not simply where objects should be stored. It is where documentary processing should occur.

---

## 4. 11 February: sender manifest preserves differentiated labour provenance

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/9`, Hodgson to Marshall, 11 Feb. 1870.

The intended `big deal box` contains, among other materials:

- 1,104 sheets of drawings;
- Hodgson's manuscript bird list;
- the native painter's Hindi list;
- one red-bound Nepal volume of bird manners, made by Hodgson's writer from viva voce statements of his Shikaris;
- eight unbound volumes of the same type made at Darjeeling;
- Hodgson's own 1846 Sikkim Tarai memoranda;
- printed catalogues and author copies.

The sender-side description therefore keeps several knowledge-producing roles distinct:

`Shikaris -> oral behavioural knowledge`

`writer -> translated/textualized manners corpus`

`native painter -> Hindi collection list`

`Hodgson -> own field memoranda/list/classification/publication states`

`artists -> drawing corpus`.

Mechanism:

`MANIFEST_PRESERVES_DISTRIBUTED_LABOUR_PROVENANCE`.

---

## 5. Receiver memorandum omits the eight unbound Darjeeling manners volumes

The receipt memorandum attached to the same transfer acknowledges:

- the 1,104 drawing sheets;
- Hodgson's manuscript list;
- native painter's Hindi list;
- one red-bound Nepal manners volume;
- Hodgson's Tarai memoranda;
- printed catalogues/reprints/papers.

It does not separately list the eight unbound Darjeeling manners volumes.

Mechanism:

`SENDER_RECEIPT_MANIFEST_MISMATCH`.

This mismatch cannot yet be translated into physical loss.

Possible states include:

- documentary omission in the receipt;
- incorporation under another receipt item;
- intended but not actually packed;
- separate handling;
- physical absence.

The archive supports only the mismatch.

## 6. April–May: the materials remain in Marshall's processing horizon

### 27 April 1870

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/11`.

Marshall says Hume's project is effectively in abeyance and that during the summer he can `arrange and systematize the Notes`. He sees the figures as useful for species identification and says the notes could later be incorporated.

### 20 May 1870

Source: `NZSL/HOD/5/3/12`.

Marshall refers to `your manuscripts` and `your notes`, proposes publishing them by family in *The Ibis*, and considers using valuable figures alongside the text.

Mechanisms:

- `FAILED_DESTINATION_REACTIVATES_LOCAL_PROCESSING`
- `ARRANGEMENT_BEFORE_PUBLICATION`
- `NOTES_FIGURES_RECOMBINATION_FOR_PRINT`.

The important source-controlled claim is that manuscript/notes material remained available for Marshall's processing after the Hume plan faltered. These letters do not enumerate the eight unbound volumes and therefore do not close the 11 February receipt mismatch.

## 7. 1870–71 *Capitonidae*: publication names the corpus through Hodgson

Source: Charles Henry Tilson Marshall and George Frederick Leycester Marshall, *A Monograph of the Capitonidae or Scansorial Barbets*, 1870–71.

In the treatment of *Megalaima asiatica*, the authors introduce a body of notes as taken from `Mr. B. H. Hodgson's manuscript observations on the ornithology of Nepal` and characterize that material as exceptionally careful and exhaustive.

Publication operation:

`selected manuscript notes -> extracted prose/evidence -> attribution to Hodgson as manuscript authority`.

Mechanism candidate:

`PUBLICATION_CREDIT_COMPRESSION_OF_DISTRIBUTED_PROVENANCE`.

### Caution

The 1870 package contains at least two manuscript provenance classes:

1. shikaree observations translated/recorded by the writer;
2. Hodgson's own field memoranda.

Without matching the published *M. asiatica* passage against HOD/1/3 or other surviving manuscripts, the exact source class of this published note remains unresolved.

The secure claim is narrower:

**the publication interface presents the reused manuscript corpus under Hodgson's name, whereas the private transfer manifest preserves finer-grained labour provenance.**

Whether this is a direct transformation of the same individual entries must be tested.

## 8. Three-volume survival creates a provenance hypothesis for the nine-volume manners corpus

Current ZSL `NZSL/HOD/1/3` preserves:

- No.1 / Vol.1: 1–169;
- No.3 / Vol.3: 340–488;
- No.6 / Vol.6: 755–855.

The 11 February 1870 sender manifest describes nine manners volumes in total:

- one red-bound Nepal volume;
- eight unbound Darjeeling volumes.

Hypothesis:

`CURRENT_HOD_1_3_AS_PARTIAL_SURVIVAL_OF_1870_NINE_VOLUME_MANNERS_CORPUS`.

Confidence: `medium-low / testable`.

Reasons for considering the hypothesis:

- identical subject/function: manners and habits of birds;
- identical labour architecture: shikarees + writer;
- surviving volume numbers 1, 3, 6 imply a larger numbered volume series;
- the 1870 transfer shows such a multi-volume corpus actively circulating with the drawings.

Reasons it remains unproven:

- public ZSL catalogue does not state accession provenance for HOD/1/3;
- the surviving three volumes are not publicly identified as the 1870 Marshall loan items;
- red-bound versus unbound physical states are not available in the current catalogue description;
- volume numbering and the nine-item count may belong to overlapping but not identical historical states.

Direct binding/title/ownership/accession evidence is required.

## 9. A more precise authorship transformation model

Avoid the simple binary:

`local knowledge -> European appropriation`.

The archive supports a multi-stage documentary transformation:

`field observer`

`-> viva voce statement`

`-> writer translation / inscription`

`-> numbered manners volume`

`-> portfolio/corpus custody`

`-> metropolitan species/group reassembly`

`-> taxon-selective abstraction`

`-> arrangement/systematization`

`-> publication prose`

`-> bibliographic attribution to Hodgson`.

Mechanism:

`DISTRIBUTED_PROVENANCE_TO_BIBLIOGRAPHIC_AUTHOR_COMPRESSION`.

This formulation makes paper operations analytically causal. Credit compression, if text-level collation confirms it, occurs through operations of selection, regrouping and publication rather than through a single act of erasure.

## 10. Paper-history significance

The same material changes epistemic identity as it moves through paper states:

### Manners notebook

Function:

`store translated behavioural observations keyed to drawings`.

### Four-portfolio archive

Function:

`distributed storage of drawings and notes, physically scattering taxon relations`.

### Marshall working arrangement

Function:

`reassemble species/group relations and select useful memoranda`.

### Printed monograph

Function:

`stabilize selected observations inside taxonomic prose and consolidate source authority bibliographically`.

Mechanism:

`PAPER_STATE_CHANGE_RECONFIGURES_PROVENANCE_VISIBILITY`.

The paper-centred article can therefore make a specific claim: **the visibility of labour provenance is not constant across documentary states**.

## 12. Immediate falsification tests

1. Collate the *Capitonidae* passage for *Megalaima asiatica* against HOD/1/3 entries keyed to the relevant Hodgson drawing number.
2. Search the monograph for every explicit citation to Hodgson manuscript observations and build a passage list.
3. Compare each passage against surviving HOD/1/3 volumes and Hodgson's own Tarai memoranda to distinguish shikaree/writer material from Hodgson-authored observations.
4. Inspect HOD/1/3 bindings, cover labels and volume numbering for `red bound`, `unbound`, Nepal/Darjeeling and 1870 custody traces.
5. Recover ZSL accession/provenance documentation for HOD/1/3 and determine whether the three surviving volumes arrived with the 1874 drawing donation or through another route.
6. Search post-May 1870 Marshall correspondence/publications for explicit references to carrying Hodgson manuscript parts to India or returning them.
7. Determine whether Marshall's 1870–71 barbet publication reproduces wording, numbers or distinctive behavioural details from the surviving manners volumes.
8. Keep `receipt omitted` distinct from `physically absent` for the eight Darjeeling volumes until stronger custody evidence appears.

## Bottom line

The 1870–71 episode may supply one of the strongest bridges between Hodgson's local research office and metropolitan publication. The private manifest preserves a differentiated knowledge chain in which shikarees speak, a writer translates/records, artists draw, a native painter lists, and Hodgson adds his own field and classificatory layers. Marshall then physically reorganizes and extracts from that corpus, while the resulting monograph presents reused manuscript authority under Hodgson's name. The transformation from distributed provenance to bibliographic authorship is visible structurally; passage-level collation is now the decisive test.
