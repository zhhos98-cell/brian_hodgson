# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 48
## Post-1874 annotated print branching: Hodgson's `Essays` continue to generate handwritten version states

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: publication-afterlife follow-up to Wave 47. This wave does not yet transcribe the military-tribe annotation itself; it reconstructs the existence, chronology and branching of annotated 1874 printed copies from Harihar Raj Joshi and Indu Joshi's 2002 bibliography of fugitive Hodgson materials.

## Executive result

The 1874 collected `Essays` did not become one terminal printed state.

At least several physical copies were subsequently annotated, corrected and augmented by Hodgson. Joshi reports:

- a corrected copy of `Essays` bearing `own copy corrected`, with pencil and black-ink additions and corrections;
- evidence that annotation on that copy began by 1876 and continued into/after the 1890s;
- another copy signed/dated `BHHodgson / Alderley Oct 1874`, with a long list of annotated pages;
- in that Alderley copy, Part II p.37 — the opening page of `Origin and Classification of the Military Tribes of Nepal` — is explicitly listed among annotated pages;
- still another copy inscribed to Mr Townshend on 1 October 1874 with further annotations.

Thus the publication stemma now has **branching after print**:

`1874 printed edition`
-> `annotated copy A`
AND
-> `annotated copy B`
AND
-> `annotated presentation copy C`.

Mechanisms:

- `POSTPUBLICATION_ANNOTATED_PRINT_BRANCHING`
- `PRINT_COPY_BECOMES_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION`
- `AUTHOR_COPY_AS_LONG_DURATION_REVISION_SURFACE`
- `PARALLEL_ANNOTATED_PRINT_STATES`

## 2. `Own copy corrected`: a long-duration author-copy revision surface

Joshi reports an `Essays of 1874` copy on whose title page Hodgson deleted a Susan Hodgson ownership inscription and wrote `own copy corrected`.

The copy contains:

- substantial additions;
- corrections;
- remarks in lead pencil;
- later black-ink writing over some pencil annotations;
- an annotated/corrected map of Nepal and Tibet.

Joshi argues from dated references that Hodgson was annotating this `Essays` copy from at least 1876 and that annotations include references to publications appearing after 1890.

The important material point is independent of the exact provenance speculation:

**one printed codex remained an active authorial working surface over many years and multiple writing media.**

Mechanisms:

- `LONG_DURATION_AUTHOR_COPY_REVISION`
- `PENCIL_TO_INK_ANNOTATION_LAYERING`
- `PRINTED_CODEX_AS_REUSABLE_RESEARCH_SURFACE`.

## 3. An Alderley October 1874 annotated copy includes Part II p.37

Joshi describes another `Essays` copy whose title page is recorded as:

`BHHodgson`

`Alderley Oct 1874`.

For Part II Joshi lists annotated pages including:

`2, 3, 4, 9, 13, 14, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 45, 37, 61, 65, 66, 67, 68, 76, 81, 92, 97, 104, 121`.

Part II p.37 is the opening page of `Origin and Classification of the Military Tribes of Nepal` in the 1874 edition.

Therefore:

`1874 military-tribe reprint`
-> `at least one later handwritten annotation state on its opening page`.

This is a high-value publication-afterlife fact.

Source-control:

- the annotation's exact wording is not exposed in Joshi's transcription;
- the page image was not retrievable in the present web session;
- do not infer that the annotation changes the classificatory table, recruitment argument, or any specific sentence.

Mechanism:

`MILITARY_TRIBES_PRINT_REOPENED_FOR_HANDWRITTEN_REVISION`.

## 4. Parallel copy with no military-tribe-page annotation listed

Joshi also describes `Yet Another Annotated copy of Hodgson's Essays of 1874`.

Its Part II annotated-page list is:

`1, 4, 13, 14, 15, 16, 29, 30, 32, 33, 34, 46, 61, 65, 66, 67, 68, 83`, with a reference added on p.55.

Pages 37–44 are not listed.

This matters because annotated print afterlife is **copy-specific**.

The fact that one annotated copy carries a p.37 intervention while another does not means the postpublication history cannot be represented as one universal `author's corrected 1874 text`.

Instead:

`same edition -> copy-specific manuscript layers`.

Mechanism:

`COPY_SPECIFIC_ANNOTATION_DIVERGENCE`.

## 5. Townshend presentation copy: a third branch

Joshi reports another annotated copy with Hodgson's inscription:

No military-tribe p.37 annotation is reported in the short list reproduced for this copy.

Again the important point is structural:

- edition identity remains shared;
- copy identity diverges;
- annotation histories diverge.

## 6. 1972 supplement confirms an author-copy annotation tradition, but exact witness still needs control

Bibliographic sources describe the 1972 Amsterdam reprint as including:

`Supplement of additions and corrections to Brian Houghton Hodgson's Essays on Nepal and Tibet`, edited by Mahadeva Prasad Saha from handwritten annotations in the author's copy.

This confirms that a later editor had access to an annotated Hodgson copy and treated its marginalia as a separate publishable textual layer.

However:

- the currently located web sources do not expose the full supplement text;
- it is not yet established which of the multiple annotated copies recorded by Joshi corresponds to Saha's source witness;
- do not merge all annotated copies into a single hypothetical exemplar.

Mechanisms:

- `HANDWRITTEN_MARGINALIA_REENTER_PRINT_IN_1972`
- `ANNOTATED_COPY_REQUIRES_WITNESS_IDENTITY_CONTROL`.

## 8. Paper-centred consequence

The relevant historical unit is no longer merely `edition`.

For Hodgson's later career:

`printed edition identity != textual/version identity`.

Individual physical copies become writable research objects.

A book can accumulate:

- pencil notes;
- black-ink overwriting;
- corrections;
- references to later publications;
- ownership and dating marks;
- map corrections;
- page-specific interventions.

Thus the paper article can follow a recursive cycle:

`manuscript -> print -> handwritten annotation on print -> revised/reprinted text`.

Mechanism:

`PRINT_MANUSCRIPT_RECURSION`.

## 9. Relation to intermediate-state fixation

Earlier repo work identified provisional manuscript states becoming durable through copying or print.

The annotated-print evidence shows the inverse movement:

`durable printed state -> reopened provisional working state`.

A supposedly fixed publication can be transformed back into an intermediate state by writing on the copy.

Mechanism:

`PRINTED_FINALITY_REVERSED_BY_ANNOTATION`.

This is a strong bridge between the paper article and the repo's earlier `INTERMEDIATE_STATE_FIXATION` mechanism.

## Bottom line

The 1874 edition did not stabilize Hodgson's military-tribe paper into one terminal text. Multiple physical copies of the same edition acquired divergent manuscript afterlives. At least one Hodgson-signed Alderley copy has a recorded annotation on Part II p.37, while another annotated copy has no military-tribe pages in its annotation list. A printed edition therefore branches into copy-specific handwritten versions, and at least one author-copy annotation tradition was later republished in Saha's 1972 supplement. Paper versioning continues after print at the level of the individual codex.
