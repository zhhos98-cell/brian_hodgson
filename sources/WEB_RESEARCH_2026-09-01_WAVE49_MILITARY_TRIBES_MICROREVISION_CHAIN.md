# Web research 2026-09-01 — Wave 49
## Military-tribes microrevision chain: 1833 → 1857 → 1874 → post-1874 annotated copies → 1972 selective reprint

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: paper-centred publication/version follow-up to Waves 45–48. This wave tests whether the 1874 corrections to `Origin and Classification of the Military Tribes of Nepal` restore earlier readings or constitute retrospective revisions.

## Executive result

The two explicit 1874 corrections within the military-tribes paper are not well described as simple restoration of the 1833 print.

Two microrevision histories are now visible.

### A. Historical dating

1833 JASB print:

`issued, 70 years ago, under the guidance of Prithvi Narayan`

1874 printed body:

`issued, some years ago, under the guidance of Prithvi Narayan`

1874 `List of Additions and Corrections`:

`p.40 line 4 top, for "some" read "about 100"`

Therefore the observable print history is at least:

`70 years -> some years -> about 100 years`.

The 1874 correction is not a return to the 1833 reading.

Mechanism:

`RETROSPECTIVE_CHRONOLOGICAL_RECALIBRATION`.

### B. Physiognomic wording

The 1857 `Selections` reprint preserves:

`Their physiognomies ... with the general caste and character fully developed in both.`

The 1874 correction apparatus says:

`p.39 line 17, for "caste" read "cast".`

Thus `caste -> cast` persists after at least one later printed state and is best treated as a deliberate semantic correction unless the October 1832 manuscript proves an earlier `cast` reading.

Mechanism:

`RETROSPECTIVE_SEMANTIC_DISAMBIGUATION`.

The correction shifts the immediate phrase from a social-hierarchical term (`caste`) toward a descriptive/type term (`cast`) within a physiognomic sentence.

## 1. 1833 print fixes the early chronological state at `70 years`

The 1833 JASB article is publicly searchable. In the paragraph introducing the Khás, it states that they issued from Gorkha `70 years ago` under Prithvi Narayan.

https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Nepal/1833%20Origin%20and%20Classification%20of%20Military%20Tribes%20of%20Nepal%20by%20Hodgson%20from%20JASBv2%20s.pdf

The PDF screenshot endpoint returned 403 during this wave. The searchable source text is therefore used, and image-level confirmation remains desirable.

## 2. 1874 base print no longer says `70 years`

The 1874 `Essays` body is searchable in a full-text transcription. At the corresponding sentence it reads:

`immediately that they issued, some years ago, under the guidance of Prithvi Narayan`.

Source:
https://www.scribd.com/document/299772597/Hodgson-Essays-on-the-Language

This establishes an intermediate print state:

`1833: 70 years`

→

`1874 base: some years`.

The mechanism cannot be reduced to a typo because the wording changes from a precise numeral to deliberate vagueness.

## 3. 1874 correction apparatus then changes `some` to `about 100`

The same 1874 volume's `List of Additions and Corrections` states:

`40, line 4 top, for "some" read "about 100".`

Thus the intended corrected 1874 reading becomes:

`about 100 years ago`.

The full microhistory is therefore:

`70 -> some -> about 100`.

This is a strong example of a printed historical claim being reopened and chronologically recalibrated across edition states.

Mechanism:

`PRINTED_CLAIM_REOPENED_FOR_RETROSPECTIVE_DATING`.

## 4. `caste -> cast` is also a real cross-state revision problem

The 1857 `Selections` state is publicly indexed with the physiognomic sentence:

`Their physiognomies, too, have peculiarities proper to each, but with the general caste and character fully developed in both.`

Source:
https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Nepal/1857%20Papers%20on%20Colonization%20Commerce%20Physical%20Geography%20%26c.%20%26c.%20to%20Himalaya%20Mountains%20and%20Nepal%20by%20Hodgson%20s.pdf

The source PDF itself returned 403 when opened in this wave, so this evidence is retained as indexed-text evidence rather than direct page transcription.

The 1874 correction apparatus then instructs:

`p.39 line 17, for "caste" read "cast".`

This makes `caste -> cast` a later semantic correction across printed states.

Until MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1 is physically read at the corresponding sentence, do not decide whether:

- `cast` restores the October 1832 fair manuscript reading;
- or Hodgson introduced `cast` only retrospectively.

## 5. October 1832 manuscript remains the decisive missing witness

Cambridge describes MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1 as:

- `Origin and classification of the Military tribes of Nepal`;
- dated `10-1832`;
- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- tabular view of Brahman, Khasa, Magar and Gurung family names;
- another copy survives in vol.9/10;
- the item is cross-referenced directly to the 1833 and 1874 publications.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol006.html

The online catalogue does not transcribe the prose sentence containing `cast/caste` or the Gorkha dating sentence.

Therefore the highest-value physical checks are:

1. Does the October 1832 manuscript read `cast` or `caste`?
2. Does it read `70`, `some`, `about 100`, or another chronological value?
3. Does vol.9/10 agree with vol.6/1?

Those readings can distinguish restoration from late authorial revision.

## 6. 1972 Amsterdam supplement is copy-specific and selective

Bibliographic descriptions identify the Philo Press Amsterdam 1972 edition as containing a 35-page:

`Supplement of additions and corrections ... edited from the handwritten annotations in the author's copy` by Mahadeva Prasad Saha.

Contemporary/near-contemporary bibliographic descriptions confirm this structure.

Harihar and Indu Joshi's 2002 bibliography adds critical provenance information. They describe a Hodgson 1874 `Essays` copy marked:

`Own Copy corrected 1876`.

Joshi then states that the Amsterdam edition was based on material from his collection and criticizes Saha for not incorporating all Hodgson annotations from that corrected copy.

Source:
https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Nepal/2002%20Harihar-Indus%20Bibliography%20of%20Hodgson--bibliography%20of%20fugitive%20but%20extant%20materials%20by%20Joshi%20s.pdf

The screenshot endpoint returned 403 for the relevant pages; searchable line text was available.

Source-control:

- treat `Saha used Joshi's corrected copy` as Joshi's assertion until Saha's own prefatory provenance statement is recovered;
- do not equate this exemplar with the separate `BHHodgson / Alderley Oct 1874` annotated copy;
- 1972 is a selective editorial extraction from an annotated print witness, not necessarily a diplomatic reproduction of all marginalia.

Mechanisms:

- `ANNOTATED_PRINT_TO_SELECTIVE_EDITORIAL_REPRINT`
- `EDITORIAL_EXTRACTION_IS_NOT_EXEMPLAR_REPLICATION`.

## 7. Post-1874 print branches remain multiple

Joshi records multiple Hodgson-annotated copies of the 1874 `Essays`.

One private copy bears:

`BHHodgson / Alderley Oct 1874`

and its Part II annotated-page census includes p.37, the first page of the military-tribes paper.

Another corrected copy is described as `Own Copy corrected 1876`.

Another is `Own Copy corrected since 1875`.

A Townshend presentation copy dated 1 October 1874 contains yet another annotation history.

Thus:

`1874 edition identity != physical-copy version identity`.

The correct late stemma is branching, not linear.

## 8. Revised version model

The military-tribes publication chain should now be represented as:

`1825 military/recruitment problem genealogy`

→ `1831 classificatory working pool`

→ `Oct 1832 fair machine-paper state(s)`

→ `1833 JASB print [70 years]`

→ `1857 reprint state [caste retained; dating state still to verify]`

→ `1874 printed base [some years]`

→ `1874 correction apparatus [cast; about 100]`

→ `multiple physical 1874 copies acquire new manuscript annotations`

→ `1972 Saha selectively extracts one corrected-copy branch into print`.

The late history is therefore:

`print -> corrected print -> manuscript-on-print branches -> selective print extraction`.

## 9. Article consequence

This scene strengthens the paper article in three ways.

### First

A printed page is not a terminal epistemic state.

It can be re-opened as a handwritten correction surface.

### Second

Edition-level and copy-level versioning must be distinguished.

### Third

Specific historical claims can move non-monotonically across versions:

`precision (70)`
→ `vagueness (some)`
→ `new precision (about 100)`.

This is not merely textual variation. It is retrospective recalibration performed through paper versions.

Mechanisms:

- `PRINT_IS_REOPENABLE_WORKING_SURFACE`
- `EDITION_STATE_DIFFERS_FROM_COPY_STATE`
- `RETROSPECTIVE_CHRONOLOGICAL_RECALIBRATION`
- `RETROSPECTIVE_SEMANTIC_DISAMBIGUATION`.

## Bottom line

The military-tribes paper now has a demonstrable microrevision history in which a chronological claim moves from `70 years` in 1833 to `some years` in the 1874 base text and then to `about 100` in the 1874 correction apparatus, while a physiognomic phrase retained as `caste` in the 1857 print is explicitly corrected to `cast` in 1874. The publication history then branches into multiple annotated physical copies, one of which was selectively mined for the 1972 Amsterdam supplement. The decisive unresolved witness is the October 1832 machine-paper fair manuscript: its readings can tell whether these later changes restore an early manuscript state or constitute retrospective authorial revision.
