## Finding 39 — Reel Three is demonstrably relevant to Campbell's 1830s Nepal investigations

Evidence status: **SECURE published citation into the Stiller Typescript.**

Mahesh C. Regmi and later Regmi-based scholarship cite Campbell materials directly to Stiller Typescript Reel Three, including:

- A. Campbell, `Principal Transactions and Early Intercourse`, 1834, cited to Stiller Typescript Reel Three;
- A. Campbell, `Report on the State of Agriculture in Nepal`, 1836, cited to Stiller Typescript Reel Three;
- A. Campbell, `Notes on the Agriculture and Rural Economy of the Valley of Nipal`, 20 February 1837, cited to Stiller Typescript Reel Three, with a page reference around p.220 in the cited typescript/publication sequence.

Public cross-controls:
- Mahesh C. Regmi, *An Economic History of Nepal 1846–1901*;
- Regmi Research Series index / issues;
- Regmi, *The State and Economic Surplus*.

This proves that the Kathmandu Stiller material is not merely a generic diplomatic microfilm set. It contains precisely the Campbell-era English administrative/economic investigations that form the immediate background to the 1837 Nepal-paper inquiry.

## Finding 40 — Kathmandu should be used as a content-level locator before Delhi for the 1801–1839 material

The project had previously treated the TUCL/NGMPP copy chiefly as a backup copy of NAI Foreign Political Records. The Stiller Typescript changes its practical value.

`Stiller Typescript / TUCL Kathmandu`

## Finding 41 — the 1837→1841 continuity remains plausible but unproven

P. K. Gode's later summary of Abdul Ali places the events adjacent:

`Lord Auckland made an inquiry about Nepal paper in 1837`
-> `British Government opened a factory at Darjeeling in 1841`.

Current searches have not recovered a primary text in which the 1841 Auckland -> Hodgson request for skilled papermakers explicitly cites or continues the 1837 inquiry.

Therefore preserve the source hierarchy:

- 1837 Auckland inquiry = supported by Abdul Ali/Gode secondary archival extraction; exact primary wording still to recover;
- Campbell's Nov 1837 procurement/testing correspondence = directly published / independently controlled;
- 1841 Auckland -> Hodgson request for skilled makers = directly reported by Campbell in the 1842 printed correspondence;
- `1837 inquiry directly caused the 1841 maker-transfer programme` = **NOT PROVEN**.

The Stiller Typescript is now the best bounded route to test this without broad archive trawling.
