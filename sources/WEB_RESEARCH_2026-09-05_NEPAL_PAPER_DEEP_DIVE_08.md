## Finding 33 — Campbell's early Darjeeling labour supply was politically mediated, not simply an open wage market

Evidence status: **SECURE historiographical reconstruction from India Office / NAI records; CONTEXTUAL for the papermaker case, not a direct causal join.**

Catherine Warner, `Flighty Subjects: Sovereignty, Shifting Cultivators, and the State in Darjeeling, 1830–1856`, *Himalaya* 34.1 (2014), reconstructs Campbell's labour mobilisation from India Office Board's Collections and National Archives of India records.

Key findings relevant to the 1841 paper experiment:

- in the early Darjeeling settlement, Campbell repeatedly obtained labour/craftsmen through cross-border patrons and intermediaries rather than through an impersonal labour market;
- a Tibeto-Sikkimese Kazi of Bhadaong sent craftsmen after Campbell supplied gifts/presents and a requested gun;
- Campbell himself reported that the authorities at Ilam had formerly discouraged or prohibited Nepalis from resorting to Darjeeling for service or trade, but later encouraged such movement as a way for people to obtain cash to pay rents;
- Kazi/Lama/intermediary figures could mobilise dependents across borders and negotiate political benefits in return;
- Company superiors themselves recognised that labour movement across Nepal/Sikkim/Darjeeling had diplomatic and sovereignty implications.

Useful source addresses in Warner:

- `Proceedings relating to Sikkim and Darjeeling`, Board's Collections, `F/4/1934`, no. 83500;
- `Nepaul and Catmandoo Residency`, Board's Collections, no. 87211, 1842–43 (Warner article gives F/4/1981; her dissertation citation should be cross-checked because it gives a nearby but inconsistent volume reference elsewhere);

### Consequence for the five papermakers

The known paper-transfer chain is:

`Auckland request -> Hodgson -> Nepal Durbar -> five skilled papermakers -> Darjeeling`.

The current evidence **does not tell us** what happened inside the crucial middle step:

`Nepal Durbar -> actual selection / obligation / agreement / dispatch of five persons`.

Do not assume:

- free individual wage contracting;
- coercive conscription;
- recruitment by a named Kazi;
- selection from Kimchat, Dholoka, Ilam or any other known papermaking centre;
- that the head maker personally recruited the other four.

All of these remain open.

But the contemporaneous labour history makes the missing step historically important rather than administrative trivia. In early Darjeeling, the movement of skilled bodies could be organised through patronage, political authority, dependency and cross-border bargaining even when colonial reports represented labour in wage-market language.

### Article value

Use this only to formulate a source question and to prevent a flattened transfer narrative:

**Who selected and authorised the five men whom the Nepal Durbar sent at Hodgson's request, and what obligations or bargaining relations made their movement possible?**

Do not write generic `local agency` or `coercive labour` unless the papermaker records establish it.

## Finding 34 — Campbell's 1841 papermaker party should be situated in a wider but distinct movement of artisans and labour into Darjeeling

The paper experiment occurred during the same formative period in which Darjeeling was being built through imported / cross-border labour and craft skill. This changes the appropriate background scale:

- the five papermakers were not the only skilled people physically relocated into the new hill station;
- Campbell's administrative practice already involved attracting craftsmen, road labour, servants and settlers across political frontiers;
- therefore `moving the makers` was simultaneously a paper experiment and one highly specialised instance of Darjeeling's wider labour-building process.

Analytical limit:

The article should not become a general history of Darjeeling labour migration. One or two paragraphs are enough unless a direct papermaker recruitment record links the case to a named intermediary or Ilam/Durbar labour policy.
