# Archive research wave 40 — paper folds into infrastructure: packets, wrappers, envelopes, covers, and functionally reassigned lists

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred reconstruction from Royal Asiatic Society Hodgson archive descriptions, extending Waves 35–39 from paper as support/revision/catalogue surface to paper as **container, boundary, index and reassignable documentary object**. Catalogue descriptions establish object relations but do not substitute for direct fold/edge/conjugacy inspection.

## Executive result

Hodgson's archive contains unusually explicit cases in which paper changes functional class without changing material class.

A sheet can become:

- a **packet** by folding;
- a **wrapper** around another document;
- an **index/list surface** on the outside of the packet;
- a **new writing surface** on the verso of an old letter;
- a **storage inventory** that later becomes a gift/donation list;
- an **envelope** whose primary operation is enclosure/routing;
- a **printed cover** that acquires ownership, dedication, privacy or revision metadata.

The central mechanism is:

`PAPER_FUNCTIONAL_REASSIGNMENT`.

This is stronger than generic `reuse` because the archive often preserves the earlier and later functions together.

## 1. BHH/11/15 — a large sheet is folded into a packet

Royal Asiatic Society shelfmark:

`GB 891 BHH-BHH/11-BHH/11/15 — Photos of Himal-races`, 1854–1879.

Catalogue description:

- a list of photographs of different Himalayan ethnic groups;
- one piece of paper is enclosed inside a **larger paper folded as a packet**;
- the folded outer paper itself carries a **similar list**;
- a newspaper header from the *Bengal Hurkaru*, 27 May 1854, is also enclosed;
- Hodgson writes on the newspaper piece `Nepal series No. 1 to 26 Besides duplicates. Nov 79`;
- total: handwritten and printed material, three pieces.

### Mechanisms

- `SHEET_TO_PACKET_CONVERSION`
- `PAPER_PACKET_AS_CONTAINER_AND_INDEX`
- `WRAPPER_METADATA_REDUNDANCY`
- `HETEROGENEOUS_PAPER_ENCLOSURE`

### Strong paper-history point

The outer paper performs two operations simultaneously:

1. **physical enclosure**;
2. **semantic indexing**.

The packet does not conceal its contents behind an opaque container identity. It externalizes a parallel description of what it holds.

Sequence:

`inner list + newspaper fragment -> outer sheet folded -> packet -> similar list written on packet exterior`.

The container and metadata surface are the **same sheet**.

---

## 2. Folding creates a new paper object through geometry

Before folding, the large paper is primarily a planar writing surface.

After folding, it acquires:

- interior/exterior distinction;
- enclosure capacity;
- edges/opening sequence;
- packet identity;
- outer metadata zone.

### Mechanism

`FOLDING_AS_FUNCTIONAL_OBJECT_TRANSFORMATION`.

### HPS formulation

A material transformation can occur without chemical or manufacturing change:

`sheet geometry + fold operation -> new documentary affordance`.

### Direct-inspection requirements

To reconstruct the packet historically, inspect:

- fold sequence;
- writing orientation relative to folds;
- whether outer list was written before or after folding;
- wear/dirtying on exterior panels;
- whether an address/title is visible when closed;
- whether folds match the size of enclosed pieces.

The catalogue does not supply these details.

## 3. BHH/11/15 — redundancy protects content identity

The inner paper and folded outer packet carry similar lists.

### Mechanism

`CONTAINER_CONTENT_METADATA_REDUNDANCY`.

Potential functions:

- identify packet without opening;
- check contents against outer description;
- preserve list information if one surface is separated;
- distinguish one photograph series/packet from another.

### Caution

The archive description supports `similar list`, not exact duplication.

### Research test

Compare:

`outer list -> inner list -> surviving photographs / numbers -> newspaper note`.

Differences may reveal different temporal states of the packet.

## 4. A newspaper fragment is reassigned as collection metadata

The packet encloses a newspaper header from 1854 on which Hodgson later writes a note marked `Nov 79` concerning the Nepal photograph series and duplicates.

### Mechanisms

- `PRINTED_FRAGMENT_TO_METADATA_SURFACE`
- `TEMPORALLY_LAYERED_REUSE`.

### Importance

A printed fragment whose original informational function was periodical identification acquires a new handwritten collection-management function decades later.

### Caution

Do not call it `waste paper` unless disposal/recovery from waste is independently demonstrated. `Printed fragment reused as metadata surface` is secure.

## 5. BHH/11/17 — a Hooker letter is later used as a wrapper

Royal Asiatic Society shelfmark:

`GB 891 BHH-BHH/11-BHH/11/17 — Memo of published work - books - now here Dec/82`, 1868–1882.

Catalogue description:

- rough list of books;
- **wrapped round this list is a letter/note from Joseph Hooker** expressing pleasure that Hodgson is in England;
- Hodgson uses the **reverse of the Hooker letter** for notes concerning the return of a book to Hooker in 1868;
- two pieces, four sides.

### Mechanisms

- `LETTER_AS_WRAPPER`
- `SOCIAL_DOCUMENT_TO_ARCHIVAL_CONTAINER`
- `VERSO_REUSE`
- `MULTIPLE_FUNCTIONS_ONE_SHEET`.

### Functional biography

At least three states can be separated:

1. `interpersonal communication`;
2. `verso note surface for book-return information`;
3. `physical wrapper around a later rough book list`.

The same paper accumulates communicative, administrative and container functions.

---

## 6. Wrapper reuse may preserve a semantic relation

The inner document is a book list. The older Hooker letter's verso already contains a note about returning a book to Hooker.

### Hypothesis

`semantic relation may influence later physical wrapping relation`.

### Evidence status

`PLAUSIBLE_RELATIONAL_REUSE`, not demonstrated intention.

The archive establishes the physical wrapper relation and book-related verso note, but not Hodgson's reason for selecting that sheet.

### Falsification target

Direct inspection should determine which face was exterior and whether visible wording acted as an index for the enclosed list.

---

## 7. Reuse creates historical adjacency

Once the Hooker letter wraps the 1882 list, two documentary moments become one physical unit.

### Mechanism

`REUSE_GENERATED_ADJACENCY`.

Chronology:

`1868 Hooker communication / Hodgson book-return note`

becomes physically adjacent to

`Dec. 1882 book list`.

### Key distinction

- `HODGSON_GENERATED_REUSE_ADJACENCY` — created by historical reuse;
- `ARCHIVE_GENERATED_BINDING_ADJACENCY` — created by 1921 India Office binding.

Both alter what survives together, but at different historical stages.

## 8. BHH/11/14 — a store list becomes an institutional gift list

Royal Asiatic Society shelfmark:

`GB 891 BHH-BHH/11-BHH/11/14 — List of MSS presented to the India Office`, 1858–1864.

The catalogue states that the manuscript list appears initially to have been:

`a store list compiled for Hodgson in 1858`

and later became:

`the list of manuscripts given to the India Office in 1864`.

It survives with a copy of Hodgson's letter to Sir Charles Wood concerning acceptance of the donation and listing the articles to be given.

### Mechanisms

- `LIST_FUNCTIONAL_REASSIGNMENT`
- `STORE_INVENTORY_TO_GIFT_INSTRUMENT`
- `DOCUMENTARY_STATE_PERSISTENCE`.

### Strong point

The list carries a private storage state into an institutional transfer state.

`private collection control -> retained documentary list -> donation decision -> institutional gift instrument`.

This is a change of documentary function without necessarily requiring a new intellectual classification from scratch.

## 9. This directly extends Wave 39's catalogue-wrapper result

Wave 39 established that in the 1875 Cowell–Eggeling manuscript catalogue a wrapper could carry the only recoverable title of a manuscript (`WRAPPER_AS_SEMANTIC_IDENTITY_SURFACE`).

Wave 40 shows the practice-side analogue:

- Hodgson's own working archive uses folded paper as packet and index;
- a pre-existing letter can be reused as wrapper;
- exterior surfaces can carry metadata needed to understand the interior.

### Combined mechanism

`WRAPPER_AS_PROTECTION_PLUS_SEMANTIC_INFRASTRUCTURE`.

A later catalogue field is therefore grounded in a widespread documentary operation: paper enclosures can be epistemically significant because they externalize content identity.

- packing manifest;
- gift list;
- catalogue draft;
- content index;
- drawing enumeration;
- book-return memo;

### Mechanism

`LIST_OPERATIONAL_POLYVALENCE`.

### HPS significance

This gives a concrete route from administrative paper technologies to scientific paper practices.

## 11. Envelope, packet, wrapper and cover are distinct paper boundary objects

### Envelope

A purpose-made/conventionally formed enclosure and routing surface. Some Hodgson correspondence survives with accompanying envelope.

Code: `ENVELOPE_AS_ROUTING_CONTAINER`.

### Folded packet

A larger sheet transformed by folding into an enclosure.

BHH/11/15.

Code: `SHEET_TO_PACKET_CONVERSION`.

### Reused wrapper

A pre-existing document repurposed around another object.

BHH/11/17.

Code: `REUSED_DOCUMENT_AS_WRAPPER`.

### Printed cover

Boundary component of a printed book/offprint which can acquire later handwritten metadata.

Wave 38 examples include:

- `Own Copy`;
- dedications;
- privacy/publication status;
- contribution/correction claims.

Code: `PRINTED_COVER_AS_PARATEXTUAL_WORK_SURFACE`.

### Rule

All create boundaries, but their production histories and informational affordances differ.

## 12. Exterior surfaces are access metadata

Across packet, envelope, wrapper and printed cover, information can migrate to the outside surface:

- addressee;
- sender;
- series title/number;
- contents list;
- ownership;
- privacy status;
- destination;
- return/loan instruction.

### Mechanism

`EXTERIOR_SURFACE_AS_ACCESS_METADATA`.

### Operation

`closed/unopened object -> exterior inscription makes internal identity/routing partly legible`.

This solves an infrastructural problem: knowing enough to route or identify an enclosure without opening it.

## 13. Paper enclosure can preserve relations

BHH/11/15:

`inner list + outer similar list + newspaper series note`.

BHH/11/17:

`older Hooker/book note + newer book list`.

### Mechanism

`RELATIONAL_ENCLOSURE`.

The container is not epistemically neutral because selection and exterior writing can encode how inner materials belong together.

This restores the repo's older `relational preservation` mechanism to a specifically paper-centred scale.

## 14. Compare paper enclosure with more resistant protective materials

The 1843 Wallich bill includes:

- Nepali paper;
- wax cloth;
- thread/twine.

Earlier manuscript-production bills include binding, bundling, carpentry and porterage.

### Research question

When is paper sufficient as enclosure, and when are wax cloth, box, bundle or other materials needed?

Potential variables:

- desk/storage vs transport;
- humidity/moisture exposure;
- object weight;
- transport duration;
- reopening frequency;
- permanence of grouping.

### Mechanism

`ENCLOSURE_MATERIAL_BY_OPERATIONAL_INTERFACE`.

No hierarchy should be assumed before comparative evidence.

## 15. Folding creates documentary topology

A flat sheet has recto and verso.

A folded packet creates:

- outer panel;
- inner panel;
- hidden text;
- visible title/list;
- opening edge;
- access sequence.

### Mechanism

`GEOMETRY_GENERATES_DOCUMENTARY_TOPOLOGY`.

Paper form controls:

- visibility;
- sequence of access;
- content grouping;
- metadata exposure.

This is a literal paper tool produced through geometry.

## 17. Article-level claim

**Paper in Hodgson's archive did not remain a planar support. A large sheet could be folded into a packet and simultaneously carry a duplicate contents list; an old Hooker letter could acquire notes on its verso and later wrap a new book list; a private store inventory could survive long enough to become an institutional gift list. Such transformations made paper a container, index and routing device. Folding and reuse created new inside/outside relations and documentary adjacencies without requiring a new manufactured material.**

## Bottom line

Wave 40 makes paper geometry and reuse central to infrastructure. In Hodgson's archive, a sheet can become a packet simply by folding; the new exterior can index the interior; an old letter can become a wrapper while retaining its earlier correspondence and verso notes; and a private storage list can be reassigned to an institutional donation without losing its previous documentary history. Paper makes knowledge portable not only because it is light and writable, but because its form can be repeatedly reorganized into surfaces, containers, indexes and transfer instruments.
