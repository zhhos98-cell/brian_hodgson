# Web research 2026-08-31 — Wave 33
## Falsifying the simple actor hypothesis: writer identity, workplace access and paper selection

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: paper-centred falsification pass following Waves 31–32. The initial working hypothesis was that English derivatives produced by local/native writers might preferentially retain Nepalese handmade paper, while Hodgson/Campbell analytical states might preferentially use machine/imported paper.

## Executive result

The simple actor hypothesis fails.

There is a real cluster of English translations/notes in local or non-native English hands on Nepali handmade paper, especially in `vol.21`. But explicit counterexamples show Hodgson's local research assistants writing English on machine-made paper.

Therefore:

`actor ethnicity/origin -> support choice`

is not a defensible model.

The better variables are:

- workplace and access to stock;
- whether the derivative was made inside the Residency's analytical/administrative workflow;
- task format and intended next operation;
- fair/rough state;
- individual writer/assistant role;
- date and available inventory.

Mechanisms:

- `ACTOR_ETHNICITY_DOES_NOT_DETERMINE_SUPPORT`
- `WORKPLACE_STOCK_ACCESS_AS_CANDIDATE_VARIABLE`
- `TASK_ALLOCATION_MEDIATES_SUPPORT_SELECTION`
- `LOCAL_ASSISTANT_CAN_USE_IMPORTED_MACHINE_STOCK`

---

## 1. Why the actor hypothesis initially looked plausible

Cambridge describes MSS EUR HODGSON/21 as a volume whose translations, summaries and notes are mostly the work of one of Hodgson's native assistants.

Several English items are on Nepalese handmade paper.

Examples:

### vol.21/3

- English account of Siddhinarasimha Malla;
- Nepali hand-made paper;
- rough hand of a non-native user of English;
- linked to a Nepali original.

### vol.21/6

- English Buddhist account of Nepal;
- Nepali hand-made paper;
- rough hand of a native writer.

### vol.21/1

- English account of principal Bandya rites;
- Nepali hand-made paper;
- rough hand of a non-native user of English.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol021.html

This cluster suggested:

`local writer production layer -> retained country-paper stock`.

As a local hypothesis, this remains worth testing.

As a general actor rule, it fails.

---

## 2. Counterexample: Ashraf Ali research-assistant Q&A on machine-made paper

MSS EUR HODGSON/9 item 12, ff.72–89:

- questions and answers about Bhote/Tibetans;
- English;
- **machine-made paper**;
- answers by **Ashraf Ali**, a Kashmiri trader and one of Hodgson's research assistants;
- poor hands with many corrections/addenda.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol009.html

This directly falsifies:

`local/non-European assistant -> Nepali handmade support`.

Ashraf Ali's assistant status did not prevent his English work from entering the machine-paper research workflow.

Mechanism:

`LOCAL_ASSISTANT_CAN_USE_IMPORTED_MACHINE_STOCK`.

---

## 3. Counterexample: Ramtanu writes on machine-made paper

MSS EUR HODGSON/14 item 10:

- a note from **Ramtanu (Ram Tunnu)** to Hodgson;
- English;
- **machine-made paper**;
- fair hand;
- Ramtanu's own writing;
- concerns goods exported from highlands/lower hills to the Tarai and India.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol014.html

Nearby item 5 is Hodgson's machine-paper letter/instructions to Ramtanu to collect information on labour and market prices.

This suggests a paper-working environment shared across principal and assistant rather than separate paper regimes mapped onto personal origin.

Mechanism:

`SHARED_WORKPLACE_PAPER_ACCESS`.

## 5. Hodgson himself also uses Nepalese handmade paper

The reverse actor determinism fails as well.

Cambridge records Hodgson's own English writing on Nepalese handmade paper, for example:

- `vol.7/23`, English route from Kathmandu to Ratanpur, Nepalese handmade paper, Hodgson's own hand;
- other rough/local-route and note states in vol.7 occur on Nepalese handmade support.

Source:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol007.html

Therefore:

`Hodgson -> machine/imported`

is also false.

Mechanism:

`PRINCIPAL_ACTOR_CAN_USE_COUNTRY_PAPER`.

---

## 6. Revised actor variable

The actor field remains useful, but it must be decomposed.

Do not code only:

`British / local`.

Better fields:

- named writer;
- occupational role: Resident / Assistant Resident / munshi / writer / research assistant / informant / pandit / artist;
- handwriting attribution confidence;
- workplace/context of production;
- language competence/state;
- whether writing is source elicitation, transcription, translation, analysis, fair copy, report or correspondence;
- evidence of paper being supplied by Hodgson/Residency versus brought with source material.

The causal candidate becomes:

`role + workplace + task + stock access`

rather than ethnic/national identity.

---

## 7. Stock access may be more important than actor identity

A plausible model now is:

### Source-generated outside or at the edge of Residency workflow

Often arrives on:

`Nepali handmade / palm leaf / other locally circulating support`.

### Derivative produced inside Residency analytical workflow

Can draw on:

`machine/imported stock`

regardless of whether the writer is Hodgson, Campbell or a local assistant.

### But

Residency work can also remain on Nepalese handmade paper when:

- that stock is available;
- the state is rough/intermediate;
- a local writer continues source-side production conventions;
- or operation/format does not call for another stock.

This model fits the current evidence better but remains provisional.

Mechanism:

`WORKPLACE_STOCK_ACCESS_AS_CANDIDATE_VARIABLE`.

---

## 8. Documentary state still matters

Compare:

- Ramtanu's fair machine-paper note;
- Ashraf Ali's corrected machine-paper Q&A;
- native-writer rough English Buddhist accounts on handmade paper;
- Campbell's large machine-paper revenue chart;
- Hodgson's own rough English route on handmade paper.

No single variable among:

- language;
- writer origin;
- rough/fair state;
- subject;

is sufficient by itself.

The next model must be multivariable and object-level.

Working formulation:

`support selection = stock access × workplace × task state × format × writer role × intended next operation`.

---

## 9. Falsification rule for the article

Do not write:

`local assistants wrote translations on Nepalese paper, whereas Hodgson and Campbell converted them onto European paper`.

The archive already falsifies that sentence.

Defensible formulation:

**Paper allocation crossed personnel categories. Local assistants as well as Hodgson and Campbell could work on machine-made stock, while Hodgson himself also wrote English notes on Nepalese handmade paper. What mattered was not actor origin alone but the paper stocks available within particular work settings and the documentary state being produced.**

## Bottom line

The first actor hypothesis failed in a productive way. Paper support does not map cleanly onto British versus local writers. Hodgson's research assistants could use machine-made paper; Hodgson could use Nepalese handmade paper. The stronger historical object is a **shared but uneven paper-working environment** in which access to stock, workplace, role, task and derivative state jointly structured paper allocation.
