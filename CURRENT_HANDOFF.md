## Writing rule

The article should use the language of the sources whenever it already names the problem. Current section anchors are:

1. `Not at all successful`
2. `Each size and quality ... for experimental purposes`
3. `Altogether useless for paper-making`
4. `Twenty-four packages of Nepalese paper pulp`

Prefer concrete acts and actors: Hodgson sends specimens; manufacturers work up `Nipal Paper Stuff`; Campbell calculates carriage; the Society requests sizes and qualities; papermakers choose bark, ash and water; O’Shaughnessy bleaches; Marshman tests the machine; Hodgson routes twenty-four packages; Political Consultations and Board’s Collections preserve later documentary states.

Research shorthand such as `affordance`, `affordance reversal`, `institutional ontology`, `interface`, `custody checksum`, `reconciliation surface`, `intermediate-state fixation`, `documentary neighbourhood` and similar project labels belongs in research notes and datasets. Do not import it into article prose unless a source itself uses the term.

Keep explanations short when the source already carries the point. One fact may take one sentence. Names can flash briefly when they only identify a route, experiment or institutional handoff.

## Current factual spine

The draft can already use these points without reopening broad research:

- 1831–32: Hodgson describes manufacture, sends specimens for Bentinck and recommends the paper for records; Political Consultation, 13 January 1832, Nos. 18–20 is the controlled archival address.
- 1833: British trials of `Nipal Paper Stuff` are `not at all successful`; finished Nepal-made sheets are praised as tough and take a bank-note impression with `perfect fidelity`.
- 1837: Campbell treats Nepal paper as a government-supply question involving price, season and carriage; the Agricultural and Horticultural Society asks for `each size and quality ... for experimental purposes`.
- 1841: five papermakers work at Darjeeling; spoiled bark becomes `altogether useless for paper-making`; bleaching, filtering and waterproofing tests follow.
- 1842: Serampore tests the material on machinery; Hodgson sends `twenty-four packages of Nepalese paper pulp` toward Calcutta and asks for comparative land/water carriage costs.
- documentary chain: underlying letters and consultation dates must remain separate; Board’s Collections regroup Indian Proceedings in London for draft-despatch review.

## Remaining bounded gaps

These are source questions, not reasons to reopen generic discovery:

- Halliday’s initiating directive for the 25 March 1842 pulp shipment;
- Woodcock’s bill and actual route decision;
- terminal Calcutta receipt for the twenty-four packages;
- direct evidence of Chamber-of-Commerce receipt/testing after Campbell’s 1837 proposal;
- item-level crosswalk between Political Consultation 11 May 1842 No. 70 and IOR/F/4/2006/89528;
- exact London source chain for Political Consultation 29 November 1841 No. 147.

Do not identify the twenty-four packages as Serampore feedstock without a direct receipt or forwarding record. Do not turn temporal proximity into one continuous government programme.
