# Hodgson paper-history mainline — 2026-09-02, through Wave 70

Status: current active guardrail. Supersedes `PAPER_HISTORY_MAINLINE_STATUS_2026-09-02_WAVE66.md` for ongoing work. Waves 67–70 add **stationery supply control, paper metrology, local counting vocabularies, and context-dependent package depth** to the paper-history argument. Publication/classification/person-network work remains parked unless it directly reveals a paper operation.

## Current object

The article is a history of paper as a repeatedly remade material unit:

`fibre/raw material`
-> `manufacturing process`
-> `commercial/local sheet`
-> `counting/bundling unit`
-> `procurement/acquisition`
-> `stock/storage time`
-> `cutting/folding/trimming`
-> `working-format allocation`
-> `writing/drawing/copying`
-> `recopying onto newly allocated support`
-> `binding/bundling/packaging`
-> `transport/container`
-> `custody/rebinding`
-> `modern catalogue object`.

Paper therefore carries at least eight distinct histories:

1. **process history** — how physical properties were produced;
2. **metrological history** — how sheets were counted, bundled and priced;
3. **supply history** — how paper entered a Residency/research environment;
4. **stock-time history** — how long stock remained before use;
5. **format history** — how parent stock was cut/folded into task units;
6. **work history** — how information/images moved across supports;
7. **logistics history** — how paper was bound, wrapped, bundled, boxed and transported;
8. **custody history** — how later institutions selected, rebound and catalogued the surviving objects.

Core equation:

`research support = manufacture × local/commercial metrology × acquisition channel × stock time × format conversion × task allocation × packaging/logistics × later custody`.

## A. Manufacture and material affordance remain the opening scenes

### Cowan 1829/1833

Related Himalayan material moved as dried/prepared pulp did not reproduce the properties of successful Nepal-made finished paper when remanufactured by Charles Cowan in Britain.

Mechanisms:

- `PROCESS_HISTORY_EMBODIED_IN_SHEET`
- `MATERIAL_IDENTITY_DOES_NOT_PRESERVE_PROCESS_PROPERTIES`.

### Darjeeling 1841

Moving skilled Nepali papermakers allowed process reconstruction that material transfer alone had failed to reproduce. Washing, vessel material, bleaching and toughness/whiteness trade-offs exposed calibrated embodied practice.

Mechanisms:

- `EMBODIED_PROCESS_TRANSFER`
- `TACIT_CRAFT_KNOWLEDGE_EXCEEDS_PRINTED_RECIPE`.

### Serampore

Long-fibre/mucilaginous properties useful in hand formation could become machine-web problems.

Mechanism:

`AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

## B. Stock time and working-format standardization remain series-level

RAS 022 architectural drawings preserve multiple watermark years within one c.1844 programme, including 1821, 1837 and 1841.

Recovered numbered drawings:

- 47 with paper dimensions;
- 43 in the dominant long-format family;
- mean about `30.10 × 48.54 cm`;

## G. Wave 67: Company stationery supply makes paper quality auditable through packages and samples

A 1725 Court of Directors despatch responding to Madras complaints about paper quality supplies a clean early control model.

The complaint sample was drawn from the outside `corded quires` of a ream, which were expected to be poorer than the interior paper. The Court therefore required chest marks/numbers so defective paper could be traced because more than one London stationer supplied the Company.

Documentary/material chain:

`stationer`
-> `ream / internal quire position`
-> `chest mark/number`
-> `shipment`
-> `physical muster/sample`
-> `quality judgment`
-> `supplier attribution`.

Nearer Hodgson's period, secondary reconstruction from precise East India Company records identifies Calcutta stationery shortages, inter-presidency paper redistribution and physical paper musters in the early nineteenth century. These remain primary-retrieval targets.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_SAMPLE_HAS_POSITIONAL_BIAS`
- `PACKAGE_IDENTIFIER_LINKS_QUALITY_TO_SUPPLIER`
- `PAPER_MUSTER_AS_DISTANCE_QUALITY_CONTROL`
- `STATIONERY_SUPPLY_IS_TRACKED_BY_NESTED_PACKAGE_UNITS`.

## H. Wave 68: paper counting systems are not automatically equivalent across regions

Comparators retained:

### English/Company

`sheet -> quire -> ream -> chest`.

## I. Wave 69 recovered real Nepali paper vocabulary, but Wave 70 removes the universal numerical assumption

### Secure lexical identity

Nepali paper vocabulary includes:

- `ताउ / tau` — sheet/folio-level count;
- `ढेप / dhep` — paper bundle/count term;
- `कोरी / kori` — larger score/batch term.

Cambridge f.117's `ḍhyāp` remains a very high lexical candidate for `ढेप / dhep`.

### Ten-sheet convention

Bhupahari Paudel, *Ancient Nepal* no.103 (1987–88), printed p.33:

`10 tau = 1 dhep`

`20 dhep = 1 kori`

`1 kori = 200 tau`.

Modern Nepali lexicography stabilizes the same ten-sheet value for `dhep`.

Correct status:

`TEN_SHEET_DHEP = STRONG_DOCUMENTED_CONVENTION`.

### Twenty-sheet western-Nepal field convention

Griha Man Singh, *Ancient Nepal* no.144 (March 2000), reports his 1998–99 field study in Myagdi, Parbat and Baglung.

Printed p.16 states that **twenty dried sheets are tied with thin bast strips into one bundle called a Dhep**.

This is direct locality-specific production evidence.

Material operation:

`20 individual dried sheets`
-> `bast-strip tying`
-> `one dhep`.

### 200-sheet statement in NAST synthesis

A c.2013 NAST indigenous-technology report directly prints:

`one dhep usually contains 200 paper sheets`.

But the report explicitly relies largely on secondary sources and does not supply a locality-specific field observation for this statement. Because 200 is exactly the independently documented `kori` total under the ten-sheet convention, unit-level conflation remains a strong candidate.

Current state:

`NAST_DHEP_200 = DIRECT_TEXT / PRACTICE_STATUS_UNRESOLVED / KORI_CONFLATION_CANDIDATE_HIGH`.

### Revised mechanism

Do not write:

`dhep = 10 sheets` as a universal conversion.

Write:

**`dhep` is a secure Nepali handmade-paper package term whose reported numerical depth depends on locality, workshop or reporting tradition.**

Mechanisms:

- `LOCAL_UNIT_NAME_DOES_NOT_FIX_PACKAGE_DEPTH`
- `COUNT_UNIT_IS_PHYSICALLY_MADE_BY_BUNDLING`
- `METROLOGY_IS_EMBODIED_IN_PACKAGING_PRACTICE`
- `LEXICAL_STANDARDIZATION_CAN_HIDE_PRACTICE_VARIATION`
- `SECONDARY_SYNTHESIS_CAN_COLLAPSE_ADJACENT_UNIT_LEVELS`.

## K. Current data model

Do not store a local paper unit as `term -> one universal number`.

For each attestation record:

- exact term/script;
- romanization source;
- reported sheet depth;
- locality;
- date of practice;
- date of source;
- observation type;
- production stage;
- physical bundling method;
- price/wage function;
- source directness;
- normalization confidence.

Core rule:

`metrological value = term × locality × period × production operation × reporting practice`.

## L. Current strongest paper-history proposition

**Hodgson's paper world was organized not only by paper types but by operations that repeatedly remade paper into new units. Manufacture gave sheets their physical properties; local and Company counting systems grouped those sheets into different accountable packages; bills and collaborators moved those packages into working stocks; cutting and folding converted parent stock into task-sized surfaces; copying transferred information between supports; and binding, wrapping, boxing and archival custody produced further material units. Even a term such as `dhep` cannot be reduced to a universal sheet count: direct evidence records different package depths in different production contexts. Paper metrology was therefore part of the material history of knowledge, not merely a modern conversion problem.**

## Guardrails

1. Paper only unless another topic reveals a material paper operation.
2. Do not normalize local units before locality/date/context are controlled.
3. Direct visual page evidence outranks OCR snippets.
4. Direct text can still be secondary evidence for practice; source directness and observation directness are separate fields.
5. Do not call `dhep` universally ten, twenty or two hundred sheets.
6. Do not treat English `quire`, Nepali `dhep`, `kori`, Kashmir/North Indian `dasta/takhta` as equivalent units.
7. Keep acquisition mode, information provenance and writer identity separate.
8. Keep catalogue measurements and catalogue glosses as historically mediated data, not transparent object facts.
