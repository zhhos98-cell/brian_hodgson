# Paper cross-support argument v0.3

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: supersedes the evidential architecture of `notes/PAPER_CROSS_SUPPORT_ARGUMENT_V0_2.md` without deleting it. v0.3 adds directly documented leaf-level support conversion and explicit copy/archetype relations. The central correction is that `copy genealogy` and `support transition` must be treated as independent variables.

## Core thesis refinement

The paper argument should no longer be framed primarily as a statistical transition from palm-leaf geometry to paper geometry.

The evidence now supports a stronger process history:

**Paper changes manuscript histories through several distinct operations: it can widen or otherwise alter the available format space; it can preserve older oblong conventions; it can replace a damaged leaf without replacing the work; it can carry copied dates and colophons from an earlier support; it can reproduce a text while leaving support class unchanged; and it can be deliberately commissioned as a new surrogate object for institutional collection.**

The unit of analysis is therefore not `support` alone but:

`genealogical relation + support state + scale of replacement + chronology + hand + layout + institutional destination`.

## Evidence ladder

### Level 1 — corpus association

Cowell–Eggeling RAS 79:

- palm leaf and paper occupy different proportional regimes at corpus level;
- strongest distinction lies in short dimension/aspect ratio;
- paper permits a broader format range.

Claim permitted:

`SUPPORT_FORMAT_ASSOCIATION`.

Claim not permitted:

`PALM_LEAF_TO_PAPER_WIDTH_EXPANSION` as a universal transition rule.

---

### Level 2 — internal counterexample

RAS MSS 34–35:

- same Nāmasaṅgīti root-text ecology, different commentaries;
- palm leaf MS 34: 12 × 2¼, 7 lines;
- paper MS 35: 12 × 2, 10 lines.

Paper is narrower and denser here.

Result:

`SUPPORT_AFFORDANCE_NOT_FORMAT_DETERMINISM`.

---

### Level 3 — exact-work cross-support controls

Examples now include:

- Bodhicaryāvatāra: RAS MS 13 palm leaf vs Cambridge Add. 869 paper;
- Ekallavīra/Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa: RAS MS 46 palm leaf vs Cambridge Add. 1319 paper;
- Gaṇḍavyūha: RAS MS 2 palm leaf vs Cambridge paper witnesses;
- Aṣṭasāhasrikā: palm-leaf and multiple historically distinct paper regimes.

These hold named textual identity more constant, but most still lack direct copy lineage.

Result:

`EXACT_WORK_CROSS_SUPPORT_PAIR_WITHOUT_COPY_LINEAGE`.

---

### Level 4 — direct support conversion within one manuscript history

Cambridge Add. 1644 provides the first direct support-conversion event.

A 1205 palm-leaf Pañcarakṣā has a mutilated final leaf. A modern paper supply leaf preserves a copy of that final leaf from an earlier, less damaged state and reproduces its old date.

This relation is not inferred from dimensions or title identity. Bendall describes the material event explicitly.

Process:

`palm-leaf deterioration -> selective copying -> modern paper supply -> older textual ending/date retained -> composite manuscript continues`.

Result:

- `PALM_LEAF_DAMAGE_TO_PAPER_SUPPLY`
- `SUPPORT_CONVERSION_AS_PRESERVATION_REPAIR`
- `PARTIAL_SUPPORT_CONVERSION_WITHIN_MANUSCRIPT_OBJECT`

This should become a major article vignette.

---

### Level 5 — same genealogy, no support-class transition

Cambridge Add. 1475 supplies the control.

Its damaged final paper leaf is replaced by a fresh copy on different paper, carrying a copied old date.

Process:

`paper damage -> new paper replacement -> copied textual/temporal state`.

Result:

`copy relation = yes; support class change = no`.

Together Add. 1644 and 1475 prove:

**copy genealogy and support transition are orthogonal variables.**

Mechanism:

`COPY_GENEALOGY_ORTHOGONAL_TO_SUPPORT_TRANSITION`.

---

### Level 6 — explicit full-work genealogy

Cambridge Add. 1478 is explicitly described by Bendall as the `archetype of the Hodgson MS. (No. 15) in the India Office`.

This is currently the strongest full-work copy-genealogy statement tied directly to a Hodgson manuscript.

What is secure:

- source witness: Cambridge Add. 1478;
- source support: paper;
- source date/hand: fourteenth–fifteenth century, chiefly Bengali hand;
- relation: archetype -> Hodgson India Office MS No. 15.

What remains unresolved:

- target shelfmark in later India Office numbering;
- target support;
- target dimensions/line density/hand/date.

A false intermediate identification must be retained as a methodological caution: `General Collection No. 15 -> Catalogue No. 957` is unrelated because catalogue no. 957 is a lexicographical manuscript. Number identity without title control is insufficient.

Mechanisms:

- `EXPLICIT_ARCHETYPE_TO_HODGSON_COPY_CHAIN`
- `NUMBERING_COLLISION_REJECTED_BY_TITLE_CONTROL`

If the target support is eventually identified, this chain can become the strongest test of whether a full-work Hodgson copy changed support and geometry relative to its known archetype.

---

### Level 7 — institutional production of paper surrogates

Bendall’s Cambridge preface explicitly states that Wright was initially asked to procure copies `made to order` from works still extant in Nepal. Add. 1042 represents the initial specimen programme.

Add. 865 Divyāvadāna gives the operational version:

- paper;
- modern Nepalese hand;
- written 1873;
- copied to order for Cambridge through Daniel Wright;
- copied from the only early copy then known, still preserved at Kathmandu.

This is not passive collecting. A European library causes a new manuscript object to be manufactured in Nepal because textual access is desired elsewhere.

Mechanisms:

- `INSTITUTIONALLY_COMMISSIONED_MANUSCRIPT_SURROGATE`
- `COPY_TO_ORDER_AS_COLLECTION_TECHNOLOGY`

This allows the article to connect Hodgson’s earlier manuscript distribution to a later, more explicit institutional reproduction regime without pretending that Wright’s commissions were Hodgson’s own actions.

## The new conceptual distinction

Use a two-axis matrix rather than a single transition narrative.

### Axis 1: genealogy

1. no demonstrated relation;
2. same named work;
3. same textual portion;
4. explicit copy;
5. explicit archetype;
6. commissioned transcript.

### Axis 2: material support

1. same support and apparently same material regime;
2. same support class, different paper state;
3. palm leaf -> paper;
4. paper -> paper;
5. target support unresolved.

A third variable may be added where needed:

### Axis 3: scale

1. one leaf;
2. several leaves;
3. section/extract;
4. whole manuscript/work;
5. collection-level reproduction programme.

This produces a much cleaner HPS object than `paper replaced palm leaf`.

## Strong article paragraph

**Support change in the Hodgson-era manuscript world was not a single transition from palm leaf to paper. It occurred at different scales and for different reasons. Cambridge Add. 1644 preserves a particularly legible operation: the last leaf of a thirteenth-century palm-leaf Pañcarakṣā deteriorated, and a later paper leaf was made from it while enough of the original still survived to transfer the ending, colophon and date. The replacement paper therefore carries information about an earlier material state whose disappearance it anticipates. A second Pañcarakṣā, Add. 1475, repeats the copying operation without changing support class, replacing damaged paper with different paper. Copying and support conversion were thus independent operations: a manuscript could change support without becoming a wholly new textual object, or be recopied without changing support class at all.**

Follow with collection scale:

**The same distinction matters for nineteenth-century collecting. Bendall identified Cambridge Add. 1478 as the archetype of a Hodgson manuscript in the India Office, while Daniel Wright’s later Cambridge programme commissioned manuscripts to be copied to order from Nepalese exemplars. Collections were therefore assembled not simply by moving old manuscripts but by producing new paper objects whose relation to older witnesses ranged from selective repair to full institutional surrogate.**

## Article architecture adjustment

The manuscript-support section should now move in this order:

1. aggregate geometry: support expands possibility space;
2. counterexample: geometry is not support-determined;
3. exact-work controls: hold text relatively constant;
4. Add. 1644: direct leaf-level palm leaf -> paper conversion;
5. Add. 1475: paper -> paper control;
6. Add. 1478: explicit Hodgson archetype chain, target material pending;
7. Wright/Add. 865: institutional commissioning of paper surrogates.

This sequence escalates evidence rather than merely accumulating examples.

## Immediate research priority

The single highest-value task is now to resolve `Hodgson MS No. 15` in the India Office by title, not number alone.

Search strategy:

- use `Śikṣāsamuccaya` title variants and Bendall’s historical `Çikṣā-samuccaya`;
- use old India Office/Hodgson collection lists rather than the General Collection concordance;
- search later British Library shelfmark concordances;
- once resolved, record support, dimensions, leaf count, lines, hand, date and whether the catalogue independently repeats the relation to Add. 1478.

Second priority: systematically mine the India Office Hodgson descriptions for `copy`, `another copy`, `transcript`, `original`, `from`, `identical`, and copyist/procurement language.

## Bottom line

The evidential centre has shifted. The article no longer needs to argue support history mainly from aggregate dimensions. It now has a directly observed material operation in which a deteriorating palm leaf is converted into a paper surrogate while the old leaf and its copied temporal metadata remain physically and intellectually linked. That case gives paper a precise historical verb: **to preserve by selective substitution**. It also makes the next question sharper: when Hodgson and later institutions ordered or distributed whole manuscript copies, when did that same logic scale from one endangered leaf to an entire textual object?
