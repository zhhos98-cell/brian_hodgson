# Hodgson paper-history mainline — 2026-09-02, through Wave 60

Status: current active guardrail after user instruction to concentrate on **paper history**. This note supersedes the broad mixed-topic mainline for ongoing execution. Earlier publication/classification waves remain in the repository as supporting history but are not active research priorities unless they directly expose paper manufacture, stock, formatting, allocation, use or custody.

## Current article object

The article is about paper as a changing material and operational unit.

Current lifecycle:

`fibre/raw material`
-> `preparation + sheet formation + sizing/finishing`
-> `commercial parent sheet`
-> `procurement + transport`
-> `stock/storage time`
-> `cutting/folding/trimming`
-> `working-format unit`
-> `drawing/writing/copying/testing/packing operation`
-> `mounting/binding/containerization`
-> `institutional custody/rebinding`
-> `modern catalogue/archive object`.

The paper history therefore now has four linked forms of memory:

1. **process memory** — manufacture embodied in sheet properties;
2. **stock memory** — procurement, movement and time retained before use;
3. **format memory** — conversion from parent sheet into working unit;
4. **custody memory** — mounting, binding and archival remaking after use.

Core equation:

`research support = production history × supply/stock history × format-conversion history × operation × custody history`.

## 1. Manufacture remains the first main scene

### Nepal finished sheet versus Cowan remanufacture

The 1829/1833 British trial remains the cleanest material experiment:

`Himalayan material moved as prepared/dried pulp -> metropolitan remanufacture -> disappointing sheet`

while finished Nepal-made paper itself travelled as a successful material object.

Mechanisms:

- `PROCESS_HISTORY_EMBODIED_IN_SHEET`
- `MATERIAL_IDENTITY_DOES_NOT_PRESERVE_PROCESS_PROPERTIES`
- `METROPOLITAN_REMANUFACTURE_FAILURE_DESPITE_EXPERTISE`.

### Darjeeling 1841

After material transfer proved insufficient, five Nepali papermakers were moved to Darjeeling.

`material transfer failure -> skilled-practice transfer -> process reconstruction`.

Iron vessels, washing, bleaching and toughness/whiteness trade-offs show that the historical object was a calibrated sequence of operations rather than a plant recipe.

Mechanisms:

- `EMBODIED_PROCESS_TRANSFER`
- `TACIT_CRAFT_KNOWLEDGE_EXCEEDS_PRINTED_RECIPE`
- `WHITENESS_STRENGTH_TRADEOFF`.

### Serampore

Machine formation reverses the value of some fibre properties.

`property value = material property × operation × interface`.

Mechanism:

`AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

## 2. Supply and stock remain the second main scene

Calcutta should be treated as:

`aggregation + storage + issue + evaluation + redistribution`,

not as a paper origin.

Hodgson-associated objects directly preserve multiple imported watermark/mark classes alongside Nepalese paper.

Important imported classes already in repo:

- Whatman;
- Joynson;
- Moinier's Patent;
- Newey/Darnford transcription;
- Britannia;
- date-only marks.

Watermark date can predate use substantially.

RAS 022 c.1844 architectural corpus now includes catalogue watermark dates of at least:

- 1821;
- 1837;
- 1841.

Thus stock time is a **series-level condition**, not a single anomaly.

Mechanisms:

- `MIXED_PAPER_STOCK_WITHIN_RESEARCH_WORKFLOW`
- `WATERMARK_DATE_NOT_USE_DATE`
- `PAPER_STOCK_RETENTION_OR_REUSE`
- `STOCK_TIME_IS_SERIES_LEVEL_NOT_OUTLIER`.

## 3. Wave 60 adds the missing middle operation: format conversion

Earlier work moved too quickly from `stock` to `use`.

The new material question is:

**what happened to the commercial sheet between storage and the research operation?**

Evidence now indicates systematic cutting/folding/formatting.

### RAS 022 long architectural format

Dominant c.`30 × 48.5 cm` family.

Old English Royal book/drawing sheet:

`19 × 24 in = 48.26 × 60.96 cm`.

Half Royal:

`30.48 × 48.26 cm`.

A diagnostic seventeen-sheet RAS sample averages:

`30.11 × 48.63 cm`.

Current state:

`HALF_ROYAL_DRAWING_SHEET_CANDIDATE_HIGH`.

This family includes at least paper catalogued with watermark dates 1837 and 1841.

Therefore:

`different stock ages/batches -> same working format`.

Mechanism:

`FORMAT_STANDARDIZATION_OVER_HETEROGENEOUS_STOCK`.

### RAS short architectural format

Several objects around `27–28 × 44–45 cm` are compatible with trimmed half Medium drawing paper.

Medium parent:

`17.5 × 22.5 in = 44.45 × 57.15 cm`.

Half Medium:

`28.58 × 44.45 cm`.

Current state:

`HALF_MEDIUM_CANDIDATE`, lower confidence.

RAS 022.014 proves that 1841-dated paper occurs in this short family too. Thus watermark year does not determine format.

### Zoological format

Christie's bird corpus uses repeated `25.1 × 20.3 cm` and `30.5 × 19.7 cm` units across multiple visible watermark classes.

The first closely approximates quarter Demy drawing paper:

`Demy 15.5 × 20 in -> quarter 19.69 × 25.4 cm`.

Current state:

`QUARTER_DEMY_CANDIDATE`.

The second format remains unresolved.

Mechanisms:

- `PARENT_SHEET_TO_WORKING_UNIT_CONVERSION`
- `CUTTING_IS_PAPER_ALLOCATION`
- `COMMERCIAL_SHEET_SIZE_CAN_CONSTRAIN_RESEARCH_FORMAT`
- `RESEARCH_SHEET_NOT_NECESSARILY_MILL_SHEET`.

## 4. Mixed paper can converge on one work format

British Library Add Or 5338 gives direct evidence independent of watermark inference.

`Add Or 5338(1–17)`:

- Nepalese **and** European paper;
- some leaves from a folded exercise book;
- various sizes, mostly `25 × 40 cm`;
- mainly pen-and-ink iconography.

Related `18–28` also mostly `25 × 40 cm`.

Thus:

`different paper origins/forms -> convergent iconographic working format`.

This falsifies any attempt to use finished object size as a direct proxy for European/Nepalese manufacture.

Mechanisms:

- `FORMAT_CAN_CROSS_PAPER_ORIGIN`
- `WORKFLOW_CAN_NORMALIZE_HETEROGENEOUS_SUPPORTS`
- `EXERCISE_BOOK_TO_LOOSE_WORKING_LEAF_CONVERSION`
- `SUPPORT_ORIGIN_NOT_OPERATIONAL_FORMAT`.

Contrast:

`Add Or 5332–5334` are three `44 × 34 cm` sheets on Nepalese country paper, used for caitya/mandala drawings.

This creates a testable operational-format ecology rather than a national-paper binary.

## 5. Watermark method must now account for cut position

Visible watermark frequency is not stock frequency.

General watermark topology places the mark often, though not invariably, in one half of the parent sheet and countermark in the other.

After halving/quartering, working pieces can contain:

- watermark;
- countermark;
- fragment;
- neither visible mark.

Therefore Christie's counts such as:

- Britannia (17);
- Newey/Darnford 1847 (11);
- Moinier's Patent 1848 (5);
- Joynson 1848 (2);
- 1840 (1)

are observations of **marked surviving working units**, not stock shares.

Likewise the single WHATMAN-marked leaf in a 41-page sketch corpus proves Whatman presence but not a 1/41 stock proportion.

Mechanisms:

- `WATERMARK_OBSERVATION_IS_CUT_POSITION_DEPENDENT`
- `VISIBLE_MARK_COUNT_UNDERCOUNTS_STOCK_FREQUENCY`
- `UNMARKED_WORKING_UNIT_CAN_SHARE_MARKED_PARENT_STOCK`
- `PARENT_SHEET_RECONSTRUCTION_REQUIRED`.

## 6. The unit `sheet` must now be disaggregated

Use four fields rather than one generic sheet:

### A. manufacturing sheet

Unit formed on mould or machine.

### B. commercial parent sheet

Unit sold, shipped and stored; may correspond to named sizes such as Royal, Medium or Demy.

### C. working sheet/unit

Unit cut, folded or trimmed for a task.

### D. archive/display object

Working support after mounting, guarding, binding, trimming or rebinding.

This distinction matters because present catalogue dimensions may record C, D, or a later trimmed state rather than A/B.

Mechanisms:

- `SHEET_IS_A_VERSIONED_MATERIAL_UNIT`
- `WORKING_SUPPORT_IS_PRODUCED_AFTER_PAPERMAKING`
- `CUTTING_HISTORY_IS_PART_OF_PAPER_HISTORY`
- `MOUNT_SIZE_NOT_WORKING_SHEET_SIZE`.

## 7. Current paper-history scenes for the article

The article should now be built around operations rather than Hodgson's intellectual topics.

### Scene I — making

Nepal manufacture, Cowan remanufacture failure, Darjeeling skilled-labour transfer, Serampore machine mismatch.

Question:

`what material properties survive movement, and which require process reconstruction?`

### Scene II — supplying and storing

Calcutta supply plurality, imported marks, stock time, old paper entering later work.

Question:

`how did sheets become available in Kathmandu/Darjeeling, and for how long did they remain available?`

### Scene III — formatting and allocation

Half-Royal/half-Medium/quarter-Demy candidates, exercise-book leaves, local/European paper convergence.

Question:

`how was stock converted into task-sized working supports?`

### Scene IV — using

Pencil, ink, wash, watercolour, fair versus preliminary work, iconography versus zoology versus architecture.

Question:

`which material/format properties were selected for which operations?`

### Scene V — remaking after use

Mounts, portfolios, wrappers, bindings, 1921 dimension-based archival rebinding.

Question:

`how did later physical organisation preserve or destroy earlier paper relations?`

This is enough for an article spine. Publication variants should enter only where handwritten/printed surfaces demonstrate reuse or remaking of paper.

## 8. Current strongest formulation

**Paper entered Hodgson's research more than once. It first entered as a manufactured sheet whose fibre preparation, formation and finishing determined its properties; it entered again as purchased and stored stock of different origins and ages; and it entered a third time when those parent sheets were cut, folded or selected into recurrent working formats. The dominant c.1844 architectural sheet is almost exactly half a standard Royal drawing sheet even though its watermarks span different years, while British Library iconographic sheets converge around a common size despite mixing Nepalese and European paper. The research surface was therefore not simply supplied by a mill. It was produced inside the research workflow by an additional operation of material standardization.**

## Guardrail

Do not expand into military classification, intellectual genealogy, publication stemma, Buddhist doctrine, zoological taxonomy or biography unless the evidence directly answers a paper question.

For every new source ask first:

- What paper is this?
- What was its manufactured size/state?
- How did it reach the working stock?
- Was it stored before use?
- Was it cut/folded/trimmed?
- What operation was assigned to the resulting support?
- What later mounting/binding altered it?

If a source cannot materially advance one of those questions, park it outside the active paper-history run.
