`paper-object history = production process × stock/supply × work allocation × classification regime × documentary scale × version state × locus-specific revision × paratext placement × copy-specific annotation × survival/custody/reclassification`.

Central question:

**what survives when material, information, a document, a printed locus or an archival relation is remade into another operational state, and what has to be materially reconstructed?**

Wave 58 adds a crucial correction: administrative knowledge in the military-tribes genealogy was already classificatory. The historical transition is not `administration -> classification`; it is a change in **classification regime, documentary form and aggregation scale**.

## B. Early military-tribes history now has four evidential layers

Do not collapse:

1. **historical document attestation**;
2. **surviving physical witness identity**;
3. **neighboring institutional classification infrastructure**;
4. **textual/copy descent**.

### Historical attestation

The 1868–69 privately printed service memorandum, closely echoed by Rajendralala Mitra in 1882 and contextualized by Hunter in 1896, retrospectively attests an early military-tribes memoir covering race, tribal names/classification and physical/moral soldier qualities.

Its physical identity remains unresolved.

### Surviving Hodgson witnesses

Current early family:

- `vol.10/1` — February 1825 `Memoir on the Army of Nepal`, 39 headings, administrative/recruitment memorandum;
- `vol.9/23` — 1825 rough Nepal-army report, Hodgson rough hand, original cross-reference to `the red leather volume`;
- `vol.9/22` — April 1828 rough military memoir / Bayley-query reworking;
- `vol.7/20` — 1831 rough `Castes of Parbattiah army tribes`, Khasa thars, Brahman/Magar names, Gurung clans, original-home material;
- `vol.6/1` — October 1832 fair publication-facing tabular copy;
- `vol.9/10` — parallel fair copy;
- 1833 JASB print.

### Neighboring 1830 Bengal military classification infrastructure

Bentinck's 27 July 1830 minute concerning the Nasiri and Sirmoor battalions requested variables including caste, district/province and service length and explicitly requested an **abstract** instead of full `Description Roll` detail.

Kennedy and Young returned aggregate data by origin/service and caste/class. The class table used broad categories including Brahman, Rajput, Khas Gorkha/Chhetri, Magar/Gurung/Garhwali and others.

A Bengal Military letter of 6 November 1830 to the Court of Directors then acknowledged that the apparent caste difference between the two corps was produced by a `capricious system of classification`.

This infrastructure is temporally and categorically adjacent to Hodgson's 1831 rough classification, but direct transmission is unproven.

### Textual descent

Only content-level comparison can create textual/copy edges. Do not infer that Hodgson saw the Kennedy/Young returns or that vol.7/20 copied Bengal military abstracts.

### 1830 military-administrative aggregation

Bentinck's request performs:

`person-level/service information`
-> `selected variables`
-> `abstract`
-> `corps-level comparison`.

The crucial documentary contrast is:

`Description Roll`
vs
`abstract`.

The abstract detaches selected attributes from individual soldiers and makes distributions visible at battalion scale.

### 1831–32 Hodgson ethnological intensification

The surviving Hodgson classificatory sequence shifts the unit of analysis toward:

`thar + family name + clan + group + original home/origin relation`.

This is classification of a different documentary and epistemic kind. It is not simply the first moment when categories appear.

### 1833 print

The fair classificatory infrastructure enters learned print and remains attached to a recruitment argument.

Current best formulation:

**military administration already made hill soldiers legible through categories; Hodgson's 1831–33 paper sequence intensified and reconfigured classification by moving from personnel/manpower variables toward thars, clans, family names, origins and genealogical relations, then circulated that infrastructure through learned print.**

## Current strongest article formulation

**Hodgson's paper world was not a sequence in which an unclassified administrative problem became an idea, then a text, then an archive. Military and Residency paperwork already rendered people, forces and places through selected categories, but different paper forms operated at different scales: personnel records attached attributes to individuals, abstracts converted them into comparable distributions, Hodgson's rough and fair classificatory papers reorganized them into thars, clans, origins and genealogical relations, and print reopened those relations to later annotation and revision. The archive then remade the relations again through transfer, binding and cataloguing. The history is therefore a material version graph in which classification itself changes with documentary form, aggregation procedure and surviving surface.**
