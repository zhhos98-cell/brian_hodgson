# Hodgson paper mainline status — 2026-09-01, through Wave 45

Status: current active paper-centred article mainline. This file supersedes earlier status notes for current execution while retaining them as research-history states.

## Current question

How did paper participate in the making, versioning, circulation, control, bundling, institutional reclassification and later reconstruction of Hodgson's Himalayan knowledge objects?

The project now treats paper at three distinct scales:

1. **sheet/material process** — fibre, papermaking sequence, machine/hand interfaces, watermark/stock history;
2. **documentary version state** — support, hand, layout, correction layer, language, selection, copy state, table/list/notebook form, intended operation;
3. **custody/control architecture** — shipment identifiers, box/sack/bundle relations, manifests, subordinate lists, rebinding and modern relation repair.

Combined model:

`paper-object history = production history × supply/stock × work allocation × documentary versioning × identifier/provenance surfaces × custody architecture × archival reclassification`.

Hodgson-associated objects preserve Whatman, Joynson, Moinier's Patent, Newey/Darnford, Britannia and dated imported stocks alongside Nepali handmade paper.

Watermark date is not document-use date.

Mechanisms:

- `HODGSON_OBJECT_LEVEL_IMPORTED_PAPER_PLURALITY`
- `WHATMAN_PRESENT_BUT_NOT_DEFAULT`
- `PAPER_STOCK_RETENTION_OR_REUSE`.

## 3. Paper allocation is a workflow variable, not a cultural binary

The simple models

`English/British -> imported paper`

and

`local language/local writer -> Nepali paper`

are falsified.

Support selection is now modelled through:

`stock access × workplace × task state × derivative format × writer role × intended next operation`.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_ALLOCATION_AS_RESEARCH_INFRASTRUCTURE`
- `ACTOR_ETHNICITY_DOES_NOT_DETERMINE_SUPPORT`
- `SHARED_WORKPLACE_PAPER_ACCESS`.

---

## 4. Documentary versioning has four observed material outcomes

### A. Support switch

Source and derivative use different supports.

### B. Support continuity with documentary-state change

Support class remains the same while hand/layout/correction/selection/format change.

### C. Branching

One information lineage generates multiple derivatives with different support states.

### D. Medium change to print

Manuscript state becomes a typeset publication surface.

The typology is now:

- `SUPPORT_SWITCH`;
- `SUPPORT_CONTINUITY_WITH_STATE_CHANGE`;
- `BRANCHED_SUPPORT_STATES`;
- `MEDIUM_CHANGE_TO_PRINT`.

AND

Hodgson's 1856 publication further distinguishes literal translation from his own additions by brackets and footnotes.

Mechanism:

`PROVENANCE_GRAMMAR_ON_DERIVATIVE_SURFACE`.

## 6. Multilingual classificatory ecology: 1864 Item 12 / `thar`

A modern dispersed family around `classification of the people (thar)` contains:

- English + machine-made states (`vol.6/1`, `vol.9/10` and related English working);
- Farsi + machine-made state (`vol.101/8`);
- Nepali + handmade states (`vol.56/2`, `vol.51/14`, `vol.58/1`);
- Urdu + handmade state (`vol.103/18`);
- Farsi + handmade counterexample (`vol.96/14`).

Thus language does not determine support even inside a single classificatory problem.

The network is coded as a **micro-stemma without forced direction** because many Cambridge `see` relations identify a shared family rather than exact textual descent.

Mechanisms:

- `SAME_CLASSIFICATORY_PROBLEM_MULTIPLE_MATERIAL_VERSION_STATES`
- `MULTILINGUAL_VERSION_ECOLOGY_ACROSS_SUPPORTS`
- `CATALOGUE_LINK_REQUIRES_SEMANTIC_VALIDATION`.

## 7. New Wave 45 result: English production sequence within constant support

The military-tribe paper gives the strongest example of versioning **without** a support-class change.

### 1831 rough source pool — `vol.7/20`

- machine-made paper;
- Hodgson rough pen/pencil working;
- Khasa thars;
- Magar names and Bara-Magarant original-home note;
- Gurung clans;
- broader genealogical/caste material.

### October 1832 fair tabular state — `vol.6/1`

- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- exact article title;
- tabular Brahman/Khasa/Magar/Gurung family-name classification;
- another copy explicitly survives as `vol.9/10`;
- modern catalogue links it to the 1833 publication.

Oldfield independently identifies the paper he condensed as a Hodgson paper dated **October 1832**.

Current status:

`PREPUBLICATION_CANDIDATE_HIGH`.

Do not call it printer's copy without page-level descent evidence.

### Undated parallel fair copy — `vol.9/10`

- machine-made;
- fair writing;
- same paper title/family;
- copy direction relative to `vol.6/1` unresolved.

### 1833 institutional/print states

- read at Asiatic Society meeting, 9 January 1833;
- published in JASB, May 1833;
- narrative argument plus detailed tabular classification.

The distinctive Bara-Magarant component in the 1831 rough working survives into print, while the final print table preserves the same broad classificatory organization visible in the October 1832 fair state.

Mechanisms:

- `ROUGH_SOURCE_POOL_TO_FAIR_PUBLICATION_STATE`
- `PREPUBLICATION_FAIR_COPY_ON_MACHINE_PAPER`
- `TABULAR_CLASSIFICATION_AS_DOCUMENTARY_REFORMATTING`
- `VERSIONING_WITHIN_CONSTANT_SUPPORT_CLASS`.

This adds an important correction to the paper argument:

**material versioning is not equivalent to changing paper type.**

A machine-paper rough note and a machine-paper fair table can be materially different epistemic objects.

## 8. 1864 custody architecture preserves version families

The 1864 India Office transfer repeatedly co-bundles source, translation, abstract and multilingual derivative states.

Examples:

- Newari chronicles;
- Gurkhali chronicles + five English translation volumes;
- itineraries in Nagri, Persian and English;
- palm-leaf Newar institutional evidence plus English/Persian translations;
- army/classification/judicial/customs families;
- Jumla Khas + English revenue statement.

Mechanism:

`VERSION_FAMILY_CO_BUNDLING_AS_ARCHIVAL_DESIGN`.

The transfer interface also uses layered control surfaces:

`master manifest -> English/Hindi contents lists -> subordinate Hindi/Nagri lists -> physical bundles`.

## 9. 1921 paper-size rebinding breaks historical relations

Cambridge's catalogue history explicitly states that the Hodgson papers were rebound in 1921 partly by physical dimensions and that originals and translations could be separated because they were written on different sizes of paper.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_DIMENSION_DRIVES_ARCHIVAL_REBINDING`
- `ARCHIVAL_REBINDING_BREAKS_VERSION_FAMILIES`
- `MATERIAL_FORMAT_HAS_LONG_ARCHIVAL_AFTERLIFE`.

Modern shelfmark proximity therefore cannot substitute for historical version/container relations.

## 11. Probable Box 2 / Item 12 -> 1864 Item 12 mapping

`vol.6` preserves exact old address `Box 2 / Item 12` and title fields covering tribes, judicial system, trade/customs and army.

The 1864 manifest's Trunk 2 / second large bundle / Item 12 describes three English volumes on classification of the people, judicial system, customs and army, with Persian and Khas companion papers on the same subjects.

Modern dispersed companion candidates include:

- Farsi courts / thar / customs in vol.101;
- Urdu army/revenue materials in vol.103;
- Nepali/Khas classificatory and institutional families across vols.51, 56, 58, 60 and related volumes.

The mapping is `PROBABLE`, not `PROVEN`, because `Box 2` and `Trunk 2 / second large bundle` are not automatically identical historical hierarchy levels.

Mechanism:

`OLD_ADDRESS_PLUS_CONTENT_MATCH_AS_PROBABLE_CUSTODY_MAPPING`.
