## 1. Manufacture/process line retained

Strong scenes remain:

## 9. Wave 70 remains the control on `dhep`

`ढेप / dhep` is securely a Nepalese handmade-paper bundle/count term.

Its numerical depth is **not universal**.

Current direct/printed attestations:

- ten-sheet convention: Paudel 1987–88 + modern lexicography;
- twenty-sheet convention: direct 1998–99 field study in Myagdi/Parbat/Baglung, with twenty dried sheets tied by bast strips;
- 200-sheet statement: NAST c.2013 synthesis, retained as direct printed text but practice status unresolved and possible conflation with `kori` high.

Mechanisms:

- `LOCAL_UNIT_NAME_DOES_NOT_FIX_PACKAGE_DEPTH`
- `COUNT_UNIT_IS_PHYSICALLY_MADE_BY_BUNDLING`
- `METROLOGICAL_VALUE_IS_CONTEXTUAL_ATTRIBUTE_NOT_LEXEME_CONSTANT`.

## 10. Wave 71 moves the ten-sheet hierarchy back to 1928

Perceval Landon, *Nepal*, vol. II, Appendix VI `Weights and Measures`, printed p.256:

`10 Tao = 1 Dhep`

`20 Dheps = 1 Kori`.

Footnote:

`a tao is a sheet of any size`.

This establishes two strong rules.

### A. Count and format are independent

`tau` counts one sheet without specifying its dimensions.

Thus:

`COUNT_METROLOGY != DIMENSIONAL_METROLOGY`.

### B. Ten-sheet convention is directly printed by 1928

This shortens the temporal gap between Hodgson f.117 (1843) and late-twentieth-century lexicography, although it does not prove continuous transmission.

Landon’s immediate source remains unresolved. His book-level source ecology included Nepalese archives, original documents, officials/informants and earlier British scholarship.

Do not infer a Hodgson source edge from acknowledgement alone.

## 11. Wave 72: `kori` is a general score/twenty applied to paper

The accessible online Turnbull object has a bibliographic trap.

Internet Archive metadata labels it 1887, but the scanned text is a 1992 AES reprint of the 1923 third edition. Turnbull’s internal 1904 preface says his second edition was `really an entirely new work` relative to the 1887 attempt; Kilgour’s 1923 preface says the third edition is practically page-for-page reproduction of the earlier work with obvious errors corrected.

Therefore:

`DIRECT_1887_LEXICAL_WITNESS = NOT_ESTABLISHED_FROM_THIS_SCAN`.

In the directly readable textual state, `kori` appears under `special collectives` and means:

`a twenty, score`.

It is used in an ordinary numerical example outside paper.

Correct ontology:

- `tau = paper sheet count in recovered metrology`;
- `dhep = paper bundle term with contextual package depth`;
- `kori = GENERAL TWENTY-COUNT COLLECTIVE applied to paper bundles`.

Thus:

`20 dhep = 1 kori`

should normally be read as:

`one score of dhep bundles`,

not as a uniquely paper-specific physical container.

Mechanisms:

- `GENERAL_COLLECTIVE_CAN_BECOME_DOMAIN_METROLOGY`
- `GENERAL_COUNT_COLLECTIVE_DISTINCT_FROM_PACKAGE_DEPTH`
- `UPPER_LEVEL_COUNT_CAN_BE_STABLE_WHILE_LOWER_PACKAGE_DEPTH_VARIES`.

Important consequence:

`kori = 20`

does not determine how many sheets are in a `dhep`.

Under a ten-sheet dhep convention a paper kori is 200 sheets; under a twenty-sheet dhep convention a literal score of dheps would be 400 sheets unless local practice redefines the hierarchy. This must be observed, not assumed.

## 14. Current strongest proposition

**Paper in Hodgson’s research system was made into accountable units more than once. Manufacture created a sheet; local metrologies counted or bundled sheets; commercial and collaborator networks moved those units into stock; cutting and folding made task formats; copying moved information between supports; and binding and transport created further packages. The Nepalese hierarchy itself was semantically layered: `tau` counted a sheet without fixing its size, `dhep` named a physical paper bundle whose depth could vary by production context, while `kori` was the ordinary collective ‘twenty’ applied to those bundles. Paper quantity was therefore neither a universal number nor a purely lexical fact. It was an operation produced at the intersection of counting vocabulary, physical bundling, locality and documentary accounting.**
