## Working title

**Paper Empires: Brian Houghton Hodgson and the Material Infrastructures of Himalayan Knowledge**

Possible sharper subtitle:

**Paper Empires: Paper Technologies and the Making of Himalayan Science**

Avoid final title choice until the watermark / paper-pair census is further advanced.

---

## Central question

**How did changes in paper manufacture, paper support, sheet format, inscription, copying, correction, packaging and archival arrangement make Himalayan knowledge observable, comparable, portable and revisable?**

This asks more than what paper Hodgson used.

The relevant historical sequence is:

`material/fibre -> sheet production -> paper selection -> writing/drawing/copying -> spatial composition -> counting -> correction -> binding/packaging -> circulation -> institutional handling -> rebinding/restoration/digitization`.

At each transition, paper changes what kind of object exists and what relations remain recoverable.

---

## One-sentence thesis

**Hodgson's Himalayan knowledge was not simply inscribed on paper: it was repeatedly constituted by changing paper operations, as Nepali sheets became experimental materials and commodities, imported sheets became reporting and accounting interfaces, old supports became paper surrogates, blank and broad-sheet space organized translation and comparison, paper units defined what could be counted, and later bindings and digital surrogates rearranged the relations through which those knowledge objects could still be reconstructed.**

---

## Historiographical intervention

### Against `paper as substrate`

The article treats paper as historically variable infrastructure rather than an inert carrier for already constituted scientific content.

### Against a simple `European paper / indigenous paper` binary

Machine-made/imported and Nepali hand-made paper cross subjects, languages and epistemic states. Neither maps consistently to `official/scientific/final` or `local/religious/original`.

### Against a narrowly instrumental `paper tools` account

Some Hodgson paper objects are clearly tools for comparison or counting, but much of the infrastructure comes from administrative, household and commercial paper practices: bills, lists, translation copies, issue registers, wrappers, notebooks, receipts, bundles and later bindings.

### Against generic `materiality`

Every material claim should identify an operation:

- permits what?
- constrains what?
- changes at what interface?
- preserves which relation?
- creates which unit?
- produces which failure?

## Historiographical coordinates

### Georgina Wilson

Use `Authorship / Composition / Form / Reuse` as a question architecture, not a transferable theory.

Hodgson translation:

- **authorship** -> documentary labour materially present beneath/around the inscribed scientific surface;
- **composition** -> heterogeneous evidence assembled on one paper surface or within one paper object;
- **form** -> sheet, leaf, page, folio, notebook, broad-sheet, pothi and bound volume as constraints/units;
- **reuse** -> verso reallocation, correction, recopying, rebinding and digitization.

### Boris Jardine

Use paper's `flickering materiality`: sometimes material is conspicuous, sometimes it recedes behind marks.

Hodgson offers an especially clear sequence because Nepali paper itself becomes a scientific/technical object before paper returns to apparently transparent use as letters, bills, route notes and catalogues.

### Hess & Mendelsohn

Use their broader history of paper technology to connect scientific ordering to bookkeeping, administration, seriality and labour organization.

Hodgson's Residency/private archive is a strong imperial case of administrative paper practices becoming scientific infrastructure.

### Simon Werrett

Use reuse/repurposing as potentially productive operation, but do not infer thrift without economic evidence.

### Whatman / Balston paper history

Use paper-manufacture and watermark scholarship for provenance reconstruction.

Important context:

- East India Company required large quantities of paper at home and abroad;
- Whatman had a significant share in Company supply according to Balston;
- `VEIC` watermarks are known with multiple maker countermarks;
- `J WHATMAN` alone is not secure maker proof;
- half-sheet survival can separate watermark from countermark.

Therefore:

`EIC/Whatman supply context != identification of any individual Hodgson sheet`.

# Narrative architecture

## Scene 1. December 1831 — paper describes paper

### Object

`MSS EUR HODGSON/6 ff.77–81`.

Hodgson writes to H. T. Prinsep about Nepali paper-making and a sample of Nepali paper. The surviving reporting letter is itself catalogued as machine-made paper.

### Narrative opening

Begin with two papers occupying different roles in one knowledge event:

- Nepali paper: object/sample/technology under description;
- machine-made paper: correspondence interface that carries the description.

### Mechanism

`PAPER_ON_PAPER_KNOWLEDGE_INTERFACE`.

### Claim

The history begins not with writing on a neutral surface, but with one paper regime turning another paper regime into a transportable object of technical knowledge.

### What this scene establishes

- paper can move between object and medium;
- paper origin alone does not determine epistemic rank;
- material visibility itself changes by operation.

### Required archival follow-up

- inspect whether the described/sample Nepali paper survives physically with the letter;
- identify machine paper as laid/wove, whole/half sheet, watermark/countermark;
- establish relation between note, letter and sample.

---

## Scene 2. 1841–42 — paper properties reverse under new interfaces

### Objects/events

Darjeeling paper-maker transfer, Campbell experiments, O'Shaughnessy chemical intervention, Serampore machine trial.

### Process

`plant/bark -> transport -> soaking/cooking -> pulp -> hand-sheet formation -> bleaching/filtering/waterproofing -> machine web`.

### Key failures

- wet bark mildews in transport;
- iron contact darkens sheet;
- bleaching increases whiteness but can destroy toughness;
- strength in filtration does not imply speed/clarity;
- long/mucilaginous fibre that makes tough handmade sheets obstructs machine flow/thickness control.

### Mechanism

`AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

### Claim

Paper properties do not possess fixed values. Toughness, fibre length, mucilage, colour and absorbency change historical meaning when the technical interface changes.

### Historiographical role

This is the article's most explicit experiment-history section. It converts paper history into HPS through observed troubleshooting and failed transfer between technological systems.

### Comparison with Whatman

Whatman material history supplies a mature British paper-manufacturing context, but the argument should not become a diffusion story from Britain to Nepal. The Darjeeling episode instead tests how a local material system fails/succeeds when moved across labour, chemical and machine interfaces.

## Scene 3. 1843 — one machine-made sheet prices a thousand bundles of Nepali paper

### Object

`MSS EUR HODGSON/12 f.117`.

Machine-made bill for:

- 1,012 bundles (`dhyap`) Nepali paper;
- ten sheets per bundle;
- price categories;
- wax cloth;
- thread/twine;
- monetary totals/conversion.

Verso contains a separate military note.

### Mechanisms

- `PAPER_ON_PAPER_ACCOUNTING_INTERFACE`
- `SHEET_AS_OPERATIONAL_COUNT_UNIT`
- `VERSO_REUSE_AS_SURFACE_REALLOCATION`

### Claim 1 — material becomes enumerable

The bill converts paper stock into a nested quantitative ontology:

`bundle -> sheet -> price -> packing material -> money`.

The administrative surface makes a local paper commodity remotely inspectable and accountable.

### Claim 2 — sheet space is reallocatable

The verso's military note demonstrates that a paper surface can cross later disciplinary/archival categories within one physical object.

### Link to Hess & Mendelsohn

Scientific material infrastructure is inseparable from bookkeeping and administrative paper techniques.

### Required archival follow-up

- inspect ruling, folds, recto/verso orientation and reuse chronology;
- determine whether verso note follows the bill temporally;
- inspect watermark/countermark.

## Scene 4. Copying changes support — but not according to one rule

This should be the article's strongest anti-binary section.

### Case A — palm leaf -> Nepali paper -> machine paper

`MSS EUR HODGSON/29 ff.131–154`:

24 old palm-leaf property documents copied onto Nepali handmade paper, including content list and seal sketches.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/7 ff.60–62`:

four selected land documents translated into English on machine-made paper in two hands.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/14 ff.208–218`:

larger Urdu translation dossier of royal orders/palm-leaf documents on machine-made paper, with pencil corrections/addenda.

### Mechanisms

- `PALM_LEAF_TO_PAPER_SURROGATE`
- `SUPPORT_CHANGE_AS_COPY_STATE_CONVERSION`
- `STRATIFIED_INSCRIPTION_TIME_ON_PAPER`

### Case B — translation without support change

`MSS EUR HODGSON/55 ff.31–71`:

Nepali handmade-paper chronicle.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/101 ff.152–167`:

Farsi/Urdu genealogical translation still on Nepali handmade paper.

`MSS EUR HODGSON/7 ff.171–196`:

English routes explicitly translated from Nepali originals, yet written on Nepali handmade paper.

### Mechanism

`HAND_LANGUAGE_SUPPORT_NONCOINCIDENCE`.

### Claim

Paper support is a state variable rather than a code for language, authority or epistemic maturity.

The proper unit of analysis is a tuple:

`support + language + hand + format + copy relation + correction state + destination`.

### Strong negative result

Reject:

`translation = machine paper`;

`original = local paper`;

`English = imported paper`.

This falsification is historiographically valuable.

## Scene 5. Paper makes comparison by delimiting a planar field

### Case A — Buddhist symbols, 1860

Two sheets collectively carry 143 symbols extracted from:

- printed coin images;
- Nepalese temples;
- statues/images;
- Hodgson's larger drawing corpus.

### Mechanisms

- `VISUAL_EXTRACTION_TO_SHEET_ARRAY`
- `COMPARISON_BY_PLANAR_AGGREGATION`
- `PAPER_AS_HETEROGENEOUS_COMPOSITION_SURFACE`.

### Claim

The sheet produces a new object that never existed in the field: a finite comparison array assembled from dispersed sources.

The scientific operation is not merely copying images. It is:

`extract feature -> reduce context -> co-locate -> compare`.

### Case B — broad-sheet mandalas

Machine-made broad-sheet paper supplies expanded planar space for complex iconographic relations.

### Case C — translation reserve

Lepcha copies leave blank right-side paper for future translation; partial Nepali translation later fills some of it.

### Mechanism

`PAPER_LAYOUT_AS_FUTURE_OPERATION_RESERVE`.

### Claim

Blank space is historically significant when allocated by design. Paper can encode future work before the work is performed.

### Caution

Do not generalize from translation reserve to all blank leaves.

## Scene 6. The sheet creates a counting problem

### Zoological checksum

Later Hodgson table counts drawings as:

- 1,241 bird sheets;
- 557 mammal sheets;
- 55 reptile etc. sheets;
- 1,863 total sheets.

The footnote explicitly says all drawings are on folio sheets and many sheets contain several subjects.

### Mechanisms

- `SHEET_AS_OPERATIONAL_COUNT_UNIT`
- `MATERIAL_SEMANTIC_CARDINALITY_DIVERGENCE`.

### Claim

A physical support unit can be the historically meaningful quantity even when it does not map one-to-one to represented subjects.

This permits a cross-domain comparison:

- sheet != subject;
- leaf != work;
- physical volume != textual work;
- bundle != manuscript work;
- box != internal object count.

### Scientific-history payoff

Many apparent archival numerical conflicts are not errors until the counted unit is known.

The paper history therefore changes how quantitative evidence is interpreted.

---

## Scene 7. Working paper records time by accretion

### Objects

- 1834 Governor-General letter on thick and thin imported paper with numerous corrections/addenda;
- 1828 Buddhist essay on multiple machine paper types with corrections, pencil pages, later republication;
- commerce report drafts on multiple machine paper types;
- corrected printed copies and authorial additions.

### Mechanism

`STRATIFIED_INSCRIPTION_TIME_ON_PAPER`.

### Claim

Paper can preserve multiple temporal states in one object:

`base inscription -> correction -> pencil addition -> attached sheet -> later annotation`.

This gives `INTERMEDIATE_STATE_FIXATION` a specifically paper-historical basis.

### Key falsification test

Inspect whether changes in paper thickness/type align with additions/replacement sheets or merely reflect available stock.

Do not infer function from thickness before conjugacy and handwriting are checked.

---

## Scene 8. 1921 — the archive remakes Hodgson's paper relations

### Event

India Office Library binds the collection into volumes, partly grouping documents by physical dimensions.

Catalogue introduction explicitly says intellectually related documents, including original and translation, can now be physically separated simply because their paper sizes differ.

### Mechanisms

- `PAPER_DIMENSION_AS_ARCHIVAL_RECLASSIFIER`
- `SIZE_BASED_RELATIONAL_BREAK`
- `ARCHIVE_AS_PAPER_TECHNOLOGY`
- `ARCHIVAL_AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

### Claim

A material property produced under one historical workflow becomes a classification variable under a later archive workflow.

`production-time size -> archive-time binding criterion -> altered adjacency -> altered research visibility`.

### Strong HPS point

The object of historical science does not stop being made when Hodgson stops writing. The archive performs a later paper operation that changes the recoverability of relations.

### Current evidential limit

The catalogue establishes that this happened in `many cases`, but online item descriptions do not provide enough dimensions to compute a defensible percentage of separated original/translation pairs.

Do not quantify yet.

## Coda. Digital paper reuse

The current research itself works through:

- archival catalogue descriptions;
- scanned pages;
- PDF/OCR/VLM JSON;
- search indexes;
- GitHub structured datasets.

### Mechanism

`DIGITAL_REUSE_AS_EPISTEMIC_FILTER`.

### Digital gains

- dispersed cross-references become searchable;
- paper vocabulary can be aggregated;
- count-state conflicts can be detected;
- source/copy relations can be reconstructed across volumes.

### Digital losses

- watermark/countermark relation;
- chain lines;
- thickness/texture;
- sheet conjugacy;
- fold sequence;
- physical scale;
- binding order can disappear or be flattened.

### Methodological ending

The article can close by treating digitization as the latest stage in paper's history rather than as transparent access to a finished archive.

# Whatman section: strict evidential policy

## Context-level claim allowed

Balston supports that:

- East India Company required large paper supplies at home and abroad;
- Whatman had a considerable share in supplying Company paper;
- the Whatman family/business had connections to EIC leadership;
- Company-associated watermark forms such as `VEIC` existed with multiple makers.

## Object-level claim not allowed without sheet evidence

Do not label a Hodgson item `Whatman paper` merely because it is:

- British;
- imported;
- machine-made;
- high quality;
- EIC/Residency paper;
- a drawing sheet.

## Required object-level evidence

- watermark text/design;
- countermark;
- full/half-sheet relation;
- chain/wire structure;
- dimensions;
- document date;
- maker/mill chronology;
- comparison reference;
- forgery/brand-imitation possibility.

# Evidence hierarchy

## Level 1 — object/material inspection

Can support:

- watermark/countermark;
- laid/wove structure;
- fold/conjugacy;
- thickness relative to neighboring sheets;
- recto/verso sequence;
- pasted layers;
- insertion/replacement.

## Level 2 — archival catalogue physical description

Can support:

- catalogued paper type;
- condition;
- hand/language;
- copy/translation relation;
- broad-sheet/notebook/pothi format where recorded;
- cross-reference between documents.

## Level 3 — textual statement about paper/material operation

Can support:

- procurement quantity;
- paper-making method;
- experimental performance;
- shipping/receipt/translation intention.

## Level 4 — later secondary bibliographical/manufacturing context

Can support:

- likely supply context;
- maker chronology;
- watermark comparison;
- historiographical positioning.

Never let Level 4 identify a Level 1 object by itself.

# Bottom line

The article should move through paper's changing status. It begins with paper visibly under investigation, follows the fibre and sheet through failed technological transfer, watches another paper surface convert that material into accounts and quantities, tracks information through changes and non-changes of support, shows finite sheet space producing comparison and counting units, and ends with later binders and digital systems remaking the historical relations among paper objects. Hodgson is valuable not because he used unusually many kinds of paper in the abstract, but because his archive makes visible repeated moments when **changing the paper operation changes the knowledge object**.
