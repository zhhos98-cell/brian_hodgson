# Paper-and-science mainline reset

Date: 2026-08-31

## Decision

The governing project remains a history of science centered on **paper and material knowledge infrastructure**. Archive Waves 29–34 generated valuable auxiliary mechanisms about distributed zoological labour, catalogue/distribution asynchrony, local research organization, funding status and custody failure. These mechanisms remain available as evidence, but they do not become independent narrative spines unless they directly explain a paper/document operation.

## Governing question

How did paper selection, sheet formation, copying, drawing, annotation, list-making, wrapping, binding, receipt, cataloguing, correction and later rebinding make Himalayan scientific knowledge portable, auditable and revisable?

## Admission rule for new evidence

A new finding belongs in the article mainline only if it helps answer at least one of the following:

1. Why was a particular paper/support used at a particular stage?
2. What operation did the sheet/leaf/slip/wrapper/list enable?
3. What relation did paper preserve between separated objects or representations?
4. What characteristic failure did the material/support/layout generate or prevent?
5. How did a copy-state, paper-stock change, fold, wrapper, mount, binding or rebinding alter epistemic status?
6. How did paper turn observation/specimen/text into an institutionally usable scientific object?
7. How did later material intervention change what could be reconstructed from the archive?

Actor, labour, institutional and funding evidence enters only when it explains one of these operations.

## Core historiographical stack

### Wilson

Paper is not a mute vehicle. Begin with the sheet and reconstruct material biography, assembly, affordance, reuse and failure.

Operational formula:

`material affordance = enabled operation + constrained operation + characteristic failure behaviour under specific conditions`.

### Balston / Whatman calibration

For British/imported papers distinguish:

- `J WHATMAN` vs `J WHATMAN TURKEY MILL`;
- probable mill/firm;
- handmade laid / handmade wove / machine-made state;
- watermark technology and date;
- thickness, stiffness, surface and commercial size;
- trimming/mounting effects on mark visibility;
- East India Company supply ecology as a testable procurement question, not an assumed direct chain.

### Hodgson's Nepali paper account

Treat `Nepali paper` as a technologically resolved production ecology, not a symbolic contrast class:

`inner bark -> alkaline ash liquor -> boiling -> beating -> dilution/churning -> screening -> floating cloth-bottom frame -> manual distribution -> drainage -> fire drying -> peeling -> optional polishing -> folding/bundling/transport/use`.

Important variables:

- deliberate thickness variation;
- no routine sizing in Hodgson's account;
- optional burnishing/polishing;
- large-sheet capability;
- pulp-brick transport state;
- historical category `Nepali paper` distinct from exact production geography;
- strength/durability as an explicit office-record claim to be tested against surviving use.

## Strong anti-binary evidence already in the census

1. **Mss Eur Hodgson/6 item 6, 1831**: a machine-made-paper letter about a sample and manufacture of Nepali paper. Content and support cross paper regimes.
2. **Mss Eur Hodgson/1 item 4, 1834**: one corrected official letter uses two imported paper types, thick and thin. Potential within-object workflow differentiation.
3. **Mss Eur Hodgson/18 item 12, 1828**: one intellectual composition uses two machine-made paper types plus corrections/addenda/pencil pages.
4. **Mss Eur Hodgson/58 item 3**: animal and animal-bone list on Nepali handmade paper. Local handmade support is directly inside natural-history work.
5. **Mss Eur Hodgson/55 item 20**: originals/copies, royal orders, seals and official notes across mostly Nepali handmade plus some machine-made paper. Best controlled case for support versus original/copy/evidentiary status.
6. **Mss Eur Hodgson/94 item 1**: rough working notes mix Nepali handmade and machine-made papers.

These already falsify a simple equation:

`European/machine paper = scientific/official authority`

versus

`Nepali handmade paper = indigenous/religious/local knowledge`.

The empirical question is workflow position and material performance.

## Paper as instrument: highest-value object classes

Prioritize paper units whose shape/layout performs an operation:

- numbered zoological drawings;
- specimen tickets/labels;
- translation-ready manuscript pages with reserved blank field;
- questionnaires;
- tabular lists and distribution ledgers;
- bundle/box contents lists;
- receipts/checksums;
- wrappers/title slips;
- folded packets;
- inscription/palm-leaf paper surrogates;
- pasted/harital-treated supports;
- mounted drawings;
- paper used for drying, interleaving or protective wrapping where directly documented.

## Immediate empirical sequence

### Phase A — within-object paper transitions

Highest value because they control for author, genre and date.

1. Mss Eur Hodgson/1 item 4: thick vs thin imported papers within the 1834 Governor-General letter.
2. Mss Eur Hodgson/18 item 12: two machine-made stocks within the 1828 Buddhist essay.
3. Mss Eur Hodgson/94 item 1: Nepali handmade + machine-made in one rough-notes corpus.

Question:

`does paper-stock change correlate with draft/addendum/copy/revision stage?`

### Phase B — cross-domain support tests

1. animal/bone list on Nepali paper;
2. official originals/copies/seals on mixed supports;
3. large-format machine-made drawing;
4. manuscript/shipment lists on Nepali paper.

Question:

`does one support recur because of material operation rather than modern discipline?`

### Phase C — paper transformation into operative object

1. palm-leaf documents recopied onto Nepali paper;
2. inscription copies on harital-pasted Nepali paper;
3. original shipment bundle list;
4. seven-box receipt;
5. translation-ready Lepcha copy layout.

Question:

`how does changing support, layout or assembly create a new portable evidential object?`

### Phase D — paper failure and afterlife

1. folds and tearing;
2. brittle/rotten paper;
3. ink/pencil/wash interaction;
4. trimming/mounting and lost watermark evidence;
5. later rebinding by physical dimensions;
6. wrapper/list separation;
7. paper-stock changes across corrected/private-print states.

Question:

`what epistemic relation is lost or transformed when the paper object is materially altered?`

## Evidence thresholds

### Ordinary reflected-light image supports

- layout;
- visible folds;
- tears;
- stains;
- ruling;
- attachment/pasting;
- handwriting/media differences;
- gross colour/size differences where calibrated cautiously.

### Requires transmitted light / physical inspection

- reliable watermark absence;
- watermark/countermark reconstruction;
- chain/laid structure where not clearly visible;
- fibre diagnosis;
- sizing diagnosis;
- thickness measurement;
- formation pattern;
- burnishing/polishing diagnosis;
- hidden marks under mounts/bindings.

Do not promote ordinary-scan absence to watermark-negative evidence.

## Article-level claim to test, not assume

A strong eventual claim would be:

**Hodgson's scientific infrastructure did not depend on one privileged paper regime. Imported British papers, Himalayan handmade sheets and other South Asian stocks entered different moments of drawing, drafting, copying, accounting, wrapping and institutional circulation. Their scientific significance lay in operation-specific material affordances and failure profiles. Paper did not simply carry already-made knowledge: through format, surface, copy-state, folding, attachment and assembly it helped determine what could be compared, authenticated, transported, corrected and reconstructed.**

The next work should generate object-level evidence for or against each clause of this statement.
