# Hodgson paper mainline status — 2026-09-01

Status: active paper-centred article mainline, updated through Wave 37. Supersedes earlier mainline status states for current work while retaining them in repo history.

## Current question

How did paper move through manufacture, procurement, stock, work allocation, documentary versioning, provenance control, storage, transfer, rebinding and later catalogue reconstruction, and how did those material transitions affect what Hodgson and collaborators could translate, tabulate, revise, publish, bundle and later recover as related evidence?

## Combined model

`paper-object history = production history × supply/stock history × work allocation × documentary versioning × provenance marking × custody architecture × archival reclassification`.

Paper is one material field inside a larger version state. It is not a symbolic proxy for `local/colonial`, writer ethnicity or research discipline.

---

## A. Production history

1833 Cowan trial:

`same broad material source + different process history -> different paper properties`.

Mechanism:

`PROCESS_HISTORY_EMBODIED_IN_SHEET`.

Nepal-made finished sheets survived circulation with valued toughness/flexibility, whereas transported pulp remade in Britain did not reproduce the same operational qualities.

---

## B. Interface-relative affordance

Darjeeling and Serampore:

`property value = material property × technical interface × intended operation`.

Mechanism:

`AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

Moving skilled papermakers after material-transfer failure exposed tacit process variables; Serampore showed that a material bundle advantageous in hand sheet formation could become problematic at a machine-web interface.

## C. Mixed-stock ecology and stock time

Hodgson-associated objects document Whatman, Joynson, Moinier's Patent, Newey/Darnford, Britannia and dated imported stocks alongside Nepalese handmade paper. Watermark dates can substantially predate use dates.

Mechanisms:

- `HODGSON_OBJECT_LEVEL_IMPORTED_PAPER_PLURALITY`
- `MIXED_STOCK_OPERATIONAL_ECOLOGY`
- `PAPER_STOCK_RETENTION_OR_REUSE`.

Whatman is demonstrably present but cannot stand for imported paper as a whole.

---

## D. Work allocation

Cambridge evidence suggests support tracks task/work setting and version state more strongly than subject or actor nationality.

The simple model

`British writer -> imported paper`

`local writer -> local paper`

is falsified.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_ALLOCATION_AS_RESEARCH_INFRASTRUCTURE`
- `ACTOR_ETHNICITY_DOES_NOT_DETERMINE_SUPPORT`
- `WORKPLACE_STOCK_ACCESS_AS_CANDIDATE_VARIABLE`
- `TASK_ALLOCATION_MEDIATES_SUPPORT_SELECTION`.

Active explanatory variables are stock access, workplace, task state, derivative format, writer role and intended next operation.

## E. Documentary conversion is material versioning

Explicit source families show three outcomes:

- `SWITCH`;
- `CONTINUITY`;
- `BRANCHING`.

Working model:

`documentary version state = information selection + language + writer + support + format + scale + correction state + completeness + intended next operation`.

Mechanisms:

- `SUPPORT_SWITCH_AT_KNOWLEDGE_CONVERSION`
- `SUPPORT_CONTINUITY_THROUGH_KNOWLEDGE_CONVERSION`
- `BRANCHED_DERIVATIVE_SUPPORT_STATES`
- `DERIVATIVE_STATE_SELECTS_SUPPORT_WITHIN_SHARED_INFORMATION_LINEAGE`.

The strongest quasi-experiments remain Jumla revenue and the Kathmandu–Peking route.

AND

-> `vol.102/21` — Farsi Nepali-handmade neat table, 2 folios.

### Peking route

Jumla:

## G. Provenance grammar on derivative surfaces

Hodgson's 1856 Peking-route publication describes its route papers as literal translations and says information added by Hodgson was distinguished by brackets, with further additions in footnotes.

The derivative/publication surface can therefore distinguish:

`source-derived translation`

from

`Hodgson insertion`

from

`explanatory note`.

Mechanism:

`PROVENANCE_GRAMMAR_ON_DERIVATIVE_SURFACE`.

Do not yet assert that surviving `vol.2/3` itself carries the identical graphical convention until physically compared with print.

## H. 1864 version-family co-bundling

The August 1864 India Office transfer repeatedly places source, translation, abstract and multilingual derivative states within common larger custody families.

Examples:

- Newari chronicles: source histories + Persian translation + Jit Mohan abstract + repetitions + two English translation volumes + Persian addenda;
- Gurkhali chronicles: twelve source/history states followed explicitly by five volumes of `English translations of the above` made by Hodgson's office people;
- itineraries: `Various Itineraries, in Nagri, in Persian, and in English`;
- Newar institutions: original palm-leaf title deeds with some English/Persian translations;
- army/classification/judicial/customs materials: English volumes with Persian/Khas papers on the same subjects;
- Jumla revenue: Khas + English statement in one large bundle.

Mechanisms:

- `VERSION_FAMILY_CO_BUNDLING_AS_ARCHIVAL_DESIGN`
- `CUSTODY_RECOMBINES_VERSION_STATES`
- `TRANSFER_CONTAINER_PRESERVES_DERIVATIVE_RELATIONS`
- `ARCHIVAL_PACKING_AS_RELATIONAL_METADATA`.

The transfer container can function as a version-family carrier.

## I. 1921 dimension-based rebinding changes the archival ontology

Wave 36 identifies the institutional operation that explains much modern dispersion.

The Cambridge catalogue history states that:

- the papers were deposited in the India Office Library in 1864;
- in 1921 they were bound into 95 volumes;
- numbering extends to 108 because 13 scrolls occur in the sequence;
- arrangement decisions were taken partly with regard to **physical dimensions**;
- documents of similar size were bound together;
- consequently related items, explicitly including an **original manuscript and its translation**, were in many cases bound in different places simply because they were written on different sizes of paper.

Thus two archival ontologies collide:

### 1864

`source + translation + abstract + multilingual derivative -> transfer family`

### 1921

`similar physical dimensions -> bound-volume proximity`.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_DIMENSION_DRIVES_ARCHIVAL_REBINDING`
- `ARCHIVAL_REBINDING_BREAKS_VERSION_FAMILIES`
- `MATERIAL_FORMAT_HAS_LONG_ARCHIVAL_AFTERLIFE`
- `MODERN_SHELFMARK_DISPERSION_IS_PARTLY_SIZE_PRODUCED`
- `ARCHIVAL_CLASSIFICATION_CAN_OVERRIDE_HISTORICAL_RELATION`.

Source-control: the size rule is explicit at collection level; do not attribute every specific modern separation solely to size without binding-level evidence.

## K. The transfer manifest itself has a prehistory and version chain

Wave 37 moves the custody argument behind 1864.

The Royal Asiatic Society `BHH/11` list series documents repeated inventorying for storage and transport. Its catalogue explicitly notes that Hodgson's lists were sometimes needed for transportation or storing the objects listed.

Key surviving states:

### 1837–1844 — BHH/11/1

Five boxes of Buddha books, drawings and Nepal statistics left in the Residency, plus a sixth package. Later annotations record **repackaging in 1844 to take home**.

Mechanisms:

- `INVENTORY_SURFACE_ACCUMULATES_CUSTODY_STATES`
- `REPACKING_EVENT_WRITTEN_ON_PRIOR_LIST`.

### 1842–1844 — BHH/11/3

`Details of Contents of Bastas or Bundles in Trunk No.1 Cathmandu`.

Nested trunk/basta inventory including Buddhism, Vaisavalis, Jatmalas, army details, catalogues and Sastras. Later note says contents were repacked in 1844 and placed aboard the *Hardwicke* with Hodgson.

Mechanisms:

- `NESTED_CONTAINER_ONTOLOGY_BEFORE_1864`
- `BUNDLE_LIST_AS_TRANSPORT_INTERFACE`.

### 1844–1852

Further lists track drawings, literary boxes, trunks, chests and repeated geographic moves between Canterbury, shipping and Darjeeling. `BHH/11/9` even includes a summary intended to guide Khardan in producing further lists.

Mechanism:

`LIST_GENERATES_FURTHER_LISTING_LABOUR`.

### 1854–1858 — BHH/11/11

`A List of Lists` inventories nine separate lists concerning Hodgson's possessions.

Mechanisms:

- `META_INVENTORY_OF_CONTROL_SURFACES`
- `LIST_SYSTEM_BECOMES_SELF_INDEXING`.

### 1855 — BHH/11/12

Detailed Buddhist pictorial inventory plus further list of papers in Trunk No.2 at Darjeeling.

Mechanism:

`PRIVATE_TRUNK_INVENTORY_CONTINUES_BEFORE_DONATION`.

### 1858–1864 — BHH/11/14

Highest-priority new source-control object.

RAS states that this list **seems initially to have been a private store list compiled in 1858 but became the list of manuscripts given to the India Office in 1864**. It survives with a copy of Hodgson's letter to Sir Charles Wood concerning acceptance of the donation.

Mechanisms:

- `PRIVATE_STORE_LIST_TO_PUBLIC_TRANSFER_LIST`
- `STORE_ONTOLOGY_TO_TRANSFER_ONTOLOGY` — candidate pending direct comparison.

### 2 August 1864 — BHH/5/18

Draft donation letter to Sir Charles Wood. RAS states that the Kathmandu collection had already been inspected by Mr Hall, India Office Librarian before Hodgson tendered it for deposit.

Mechanism:

`INSTITUTIONAL_INSPECTION_PRECEDES_TRANSFER_CLOSURE`.

### August 1864 — BHH/19/5

Printed pamphlet version of the India Office donation list, surviving **with annotations**.

Mechanisms:

- `MANIFEST_HAS_HANDWRITTEN_AND_PRINTED_STATES`
- `ANNOTATED_PRINTED_MANIFEST_AS_WORKING_STATE`.

Do not assign the annotations to Hodgson until handwriting/provenance is checked.

## M. The archive itself undergoes documentary versioning

The paper article can now follow two parallel but materially connected version histories.

### Research object

`source -> translation -> abstract/chart/report -> publication`.

### Archival control object

`box/trunk contents -> inventory -> repacking annotation -> new store list -> meta-list -> private store list -> institutional donation list -> printed annotated manifest -> later reproduction -> digital catalogue`.

Mechanisms:

- `ARCHIVE_ITSELF_HAS_VERSION_HISTORY`

This does not require claiming Hodgson held a modern theory of archives. It documents repeated operational list-making across storage, transport and donation.

## N. Active falsifiable question

**When did version-family co-bundling emerge?**

The critical comparison is now:

`BHH/11/14 1858 private store list`
vs
`BHH/19/5 1864 printed annotated donation list`
vs
`Hunter 1896 reproduction`
vs
`earlier BHH/11 trunk/box inventories`.

Possible outcomes:

1. 1858 already preserves essentially the 1864 source/translation/abstract family structure -> private archival practice predates donation.
2. 1864 substantially restructures the 1858 store order -> version-family architecture is strengthened at transfer stage.
3. Mixed outcome -> some relations inherited, others constructed during donation preparation.

No outcome should be selected before direct page comparison.
