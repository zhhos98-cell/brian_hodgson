# Paper argument module — print as a reopenable working surface v0.1

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: article-facing synthesis from Waves 47–50. This module extends documentary material versioning beyond manuscript-to-print conversion.

## Core proposition

A printed paper state is not necessarily terminal.

In Hodgson's archive/publication practice, print could become the substrate for renewed manuscript work and could then generate another print state.

The observable sequence is:

`manuscript`
→ `print`
→ `manuscript-on-print`
→ `serial/collected reprint`
→ `copy-specific handwritten branches`
→ `selective editorial reprint`.

Mechanism:

`PRINT_AS_REOPENABLE_WORKING_SURFACE`.

---

## 1. Military-tribes paper: material states rather than abstract editions

The military-tribes paper currently has the following recoverable state sequence.

### October 1832

MSS EUR HODGSON/6 item 1:

- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- tabular classification;
- close prepublication candidate.

### 1833

JASB publication.

Known chronological reading:

`70 years ago`.

### 1857

`Selections from the Records of the Government of Bengal, No. XXVII`.

Direct full-text readings:

- `general caste and character`;
- `seventy years ago`.

Thus the early print readings remain stable through 1857 at these two loci.

### Between 1857 and 1874

Hodgson writes manuscript additions/corrections on his own printed copy of the `Selections`.

The 1874 `NOTICE` explicitly describes this material practice.

### August 1872

`The Phoenix`, III, no.26, pp.31–35, republishes the military-tribes paper.

The physical/digital witness is located, but the two target readings remain unread because current Google Books access blocks text export.

### 1874 base print

The chronological phrase now reads:

`some years ago`.

### 1874 correction apparatus

Hodgson's intended corrections include:

- `caste -> cast`;
- `some -> about 100`.

### post-1874

Multiple physical copies of the 1874 book receive further Hodgson manuscript annotations.

At least one `Alderley Oct 1874` copy has an annotation on p.37, the opening page of the military-tribes paper.

### 1972

Mahadeva Prasad Saha's Amsterdam edition prints a supplement extracted from handwritten annotations in an author's copy; Joshi later says not all annotations from the corrected copy were incorporated.

Thus a printed edition becomes the source surface for another print edition.

## 2. Two distinct forms of material revision

### A. Edition-level revision

A text is reset or reprinted into a new edition/serial state.

Examples:

- 1833 JASB -> 1857 Selections;
- 1857 Selections -> Phoenix 1872;
- Phoenix/Selections materials -> 1874 Essays.

### B. Copy-level revision

One particular printed physical copy acquires handwritten marks not shared by all exemplars of the edition.

Examples:

- Hodgson's manuscript additions in his own `Selections` copy;
- multiple differently annotated copies of the 1874 `Essays`.

Therefore:

`edition identity != copy identity`.

Mechanism:

`COPY_SPECIFIC_VERSION_BRANCHING`.

---

## 3. Why paper matters to revision history

This is not merely a metaphor of textual fluidity.

The printed sheet provides:

- fixed existing wording against which a correction can be marked;
- margins and interlinear spaces for additions;
- page and line references for correction apparatus;
- a stable citation surface (`p.39 line 17`, `p.40 line 4 top`);
- a portable physical exemplar from which an editor can later extract annotations;
- a copy-specific material history that edition-level bibliography can conceal.

The physical page therefore supports a distinctive operation:

`revision by attachment to an already fixed text`.

That is materially different from drafting on a blank sheet.

Mechanism:

`FIXED_PRINT_SURFACE_ENABLES_POSITIONAL_REVISION`.

## 4. The microrevision sequence demonstrates non-monotonic knowledge change

The Gorkha chronological phrase changes:

`1833: 70 years`
→ `1857: seventy years`
→ `1872 Phoenix: unread`
→ `1874 base: some years`
→ `1874 correction: about 100 years`.

This is not a simple progression from error to truth.

It is:

`precision`
→ `persistence`
→ `unknown intermediate`
→ `deliberate vagueness`
→ `new precision`.

Mechanism:

`RETROSPECTIVE_CHRONOLOGICAL_RECALIBRATION`.

The physiognomic phrase similarly changes from a print state preserving `caste` in 1857 to an intended `cast` reading in 1874.

Mechanism:

`RETROSPECTIVE_SEMANTIC_DISAMBIGUATION`.

## 5. Strong formulation for article

**Hodgson's printed papers did not end manuscript work. They repeatedly became its substrate. A fair machine-paper manuscript passed into print; the printed `Selections` was then reopened by Hodgson's handwriting; serial and collected reprints fixed new states; particular copies of the 1874 edition were subsequently reopened again by manuscript annotation; and a twentieth-century editor extracted one of those handwritten branches back into print. The material sequence was therefore recursive rather than linear: manuscript became print, print became manuscript surface, and manuscript-on-print became print again.**

---

## 6. Connection to the manufacturing argument

This recursive publication history parallels, without collapsing into, the paper-manufacturing evidence.

Manufacture:

`same fibre/material -> new process state -> different paper properties`.

Documentary production:

`related textual claim -> new material/version state -> different correction, circulation and citation affordances`.

In both domains, conversion produces state-specific properties.

The common historical question is:

**what remains fixed when an object enters a new operational state, and what becomes revisable again?**

---

## 7. Source-control limits

Do not yet claim:

- the October 1832 fair manuscript reads `cast` or any particular chronological number;
- Phoenix 1872 reads `some`, `seventy`, `cast`, or `caste`;
- the 1874 corrections restore the 1832 manuscript;
- Saha's 1972 source exemplar is the Alderley Oct 1874 copy;
- all Hodgson annotations entered the 1972 supplement.

Those are separate physical-witness problems.

## Bottom line

The paper article can now make a precise claim about print: the printed page is an operationally fixed but materially reopenable surface. Hodgson's publication history repeatedly cycles information through manuscript and print states, and the physical copy—not merely the abstract edition—is therefore a unit of epistemic history.
