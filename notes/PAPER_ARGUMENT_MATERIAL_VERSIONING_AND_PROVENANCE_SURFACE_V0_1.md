# Paper argument — material versioning and provenance surface v0.1

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: article-facing synthesis after Wave 34. This note sharpens the earlier support-conversion argument by treating derivative paper states as documentary versions with distinct compression, revision, provenance and custody affordances.

## The key shift

The paper article should no longer ask only:

`does a derivative change support?`

The stronger question is:

**what kind of documentary version is created, and what does its material surface make possible?**

A derivative can differ from its source in:

- support;
- sheet scale;
- folio extent;
- layout;
- language;
- writer;
- neatness;
- correction practice;
- completeness;
- binding/notebook incorporation;
- publication readiness;
- later custody grouping.

Paper is therefore one coordinate in a multidimensional version state.

---

## 1. Jumla gives the cleanest branching quasi-experiment

The source is `vol.59/38`, a 36-folio Nepali revenue record on Nepali handmade paper.

It generates at least two declared derivative states:

### English/Campbell state

`vol.8/1`

- machine-made paper;
- 48.5 × 38 cm;
- c.8 folios;
- chart form;
- written by Archibald Campbell;
- good condition;
- retains villages, households, caste breakdown, fields, taxes, duties and fines.

### Farsi state

`vol.102/21`

- Nepali handmade paper;
- 2 folios;
- table form;
- very neat hand;
- taxes and revenues from 17 villages.

The evidential gain is substantial.

Both derivatives are tabular. Therefore table-making does not mechanically select machine paper.

Both derive from the same source family. Therefore subject matter does not select support.

One is English and one Farsi, but other cases already show both languages can occur on either broad support class. Therefore language is insufficient.

The historical object is the **target derivative state**.

Working formulation:

`target material state = stock access × writer/task allocation × desired scale × documentary compression × revision needs × next operation`.

---

## 2. Documentary compression is a material operation

Jumla moves from approximately 36 source folios to about 8 English chart folios and 2 Farsi table folios.

The Peking route moves from 8 Nepali source folios to 3 English imported-paper folios and 2 incomplete Urdu/Farsi folios.

This should be treated as more than textual abbreviation.

Compression is materially achieved through some combination of:

- larger sheet size;
- denser layout;
- tabulation;
- selective extraction;
- translation;
- aggregation;
- omission;
- copying completeness.

Required mechanism:

`DERIVATIVE_COMPRESSION_AS_VERSIONING`.

Do not calculate a numerical compression ratio from folio count alone. A large 48.5 × 38 cm chart folio is not equivalent to an unmeasured country-paper folio. Physical measurement is needed before surface-area claims.

---

## 3. The Peking English version becomes a provenance-bearing surface

The Peking route family is:

`vol.59/23` Nepali handmade source
→ `vol.2/3` fair English imported-paper route-book state
AND
→ `vol.102/19` poor, incomplete Urdu/Farsi Nepali-paper state.

The English version has:

- imported thick paper;
- fair hand;
- excellent condition;
- pencil corrections and addenda;
- incorporation into the private Route Book.

It is also linked by Cambridge to Hodgson's 1856 publication.

In that publication Hodgson explicitly describes the underlying papers as literal translations and says that his own additional information is marked off by brackets, while other additions are moved to footnotes.

This creates a paper-centred source-control mechanism:

`PROVENANCE_GRAMMAR_ON_DERIVATIVE_SURFACE`.

The surface can distinguish different epistemic strata inside one derivative:

`translated source`

`authorial insertion`

`editorial/explanatory note`.

The material form of the derivative therefore does not simply carry a translation. It makes the provenance of statements locally visible.

This is a much narrower and more defensible HPS claim than saying paper generally `shapes knowledge`.

---

## 4. Version differentiation can later be reversed by custody

The Jumla family also connects documentary versioning to archival custody.

Hunter's Appendix B reproduces the 1864 India Office transfer list. Its sixth large bundle contained the statement of the revenue of Jumla **in Khas and in English**.

Thus:

`one source family`
→ `materially differentiated Khas/English versions`
→ `later recombination inside one transfer bundle`
→ `modern archival separation into different volume/item identities`.

Mechanism:

`CUSTODY_RECOMBINES_VERSION_STATES`.

This matters because present separation can disguise a historically meaningful co-custody relation.

The inverse is also possible elsewhere: one historical corpus can later be split across institutions or auction lots.

The article can therefore distinguish:

`version relation`

from

`custody relation`.

They may diverge and later intersect again.

---

## 5. Paper is an addressable field in documentary versioning

The current article model should become:

`documentary version state = information selection + language + writer + support + format + scale + correction state + completeness + intended operation`.

Paper does not determine the other fields. It is one field through which the version becomes operational.

That field matters because support changes can alter:

- available writing area;
- legibility;
- handling;
- durability;
- capacity for pencil correction;
- table construction;
- binding compatibility;
- portability;
- archival grouping;
- publication preparation.

The relevant claim is relational:

**different versions of the same information lineage can be materially engineered for different work.**

## 6. Relation to the manufacturing argument

The paper article can now connect manufacturing and documentary history without metaphor.

### Manufacturing versioning

`same broad material source`
→ `different production sequence`
→ `different sheet properties`.

1833 Cowan is the key scene.

### Documentary versioning

`same information lineage`
→ `different documentary operation`
→ `different support/format/revision/completeness state`.

Jumla and Peking are the key scenes.

The shared HPS problem is:

**conversion preserves some identities while remaking others.**

This is the strongest conceptual bridge currently available in the repo.

---

## 7. Strong article paragraph

**The most revealing paper conversions in Hodgson's archive are not simple substitutions of one support for another. They are acts of documentary versioning. A thirty-six-folio Nepali revenue record from Jumla was remade as an eight-folio English chart on large machine-made sheets by Archibald Campbell and, separately, as a two-folio Farsi table that remained on Nepali handmade paper. A Nepali route record to Peking likewise generated a fair English copy on thick imported paper, corrected in pencil and incorporated into Hodgson's private Route Book, alongside an incomplete Urdu-Persian state on Nepali handmade paper. The English route later acquired an explicit provenance grammar: Hodgson distinguished literal translation from his own inserted information by brackets and relegated further additions to footnotes. Paper support was therefore one element in a larger version architecture comprising scale, compression, hand, layout, correction and intended use. Later custody could rearrange these relations again: in 1864 Hodgson's India Office manifest placed the Khas and English Jumla revenue statements together in a single large bundle. Material differentiation and archival recombination were successive operations in the same documentary history.**

## 8. Immediate falsification targets

1. Check whether `vol.8/1` and `vol.102/21` select exactly the same 17 villages or whether the Farsi table is a subset of the English chart.
2. Measure the country-paper source and Farsi sheets to replace folio-count compression with surface-area comparison.
3. Identify the Farsi hand and its workplace role.
4. Test whether the English Peking manuscript itself uses brackets to separate Hodgson additions.
5. Compare the manuscript wording with the 1856 print to determine copy-text relation.
6. Compare `vol.58/28` and `vol.59/23` to establish whether one is a copy, parallel source, or separately transmitted itinerary.
7. Reconstruct the 1864 Jumla bundle at wrapper/lesser-bundle level if accession evidence survives.

## Bottom line

The paper article's strongest documentary mechanism is now **material versioning**. Support change is one possible outcome, support continuity another. What matters is that versions of related information are remade into different operational packages. Jumla shows branching and later custody recombination; Peking shows branching plus a derivative surface that visibly encodes provenance. Paper is therefore not a symbolic proxy for local or colonial knowledge. It is an addressable material field inside the version-control architecture of Hodgson's research.
