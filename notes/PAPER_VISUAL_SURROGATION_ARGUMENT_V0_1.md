# Paper visual surrogation argument v0.1

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: argument note generated after Wave 22. This note supplements `notes/PAPER_CROSS_SUPPORT_ARGUMENT_V0_3.md` by separating visual cross-media copying from manuscript support replacement.

## Central claim

The Hodgson paper archive contains at least two historically explicit kinds of surrogation:

1. **preservative substitution** — information is copied from a deteriorating support onto paper so that a damaged manuscript state can survive;
2. **extractive visual surrogation** — information is copied from a still-situated architectural, ritual or pictorial object onto paper so that it can move independently of the source.

These should not be collapsed into a generic history of `copying`.

The first is exemplified by Cambridge Add. 1644:

`decaying palm leaf -> paper supply leaf`.

The second is exemplified by Hodgson’s mandala drawings:

`temple / monastery original / cloth picture / Nepalese picture -> Nepalese paper drawing`.

The crucial difference is the spatial relation between source and surrogate:

- Add. 1644: source and surrogate remain joined within one manuscript object;
- mandala set: source remains elsewhere while surrogate becomes independently portable.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_SURROGATION_WITH_SOURCE_DECAYING`
- `PAPER_SURROGATION_WITH_SOURCE_RETAINED_ELSEWHERE`

## Why the mandala evidence upgrades Scene 5

The paper-centred article already treats paper as a planar comparison field. The Hodgson mandala sequence now supplies direct source genealogy for that operation.

The historical inscriptions do not merely identify subject matter. They say where/how the represented form came from:

- `from the original structure` at Svayambhunath;
- `copied from original in Maha Vihar of Patan`;
- `copied from a picture on cloth`;
- `from a Nepalese picture`.

Thus the move to paper is historically recorded as a media transformation.

A paper diagram can then receive:

- a serial number;
- Devanagari particulars;
- English title/source metadata;
- arrangement beside other diagrams;
- archival shelf relations;
- later institutional marks.

The epistemic operation is therefore:

`source relation -> planarization -> serialisation -> annotation -> aggregation -> circulation`.

Mechanisms:

- `PLANARIZATION_AS_KNOWLEDGE_OPERATION`
- `NUMBERED_VISUAL_AGGREGATION`
- `PAPER_AS_CROSS_MEDIA_COMPARISON_SURFACE`

## The sheet does not merely shrink distance; it changes relations

A cloth painting or temple mandala participates in a spatial/material setting that can include:

- architectural orientation;
- ritual access;
- physical scale;
- material texture;
- colour;
- adjacency to other objects;
- location-specific authority.

The paper surrogate can preserve some formal relations while changing or deleting others.

What paper adds:

- portability;
- common scale or at least common bounded surface;
- numbering;
- written source metadata;
- co-location with representations derived from other media;
- potential recombination.

What paper can suppress:

- original material;
- emplacement;
- scale;
- three-dimensional relations;
- ritual sequence;
- possibly colour/surface qualities.

The correct HPS formulation is therefore not `paper represents the object` but:

**paper selectively re-constitutes which relations of the source object remain operational for distant comparison.**

Mechanisms:

- `PORTABILITY_BY_CONTEXT_REDUCTION`
- `RELATIONAL_SELECTION_IN_SURROGATION`
- `MATERIAL_SEMANTIC_REDUCTION_IN_VISUAL_SURROGATION`

## Local labour is part of the material operation

Add Or 5334 is signed `Rajbeer Chittrakar Nepal`, catalogued by the British Library as Rajbir Citrakar.

The observable documentary object therefore contains at least two distinguishable inscription/production roles:

- visual delineation by a Nepalese artist;
- English titling/source description associated with Hodgson’s documentary programme.

This matters methodologically. `Hodgson’s drawings` is a collection/provenance shorthand, not a sufficient description of production.

A stronger vocabulary is:

`Hodgson-commissioned / Hodgson-collected paper visual surrogates produced through local drawing labour`.

Use `commissioned` only where direct evidence supports commissioning; otherwise use `Hodgson-associated` or `formed within Hodgson’s collection programme`.

Mechanisms:

- `DRAWING_AND_METADATA_DIVIDED_LABOUR`
- `LOCAL_VISUAL_LABOUR_VISIBLE_ON_PAPER`
- `COLLECTION_AUTHORSHIP_VS_OBJECT_PRODUCTION`

## A new three-part surrogation typology

The paper article can now distinguish at least three operations.

### Type A — preservation surrogate

Source condition: deteriorating / endangered.

Example: Add. 1644.

Operation:

`old support -> selective paper replacement`.

Epistemic goal/effect:

retain textual ending, colophon and date.

### Type B — access surrogate

Source condition: still extant but geographically/institutionally inaccessible.

Example: Wright’s Add. 865 copied to order from an early Kathmandu manuscript.

Operation:

`remote manuscript -> new paper manuscript`.

Epistemic goal/effect:

provide a European collection with textual access.

### Type C — comparative visual surrogate

Source condition: situated architecture / ritual object / cloth picture / local picture.

Example: Hodgson mandala drawings.

Operation:

`heterogeneous situated source -> indexed paper image`.

Epistemic goal/effect:

make visual relations portable and comparable across heterogeneous sources.

This typology is stronger than a support-only narrative because each type identifies a different problem that paper solves.

## Relation to later Buddhist-symbol sheets

The 1860 `Notice on Buddhist Symbols` describes a later compression operation: 143 symbols are assembled from coins and Hodgson’s larger Nepalese visual corpus onto two sheets.

A cautious sequence is now available:

`architectural / pictorial source -> paper drawing corpus -> selected feature -> symbol sheet array`.

Only the first and last operations are currently secure as general practices. Direct object-level genealogy between specific mandala drawings and specific 1860 symbols remains unproven.

Do not write:

`the Add Or 5334 drawings were used to make the 1860 symbol sheets`.

Safe formulation:

**The mandala drawings reveal the kind of upstream paper-surrogation process that made Hodgson’s later recombination of dispersed Buddhist visual forms materially possible.**

Mechanism:

`VISUAL_SURROGATE_CORPUS_AS_PRECONDITION_FOR_RECOMBINATION`.

`format -> repair -> recopying -> institutional access -> visual extraction -> recombination`.

## Strong paragraph for draft use

**Paper did not create only substitute manuscripts. It also created substitute locations. Hodgson’s mandala drawings moved forms out of monastery interiors, temple structures and cloth paintings and into numbered paper fields. One surviving sheet, now British Library Add Or 5334, is signed by the Nepalese artist Rajbir Citrakar and labels a Dharmacakra mandala as copied from a picture on cloth. The wider sequence preserved source distinctions between temple originals, monastery originals, cloth pictures and Nepalese pictures. Once copied, these heterogeneous objects could occupy the same material regime: bounded sheets bearing serial numbers, Devanagari particulars and English source descriptions. Their comparability was therefore produced through a loss as well as a gain. Paper detached formal relations from scale, material and ritual emplacement while preserving enough source metadata to make the detached images usable as a documentary series.**

## Bottom line

The paper argument can now give `surrogate` a precise material history. Hodgson’s archive was not simply a repository in which pre-existing knowledge happened to be written down. It was a system for manufacturing second material states of things: a dying palm leaf, a remote manuscript, a cloth painting or a temple mandala could each become a paper object, but for different operational reasons and with different relations preserved or discarded.
