# Wallich 1843 Nepali-paper bill — arithmetic reconstruction and catalogue-reading warning

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: **strong provisional reconstruction; image verification required.**

Object: British Library, **Mss Eur Hodgson/12, item 3, f. 117** — bill made out to Dr Nathaniel Wallich for Nepali paper and binding/packing materials, signed by B. H. Hodgson at the Nepal Residency, 1843.

Cambridge Hodgson inventory:
https://hodgson.socanth.cam.ac.uk/chunkhtml/vol012.html

The live catalogue describes:

- total: “Nepali Rupees 79.30”, converted there to “Indian Rupees 61.12”;
- total paper: **1,012 bundles (`ḍhyāp`)**;
- 10 sheets per `ḍhyāp`;
- two price rates, catalogued as “Rupees 10/- and Rupees 14/- per bundle”;
- 3 pieces wax cloth;
- thread and twine, `/03 paisā`.

The associated image links are currently dead because the old `catalogue2.socanth.cam.ac.uk` media host returns 502. Exact transcription must therefore remain pending.

---

## 1. Problem: the catalogue’s literal price reading cannot fit its own total

If 1,012 bundles cost **Rs 10 or Rs 14 each**, the paper alone would cost at least Rs 10,120, while the stated entire bill is only about Rs 79. The literal catalogue prose is therefore arithmetically impossible.

This is likely a **minor-unit transcription/interpretation problem**, not a problem with the existence of two grades/rates.

---

## 2. Contemporary Nepalese account structure

Francis Buchanan Hamilton, *An Account of the Kingdom of Nepal* (1819), gives the account relations:

- 4 dams = 1 paisa;
- 4 paisa = 1 ana;
- 8 ana = 1 mohur;
- the `Pura Rupiya` = 2 mohurs.

Therefore:

**1 Pura Rupiya = 2 × 8 × 4 × 4 = 256 dams = 64 paisa.**

Public text:
https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/30364/pg30364-images.html

This is close enough in date and directly describes the Nepalese accounting system to provide a provisional arithmetic calibration. Currency practice and exchange value could vary; use the original 1843 folio for publication-level certainty.

## 3. A reconstruction that closes exactly

### Assumptions to test on the folio image

1. Catalogue `79.30` means **79 rupees + 30 paisa**, rather than a decimal rupee.
2. The three pieces of wax cloth were **Rs 13 each**, not Rs 13 for all three.
3. Thread/twine cost **3 paisa**.
4. The two paper rates were **10 dam and 14 dam per `ḍhyāp`**, not 10 and 14 rupees.

### Step A — convert the total to dams

79 rupees 30 paisa:

- 79 × 256 = 20,224 dams
- 30 paisa × 4 = 120 dams
- total = **20,344 dams**

### Step B — subtract packing/binding material

Three pieces wax cloth at Rs 13 each:

- 3 × 13 × 256 = **9,984 dams**

Thread/twine at 3 paisa:

- 3 × 4 = **12 dams**

Remaining for paper:

**20,344 − 9,984 − 12 = 10,348 dams.**

### Step C — solve the two paper grades

Let:

- `x` = number of bundles at 10 dam;
- `y` = number of bundles at 14 dam.

Then:

`x + y = 1,012`

`10x + 14y = 10,348`

Substitute `x = 1,012 − y`:

`10,120 + 4y = 10,348`

`4y = 228`

`y = 57`

and therefore:

`x = 955`.

### Exact solution

**955 `ḍhyāp` × 10 dam + 57 `ḍhyāp` × 14 dam = 10,348 dam.**

Together with:

- 3 wax-cloth pieces × Rs 13 = 9,984 dam;
- thread/twine = 12 dam;

this gives exactly:

**20,344 dam = 79 rupees 30 paisa.**

No remainder is left.

## 4. Why the exact integer closure matters

The pair **955 + 57 = 1,012** is not imposed in advance; it is generated by the stated total, the two stated price levels and the contemporaneous currency structure.

The exact closure makes the following reconstruction highly plausible:

- **955 bundles at 10 dam each**;
- **57 bundles at 14 dam each**;
- **3 pieces of wax cloth at Rs 13 each**;
- **thread/twine 3 paisa**;
- grand total **Rs 79 / 30 paisa**.

This in turn implies that the Cambridge inventory likely preserves the right **quantities and two rates** but has expanded the rate unit incorrectly as “Rupees”.

Do **not** silently correct the catalogue in the main dataset until f.117R is inspected. Store both:

- `catalogue_transcription`;
- `arithmetic_reconstruction`;
- `image_verified = false`.

## 5. Historical implications if the folio confirms 955/57 at 10/14 dam

### Two priced paper classes

The procurement contains a strongly unequal distribution:

- ~94.37% of bundles at the lower rate (955/1,012);
- ~5.63% at the higher rate (57/1,012).

Because each `ḍhyāp` contains 10 sheets, this would represent:

- 9,550 lower-rate sheets;
- 570 higher-rate sheets;
- **10,120 sheets total**.

The two rates may encode thickness, quality, dimensions, finish, source, or another commercial distinction. The price differential alone cannot identify the material property.

### Packing is economically substantial

Under this reconstruction, wax cloth costs Rs 39 while the paper itself costs only about Rs 40.42. Packing/water protection is therefore almost as costly as the paper stock.

That is important for the article: **the transport envelope is not a trivial supplement to the knowledge object.**

This becomes especially suggestive beside the 1842 Darjeeling experiment, where Government explored India-rubber-treated Nepali paper as a lighter substitute for Post Office wax cloth.

### Direct bridge between paper and container history

The Wallich bill physically places in one account:

**paper stock + wax cloth + thread/twine.**

It is therefore a single primary object joining two branches of the project that should not be analytically separated too early:

- paper procurement / paper grades;
- bundling, waterproofing, tying and shipment.

## 6. Cross-check against the 1842 Darjeeling dossier

The 1842 *Journal of the Agricultural and Horticultural Society of India* correspondence shows actors comparing paper by cost and performance and specifically experimenting with waterproofed Darjeeling paper as a substitute for wax cloth in wet-season packet transport.

Primary scan/OCR:
https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.109896

This does **not** prove that the 1843 Wallich wax cloth served the identical technical operation, but the chronological and material adjacency makes the comparison worth testing.

The 1843 bill therefore becomes a high-value case for asking whether the experimental performance discourse of 1841–42 intersected with Hodgson’s actual procurement practice.

## Bottom line

Until the image returns, the catalogue’s literal statement “Rs 10 / Rs 14 per bundle” should **not** be propagated. A historically calibrated arithmetic reconstruction yields an exact and much more plausible ledger:

**955 bundles @ 10 dam + 57 bundles @ 14 dam + 3 wax-cloth pieces @ Rs 13 + 3 paisa thread/twine = Rs 79 / 30 paisa.**

The exact closure is strong enough to guide image inspection and dataset design, while the final transcription remains explicitly contingent on f.117R.
