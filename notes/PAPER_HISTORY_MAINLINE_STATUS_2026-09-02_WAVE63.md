# Hodgson paper-history mainline — 2026-09-02, through Wave 63

Status: current active guardrail. Supersedes `PAPER_HISTORY_MAINLINE_STATUS_2026-09-02_WAVE60.md` for ongoing work. Waves 61–63 sharpen the paper-history article around three post-manufacture operations: **format conversion, support substitution, and dimensional standardization**. Publication/classification work remains parked unless it directly reveals a material paper operation.

## Current object

The article is now a history of paper as a repeatedly remade material unit:

`fibre/raw material`
-> `preparation + sheet formation + sizing/finishing`
-> `commercial parent sheet`
-> `procurement + transport`
-> `stock/storage time`
-> `cutting/folding/trimming`
-> `working-format unit`
-> `sketch/writing/drawing operation`
-> `copying onto newly allocated finished support`
-> `mount/frame/folder/container`
-> `binding/rebinding/custody`
-> `modern catalogue object`.

Paper therefore carries at least four histories before the historian encounters it:

1. **process history** — how the sheet was made;
2. **stock history** — how it was procured, moved and stored;
3. **format history** — how a parent sheet was converted into a task-sized unit;
4. **custody history** — how working supports were mounted, enclosed, bound, dispersed or reassembled.

Core equation:

`research support = manufacture × supply/stock time × format conversion × work-stage allocation × later custody`.

## 1. Manufacture remains the opening material experiment

### Cowan 1829/1833

Related Himalayan material transported as dried/prepared pulp did not reproduce the properties of successful Nepal-made finished paper when Charles Cowan remanufactured it in Britain.

`material identity retained`
while
`process history lost`.

Mechanisms:

- `PROCESS_HISTORY_EMBODIED_IN_SHEET`
- `MATERIAL_IDENTITY_DOES_NOT_PRESERVE_PROCESS_PROPERTIES`
- `METROPOLITAN_REMANUFACTURE_FAILURE_DESPITE_EXPERTISE`.

### Darjeeling 1841

After material transfer proved insufficient, skilled Nepali papermakers were moved. Iron vessels, washing, bleaching and toughness/whiteness trade-offs reveal a calibrated operative sequence exceeding printed recipe.

Mechanisms:

- `EMBODIED_PROCESS_TRANSFER`
- `TACIT_CRAFT_KNOWLEDGE_EXCEEDS_PRINTED_RECIPE`
- `WHITENESS_STRENGTH_TRADEOFF`.

### Serampore

Properties useful in hand sheet formation could become defects at a machine interface.

Mechanism:

`AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

## 2. Stock history is now series-level

Hodgson-associated objects preserve multiple imported paper families alongside Nepalese paper. Watermark dates include at least 1821, 1837 and 1841 within the c.1844 RAS architectural corpus.

Thus:

`watermark date != use date`

and

`stock time != isolated anomaly`.

The RAS series requires coexistence of different-age stock, even before maker identification is complete.

Mechanisms:

- `MIXED_PAPER_STOCK_WITHIN_RESEARCH_WORKFLOW`
- `PAPER_STOCK_RETENTION_OR_REUSE`
- `STOCK_TIME_IS_SERIES_LEVEL_NOT_OUTLIER`.

## 3. Wave 62: the 1844 numbered architectural programme has an extremely tight working format

A systematic online-catalogue census currently recovers explicit **paper dimensions** for 47 numbered Raj Man Singh drawings. Number positions 33, 34 and 50 remain unresolved and are not inferred.

### Dominant family

43 / 47 recovered numbered drawings occupy one long-format population:

- mean: `30.10 × 48.54 cm`;
- sample sd: `0.21 × 0.25 cm`;
- range: `29.6–30.6 × 47.5–49.0 cm`.

This is a millimetre-scale cluster across almost the whole recovered programme.

### Watermark-recorded versus catalogue-unmarked long sheets

23 long sheets have explicit dated-watermark statements:

- 22 dated 1841;
- 1 dated 1837;
- mean `30.09 × 48.56 cm`.

20 long sheets have no dated-watermark statement in the online record:

- mean `30.12 × 48.53 cm`.

The group means differ by only about 0.03 cm on each side.

Therefore:

`CATALOGUE_WATERMARK_STATEMENT != FORMAT_CLASS`.

The online catalogue's silence about a watermark cannot be used to posit a second paper-format regime.

Mechanisms:

- `WORKING_FORMAT_STANDARDIZATION_IS_SERIES_LEVEL`
- `CATALOGUE_SILENCE_IS_NOT_MATERIAL_ABSENCE`
- `WATERMARK_RECORDING_IS_A_CENSORED_OBSERVATION`
- `WORKING_FORMAT_IS_MORE_STABLE_THAN_VISIBLE_WATERMARK_STATE`.

## 4. Wave 63: the dominant format matches a contemporaneously traded half-Royal drawing sheet

Old English Royal drawing paper:

`19 × 24 in = 48.26 × 60.96 cm`.

Simple half:

`12 × 19 in = 30.48 × 48.26 cm`.

RAS observed mean:

`30.10 × 48.54 cm`.

Difference:

- short side about `-1.25%`;
- long side about `+0.58%`.

Wave 63 supplies period trade control. An 1843 advertisement for Whatman's drawing paper directly lists **Royal 19 × 24 inches** among commercially available drawing-paper sizes, together with Demy, Medium, Super Royal, Imperial, Elephant and larger classes. British artists' suppliers were advertising Whatman's drawing paper c.1840 and stocking it through the mid-1840s.

This does not identify the RAS sheets as Whatman.

It does establish that:

**Royal 19 × 24 was a live commercial drawing-paper size at exactly the period of the Raj Man Singh programme.**

Current code:

`RAS_LONG_FORMAT = HALF_ROYAL_DRAWING_SHEET_CANDIDATE_VERY_HIGH_BY_DIMENSION_AND_PERIOD_TRADE_COMPATIBILITY`.

Remaining proof is physical:

- cut/deckle edges;
- watermark/countermark topology;
- chain/laid orientation;
- complementary half-sheet relations;
- later trim evidence.

Mechanisms:

- `COMMERCIAL_PAPER_SIZE_IS_A_HISTORICAL_MATERIAL_CATEGORY`
- `PARENT_SHEET_TO_WORKING_UNIT_CONVERSION`
- `CUTTING_IS_PAPER_ALLOCATION`
- `SIZE_CLASS_IDENTITY_DOES_NOT_EQUAL_MAKER_IDENTITY`
- `CUT_LOCATION_IS_A_SUPPLY_CHAIN_VARIABLE`.

## 5. Short-format exceptions become analytically valuable

Only four recovered numbered drawings clearly leave the dominant long family:

- 022.012 — `27.1 × 45.4 cm`;
- 022.014 — `27 × 43.8 cm`, watermark 1841;
- 022.018 — `27.8 × 44 cm`;
- 022.026 — `31.6 × 43.8 cm`, watermark 1821.

The first three are compatible with a trimmed half-Medium hypothesis:

`Medium 17.5 × 22.5 in -> half 28.58 × 44.45 cm`.

State:

`012/014/018 = TRIMMED_HALF_MEDIUM_CANDIDATE`.

022.026 remains:

`OLD_STOCK_IRREGULAR_FORMAT_DIRECT`.

Its exceptional value is now double:

- old stock time;
- departure from the dominant working format.

Do not normalize it away.

## 7. Wave 61: support substitution is part of the drawing workflow

British Library `Add Or 5229–5255` supplies a second material transformation.

The group contains finished Nepal drawings made mainly 1843–46 and is catalogued as **normally on English paper watermarked 1840–42**.

At least four BL finished works explicitly identify surviving RAS Hodgson sheets as their `original pencil sketch`:

- RAS 022.058 -> BL Add Or 5233;
- RAS 022.055 -> BL Add Or 5244;
- RAS 022.054 -> BL Add Or 5246;
- RAS 022.052 -> BL Add Or 5251.

Thus the paper operation is not simply progressive elaboration of one sheet.

It is:

`working sketch support`
-> `visual transfer/copying`
-> `newly allocated finished support`.

The same RAS sketch 022.054 is also catalogued by RAS as preliminary to RAS 022.027, making it a candidate reusable copying surface feeding more than one finished branch.

Mechanisms:

- `WORKING_SKETCH_IS_COPY_INFRASTRUCTURE`
- `COPYING_TRANSFERS_INFORMATION_ACROSS_SUPPORTS`
- `FINISHING_CAN_TRIGGER_SUPPORT_SUBSTITUTION`
- `ONE_VISUAL_OBJECT_CAN_HAVE_MULTIPLE_PAPER_STATES`.

## 8. English paper and Nepalese paper occupy multiple functions

The Lawrence Nepal drawings are **normally** on English paper, but BL Add Or 5254 is directly catalogued on Nepalese paper.

The group is also preserved in a **folder of Nepalese paper** inscribed `Pictures of Nepal`.

The folder's date/original function is unresolved and must not be assumed to be 1840s transport packaging.

Current nested state:

`finished image supports, normally English paper`
inside
`extant Nepalese-paper folder`.

Meanwhile Nepalese paper can also remain an image-bearing support.

Thus:

`paper origin != paper function`.

Mechanisms:

- `PAPER_ORIGIN_DOES_NOT_FIX_FUNCTION`
- `LOCAL_PAPER_AS_IMAGE_SUPPORT_AND_CONTAINER`
- `NESTED_PAPER_OBJECT`.

British Library Add Or 5338 strengthens this further: one iconographic group uses both Nepalese and European paper, some from folded exercise-book leaves, while converging around a mostly c.25 × 40 cm working format.

Mechanisms:

- `FORMAT_CAN_CROSS_PAPER_ORIGIN`
- `WORKFLOW_CAN_NORMALIZE_HETEROGENEOUS_SUPPORTS`
- `EXERCISE_BOOK_TO_LOOSE_WORKING_LEAF_CONVERSION`.

## 9. Watermark visibility must be modelled through parent-sheet cutting

Visible watermark counts cannot be used as stock percentages.

After a parent sheet is cut, working units may carry:

- watermark;
- countermark;
- fragment;
- no visible mark.

Therefore Christie's bird-study counts of Britannia, Newey/Darnford, Moinier's Patent, Joynson and date-only marks are observations of **marked working pieces**, not a census of parent-stock shares.

Likewise a single visible WHATMAN mark among 41 loose sketch pages proves Whatman presence, not a 1/41 share.

Mechanisms:

- `WATERMARK_OBSERVATION_IS_CUT_POSITION_DEPENDENT`
- `VISIBLE_MARK_COUNT_UNDERCOUNTS_STOCK_FREQUENCY`
- `PARENT_SHEET_RECONSTRUCTION_REQUIRED_BEFORE_STOCK_FREQUENCY_CLAIMS`.

## 10. Dimensional regimes now form a material sequence

The RAS architectural programme appears standardized at more than one stage.

### Working paper

Mostly c.`30 × 48.5 cm`.

### Later mount

Many objects are on mounts c.`55 × 66 cm`.

Thus:

`commercial parent sheet`
-> `working half-sheet candidate`
-> `drawing`
-> `larger standardized mount`.

The paper object acquires successive dimensional regimes.

Mechanisms:

- `MULTI_STAGE_DIMENSIONAL_STANDARDIZATION`
- `WORKING_FORMAT_PRECEDES_MOUNT_FORMAT`
- `MOUNT_SIZE_NOT_WORKING_SHEET_SIZE`.

Measurement types must remain explicit:

- paper/sheet dimension;
- drawn/image-field dimension;
- mount dimension;
- frame dimension;
- bound-leaf dimension.

BL values stated `within black frame` are image-field measurements and cannot be compared directly with RAS paper dimensions.

Mechanism:

`CATALOGUE_DIMENSIONS_ARE_OPERATIONALLY_TYPED`.

## 11. Current strongest article proposition

**Paper entered Hodgson's research at several materially distinct moments. Manufacture determined a sheet's physical properties; procurement and storage gave it a stock history; cutting or folding converted parent sheets into task-sized working units; and visual copying could then move information from one support onto another support newly allocated for finished work. In the 1844 architectural programme, forty-three of forty-seven recovered numbered drawings occupy an extraordinarily tight c.30 × 48.5 cm format, almost exactly one half of the commercially current Royal 19 × 24 inch drawing sheet, even though the stock carries different watermark years. Preliminary sketches remain more heterogeneous, and some survive as explicit sources for finished drawings on a different paper surface. The research support was therefore not simply supplied by the mill. It was repeatedly made again inside the research workflow.**
