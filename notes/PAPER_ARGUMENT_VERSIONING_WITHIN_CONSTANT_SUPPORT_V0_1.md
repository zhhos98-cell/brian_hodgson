# Paper argument module — versioning within a constant support class v0.1

Date: 2026-09-01

Status: article-facing synthesis from Wave 45. This module complements support-switch/continuity/branching by showing that documentary state can change substantially even when the broad paper-support class does not.

## Core proposition

The paper article should not equate material versioning with support switching.

The military-tribe paper demonstrates a second mode:

`constant broad support class + changing documentary operation -> new version state`.

The surviving English sequence is:

- 1831 Hodgson rough pen/pencil source pool on machine-made paper;
- October 1832 fair tabular copy on machine-made paper;
- another fair machine-paper copy of the same paper family;
- January 1833 society-reading state;
- May 1833 printed article with narrative plus classificatory table.

Paper support remains broadly machine-made across the surviving manuscript states, while handwriting, selection, tabulation, correction density and publication status change.

Mechanism:

`VERSIONING_WITHIN_CONSTANT_SUPPORT_CLASS`.

---

## Why this matters

Earlier paper evidence demonstrates:

`source support -> derivative support switch`

or

`source support -> derivative support continuity`.

Wave 45 shows that even `continuity` is too coarse if treated as material sameness.

Two machine-paper objects can be materially and epistemically different because one is:

- rough;
- pencil-layered;
- heterogeneous;
- source-pool working;

while another is:

- fair;
- tabular;
- selected;
- title-stabilized;
- publication-facing.

The relevant unit is therefore not simply `paper type` but the whole material-documentary state.

Working formula:

`version state = support + layout + hand + correction layer + selection + ordering + completeness + intended operation`.

## Military-tribe sequence

### 1831 — rough source pool

`vol.7/20`:

- machine-made paper;
- Hodgson's own rough pen/pencil writing;
- broad set of caste/clan/genealogical notes;
- includes Khasa thars, Magar family names, Gurung clans and Bara-Magarant material.

This is a collection/working surface rather than a stable publishable paper.

### October 1832 — fair tabular state

`vol.6/1`:

- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- exact paper title;
- tabular Brahman/Khasa/Magar/Gurung classification;
- another copy explicitly survives as vol.9/10;
- linked by Cambridge to the 1833 publication.

Oldfield independently says his later condensed table derived from a Hodgson paper dated October 1832.

This is the strongest surviving prepublication candidate.

### Parallel fair state

`vol.9/10`:

- machine-made paper;
- fair writing;
- same titled paper family;
- lists Bahun/Thakuri/Chetri/Magar/Gurung family names.

Copy direction relative to vol.6/1 remains unresolved.

### Print

The 1833 article preserves narrative argument plus a detailed tabular classification.

Thus tabulation survives the manuscript-to-print transition while the whole object changes medium and institutional status.

## Strong article formulation

**Paper did not have to change type for knowledge to change material state. Hodgson's military-tribe classification survives as rough and fair machine-paper manuscripts before becoming a printed article. What changed was not merely wording: a heterogeneous pencil-and-pen working pool was selected and reformatted into a fair tabular surface, copied again, read before a learned society and typeset. Material versioning therefore operated within as well as across paper regimes. The relevant historical variable is not the identity of the substrate alone but the combination of support, hand, layout, correction layer, selection and intended operation.**

---

## Relation to the Cowan experiment

The article can now distinguish two different kinds of material persistence.

### Production experiment

`same broad fibre/material source + changed papermaking process -> changed sheet properties`.

### Documentary production

`same broad paper-support class + changed documentary process -> changed epistemic/documentary properties`.

Both reinforce an operation-dependent model of material identity.

A material label such as `Himalayan fibre` or `machine-made paper` does not exhaust what an object is operationally.

---

## Relation to support branching

Jumla and Peking show:

`shared information lineage -> different supports/formats`.

Military tribes show:

`shared information lineage -> same broad support class, different documentary states`.

Therefore the article's material-versioning typology should include:

1. `SUPPORT_SWITCH`;
2. `SUPPORT_CONTINUITY_WITH_STATE_CHANGE`;
3. `BRANCHED_SUPPORT_STATES`;
4. `MEDIUM_CHANGE_TO_PRINT`.

This prevents `continuity` from being mistaken for stasis.

## Source-control limits

Do not claim:

- vol.7/20 is the direct draft of the 1833 article;
- vol.6/1 is the printer's copy;
- vol.9/10 precedes or follows vol.6/1.

Current evidence supports:

- chronology;
- source-family relation;
- strong prepublication candidacy of vol.6/1;
- distinctive content persistence;
- tabular continuity into print.

Direct textual descent requires list-order/shared-error comparison.

## Physical inspection priorities

1. vol.7/20: transcribe the complete Khasa, Magar and Gurung lists and mark pencil versus pen layers.
2. vol.6/1: record headings, column order, corrections, paper dimensions, watermark/formation, and any printer-facing marks.
3. vol.9/10: compare list order and errors against vol.6/1.
4. 1833 print: compare heading/order/spellings against both fair copies.
5. 1874 reprint: check changes before treating it as identical to 1833.

## Bottom line

Paper-centred history must distinguish substrate class from documentary state. Hodgson's military-tribe paper shows a sequence in which machine-made paper remains constant while rough knowledge collection becomes fair tabular classification and then print. The archive therefore makes visible material versioning **within** a paper regime, not only conversion **between** paper regimes.
