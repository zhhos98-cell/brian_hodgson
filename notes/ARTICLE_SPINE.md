# Article spine — evidence-led history of science version

Date synced: 2026-08-31

Status: rebuilt through archive research Waves 25–31. This version supersedes the earlier spine where it treated Hodgson's later reputation archive as primarily individual self-curation or treated zoological drawing numbers as a singularly stable master identifier.

## Provisional titles

**Paper Empires: Brian Houghton Hodgson and the Material Infrastructures of Himalayan Knowledge**

Alternative if the final census shows that natural history carries substantially greater evidential weight:

**Paper Empires: Brian Houghton Hodgson and the Material Infrastructures of Himalayan Natural History**

The archive, not an advance disciplinary hierarchy, should determine the final scope.

## Governing question

**How were heterogeneous Himalayan knowledge objects made credible, identifiable, countable, portable, auditable after separation, and institutionally usable?**

The article follows concrete operations: procurement, copying, drawing, measurement, translation, tabulation, numbering, annotation, collation, wrapping, binding, bundling, packing, shipment, receipt, unpacking, sorting, comparison, naming, set construction, catalogue updating, redistribution, relabelling, re-identification, delayed publication, correction, reprinting and retrospective archival reorganisation.

## One-sentence claim

Hodgson's scholarly authority was produced through distributed documentary and material labour that converted Himalayan specimens, images, manuscripts, linguistic testimony, administrative information and later even the evidence of his own career into versioned, relationally addressable objects; these systems made knowledge portable and partially reconstructible after movement precisely because overlapping drawings, numbers, names, lists, labels, copies, receipts and catalogues preserved redundant relations, while the surviving mismatches reveal how copying, processing, distribution and institutional incorporation could freeze incomplete or inconsistent states.

## Provisional abstract

Brian Houghton Hodgson is conventionally divided among colonial administration, zoology, geography, ethnology and the discovery of Sanskrit Buddhist literature in Nepal. This article instead reconstructs the material infrastructure through which those fields became practicable. Hodgson's archive preserves unusually dense traces of commissioned copying, local artistic and observational labour, paper procurement and manufacture, specimen preparation, drawings and measurement tables, manuscript leaves and wrappers, labels, numbered lists, trunks and portfolios, receipt books, catalogue revisions, duplicate sets and later private-print self-documentation. These were not secondary supports for knowledge already made. They constituted the relations by which distant users could identify, compare, revise and sometimes recover objects after physical separation.

Buddhist manuscripts expose commissioned copying, translation-ready page layouts, work/volume/bundle distinctions, scribal and catalogue error, expert re-segmentation and changing object identity. Buddhist images expose differentiated access through Vajra Acharyas, Chitrakars and other local specialists, diagnostic feature extraction, unfinished explanation projects and recipient-controlled publication delay. Zoological materials expose an especially dense parallel system: Nepali artists repeatedly corrected drawings from fresh specimens; shikarees supplied behavioural observations; a writer translated those reports; drawing numbers joined pictures, specimen labels and manners notebooks; Hodgson maintained rough/master and finished/fair visual states; British Museum staff sorted, renamed, copied and distributed specimens while catalogues were still being built. By February 1845, naming, set construction, destination assignment, physical distribution and catalogue updating were demonstrably running on different clocks. Later institutional divergence could therefore be exported at the moment of initial distribution rather than created only by later curatorial corruption.

Across domains, the strongest common mechanism is not simple circulation but **relational redundancy under versioned processing**. A number can be preserved while a copier corrupts the name; a number can itself collide while morphology, locality and drawing still permit recovery; a manuscript can be physically received while its textual segmentation remains unresolved; a shipment can arrive while the receiver's memorandum omits a sender-listed component. The archive becomes historically valuable because these imperfect surfaces overlap. Hodgson's later reputation archive repeats the pattern: Anne/Annie Hodgson gathered testimonial traces in a household notebook; Brian later recovered that object and, in the versioned private print *Notes of the Services*, career evidence was repeatedly classified, corrected, expanded and renewed. The same operations that made animals, texts and images revisable eventually made Hodgson's career itself into a catalogue object.

## Core evidence domains

### A. Buddhist manuscripts and textual witnesses

- Commissioned copying for learned societies, including the 64 manuscripts copied for the Société Asiatique.
- Hodgson–Burnouf correspondence on acquisition, receipt, further copies, safe addresses and intellectual use.
- Paper versus palm leaf, dimensions, hands, condition, missing/supplied leaves, wrappers, covers, bindings and copy-state.
- Wrapper titles and Hodgson slips as identity-bearing inscriptions; later re-identifications exposing earlier catalogue errors.
- Folio numbering, missing leaves, modern pencil foliation and attempts to reconstruct sequence.
- Lists, bastas, trunks, boxes and repacking as transport/custody identities distinct from later bibliographic identities.
- Fort William 1824–1829 procurement chain: paper, scribal labour, binding, carpentry, porterage, government funding and a collection whose bundle, volume and work counts diverge.
- Rajendralal Mitra's 1882 diagnosis of false splitting and lumping when continuously paginated multi-work codices were identified only from first and last pages.
- Direct count controls such as 85 bundles / 144 works and other version discrepancies that must be preserved rather than normalized.
- Descriptive reanalysis that adds support, dimensions, folia, lines per page, script, date, correctness and internal contents, thereby changing segmentation and identity.
- Cases in which copies can become better witnesses than damaged originals.
- SOAS/India Office description of Lepcha copies prepared for future translation, with blank right-hand space and partial Nepalese translation already inserted: `TRANSLATION_READY_COPY_LAYOUT` / `FUTURE_OPERATION_RESERVE`.

### B. Buddhist visual knowledge

- Early 1827 diagnostic comparison through mudra/hand position, supporters, cognizances and colour.
- Differentiated local access roles: Vajra Acharya, Buddhist Chitrakar, Bhotiya provider and Hodgson's textual checking should be recorded by operation rather than collapsed into `informant`.
- Drawings and images as composite text-image packages with local/Sanskrit explanatory layers.
- 1860 *Notice on Buddhist Symbols*: symbols abstracted from coins, temples, statues, pictures and illuminated manuscripts into a comparative corpus originally intended for explanation by learned Saugatas; the project was interrupted and later reactivated without the planned explanations.
- `ABANDONED_EXPLANATION_CORPUS` and `INTERMEDIATE_STATE_FIXATION`: unfinished research objects can remain reusable decades later.
- 1870–72 Hodgson/Yule sketch-and-interpretation case: recipient buffering delayed publication while Yule waited for a less isolated context.
- `RECIPIENT_BUFFERING`: circulation latency can be editorial/epistemic rather than transport-based.
- Institut de France visual materials, explanatory notes, wrappers and cases as tests of relation reconstruction and container chronology.

### C. Zoology and natural history — primary article spine candidate

#### 1. Distributed production labour

- 1833–35 evidence for two/three Nepali artists continuously employed drawing from nature.
- 1835 production protocol: drawings repeatedly corrected from fresh specimens; mature colour/figure, sex and age variants, external/internal diagnostic structures, and camera/camera-lucida assistance where useful.
- Drawing is an accumulated corrected state, not necessarily one specimen event.
- Artists and Hodgson process the same fresh specimens in parallel: visual representation versus Hodgson's stomach/intestine, food, habits, locality and economy notes.
- Archibald Campbell adds a medical/anatomical expertise layer.
- Rajman Singh and Tursmoney Chittakar are secure named artist nodes; most artist labour remains anonymous.
- ZSL material explicitly assigns bird manners/habits to `native sportsmen or Shikarees` and their translation to `my writer`.
- Fieldworkers could substitute for Hodgson's direct observation where his movement was restricted.
- The 1870 `native painter's Hindi list` remains actor-unidentified; do not silently assign it to Rajman Singh.

#### 2. Drawing as composite metadata surface

- ZSL surviving sheets typically combine drawing/reference number, scientific and local names, locality, Devanagari text, anatomical figures, recto/verso notes, measurements and weight.
- Actor attribution for each field requires handwriting/object analysis; presence of a field is not proof of authorship.
- Measurement tables may represent an artist-side production layer, but this remains a high-value object-level falsification target rather than a settled universal attribution.

#### 3. Numbered relational addressability

- Numbered drawing/specimen lists already exist by 1835–38.
- HOD/1/3 manners notebooks, 1846–57, are explicitly keyed `according to the numbers attached to the drawings`; surviving ranges include 1–169, 340–488 and 755–855.
- Specimens originally carried paired labels: date/locality/local name plus drawing number in red ink, according to later reconstruction.
- Drawing number links specimen, image and behavioural memorandum, but may identify a species-series relation rather than a single biological individual.
- The numbering system appears to shift from a planned classificatory sequence toward historical accretion as gaps fill and new material is appended.
- Gray reports independent number collisions, so the core claim must be `RELATIONAL_REDUNDANCY_OVER_SINGLE_FIELD_STABILITY`, not number supremacy.

#### 4. Rough/master and finished/fair visual states

- 15 Dec. 1844: Hodgson explicitly distinguishes `original Drawings` plus `Notes and Memoranda`, needed for continued research, from a `finished series of drawings` left for British Museum use.
- British Museum was asked to appoint an artist to complete the unfinished fair series from Hodgson's original rough drawings.
- 27 Dec. 1844: Hodgson reconstructs expected/transmitted totals by consulting his own originals from which transmission drawings had been copied.
- Master/original state functions as generative research capital and audit reference; fair/finished state functions as institutional/reference material.
- `COPY_STATE_AFFORDANCE_DIFFERENTIATION`: rough and fair are different operational states, not simply inferior/superior versions.

#### 5. Publication/custody package

- 1835 Swainson agreement treats drawings, specimens and descriptions as a temporary publication package; originals were to return after use.
- `PUBLICATION_PACKAGE_CUSTODY_CONTRACT`: publication required a controlled custody cycle across heterogeneous evidence.
- Failure of *Zoology of Nipal* as a single finished publication remains analytically useful because its distributed components expose the infrastructure more clearly than a stabilized book might have.

#### 6. Routing and receipt failures

- 1837 RAS minutes: Hodgson reports 26 sheets plus Captain Robinson boxes conditionally routed depending on prior ZSL custody; RAS records none received.
- Public ZSL transcriptions disagree whether Robinson carried 5 or 8 boxes; retain as `PUBLIC_TRANSCRIPTION_BOX_COUNT_CONFLICT_5_VS_8` until the master page is checked.
- 1842–43 case is different: 31 sheets were known to have arrived, then their downstream location became unexplained.
- 1845 Howard drawings show `epistemically reconstructible != physically recoverable` when catalogue/master evidence preserves expectation but legal/custodial recovery fails.

#### 7. 1870 recomposition for new research

- `big deal box`: four portfolios / 1,104 drawing sheets, Hodgson Nepal bird list, native painter Hindi list, red-bound shikaree/writer manners volume, eight unbound Darjeeling manners volumes, Hodgson Tarai memoranda, printed catalogues/reprints.
- This package recomposes visual, vernacular, behavioural, field and printed states that had been separated for institutional custody.
- Sender manifest includes eight unbound Darjeeling volumes that Marshall's receipt memorandum does not name: `SENDER_RECEIPT_MANIFEST_MISMATCH`.
- Do not infer physical loss until later despatch/return evidence is located.

### D. British Museum sorting, catalogue and distribution — major institutional mechanism

#### 1. Independent metadata failure channels

- J. E. Gray reports a list whose **numbers were correct** while the names were `ludicrously wrong` through the ignorance of a copier.
- In the same letter Gray fears many bird numbering errors, including two very different kinds carrying the same number.
- Names and numbers therefore have independent error channels.
- Morphology, locality, images, local names, bills/anatomical characters, measurements and catalogue versions can function as redundant correction surfaces.

#### 2. Sorting before nomenclatural stabilization

- Gray says new species cannot be adequately described until the whole collection is sorted and old/new compared.
- Institutional naming is downstream of collection-level comparison.
- Duplicate distribution can begin while names are still being completed.

#### 3. Catalogue-distribution coevolution

- Gray's 1844 pledge envisages a catalogue eventually distributed with each duplicate set.
- 6 Feb. 1845: distribution has already occurred while `the catalogue we are preparing` remains unfinished.
- A Tibetan pheasant escaped marking because it was at the stuffer when the list was prepared: `PROCESS_STAGE_VISIBILITY_GAP`.
- Gray asks to borrow the bird list back so distributions made since its preparation can be added: `POST_HOC_DISTRIBUTION_WRITEBACK`.
- 13 Feb. 1845: boxes are already physically divided into British Museum + Collections 1–3 while destinations remain to be assigned; 52 poorly/unnamed specimens are of comparatively little use.
- Set construction, naming, destination assignment, despatch, catalogue completion and catalogue propagation operate on different clocks.
- `CATALOGUE_DISTRIBUTION_COEVOLUTION`: list guides handling, distribution changes the list, revised catalogue then travels with the distributed set.

#### 4. Distributed identity generated during sorting

- A specimen can become `British Museum reference series` or `Collection No. 1/2/3` before final destination/name/catalogue closure.
- `SORTING_TABLE_AS_DISTRIBUTED_IDENTITY_GENERATOR`.
- Later differences among London, Leiden, Paris, Berlin and other institutions can inherit distinct initial metadata states rather than arising only through later local corruption.
- `EXPORTED_METADATA_INTERMEDIATE_STATE` should be tested directly against recipient catalogues/labels.

#### 5. Condition and semantic usability

- Physical damage and poor/absent naming create different use failures.
- Keep `physical_condition`, `metadata_condition`, and `institutional_use_status` distinct.
- A physically present object can remain semantically unusable, while a well-described object may be materially damaged.

### E. Paper manufacture and paper regimes

- Hodgson's 1831/32 work on Nepali paper-making and samples.
- 1841 transfer of five Nepali paper-makers to Darjeeling through Hodgson, Nepal Durbar and Campbell; salaries, workshop site and Lepcha trainees make this embodied labour transfer rather than recipe transfer.
- Darjeeling trials: wet bark mildew, iron-boiler darkening, bleaching/strength trade-offs.
- O'Shaughnessy revised chemistry and Marshman Serampore machine trial form a distributed troubleshooting chain.
- `AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`: long/strong/mucilaginous fibre may assist tough hand-made paper but obstruct a machine web; the same strength can fail as filtration performance yet become useful in waterproof postal wrapping.
- Procurement records for paper, wax cloth, twine/thread and binding/packing materials.
- Paper affordances — durability, absorbency, drawing, wrapping, interleaving, filtration, waterproofing, machinability — must be tied to operation rather than treated as fixed material meanings.
- Wrapper/list/container inscriptions can record later custody events rather than production dates.

### F. Geography, survey, administration, commerce and documentary authority

- RGS `rgs317723` route material and `rgs810460` Waugh/Hodgson/Everest material.
- Sealed originals, royal orders, inscription copies, palm-leaf documents recopied onto paper.
- Routes, copying, naming, priority, bureaucratic receipt and institutional adjudication.
- Commerce writing on two-Calcutta-bazaar-maund parcels: one unit for a mountaineer's back, two for a bullock, reducing transshipment/repacking.
- `INTEROPERABLE_LOAD_UNIT`: package form can be designed around carrier interfaces to reduce handling risk.
- Administrative bundles in the 1864 gift preserve English, Khas and Hindi lists and nested large/lesser bundle relations; transport/container identity should be separated from later bibliographic identity.

### G. Recursive archive, household labour and reputation

- The 1868 testimonial notebook is **not** simply a Brian-only self-curated dossier.
- RAS catalogue identifies Anne/Annie Hodgson as compiler of a notebook of handwritten copies and newspaper cuttings; Brian's inscription says it was `collected by my darling Annie and found in her desk Mar 12 1868`.
- `HOUSEHOLD_TESTIMONIAL_COMPILATION` -> `POSTHUMOUS_HOUSEHOLD_RECOVERY` -> Brian re-inscription/custody.
- `BHH/10` is a series of two testimonial books; the full internal relation remains to be mapped.
- *Notes of the Services of B. H. Hodgson ... Collected by a Friend* is a separate but related versioned private-print regime.
- Waterhouse reports Bodleian evidence that Hodgson himself authored *Notes*; historical Hunter attribution to the anonymous `friend` should remain marked as disputed.
- Joshi copy census records multiple private-print states with Hodgson annotations such as `Own copy corrected`, `Imperfect`, `See renewed COPY` and renewed/expanded states.
- Expanded *Notes* re-segments career evidence into Vernacular Education; Hindu Law and Legal Administration; Physical Geography; Ethnology; European Settlement/Trans-Himalayan Commerce; Buddhism; Zoology.
- `HOUSEHOLD_TO_PRINT_REPUTATION_PIPELINE` and `CAREER_RESEGMENTATION`: external judgments become household evidence, then a classified, corrected, renewed career object.
- Hodgson's career itself becomes a versioned catalogue object.

## Central mechanisms after Waves 10–31

### 1. Relational redundancy over single-field stability

No number, name, label or catalogue is inherently authoritative. Reconstructibility emerges because multiple imperfect surfaces overlap. This is now the strongest general mechanism in the natural-history archive and a useful cross-domain comparison.

### 2. Intermediate-state fixation

Working annotations, provisional classifications, copy states and temporary counts can become durable through copying, print or institutional accession. Examples include the 1857 ethnographic working corrections accidentally entering print and the 1845 retained zoological copy preserving incompatible drawing counts.

### 3. Copy-state affordance differentiation

Original/rough, fair/finished, retained copy, transmission copy, printed version and corrected copy perform different historical jobs. Version difference is not merely textual variation.

### 4. Asynchronous object and metadata clocks

Receipt, preparation, sorting, naming, destination assignment, physical despatch, list update, catalogue completion and reconciliation can occur at different times. A collection state is therefore an observation at a particular processing moment.

### 5. Catalogue-distribution coevolution

Catalogues do not simply precede and describe distribution. Lists guide physical sorting; distribution events are written back into lists; revised catalogues then circulate with destination subsets. The catalogue is part of an operational feedback loop.

### 6. Material state ahead of descriptive state

A manuscript can be physically present before textual identity is resolved; a specimen can be physically sorted into a duplicate series before naming/catalogue identity is complete. Institutional material action can precede semantic closure.

### 7. Counting ontology

Leaves, subjects, sheets, copies, works, physical volumes, bundles, packages, boxes and catalogue entries are different operational units. Later printed zoological material similarly warns that one sheet can contain several subjects.

### 8. Custody checksum and partial closure

`Safe arrival`/receipt confirms only the counted unit at a custody transition. It does not prove internal completeness, semantic identity, catalogue incorporation or correspondence between sender and receiver manifests.

### 9. Container chronology

A date or note on a case, box or wrapper may belong to packing, repacking, storage or later archival intervention rather than content production/shipment.

### 10. Institutional legibility

Physical custody is not equivalent to use. Segmentation, condition, language/script, competence, classification, naming and descriptive interfaces determine whether an object can be operated on.

### 11. Segmentation failure

Material boundaries and sampling protocols can create/erase knowledge objects, especially visible in Mitra's manuscript cases.

### 12. Distributed documentary labour

Artists, shikarees, writers, pandits, translators, paper-makers, museum copyists, cataloguers, spouses and institutional experts contribute different operations. Prefer role-level provenance to an undifferentiated `informant` category or speculative personal attribution.

### 13. Recursive archive

The same documentary operations used on specimens, manuscripts and visual corpora — selection, enumeration, classification, copying, correction, re-segmentation — are later applied to Hodgson's career evidence.

## What the article should avoid

- treating `colonialism` as a sufficient explanation without specifying the material/documentary mechanism;
- imposing modern disciplinary borders before evidence is mapped;
- reducing local collaborators to generic `informants` when the archive differentiates artist, shikaree, writer, pandit, translator or craft role;
- assigning unnamed historical roles to famous named collaborators merely because the identification is plausible;
- treating drawings as illustrations appended to science rather than versioned working/metadata surfaces;
- assuming drawing number > name as a universal hierarchy;
- treating `original`, `copy`, `finished`, `rough`, `authentic`, `type`, `old` or `modern` as transparent categories;
- treating a printed catalogue as timeless metadata;
- assuming `identify -> catalogue -> distribute` as a clean institutional sequence;
- equating physical distribution with semantic stabilization;
- equating a receipt with item-level completeness;
- silently repairing mismatched sender/receiver manifests or count conflicts;
- treating container dates as content dates without independent control;
- subtracting counts across different count units;
- assuming `physical volume = textual work` or `drawing sheet = one subject`;
- treating the 1868 testimonial notebook as Brian's solo compilation after the RAS source-control correction;
- making institutional affiliations causal unless correspondence, movement, receipt, comparison, publication, custody or handling demonstrates the mechanism.

## Testable claims

1. **Distributed-production claim:** Hodgson's scientific objects were produced through differentiated roles whose contributions can sometimes be reconstructed at operation level even when actors remain unnamed.
2. **Composite-drawing claim:** zoological sheets are multi-field evidence surfaces rather than pictures alone.
3. **Parallel-processing claim:** fresh specimens generated simultaneous visual, anatomical and textual extraction streams.
4. **Copy-state claim:** rough/master and finished/fair visual states had distinct historical affordances and could generate one another.
5. **Redundancy claim:** later auditability depends more on overlapping independent relations than on one intrinsically stable identifier.
6. **Independent-error-channel claim:** name-copy corruption and number collision can occur independently; surviving fields can diagnose one another.
7. **Catalogue-distribution claim:** physical set construction and distribution could precede catalogue completion, while distribution events were subsequently written back into lists/catalogues.
8. **Exported-intermediate-state claim:** recipient institutions may inherit different metadata states from the original distribution process.
9. **Process-visibility claim:** objects temporarily at a stuffer/preparation node can be absent from an inventory without being lost.
10. **Material-before-semantic claim:** physical receipt/sorting can precede descriptive identity and institutional usefulness.
11. **Custody-mismatch claim:** sender manifests and receiver acknowledgements are independent witnesses and should be compared rather than merged.
12. **Counting-ontology claim:** collection numbers are meaningful only relative to operational count units.
13. **Segmentation-failure claim:** inspection depth and material boundaries can change the perceived number and identity of textual works.
14. **Future-operation-reserve claim:** page or package architecture can materially reserve future translation, comparison or publication work.
15. **Publication-gate claim:** recipients may delay or condition circulation for epistemic/editorial reasons.
16. **Affordance-reversal claim:** material properties change value across technical operations/interfaces.
17. **Interoperable-load claim:** package form can be designed to survive transitions between transport regimes with reduced repacking.
18. **Recursive-reputation claim:** testimonial and private-print evidence show reputation produced through household and authorial documentary labour rather than preserved passively.
19. **Career-resegmentation claim:** Hodgson eventually applies catalogue-like enumeration and classification to heterogeneous evidence of his own services.
20. **Cross-domain infrastructure claim:** manuscript, zoological, visual and reputational corpora share operations of copying, numbering, versioning, segmentation and reconciliation without being semantically identical.

## Preferred opening scenes — current ranking

### Opening A — strongest natural-history opening

J. E. Gray tells Hodgson that a copied list has **correct numbers and absurdly wrong names**, then in the same letter worries that two very different birds carry the same number. One page destroys the fantasy of a perfect identifier and makes the article's central mechanism visible: knowledge remains reconstructible because several imperfect surfaces overlap.

### Opening B — strongest institutional/process opening

6 February 1845: one Tibetan pheasant escapes a list because it is at the stuffer; birds have already been distributed; the catalogue is still being prepared; Gray asks to borrow the list back so later distribution can be written into it. The scene makes asynchronous object and metadata clocks visible immediately.

### Opening C — strongest distributed-labour opening

A shikaree speaks a bird's habits, Hodgson's writer translates the statement, the memorandum is indexed by the number attached to a drawing, and that same number appears on a specimen label. A local oral observation becomes portable by entering an addressable documentary graph.

### Opening D — strongest manuscript comparison

Mitra explains in 1882 how four, five or more works became one catalogue object because a cataloguer sampled only the first and last pages of one continuously paginated codex.

### Opening E — strongest visual-copy opening

Hodgson decides in 1844 to take his original drawings and notes back to India because they remain necessary for continued research, while leaving the finished series at the British Museum; the Museum is asked to generate missing finished drawings from the rough originals.

### Opening F — strongest reputation ending/opening

Brian Hodgson finds Anne's testimonial notebook in her desk in March 1868; decades of distributed external judgments have already been made into a household evidence object before Brian later participates in a private-print regime of correction and career classification.

The opening should expose an operation before introducing Hodgson biographically.

## Preferred ending

Return not to `Hodgson the polymath` but to the archive as a graph of imperfect surfaces. Animals, manuscripts, images and reputational claims survive movement because artists, observers, scribes, copyists, museum workers, family members, lists, labels, numbers, containers and catalogues continuously re-made their relations. What looks retrospectively like one coherent collection was repeatedly decomposed into working and finished copies, transport units, duplicate sets, destination catalogues and corrected versions. Its errors are therefore not noise around a stable core; they are evidence for the operations that made nineteenth-century Himalayan knowledge portable, comparable, auditable and revisable. Hodgson is analytically valuable because the archive preserves enough redundancy to let those operations — and their failures — be reconstructed.
