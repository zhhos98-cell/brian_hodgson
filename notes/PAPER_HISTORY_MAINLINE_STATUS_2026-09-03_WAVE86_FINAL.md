## One-sentence mainline

**Nepal paper became historically consequential through repeated institutional reclassification: as durable record support, procurement commodity, differentiated experimental specimen, bleachable manufactured sheet, packaged pulp feedstock and archival object; at the same time, evidence about those material states was itself repeatedly transformed through letters, Original Consultations, Proceedings, Board's Collections, London draft despatches, private copies and publications, so the material history and the documentary history must be reconstructed together rather than flattened into one chronology.**

# I. DRAFT-READY FACTUAL SPINE

These propositions can now be used in prose without waiting for further archive retrieval, provided the source level is stated accurately.

## 1. Nepal paper was treated as an office-record material, not merely an ethnographic craft

Abdul Ali's directly recovered 1944 article preserves Hodgson's 1831 argument that Nepal paper was cheap, abundant, tough, durable and suitable for official records.

The archival address is:

`Political Consultation, 13 Jan 1832, Nos.18–20`

with Abdul Ali specifically citing No.19 for Hodgson's 8 Dec 1831 communication to H. T. Prinsep and the transmission of paper specimens for Lord William Bentinck.

The 1937 Indian Historical Records Commission exhibition independently lists this Nos.18–20 cluster as an Imperial Record Department object concerning indigenous paper manufacture in the Himalayan tracts/Nepal.

Draft-ready claim:

**paper durability was converted into an administrative argument through the evidential use of old records and specimen circulation.**

---

## 2. By 1837 Nepal paper had entered procurement, price, transport and seasonal calculation

Campbell's 1837 correspondence, recovered through Abdul Ali and checked against the Agricultural and Horticultural Society publication context, treats Nepal paper as a possible Government stationery substitute and discusses:

- durability relative to English paper;
- price;
- market geography;
- carriage toward Patna/Ganges routes;
- seasonal purchasing and transport.

Draft-ready claim:

**Government interest translated material durability into a logistics problem: quality had to be made commensurable with price, season, market access and freight.**

Guardrail:

retain Abdul Ali as a secondary witness for exact 1837 figures unless the underlying Political record is imaged.

---

## 3. The 1837 learned-society experiment demanded differentiated samples

*Transactions of the Agricultural and Horticultural Society of India*, vol. V directly records the Society's 20 Nov 1837 request for:

`a small supply of each size and quality of paper manufactured at Nepaul for experimental purposes`.

Campbell's 28 Nov reply promises supply, proposes sending samples of each variety to the Chamber of Commerce, and encloses copies of recent official correspondence.

Secure chain:

`official correspondence`
-> `copied dossier`
-> `classified sample request`
-> `experimental sample circulation`
-> `[commercial circulation proposed]`
-> `learned-society publication`.

Draft-ready claim:

**the paper was experimental because its variation was preserved and compared, not because it was reduced to a single generic “Nepal paper” sample.**

---

## 4. The same paper programme moved between administrative, scientific and commercial institutions

The 1837 Society episode demonstrates parallel movement of documents and samples.

The proposed Chamber-of-Commerce forwarding shows an intended transition from experimental specimen to commercial specimen, although actual Chamber receipt/testing remains unproven.

Draft-ready claim:

**material samples and documentary descriptions circulated in parallel and could change institutional function at each handoff.**

---

## 5. By 1841 Darjeeling paper had become a chemical-processing problem

The 1937 IRD/IHRC exhibition identifies:

`Political Consultation, 29 Nov 1841, No.147`

as W. B. O'Shaughnessy's:

`Report on the method of bleaching paper manufactured at Darjeeling`.

Abdul Ali's 1944 article supplies the associated technical context: Campbell's paper samples, T. Maddock's Political Department role, O'Shaughnessy's bleaching work, chlorine chemistry, red lead and the avoidance of manganese oxide.

Draft-ready claim:

**the Government paper problem had shifted from identifying a durable indigenous support to modifying its colour and processing characteristics through chemical expertise.**

Guardrail:

exact London `IOR/P` / F/4 shelfmarks for No.147 remain unresolved.

## 6. The consultation system itself is now decoded

For this pre-Mutiny record context:

`O.C. = Original Consultation`.

Archival handbooks explain that original incoming letters, associated orders and reply drafts/copies could be retained under the date of the consultation and numbered within that sitting. Proceedings were derivative/copy states and might omit enclosures.

Two direct date splits:

### 1831/32

`8 Dec 1831 Hodgson letter`
-> `Political Consultation 13 Jan 1832 Nos.18–20`.

### 1842

`21 Apr 1842 Campbell -> G. A. Bushby`
-> `Political O.C. 11 May 1842 No.70`.

Draft-ready claim:

**the archival date is not necessarily the event or letter date; documentary processing generated a second chronology.**

---

## 7. Adjacent consultation numbers can constitute one transaction

The 1937 exhibition groups the 1832 paper business as:

`Nos.18–20`.

This provides direct evidence for the working rule that incoming letter, enclosure, order and reply may occupy adjacent numbers within one consultation transaction.

Draft-ready methodological claim:

**consultation retrieval must be transaction-based rather than single-number based.**

## 8. Spring 1842 is a dense but not yet causally unified paper window

Controlled chronology:

- **25 Mar 1842** — direct Hodgson letter: 24 packages of Nepalese paper pulp to be forwarded through Champaran, with Patna-land / Govind Gang-water routing alternatives based on carriage cost;
- **16 Apr 1842** — Campbell's Darjeeling experimental factory reported by Abdul Ali as closing under existing economic conditions;
- **21 Apr 1842** — Campbell paper-related letter to G. A. Bushby;
- **11 May 1842** — that business appears under Political Original Consultation No.70.

Draft-ready claim:

**pulp logistics, paper manufacture, economic evaluation and consultation processing cluster tightly in spring 1842.**

Guardrail:

`TEMPORAL_CLUSTER != PROVEN_SINGLE_CAUSAL_CHAIN`.

Do not identify the 24 pulp packages as the direct feedstock of Campbell's Darjeeling factory without further evidence.

## 11. Board's Collections prove that Indian Political Proceedings were materially recomposed in London

British Library directly defines `IOR/F/4` Board's Collections as subject papers extracted from Indian Government Proceedings and assembled for Board-of-Control review of draft despatches.

It further states that almost all Political Collections survive and can be considered a virtually complete duplicate of Indian Political Proceedings `IOR/P`.

Draft-ready claim:

**London did not merely preserve Indian proceedings; it selected and regrouped them into subject packets organised around later draft despatches.**

This is a strong paper-history/affordance point because documentary neighbourhood changed during transmission.

---

## 12. Campbell's 1842 paper experiment has a direct London Board's Collection witness

British Library directly catalogues:

`IOR/F/4/2006/89528`.

Scope explicitly includes:

`report by Dr Campbell of the result of his experiment in making paper from the bark of the paper tree`.

The same file also groups Darjeeling roads, public works and boundary administration.

Draft-ready claim:

**Campbell's paper experiment was reclassified in London as part of a broader Darjeeling administrative packet rather than surviving as an isolated scientific report.**

This is one of the strongest documentary-affordance examples in the repo.

Guardrail:

item-level equation with Political Consultation 11 May 1842 No.70 remains unproven.

# VI. STRONGEST ANALYTICAL PAYOFFS

## A. Affordance is relational and institutional

The same material can afford different actions because institutions measure and classify different properties:

- archive office -> durability;
- procurement office -> price / supply / freight;
- learned society -> comparable size/quality samples;
- chemical examiner -> bleachability/processability;
- transport administration -> packages / weight / route / cost;
- industrial experiment -> fibre/pulp feedstock;
- London Board of Control -> documentary subject packet for policy review.

This gives a non-essentialist affordance history: properties become operational only inside specific institutional practices.

## B. Paper is both object and infrastructure of evidence

The project concerns paper as manufactured material, but the evidence itself is also paper undergoing material/documentary transformations.

Thus:

`paper being investigated`
+
`paper making the investigation administratively possible`.

The object and the record medium are historically entangled.

## C. Archival survival is a derivative history

Original letter, consultation packet, proceedings copy, Board's Collection, draft despatch, private copy, publication and later archival citation are not interchangeable witnesses.

Archival loss can coexist with high documentary redundancy.

Therefore:

**missing original does not imply missing event; surviving derivative does not imply unchanged information.**

## D. Reclassification is itself historical action

When Campbell's paper experiment appears in F/4/89528 beside roads, public works and boundary administration, the document has acquired a new administrative neighbourhood.

That regrouping can be analysed as part of the history of the experiment rather than treated as neutral cataloguing noise.

# VII. PAPER-READY STRUCTURE

A clean article can now be written without waiting for the remaining archival crosswalk.

## Opening problem

Why did Nepal paper repeatedly attract Government attention between the 1830s and 1840s, yet never appear as one stable “technology-transfer programme”?

## Section 1 — Durable records and specimens, 1831–32

Hodgson, office durability, old records, specimen circulation, consultation Nos.18–20.

## Section 2 — Procurement as material calculation, 1837

Campbell, price, market, season, carriage, office substitution.

## Section 3 — Differentiated experimental samples, 1837–38

Agricultural and Horticultural Society, each size/quality, documents + samples, proposed commercial circulation.

## Section 4 — Processing the material, 1841

Darjeeling manufacture, O'Shaughnessy bleaching, Political Consultation No.147.

## Section 5 — Pulp as package and freight object, 1842

Hodgson's 24 packages, Champaran, Patna/Govind Gang route choice, freight logic.

## Section 6 — Documentary recomposition

Campbell 21 Apr -> 11 May No.70 -> London Political Proceedings derivative -> F/4/89528 -> draft-despatch architecture.

Here the material-history argument and archive-history argument join.

## Conclusion

Nepal paper was not one object with one useful property. Institutions repeatedly rendered it usable by changing what counted as the relevant property and by creating documentary forms that made those judgements mobile. The history therefore lies in repeated conversion between material states and administrative states.

The repo has crossed the point where additional open-web accumulation is the highest-value activity.
