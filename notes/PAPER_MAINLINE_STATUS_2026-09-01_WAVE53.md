## Current compression of the argument

The paper article now follows recursive material versioning across five connected scales:

1. `material/fibre/process -> sheet properties`;
2. `stock/workflow -> paper allocation`;
3. `information/problem -> manuscript documentary state -> multilingual/support branching`;
4. `manuscript -> print -> manuscript-on-print -> serial/collected reprint -> copy-specific annotation branching`;
5. `version family -> container/control system -> dimension-based rebinding -> modern relation repair`.

Combined model:

`paper-object history = production process × stock/supply × work allocation × documentary state × asynchronous revision × copy-specific annotation × identifier/provenance surfaces × custody/reclassification`.

The central question remains:

**what survives when a material, document, printed text or archival relation is remade into another operational state, and what has to be materially reconstructed?**

## F. Active microrevision chain: caste/cast phrase

The 1833 first publication is now directly readable as:

`general Calmak caste and character`.

1857 *Selections* reads:

`general caste and character`.

1874 base still reads:

`caste`.

The 1874 printed correction apparatus instructs:

`caste -> cast`.

Current chain:

`1832 manuscript = UNREAD`
-> `1833 = caste`
-> `1857 = caste`
-> `Phoenix 1872 = direct reading UNREAD; transmission predicts caste`
-> `1874 base = caste`
-> `1874 correction = cast`.

Secure consequence:

**`cast` is not a restoration of the 1833 printed reading.**

`caste` persisted in the published tradition for at least four decades.

Remaining alternatives:

1. `cast` restores an unpublished 1832 manuscript reading;
2. `cast` is a genuinely late semantic revision.

Only direct reading of vol.6/1 and vol.9/10 can decide.

Mechanism:

`LATE_SEMANTIC_RECALIBRATION_AFTER_LONG_PRINT_STABILITY`.

## G. New Wave 53 result: revision is asynchronous by layer

The 1857 reprint provides a direct quasi-controlled example.

It retains old base readings:

- `seventy years ago`;
- `general caste and character`.

But it also adds a new retrospective recruitment footnote beginning `Since this paper was written (twenty-five years back)` and reassessing Gorkhali recruitment in light of later military experience and ignored policy advocacy.

Thus the same 1857 printed state contains:

`older descriptive/historical base text`
+
`new 1857 policy paratext`.

Revision did not occur as whole-document synchronization.

Different layers changed at different times:

- recruitment/policy layer: updated by 1857;
- historical dating layer: still old in 1857, changed later;
- physiognomic wording: still old in 1857, changed later;
- detailed classificatory table: no explicit 1874 correction located in its pages.

Mechanisms:

- `REVISION_IS_LAYERED_NOT_WHOLE_DOCUMENT_SYNCHRONOUS`
- `PARATEXT_UPDATES_WHILE_BASE_TEXT_PERSISTS`
- `PRINTED_PAGE_CAN_HOST_DIFFERENT_VERSION_TIMES_SIMULTANEOUSLY`
- `ASYNCHRONOUS_REVISION_PRESERVED_ON_PRINT_SURFACES`.

Working article formulation:

**paper preserves asynchronous revision.**

## H. Phoenix 1872 now has known transmission function even though target words remain unread

Bibliography securely places the paper in:

`The Phoenix`, vol.III, no.26, August 1872, pp.31–35.

Multiple bound-volume Google Books scans are located, but direct target text remains inaccessible through current public endpoints.

A near-contemporary 1875 *Indian Antiquary* review independently states that the 1874 volume reprints papers as first reprinted in the Phoenix, and that the Phoenix versions introduced numerous marginal notes from Hodgson's own earlier printed copies into the text.

Transmission direction is therefore strongly established:

`1857 printed Selections`
-> `Hodgson manuscript marginalia on printed copy`
-> `Phoenix serial typesetting with marginalia incorporated`
-> `1874 collected base text`.

This yields strong predictions:

- Phoenix chronology probably already reads `some`;
- Phoenix physiognomic phrase probably still reads `caste`.

But predictions remain separate from direct readings.

Mechanisms:

- `PRINTED_COPY_REOPENS_AS_MANUSCRIPT_SURFACE`
- `MARGINALIA_ENTER_SERIAL_PRINT`
- `SERIAL_REPRINT_FEEDS_COLLECTED_EDITION`
- `INDIRECT_TRANSMISSION_EVIDENCE_DOES_NOT_EQUAL_DIRECT_READING`.

## I. Edition-level print and copy-specific print are different version scales

1874 `Essays` does not terminate the history.

Joshi records multiple annotated physical copies, including:

- `Own Copy corrected 1876` with layered pencil/ink annotations continuing for years;
- `BHHodgson / Alderley Oct 1874`, with Part II p.37 listed as annotated;
- another annotated copy whose Part II list does not include the military-tribes pages;
- Townshend presentation copy with another annotation history.

Thus:

`same 1874 edition -> divergent handwritten copy branches`.

1972 Saha then returns at least one author-copy annotation tradition to print through a selective supplement.

Mechanisms:

- `POSTPUBLICATION_ANNOTATED_PRINT_BRANCHING`
- `EDITION_IDENTITY_NOT_VERSION_IDENTITY`
- `PRINT_COPY_BECOMES_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION`
- `HANDWRITTEN_MARGINALIA_REENTER_PRINT`.

Do not let publication work become abstract textual criticism detached from paper. Every state here is a materially distinct writable/printable surface.
