# Paper material regimes argument v0.2

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: extension and sharpening of v0.1 after Waves 24–26. Scope remains paper itself: manufacture, material properties, procurement, storage, allocation, watermark evidence and paper-use operations. This version retains the v0.1 process-history argument and adds direct Hodgson-object evidence for mixed imported paper stocks and stock-time.

## Core proposition

The article now needs two linked propositions.

### Proposition 1 — paper is process-retentive

A sheet carries the consequences of a manufacturing sequence:

`fibre -> preparation chemistry -> beating/dispersion -> sheet formation -> drying -> sizing/finishing`.

The 1829/1833 contrast remains the strongest experimental scene: finished Nepal-made sheets travelled successfully, while related Himalayan material transported as dried pulp and worked up by Charles Cowan in Britain produced disappointing paper. Fibre provenance survived transfer; the successful production history did not.

### Proposition 2 — paper is stock-history-retentive

Once made, paper enters a second history:

`maker/mill -> merchant/procurement route -> maritime/inter-presidency movement -> store/portfolio/office stock -> duration in stock -> allocation to operation -> later custody`.

Direct Hodgson-associated drawings now show multiple imported watermark classes, including Whatman, Joynson, Moinier's Patent, Newey/Darnford-transcribed paper, Britannia-marked sheets and date-only marks. Raj Man Singh's Kathmandu architectural drawings repeatedly use watermark-dated 1841 paper, while one drawing attributed to c.1844 is catalogued on paper watermarked 1821.

Thus the paper article should treat manufacture and stock as consecutive but analytically distinct histories.

Combined formulation:

`research-paper affordance = production history × supply history × stock history × intended operation`.

## 1. The historical object is no longer a national paper type

The v0.1 rejection of `Nepali paper vs European paper` becomes stronger.

The archive instead contains a mixed material ecology:

- Himalayan locally made bark paper;
- imported British paper from multiple maker/brand families;
- watermark motifs whose makers remain unresolved;
- paper whose mark dates significantly predate known use;
- different grades and formation technologies potentially circulating through the same institutional environment.

The comparison unit must therefore be the **paper regime plus stock history**, not nation.

A useful object-level equation is:

`sheet identity = manufacture state + mark/countermark state + formation state + supply route + stock duration + use operation`.

Geography enters only when attached to one of those operations.

---

## 2. The 1833 Cowan trial remains the decisive manufacturing experiment

Wave 24 makes the British negative result substantially more exact.

The failed remanufacture was not performed by an amateur. Charles Cowan was a professional Scottish paper manufacturer embedded in the Penicuik/Valleyfield papermaking industry. John Robison of the Royal Society of Edinburgh brokered the experiment, while George Swinton connected the Calcutta/Bengal-government side of the material transfer to Britain.

The chain was:

`Himalayan paper material`
-> `Calcutta / George Swinton`
-> `Edinburgh / John Robison`
-> `Charles Cowan / paper manufacture trial`
-> `trial specimens + technical and commercial valuation`
-> `results returned to Calcutta`
-> `JASB 1833`.

This matters because metropolitan failure occurred despite access to serious papermaking expertise.

The missing variable was not generic knowledge of papermaking. It was some part of the particular process history and material condition that local Nepalese manufacture successfully embodied in the finished sheet.

Source-control remains strict: Cowan's industrial context included early mechanised papermaking, but the exact apparatus used for the Nepali pulp trial is not established. Do not turn Valleyfield's Fourdrinier context into an object-level statement about the 1833 trial sheets.

Mechanisms:

- `PROCESS_HISTORY_EMBODIED_IN_SHEET`
- `METROPOLITAN_REMANUFACTURE_FAILURE_DESPITE_EXPERTISE`
- `INDUSTRIAL_CONTEXT_NOT_TRIAL_METHOD`.

## 3. Darjeeling becomes process reconstruction by moving skilled labour

The 1841 transfer of five Nepali papermakers to Darjeeling is the practical answer to the 1833 portability problem.

Sequence:

`material moved -> reconstruction failed`

then

`makers moved -> production sequence reconstructed under observation`.

The experiment distinguishes three kinds of portability:

1. material portability;
2. descriptive portability;
3. embodied-practice portability.

Campbell's account shows that the operative system exceeded Hodgson's printed recipe. Iron vessels darkened the sheet; alternative vessels mattered; continuous washing mattered; bleaching could increase whiteness while destroying toughness.

This makes paper manufacture a historical system of calibrated operations rather than a recipe attached to a plant species.

Mechanisms:

- `TACIT_CRAFT_KNOWLEDGE_EXCEEDS_PRINTED_RECIPE`
- `WHITENESS_STRENGTH_TRADEOFF`
- `EMBODIED_PROCESS_TRANSFER`.

---

## 4. Serampore proves that properties are interface-relative

The Serampore machine trial should remain separate from the Cowan experiment.

At Serampore, Himalayan bark material met mechanised sheet formation in Bengal. Its mucilaginous/long-fibre behaviour, useful in producing tough hand sheets, interfered with flow and thickness control on the machine web.

Therefore:

`property value = property × operation × interface`.

The same material characteristic can function as strength in one regime and defect in another.

Mechanism:

`AFFORDANCE_REVERSAL`.

This should be one of the article's main HPS contributions: a material property is not an abstract ranking independent of the apparatus and operation in which it is tested.

## 5. Calcutta is a supply and evaluation interface, not a paper origin

Wave 25 clarifies the supply side.

Calcutta aggregated paper through several routes, including direct metropolitan supply and inter-presidency redistribution. Official shortages were grade-specific; complaints about inferior paper could trigger demands for physical paper musters. A c.1830 Royal Botanic Garden, Calcutta manuscript surviving on both Whatman- and Radway-marked papers gives direct institutional evidence that a single Calcutta scientific-administrative object could contain multiple maker stocks.

Thus:

`Calcutta = aggregation + storage + issue + evaluation + redistribution`.

It is analytically incorrect to code Calcutta as manufacture origin merely because paper was purchased, tested, stored, printed on or forwarded there.

The muster evidence also connects Company stationery practice to Hodgson's material-testing history. Paper quality was already made auditable across distance by moving the paper itself.

Mechanisms:

- `CALCUTTA_AS_STATIONERY_AGGREGATION_NODE`
- `PAPER_MUSTER_AS_QUALITY_CONTROL_SAMPLE`
- `PRESIDENCY_TO_PRESIDENCY_STATIONERY_REDISTRIBUTION`.

## 6. Wave 26 moves maker plurality inside Hodgson's own working archive

This is the largest evidential advance after v0.1.

A Christie's catalogue of Hodgson-associated Darjeeling/Nepalese-school bird studies records multiple watermark classes in the same working corpus:

- `MOINIER'S PATENT 1848`;
- `JOYNSON 1848`;
- `I NEWEY DARNFORD 1847`;
- `BRITANNIA`;
- `1840`.

Another Hodgson temple/deity sketch group records a `WHATMAN` watermark.

This transforms the claim from:

`Calcutta could supply multiple imported papers`

into:

`Hodgson's surviving research objects demonstrably used multiple imported paper stocks`.

Whatman is therefore historically present but analytically non-default.

Mechanisms:

- `HODGSON_OBJECT_LEVEL_IMPORTED_PAPER_PLURALITY`
- `WHATMAN_PRESENT_BUT_NOT_DEFAULT`
- `MIXED_PAPER_STOCK_WITHIN_RESEARCH_WORKFLOW`.

## 7. Watermarks are relational evidence, not self-decoding provenance labels

The new object census requires a harder watermark method.

Different marks carry different semantic functions:

- maker or brand name;
- patent/proprietary claim;
- motif/grade family;
- date;
- place;
- countermark paired with another mark on the opposite half of the original sheet.

They cannot all be entered into `maker`.

Examples:

### `JOYNSON 1848`

Joynson belongs to a British mechanised papermaking context and held a patent for watermarking machine-made paper. Yet the mark alone does not prove that a particular Hodgson sheet was machine-made. Formation structure must be checked.

### `BRITANNIA`

Britannia is a widespread motif/grade family, not a unique maker signature and not a secure national-origin marker.

### `I NEWEY DARNFORD 1847`

The auction transcription must remain exact because related archival catalogues contain both `Darnford` and `Dartford` readings. Physical inspection may change the normalized reading.

### `WHATMAN`

The mark establishes presence of the Whatman brand/family at some level. It does not establish exact mill, exact firm state, Company route or dominance over Hodgson's imported paper stock.

Required method:

`exact mark transcription + countermark + mark morphology + formation + dimensions + whole-sheet relation + maker chronology + object date`.

Mechanisms:

- `WATERMARK_SEMANTIC_TYPE_MUST_BE_CODED`
- `WATERMARK_TRANSCRIPTION_UNCERTAINTY`
- `MAKER_MARK_NOT_PRODUCTION_TECHNOLOGY_WITHOUT_FORMATION_CHECK`.

## 8. The 1821/1844 gap creates a new historical variable: stock time

Royal Asiatic Society records for Raj Man Singh's Kathmandu architectural drawings repeatedly state that papers are watermarked 1841 and used for drawings assigned to 1844.

More strikingly, `RAS Hodgson 022.026`, *Temple of Krishna and Radha, Patan*, is attributed to c.1844 while its paper watermark is catalogued as 1821.

The roughly 23-year gap is not a nuisance to be normalized away. It exposes a second temporality of paper.

A sheet can be manufactured, purchased, shipped, stored, moved again and only much later assigned to a research operation.

Possible explanations include:

- long-lived imported stock;
- institutional or household storage;
- recirculation between holders;
- reuse of older blank paper;
- or catalogue dating problems.

Object inspection must decide among them.

But one methodological conclusion is already secure:

`watermark date != use date`.

This creates a field the article did not previously have:

`paper_stock_age_at_use`.

Mechanisms:

- `WATERMARK_DATE_NOT_USE_DATE`
- `PAPER_STOCK_RETENTION_OR_REUSE`
- `STOCK_HISTORY_MODULATES_MATERIAL_USE`.

## 9. Paper choice should be analysed as allocation

Once mixed stocks and stock time are visible, paper selection becomes a research-infrastructure problem.

For each operation, ask:

- what papers were physically available;
- what grades were affordable;
- which stocks were held locally;
- which could take pencil, ink, wash, watercolour or repeated handling;
- which were selected for fair drawings versus rough work;
- which were reserved for administrative writing, manuscript copying, wrapping, filtration or printing;
- whether older stock was used opportunistically or deliberately;
- whether locally made Nepalese paper and imported paper occupied different or overlapping operational niches.

Thus:

`paper use = availability × procurement × stock duration × operation-specific selection`.

This is a stronger historical model than assuming the paper support simply reflects national origin or prestige.

Mechanism:

`PAPER_ALLOCATION_AS_RESEARCH_INFRASTRUCTURE`.

## 10. Revised article sequence v0.2

### 1. Himalayan manufacture: reconstruct the production sequence

Hodgson's 1831/32 account and surviving/sample evidence establish the locally successful paper regime.

### 2. 1829/1833: finished sheet travels, pulp reconstruction fails

The cleanest empirical demonstration that fibre/material identity does not preserve operational paper properties.

### 3. 1841: move makers, not only matter

Darjeeling reconstructs embodied process and exposes hidden variables in the printed recipe.

### 4. 1841–42: property optimisation produces new failures

Iron, washing, mildew and bleaching make process calibration visible.

### 5. 1842: Serampore machine-interface mismatch

Hand-sheet strength properties become machine-web problems.

### 6. Calcutta: aggregate, compare, sample, price, issue and redistribute

Paper supply is plural and grade-specific; physical musters make quality auditable.

### 7. Kathmandu and Darjeeling: mixed stocks inside Hodgson's research practice

Watermarks on actual Hodgson-associated drawings demonstrate maker/mark plurality and coexistence with local Nepalese paper.

### 8. Stock time: old paper in later research operations

The 1821-watermark/c.1844 drawing becomes the clearest lead for reconstructing inventory duration and recirculation.

### 9. Britain and Europe: reconstruct makers and technologies without national flattening

Whatman, Joynson, other British makers, Dutch/Continental papers, imitated brands, laid/wove/machine states and differentiated grades remain separate historical objects.

### 10. Object census: compare paper allocation across research operations

Only after sheet-level watermark, formation, dimensions and medium are recorded should the article infer operation-specific selection.

## 11. Strong article paragraph v0.2

**Hodgson's paper history contains two different kinds of memory. The first was manufactured into the sheet. Himalayan bark sent to Britain as dried pulp could retain its nominal material identity while losing the process history that had made Nepalese paper tough, flexible and printable; Charles Cowan's failed remanufacture in 1833 made that divergence visible. The second memory accumulated after manufacture. Hodgson's surviving drawings were made on mixed imported stocks carrying Whatman, Joynson, Moinier's Patent, Newey/Darnford, Britannia and dated watermarks, while Raj Man Singh's Kathmandu architectural series includes paper whose watermark predates the assigned drawing date by more than two decades. A research sheet therefore arrived at an operation already carrying two histories: the sequence that produced its material properties and the sequence of procurement, shipment, storage and allocation that placed that particular stock under a pencil, pen or brush. Paper was not a neutral surface moving through Hodgson's science. It was a process-retentive and stock-retentive component of the research system.**

## Bottom line

The article's paper object is now sharper than in v0.1. Paper carries **manufacturing history**, but Hodgson's archive also shows that sheets carry **supply and stock history** before they enter research use. The relevant unit is therefore neither a plant species nor a national paper type nor a prestige maker. It is a historically specific sheet produced by one regime, circulated through another, held for some duration, and allocated to a particular operation. The 1833 Cowan failure makes process history visible; the mixed watermarks and the 1821/1844 gap make stock history visible. Together they allow a materially exact history of how paper entered Himalayan knowledge production.
