# Hodgson paper census pilot queue v0.1

Date: 2026-08-31

Purpose: select a deliberately heterogeneous 15-case pilot for the sheet-level protocol before archive-scale extraction. This queue distinguishes **catalogue-described paper** from **physically observed paper**. At present most BL cases below are catalogue-level candidates, not material diagnoses.

Status vocabulary:

- `CATALOGUE_ONLY` — repository description gives useful material metadata but no adequate object image inspected yet.
- `IMAGE_NEEDED` — ordinary full-sheet recto/verso image would materially advance analysis.
- `TRANSMITTED_LIGHT_NEEDED` — watermark/formation claims require transmitted-light or equivalent imaging.
- `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE` — dimensions, fibre, sizing, mounting, edge or formation questions justify in-person inspection if feasible.
- `COMPARATIVE_CONTROL` — included primarily to compare support regimes rather than because it is individually exceptional.

## Pilot 01 — 1831 Hodgson paper-making letter to Prinsep

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/6, item 6, ff. 77–81  
**Date:** Dec. 1831  
**Content:** Hodgson to H. T. Prinsep regarding a sample of Nepali paper and papermaking in Nepal; attached account of paper-making.  
**Catalogue support:** machine-made paper, very fine condition.  
**Why in pilot:** the intellectual object is explicitly about Nepali paper while the surviving letter itself is on machine-made paper. This is an ideal Wilson-style anti-binary case: content and support cross paper regimes.  
**Questions:**
- maker/watermark?
- wove or laid?
- whether attached papermaking account is same stock as letter?
- fold/attachment pattern?
- was a physical sample originally enclosed and later separated?
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `TRANSMITTED_LIGHT_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

## Pilot 02 — 1841 Darjeeling papermaker-transfer report

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/6, item 9, ff. 181–184  
**Date:** July 1841  
**Content:** A. Campbell to J. R. Colvin reporting arrival of five Nepalis skilled in local papermaking, experiments and salaries.  
**Why:** technology transfer described on a paper document; potentially compare support chosen for administrative reporting with the technology being transferred.  
**Questions:** machine/imported/local support? watermark? ruling? folds from transmission?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `TRANSMITTED_LIGHT_NEEDED`.

## Pilot 03 — 1843 bill for 1,012 bundles of Nepali paper

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/12, item 3, f. 117  
**Date:** 1843  
**Content:** bill to Dr Wallich for 1,012 bundles (ḍhyāp), 10 sheets per bundle, different prices, wax cloth, thread and twine.  
**Why:** strongest commodity/procurement object. The bill itself can be compared to the paper being commodified.  
**Questions:** what support is the bill written on? paper category, dimensions, watermark, folds, ink, calculation layout; does the bill encode grades without preserving physical samples?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

## Pilot 04 — 1827 manuscript-production bills

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/12, item 8, ff. 128–130  
**Date:** Apr./Jun./Nov. 1827  
**Content:** copying, binding, bundling, packaging, shipment, paper/material purchases, carpenters, labourers, porters.  
**Why:** paper becomes an accounting instrument for the transformation of loose textual matter into transportable objects.  
**Questions:** one stock or multiple? reused sheets? ruling/table structure? folds? pasted receipts? handwriting/media changes?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

## Pilot 05 — 1827 list with leaves/verses/prices

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/12, item 25, ff. 179–182  
**Date:** 1827  
**Content:** manuscript list recording leaf counts, śloka counts and prices.  
**Why:** paper as counting/comparison instrument. Can test whether layout/ruling/column dimensions materially structure auditability.  
**Questions:** pre-ruled? hand-drawn columns? fold-defined columns? paper size relative to amount of tabular data?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `COMPARATIVE_CONTROL`.

## Pilot 06 — 1829 seven-box receipt

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/12, item 15, f. 147  
**Date:** Mar. 1829  
**Content:** receipt for seven boxes of Nepali/Tibetan books: 103 loose leaves, 13 packages, 101 copies.  
**Why:** container-level audit encoded on one paper witness. Test relation between physical sheet and quantities that define a shipment as complete.  
**Questions:** receipt support, folds, seals, annotations, later archival marks, whether totals visibly corrected.  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`.

## Pilot 07 — palm-leaf documents recopied onto Nepali paper

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/29, item 14, ff. 131–154  
**Content:** copies of 24 land documents originally on palm leaf, recopied onto Nepali handmade paper.  
**Why:** controlled substrate conversion. Excellent case for separating textual identity from material affordance and portable surrogate production.  
**Questions:** did copy format imitate palm-leaf proportions? how were multiple documents distributed across sheets? ruling? fold/bundle traces? fibre/sheet formation?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

## Pilot 08 — inscription copies on haritāl-pasted Nepali paper

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/29, item 20, ff. 162–163  
**Content:** copies of two historical inscriptions on haritāl-pasted Nepali handmade paper.  
**Why:** composite paper object with deliberately modified surface. This is exactly the kind of case for which `paper = substrate` is inadequate.  
**Questions:** what is pasted/coated, where, how thick, what optical/surface effect, what media behaviour, whether treatment supports copying/visual fidelity/authentication?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

## Pilot 09 — originals/copies/seals on mixed supports

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/55, item 20, ff. 114–172  
**Date range:** 1802–1854  
**Content:** original and copied royal orders/official notes, mostly Nepali handmade paper with some machine-made paper; red/black seals.  
**Why:** best controlled corpus for testing whether support correlates with original/copy/seal/evidentiary status without assuming it.  
**Questions:** same document type across different supports? seal interaction? folds? attachment? support change in copies?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`, `COMPARATIVE_CONTROL`.

## Pilot 10 — 1834 Governor-General letter on thick and thin imported papers

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/1, item 4, ff. 216–223  
**Date:** 20 Dec. 1834  
**Content:** Hodgson to Governor-General; extensive corrections/addenda; local ink.  
**Catalogue support:** two types of imported paper, thick and thin; dimensions ca. 24.8 × 20 cm.  
**Why:** controlled within-object material differentiation. Potentially shows different paper types entering one writing/revision workflow.  
**Questions:** which folios are thick vs thin? same watermark family? are additions on one stock? paper stock change correlated with drafting sequence?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `TRANSMITTED_LIGHT_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

## Pilot 11 — 1828 Buddhist essay on two machine-made paper types

## Pilot 12 — Kalachakramandala drawing on machine-made broad-sheet

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/28, item 11, ff. 42–43  
**Content:** Kalachakramandala drawing.  
**Catalogue support:** machine-made broad-sheet paper.  
**Why:** large-format drawing support, ideal for testing paper size, surface, media and possible edge/countermark location.

## Pilot 13 — animal and bone list on Nepali handmade paper

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/58, item 3, ff. 4–14  
**Content:** list of animals and animal bones; some pencil.  
**Catalogue support:** Nepali handmade paper.  
**Why:** decisive cross-domain control. Nepali paper is here inside natural-history work, directly falsifying a clean manuscript/science support divide.  
**Questions:** pencil response/tooth, tabular organization, folding, dimensions, fibres, repeated handling wear, labels/numbers correlated with surviving zoological objects?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`, `COMPARATIVE_CONTROL`.

## Pilot 14 — mixed-support rough Mahayana notes

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/94, item 1  
**Content:** rough Mahayana notes.  
**Catalogue support:** Nepali handmade + machine-made paper.  
**Why:** a rough working corpus mixing supports, valuable for testing whether support choice follows availability, insertion chronology, copy-stage or operation.  
**Questions:** are supports interleaved or sequential? same hand/ink? reused scraps? sheet dimensions? later binding reordered them?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`, `COMPARATIVE_CONTROL`.

## Pilot 15 — original shipment bundle list, separated leaves

**Shelfmark:** Mss Eur Hodgson/94, item 7, ff. 40–44  
**Content:** separated leaves recording contents of manuscripts shipped from Kathmandu; preserves bundle numbers/categories for an original shipment.  
**Why:** combines paper object, identifier system, separation history and container reconstruction. A direct test of composition/decomposition.  
**Questions:** do physical folds/edges/tears recover original packet relation? are bundle numbers written before/after separation? same stock across leaves? later foliation?  
**Status:** `CATALOGUE_ONLY`, `IMAGE_NEEDED`, `PHYSICAL_CHECK_HIGH_VALUE`.

# Deliberate gaps in pilot v0.1

The pilot is currently BL-heavy because the BL inventory exposes unusually rich paper metadata. It still lacks:

1. a securely identified **Hodgson zoological drawing on Whatman paper** with an image of the mark;
2. a drawing with `J WHATMAN TURKEY MILL` vs `J WHATMAN` distinction;
3. a ZSL specimen ticket/label with physical imagery;
4. a Kew botanical drying/interleaving sheet or paper instruction directly tied to Hodgson;
5. a RAS manuscript wrapper/leaf with high-resolution transmitted-light images;
6. a genuine ordinary digital-image negative case where watermark absence can be tested against better imaging.

These are next acquisition targets rather than reasons to delay the BL pilot.

# Pilot design principle

The first successful paper census should not maximize item count. It should maximize **contrast in operations and paper states**:

- imported vs local;
- thick vs thin;
- handmade vs machine-described;
- drawing vs writing vs accounting vs copying;
- original vs copy;
- plain sheet vs treated/pasted/composite support;
- single-stock vs mixed-stock dossier;
- textual vs natural-history use;
- working object vs shipment/receipt object.

Only after these cases survive the observation → technical interpretation → historical inference distinction should the census scale up.
