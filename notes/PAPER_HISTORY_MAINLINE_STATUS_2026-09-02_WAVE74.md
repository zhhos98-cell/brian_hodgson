## Current paper object

The article now follows paper through materially distinct conversions:

`fibre/raw material`
-> `manufacturing process`
-> `pulp / sheet commodity state`
-> `counting/bundling system`
-> `procurement/acquisition`
-> `package formation`
-> `transport route / custody handoff / freight accounting`
-> `stock/storage time`
-> `cutting/folding/trimming`
-> `working-format allocation`
-> `writing/drawing/copying`
-> `support substitution / recopying`
-> `binding/bundling/packaging`
-> `container/shipment`
-> `custody/rebinding`
-> `modern catalogue object`.

Paper carries at least nine analytically separable histories:

1. manufacture/process;
2. commodity/material-state;
3. count/metrology;
4. acquisition/supply;
5. logistics/route/custody;
6. stock time;
7. format/work allocation;
8. copying/containerization;
9. later custody/catalogue.

Core equation:

`paper object = manufacture × material state × count metrology × acquisition × packaging × route/custody × stock time × format conversion × work allocation × later custody`.

New rule from Waves 73–74:

**an experimental material has a history before the experiment.**

Mechanisms:

## F. Rankine 1838/1839 explains the material stakes of the route comparison

Robert Rankine, *Notes on the Medical Topography of the Districts of Sarun*, report dated 22 January 1838, printed Calcutta 1839, directly describes the transport environment of Sarun/Chumparun shortly before the pulp shipment.

Secure observations:

- Great and Little Gunduck navigable for small boats through the year;
- Great Gunduck navigation nevertheless difficult, with shifting sands and dry-season stretches suitable only for very small boats;
- bad-road remarks explicitly apply to `Govind Gunge` and Bettiah roads;
- high road to Chumparun via Sutter Ghat `hardly passable` in many places for five or six months of the year;
- bridges urgently needed to make it passable for wheeled carriages in rainy season;
- cross roads severely neglected;
- in many places for roughly six months produce had to move to market by human head-load, with resulting expense.

This is context, not proof of the actual pulp route.

Correct inference:

`route cost = road condition × season × bridge/ferry availability × transport mode × package handling × custody/security`.

Mechanisms:

- `ROUTE_COST_IS_INFRASTRUCTURE_DEPENDENT`;
- `SEASONAL_ROAD_PASSABILITY_SHAPES_MATERIAL_CARRIAGE`;
- `WATER_CARRIAGE_HAS_SEASONAL_AND_CHANNEL_CONSTRAINTS`;
- `HUMAN_CARRIAGE_IS_A_FALLBACK_TRANSPORT_TECHNOLOGY`.

Do not claim the pulp itself moved by coolies or at Rankine's cited twenty-seer human-load limit.

## I. Logistics now modifies the process-history argument

Earlier strong proposition:

`finished sheet embodies manufacturing process history`.

Waves 73–74 add:

`experimental pulp embodies preparation + packaging + transit + custody history before remanufacture`.

Thus possible material state at test site is a function of:

`preparation`
× `package construction`
× `storage/transit duration`
× `moisture/exposure`
× `route/mode`
× `handling/custody`.

Current evidence does not prove deterioration in the 1842 shipment.

But the paper-history model must treat transport as a potentially material operation, especially because the 1833 British report already considered deterioration of transferred pulp a live explanation for failed remanufacture.

Mechanisms:

- `TRANSPORT_HISTORY_CAN_MODIFY_EXPERIMENTAL_MATERIAL`;
- `LOGISTICS_IS_PART_OF_MATERIAL_PROCESS_HISTORY`;
- `EXPERIMENT_BEGINS_BEFORE_THE_TEST_SITE`.

## Current strongest proposition

**Hodgson's paper history now begins before the sheet and before the laboratory. Himalayan fibre was converted into pulp, pulp into transport packages, packages into administratively routed and costed consignments, and only then into experimental material at another site. In March 1842 twenty-four packages of Nepalese paper pulp left the Nepal Residency for Calcutta through a forwarding system in Chumparun where land carriage and river carriage were explicit alternatives. Contemporary district evidence shows why this mattered: roads could be nearly unusable for months, bridges constrained wheeled traffic, and river navigation had its own seasonal limits. The experimental sample therefore arrived with a logistics history as well as a manufacturing history. Paper properties, quantities, formats and later archival states all have to be reconstructed from these successive material operations rather than from a single geographical label such as 'Nepalese paper'.**
