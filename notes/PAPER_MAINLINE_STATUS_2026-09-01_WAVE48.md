## Current compression of the argument

The paper article now follows material versioning across four recursively connected scales:

1. `material -> sheet`;
2. `information/problem -> manuscript documentary state`;
3. `manuscript -> print -> annotated physical print copy -> revised/reprinted state`;
4. `version family -> historical container/control system -> rebinding/dispersion -> modern relation reconstruction`.

Combined formula:

`paper-object history = production process × stock/supply × work allocation × documentary state × print/copy-specific revision × identifier/provenance surfaces × custody/reclassification`.

The central historical question remains operation-dependent identity:

**what survives when a material, document, printed text or archival relation is remade into another usable state, and what has to be materially reconstructed?**

## New Wave 46 distinction: problem genealogy is not textual stemma

The 1825 `Memoir on the Army of Nepal` securely predates the 1833 military-tribe paper as an administrative/recruitment problem state.

It concerns:

- Nepal military organisation;
- military manpower;
- absorption/recruitment into British service;
- William Bayley's queries.

The military problem continues through an 1825 rough report and an 1828 rough Bayley-query memoir.

But current catalogue evidence does not show the later Khasa/Magar/Gurung `thar` classificatory apparatus in these 1825/1828 states.

The first currently identified explicit classificatory source pool is:

`1831 vol.7/20` — rough Hodgson pen/pencil notes with Khasa thars, Bara-Magarant, Magar names and Gurung clans.

Then:

`October 1832 vol.6/1` — fair tabular paper;

`undated vol.9/10` — parallel fair copy;

`January/May 1833` — society-reading/print state.

Thus:

`1825 administrative problem genealogy`
-> `1831-32 classificatory documentary recoding`
-> `1833 learned-society argument`.

Mechanisms:

- `PROBLEM_GENEALOGY_NOT_TEXTUAL_STEMMA`
- `ADMINISTRATIVE_PROBLEM_TO_CLASSIFICATORY_RECODING`
- `POLICY_CONTINUITY_ACROSS_DOCUMENTARY_STATES`.

Do not write `1825 memo -> direct draft of 1833 article` without passage/heading evidence.

Image-level inspection of the publicly linked 1825 rough-draft pages was attempted in Wave 48 work, but the Cambridge image server returned 502. Absence of visible `thar` evidence therefore cannot be treated as negative proof.

## New Wave 47 result: 1833 print remains revisable in 1874

The 1874 `Essays` reprints the military-tribe paper at Part II pp.37–44.

The collection's `NOTICE` states that manuscript additions/corrections in Hodgson's own earlier printed copies were introduced into the collected reprint and could represent phases of his views spanning nearly thirty years.

For the military-tribe paper, the explicit 1874 Part II correction apparatus records two changes:

1. p.39 `caste` -> `cast` in the Magar/Gurung physiognomic passage;
2. p.40 `some` -> `about 100` in the statement about the Khas/Gorkhali expansion from Gorkha under Prithvi Narayan.

The correction apparatus lists no changes to pp.42–44, where the detailed classificatory table sits.

Current interpretation:

- classificatory architecture is persistent in the explicit correction apparatus;
- physiognomic vocabulary is disambiguated (`cast`, not social `caste`);
- historical dating is made more precise (`about 100 years ago`).

Mechanisms:

- `PRINT_STATE_REMAINS_REVISABLE`
- `CORRECTION_LIST_AS_VERSION_DIFFERENCE_SURFACE`
- `PHYSIOGNOMIC_SEMANTIC_DISAMBIGUATION_IN_REPRINT`
- `RETROSPECTIVE_CHRONOLOGICAL_PRECISION`.

Do not infer full diplomatic identity of the 1833 and 1874 tables until line-by-line comparison.

## New Wave 48 result: publication branches at the level of the physical copy

Joshi's 2002 bibliography records several physical copies of Hodgson's 1874 `Essays` bearing handwritten authorial corrections/annotations.

### Branch A — `own copy corrected`

Reported characteristics:

- pencil annotation;
- black-ink annotation, sometimes over pencil;
- corrected/annotated map;
- annotation beginning by at least 1876;
- references reaching after 1890.

### Branch B — Alderley October 1874 copy

Title-page marks reported as:

`BHHodgson / Alderley Oct 1874`.

Joshi's Part II annotated-page list includes p.37, the opening page of the military-tribe article.

Exact annotation content remains unresolved.

### Branch C — another annotated 1874 copy

Its Part II page list does **not** include pp.37–44.

### Branch D — Townshend presentation copy

Inscribed by Hodgson at Alderley, 1 October 1874, with a different limited annotation history.

Therefore:

`same printed edition -> divergent copy-specific manuscript layers`.

Mechanisms:

- `POSTPUBLICATION_ANNOTATED_PRINT_BRANCHING`
- `PRINT_COPY_BECOMES_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION`
- `COPY_SPECIFIC_ANNOTATION_DIVERGENCE`
- `PRINT_MANUSCRIPT_RECURSION`
- `PRINTED_FINALITY_REVERSED_BY_ANNOTATION`.

The Joshi PDF page-image screenshots were attempted but returned 403; use his observations as secondary eyewitness/cataloguing evidence until physical copies are inspected.
