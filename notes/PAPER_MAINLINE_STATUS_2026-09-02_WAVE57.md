## Current compression of the article

The paper follows recursive material/documentary versioning across five connected scales:

1. `material/fibre/process -> sheet properties`;
2. `stock/workflow -> paper allocation`;
3. `problem/information -> documentary state -> multilingual/support branching`;
4. `manuscript -> print -> manuscript-on-print -> serial/collected reprint -> copy-specific annotation branching`;
5. `version family -> historical control surfaces -> institutional rebinding -> modern relation repair`.

Combined model:

`paper-object history = production process × stock/supply × work allocation × documentary state × locus-specific revision × paratext placement × copy-specific annotation × identifier/provenance surfaces × survival/custody/reclassification`.

The central question remains:

**what survives when a material, document, printed text or archival relation is remade into another operational state, and what has to be materially reconstructed?**

Waves 54–57 sharpen that question in two directions:

- later paratext can date and revise an earlier paper while itself acquiring a version history;
- the historical document graph can contain attested documents whose surviving physical witnesses are missing or unidentified.

## H. Editorial incorporation is locus-specific, not a whole-document transform

The 1874 NOTICE states that Hodgson's manuscript additions in his own earlier printed copies were introduced into the reprint text and places the Phoenix serial project inside that transmission process.

Earlier working model:

`printed copy -> Hodgson handwriting -> Phoenix incorporation -> 1874 collected text`.

Wave 56 adds an important constraint:

`introduced into the text` did not mean typographic homogenization at every locus.

At the recruitment locus, an intervention already present as a retrospective 1857 footnote remains visibly paratextual in 1874.

Therefore the same editorial remaking can produce different local outcomes:

- handwritten marginal addition -> running text;
- prior footnote -> retained footnote;
- base reading -> revised base reading;
- late change -> printed correction apparatus.

Mechanisms:

- `EDITORIAL_INCORPORATION_IS_LOCUS_SPECIFIC_NOT_DOCUMENT_GLOBAL`
- `PARATEXT_PLACEMENT_IS_A_VERSION_VARIABLE`
- `DIRECT_PRESENCE_DISTINCT_FROM_EXACT_WORDING`
- `PARATEXT_SURVIVES_SELECTIVE_TEXTUAL_INTEGRATION`.

The operational unit of revision is therefore often the **locus/layer on a particular surface**, not the whole document or edition.

## I. Asynchronous revision remains central, now with a stronger material formulation

The 1857 state already demonstrated:

`older descriptive/historical base text + new policy paratext`.

By 1874 the same work can contain simultaneously:

- old readings carried from earlier print states;
- body readings changed through reprint transmission;
- an 1857 retrospective footnote still typographically marked as footnote;
- a printed correction apparatus proposing later changes;
- physical copies that Hodgson annotates again by hand.

Better formulation:

**revision attaches to loci and layers on particular paper/print surfaces. A remade edition can integrate one intervention into running text, preserve another as paratext, leave another unchanged, and reopen the resulting copy to handwriting.**

Mechanisms:

- `REVISION_IS_LAYERED_NOT_WHOLE_DOCUMENT_SYNCHRONOUS`
- `PRINTED_PAGE_CAN_HOST_DIFFERENT_VERSION_TIMES_SIMULTANEOUSLY`
- `PRINT_COPY_BECOMES_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION`
- `EDITION_IDENTITY_NOT_VERSION_IDENTITY`.

## Current strongest article formulation

**Hodgson's paper world was not a sequence in which information moved once from manuscript to print and then into an archive. Problems, classifications, corrections and relations were repeatedly attached to particular paper surfaces, recopied, printed, reopened to handwriting, selectively reincorporated into print, branched across individual copies, and later dispersed or reconnected by archival control systems. Because different loci and relations survived these remakings differently, the history has to be reconstructed as a material version graph containing both surviving objects and missing but historically attested nodes.**
