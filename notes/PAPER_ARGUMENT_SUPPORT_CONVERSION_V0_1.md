# Paper argument module — support conversion at knowledge conversion v0.1

Date: 2026-08-31

Status: article-facing synthesis from Cambridge Wave 28 and paired-case Wave 30. This note does not replace `PAPER_MATERIAL_REGIMES_ARGUMENT_V0_2.md`; it adds a third mechanism to the paper article.

## Three material histories now visible

The paper article can distinguish three analytically separate histories.

### 1. Production history

The 1829/1833 Nepal-sheet versus British-remade-pulp experiment shows that paper properties retain manufacturing sequence.

`material source != reproducible paper affordance`

Mechanism:

`PROCESS_HISTORY_EMBODIED_IN_SHEET`.

### 2. Supply and stock history

Watermarks and mixed imported/local stocks show that a sheet reaches research use through procurement, transport, storage and allocation.

`paper at use = manufactured sheet + supply route + stock duration`.

Mechanisms:

- `MIXED_STOCK_OPERATIONAL_ECOLOGY`
- `PAPER_STOCK_RETENTION_OR_REUSE`
- `PAPER_ALLOCATION_AS_RESEARCH_INFRASTRUCTURE`.

### 3. Conversion history

Cambridge cross-references preserve explicit source-to-translation and source-to-abstract chains in which the informational relation continues while support changes.

Examples:

- Kathmandu population data: Nepali handmade local-language source -> machine-made English survey/copy;
- yearly revenue: Nepali handmade list -> machine-made English comparative working state;
- official salaries: Nepali handmade original -> machine-made English abstract;
- Newar festival cycle: Nepali handmade Nepali/Newari source -> machine-made English notebook;
- Tarai revenue contracts: Nepali handmade original -> machine-made English copy;
- older land documents: palm leaf -> Nepali handmade copy -> machine-made Urdu translation.

Mechanism:

`SUPPORT_SWITCH_AT_KNOWLEDGE_CONVERSION`.

---

## Core claim

A Hodgson paper object should not be analysed only by asking what paper it is made from.

Ask instead:

1. how was the sheet manufactured;
2. how did that stock reach the research setting;
3. how long and where was it held before use;
4. which operation was it allocated to;
5. whether a knowledge object changed support as it moved into another linguistic, analytical or institutional state;
6. how later custody changed access to the material evidence.

Combined model:

`paper-object history = production history × supply/stock history × allocation × conversion history × post-use custody`.

This is a material biography, but the historical point is operational rather than biographical metaphor.

---

## Why support conversion matters

The crucial observation is not that English writing occurs on machine-made paper and Nepali writing on handmade paper. Counterexamples rule out that simple mapping.

The stronger phenomenon is relational:

**in some explicit source-to-derivative chains, changing the epistemic state of information also changes its material support.**

A source can become:

- an abstract;
- a translation;
- a table;
- a survey list;
- a report;
- a forwarding manifest;
- a notebook entry.

The derivative remains connected to the source but gains different operational affordances.

A support switch can accompany changes in:

- sheet dimensions and writing area;
- correction density;
- pencil/ink layering;
- tabulation;
- headings and indexing;
- notebook/binding incorporation;
- portability;
- filing compatibility;
- copying/publication readiness;
- the actors able to read and use the object.

Thus material conversion is part of making information newly operable.

---

## Strong formulation for the article

**Hodgson's archive did not merely preserve information on different kinds of paper. It repeatedly remade information as a different paper object. A Nepali revenue list could become an English machine-paper table; a Newar festival account could become an English notebook; a palm-leaf land record could pass through a Nepal-paper copy into a machine-paper Urdu translation. In these chains, informational continuity coexisted with material discontinuity. Translation and abstraction were therefore not immaterial acts performed on stable content. They produced new documentary states with new surfaces for correction, tabulation, binding, circulation and institutional use. Paper entered the conversion itself.**

---

## HPS payoff

This offers a narrower claim than `paper shapes knowledge`.

The mechanism is:

`epistemic transformation can include support transformation`.

That claim can be tested object-by-object.

It also connects the manufacturing experiment to the documentary archive without analogy alone.

In manufacturing:

`same fibre -> different process -> different paper properties`.

In documentary work:

`related information -> different documentary operation -> different material state`.

Both cases show that identity is operation-dependent.

The article can therefore use paper to join technological and documentary histories through a common question:

**what survives when a thing is converted into another operational state, and what must be remade?**

---

## Necessary limits

Do not write:

`Nepali paper = local knowledge`

or:

`machine paper = colonial knowledge`.

The archive does not support those symbolic equations.

Counterexamples include English translation on Nepali handmade paper, Nepali text on machine-made paper and mixed-support dossiers.

The claim is instead probabilistic and processual:

`support selection is conditioned by availability, actor, document state, intended operation and conversion pathway`.

A denominator-complete census is required before frequency claims are published.

## Best paired cases for physical inspection

1. `vol.58/11 -> vol.20/5`: Newar festival source to English notebook.
2. `vol.53/16 -> vol.14/14 ff.152-159`: salaries original to English abstract.
3. `vol.53/1 -> vol.14/6`: Tarai revenue original to English copy.
4. `vol.59/6 -> vol.6/3 / vol.3/15`: population data to English survey states.
5. `vol.29/14 + vol.53/29 -> vol.14/17`: palm-leaf/copy to Urdu translation.

For each pair record:

- dimensions;
- laid/wove/machine structure;
- watermark/countermark;
- thickness;
- colour/surface;
- handwriting;
- ruling/tabulation;
- correction density;
- binding;
- headers/titles;
- evidence for direct copying sequence.

## Bottom line

The paper article now has a third empirical axis beyond manufacture and supply. Hodgson's archive sometimes changes support when it changes the operational state of knowledge. These paired transformations make paper an observable component of translation, abstraction and administrative/scientific recoding. The strongest future article will therefore follow not `Nepali paper versus European paper`, but **paper through conversion**.
