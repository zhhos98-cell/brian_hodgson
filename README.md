## Current research rule

Broad discovery is no longer the default for the Nepal paper article. Open a new search only when the draft exposes a specific factual or citation problem.

The main unresolved items are bounded: Halliday’s initiating directive for the March 1842 pulp shipment, Woodcock’s bill and route decision, the terminal Calcutta receipt, direct evidence for Chamber-of-Commerce receipt/testing, the item-level No. 70 to IOR/F/4/2006/89528 crosswalk, and the exact London chain for Political Consultation No. 147.

Do not identify the twenty-four packages as direct Serampore feedstock without a receipt or forwarding record. Keep underlying letter dates separate from consultation dates. Keep proposed circulation separate from completed receipt.

## Larger Hodgson project

The wider repository contains work on Buddhist manuscripts, zoological specimens and drawings, geographical papers, administrative records, paper technologies, catalogues, household testimonial material and later archive states. These branches remain available when a specific article or source question requires them.

The larger research project asks how Himalayan materials were copied, drawn, counted, packed, sent, received, identified, catalogued and reused after separation from the settings in which they were produced. That broader vocabulary belongs to the research archive. Current article prose should stay with the people, objects, dates and words that can be shown directly in the sources.
