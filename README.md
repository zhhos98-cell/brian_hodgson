# Brian Houghton Hodgson — history of science working repository

This repository treats Brian Houghton Hodgson primarily as a problem in the history of science, natural history, and material knowledge infrastructures.

## Core framing

The central question is not Hodgson as an early Buddhist scholar. Buddhist manuscripts and classifications remain in the archive only insofar as they expose techniques of inscription, paper choice, copying, annotation, tabulation, circulation, authentication, and institutional translation.

The main analytical axes are:

1. **Colonial natural history and classification** — how Hodgson organized Himalayan fauna, specimens, drawings, lists, tables, and descriptions within metropolitan scientific systems.
2. **Paper technologies and material epistemology** — British machine-made wove papers (including Whatman/Towgood), Nepali handmade lokta, Calcutta machine paper, administrative blue paper, watermarks, formats, annotation, recopying, and the epistemic distinctions materially encoded by them.
3. **Circulation and institutional infrastructure** — Hodgson's movement through and against institutions and networks such as the Linnean Society, Zoological Society of London, Kew, the Royal Asiatic Society, British Library collections, and other metropolitan repositories.
4. **Scientific persona and retrospective annotation** — later marginalia and self-correction are treated as practices of revision, evidential control, priority-making, and self-archiving rather than primarily as Buddhist hermeneutics.
5. **Colonial knowledge as paperwork** — paper, copying, correspondence, cataloguing, specimen-description, and archival survival as active components of scientific authority.

## Working title

**Paper Empires: Material Infrastructures and Brian Houghton Hodgson's Epistemic Politics of Colonial Natural History**

Alternative titles can be developed later, but this is the current organizing title because it already appears in the 2025 research materials.

## Buddhism policy for this repo

Buddhist Studies material is compressed to contextual evidence. It should be retained only when it demonstrates a scientific or knowledge-making operation: classification, comparison, manuscript materiality, copying, annotation, evidential hierarchy, authenticity, circulation, or institutional transfer. Historiography centered on Buddhist doctrine, colonial Buddhist Studies as a field in itself, or anachronistic postcolonial self-description is secondary and should not drive the argument.

This is a research reconstruction repository, not merely an email dump. Gmail provenance should be preserved, while duplicated CVs, unrelated proposal bundles, and Buddhist-studies material without relevance to scientific practice should be excluded or listed as checked-and-excluded.
